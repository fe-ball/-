## Central V23 result

The 1945 Soviet winter offensive still breaks tactical fronts, but no historical-form Vistula–Oder collapse occurs. Germany uses preserved mobile reserves, a mature Luftwaffe/jet ISR ecosystem, healthier fuel/rail/industry and repeated elastic-defense/counterstroke behavior to prevent army-group destruction and to impose high operational costs on Soviet mobile echelons.

The USSR remains far from state-level exhaustion. Its constraint becomes the repeatability of high-quality deep operations: experienced tank crews, junior commanders, pilots/flight leaders, engineers, signals and maintainers are harder to replace than tanks and aircraft.

Neither Moscow nor Tokyo denounces the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact by the 1945-04-25 gate. The pact therefore auto-extends. Japan remains an armed-neutral great power rather than entering the anti-Soviet war.

During 1945 the USSR adapts by shifting strategic emphasis away from repeated direct central-Poland races and toward Germany's allied/resource structure. Romania leaves the Axis in the summer; Ploiesti is lost, while German formations avoid a historical-scale southern annihilation. Hungary becomes a political/military crisis later in 1945; German intervention prevents an immediate Romanian-style flip but increases occupation/security costs and weakens the alliance structure.

Germany remains militarily coherent into 1946. It is not historical May-1945 Germany: the state, industry, railways, Luftwaffe, mobile reserves and a mature multi-family jet system survive, although manpower/cadre quality, allied cohesion and fuel flexibility are progressively worse.

In early 1946 Berlin explores a general armistice through multiple real diplomatic channels rather than a West-only separate peace. Sweden/Switzerland carry Western feelers; Japan is a plausible Moscow channel. The first German position is too strong. A second position trades the non-German empire/occupation structure for preservation of the German state, central government and a reduced Wehrmacht.

By the V23 stop line, a General European Armistice is working-closed in the May–June 1946 class. This is not a final peace treaty.

## Armistice working structure

- Germany withdraws from non-German occupied territories under monitored schedules.
- Austria is separated/re-established as an independent state.
- Sudeten territory returns to Czechoslovak sovereignty; minority/property questions remain political issues.
- East Prussia final sovereignty is deferred; the armistice solution emphasizes demilitarization/inspection rather than immediate forced final disposition.
- Germany keeps a central government and a reduced national armed force; immediate total disarmament/occupation is not the armistice mechanism.
- U-boat offensive and V-weapon attacks stop; heavy/offensive assets become subject to concentration/inspection/limitation.
- Reparations are handled mainly through later in-kind/industrial arrangements rather than immediate destruction of German productive capacity.
- The armistice grants no blanket amnesty for war crimes. Neutral/humanitarian access, detainee/forced-labor handling and evidence preservation become immediate post-armistice issues.
- Hitler remains the effective German head of government at the armistice stop line; the long-run political settlement is not closed.
