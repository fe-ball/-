## 1. What V23 closes

V22B left 1945 strategic behavior OPEN. V23 processes that open gate through a second China-peace-route close.

The branch does not turn Germany into an unstoppable power and does not make the USSR collapse. Instead, the 1945–46 war becomes a contest between:

- Germany's preserved army, mobile reserves, healthier industry/fuel/transport and mature jet/ISR/service culture;
- Soviet superior scale, artillery, production and operational art, but with increasingly costly experienced-human-capital replacement.

The result is a long, expensive 1945 in which Soviet territorial advance continues but historical 1945 German military collapse does not occur.

## 2. Soviet operational limit

The USSR remains capable of mobilizing men and replacing tanks/aircraft. State-level exhaustion is not close.

The tighter constraints are qualitative:

- experienced tank crews;
- company/battalion commanders;
- artillery observers;
- combat engineers;
- signals personnel;
- pilots/flight leaders;
- maintainers and recovery specialists;
- fresh second-echelon formations able to convert a breakthrough into a long deep exploitation.

Therefore Soviet offensives remain powerful at the breakthrough point but become harder to repeat at historical 1944–45 tempo.

## 3. 1945 winter / spring center

The Soviet winter offensive occurs, but with smaller bridgeheads, higher prior losses and more careful mobile-force commitment than history.

German behavior centers on active mobile defense rather than immediate grand strategic offensive:

- allow tactical penetrations where necessary;
- keep armored reserves out of initial artillery destruction zones;
- track Soviet mobile/supply axes through persistent reconnaissance;
- counterstroke extended mobile echelons rather than trying to hold every city;
- withdraw before the counterstroke itself becomes trapped.

January and February produce Soviet territorial gains but not a historical Vistula–Oder collapse. German counterstrokes temporarily reduce the operational availability of Soviet tank armies without destroying them.

A later limited German spring attack can still impose costs, but repeated counterstrokes also consume German Panzergrenadiers, junior leaders and other experienced manpower. Germany cannot repeat the method indefinitely for free.

## 4. Soviet–Japanese treaty gate

By 1945-04-25 neither Moscow nor Tokyo denounces the Neutrality Pact.

Reasons include:

- Japan has not attacked the USSR;
- Japan is not at war with the US/UK and remains economically/militarily strong;
- Germany is still an active major military problem;
- denunciation would increase Japanese northern readiness and make Soviet Far-East release harder;
- there is no historical Pacific-war Allied requirement for Soviet entry against Japan.

The pact therefore auto-extends under the branch center.

After extension Moscow can release somewhat more Far-East manpower/specialists westward, but Japan's military strength means the Far East cannot be stripped.

## 5. Summer strategic adaptation

The USSR adapts away from repeatedly maximizing central deep races against German mobile reserves.

The strategic target shifts toward Germany's resource/alliance system.

Romania becomes the principal summer problem:

- Soviet pressure breaks into Romania at high cost;
- Romanian political exit becomes executable;
- Germany prepares withdrawal earlier than history and avoids a historical Jassy–Kishinev-scale destruction of its main southern formations;
- Romania nevertheless leaves the Axis;
- Ploiesti leaves the German economic system.

Ploiesti loss does not instantly stop German war-making because synthetic-fuel infrastructure is much healthier than history. It reduces flexibility: training, theater-to-theater mobile reserve movement and non-priority transport become harder to sustain simultaneously.

## 6. Hungary

After Romania, Hungary becomes both a military and political front.

The branch center does not auto-import the historical March 1944 German occupation. Horthy's regime therefore remains a meaningful political actor into the later crisis.

As Hungarian exit contacts become dangerous, Germany intervenes before a Romanian-style full military flip can occur. A pro-German political arrangement is imposed/maintained, while much of the Hungarian military bureaucracy is retained to avoid immediate army-wide collapse.

This keeps the front alive but converts Hungary from a low-cost ally into a high-cost security/occupation problem.

## 7. Late-1945 technology state

Germany remains a functioning advanced military system.

- Me 262 / E6-D / Ar 234 are mature operational families rather than isolated novelties.
- Bf 109 / Fw 190 and conventional aviation remain essential.
- Type XXI transitions toward meaningful operational use because shipyards, railways, training and fuel are healthier.
- HeS 011-class work may reach low-rate/service-development territory; do not convert this into mass speculative Luft-46 fleets.
- Emergency-collapse programs dependent on historical German desperation are not automatically generated at historical scale.

The USSR continues to field strong piston aircraft and armored systems. New vehicle generations can appear at limited/evaluation scale, but hardware replacement does not erase crew/cadre losses. A full Soviet jet industrial ecosystem is not automatically accelerated to postwar historical outcomes, especially because Germany has not been occupied and its turbine factories/tooling are not captured wholesale.

## 8. 1946 armistice path

The German political objective narrows from preserving an empire to preserving:

- the German homeland from comprehensive occupation;
- the German central state;
- a reduced but real national military establishment.

Berlin uses multiple real-channel feelers:

- Sweden/Stockholm toward Britain;
- Switzerland/Bern for confidential multilateral contact;
- Japan as a plausible Moscow communication channel.

A West-only peace is not the center. The armistice concept is general/all-front.

The first German offer is too strong. The second accepts withdrawal from non-German occupied territories and significant military limitation in exchange for non-occupation/state continuity.

This becomes the basis of a General European Armistice in the 1946 May–June class.

## 9. What the armistice does not settle

It does not automatically settle:

- final borders;
- East Prussia;
- permanent military ceilings;
- final reparations;
- war-crimes responsibility at the top political level;
- French political reconstruction;
- German postwar regime evolution;
- later Soviet/Japanese/Chinese strategic rivalry.

The route stops before those long-tail outcomes are canonized.
