# IJN FLEET / AIRCRAFT / MARINE GT — 1943-44 CONSOLIDATION

This file records session-level closes and working closes. Detailed inherited technical files remain authoritative where not explicitly superseded.

## Battleships

- Yamato / Musashi: fully commissioned, trained first-line ships.
- Mutsu: historical 8 Jun 1943 explosion does **not** recur in the central branch; ship survives.
- Nagato/Mutsu remain valuable first-line/strategic-reserve heavy units.
- Kongo class: retained first-line longer than slower older battleships because speed keeps fleet value high.
- Fuso/Yamashiro and Ise/Hyuga: progressively shift from first-line combat to training/strategic reserve during 1943-46; no historical emergency aviation-battleship conversion.
- Shinano: remains Yamato-class battleship, launched/fitting in the 1943 working clock; no Midway-driven carrier conversion.
- No.111: historical scrapping is not imported. Low-priority construction is resumed as a long-horizon hedge against US heavy-battleship programs.
- Later Yamato hulls: no automatic construction.

## Carriers

No historical loss-replacement panic.

- Akagi/Kaga/Soryu/Hiryu/Shokaku/Zuikaku survive.
- Taiho proceeds.
- No.800: deliberate standard medium fleet carrier line, not historical emergency mass Unryu production.
- 801/802: improved heavy/armored carrier bridge designs remain current planning.
- A separate next-generation large fleet-carrier replacement requirement begins around 1943 so Akagi/Kaga can leave the first line around 1946-47 rather than waiting for obsolescence.
- Historical mass emergency liner/seaplane-carrier conversions are not automatic. Taigei remains submarine-support oriented rather than automatically becoming Ryuho; Chitose/Chiyoda remain valuable seaplane/air-support hulls; later merchant conversions are sharply reduced.

## Destroyer architecture

Three 1943 lines:
1. **Ko / torpedo line**: Kagero -> Yugumo. Yugumo is the mature pure-steam fleet-torpedo destroyer and ends that design generation.
2. **Otsu / air-defense line**: Akizuki. Prewar planned build is largely worth completing because carrier fleet remains large.
3. **Hei / high-speed technology line**: Shimakaze.

V21 Shimakaze interpretation:
- not merely historical high-pressure-steam one-off;
- high-pressure steam plus E6-class marine GT / waste-heat combined propulsion experimental fleet destroyer;
- ~40 kt remains a test/performance goal, but value is fuel efficiency, acceleration, redundancy, electrical margin and future machinery architecture rather than ever-higher record speed.

Working marine-GT clock:
- C1 exhaust-boiler integration: closed by early 1942 working clock;
- E6 marine M2: 1942 working close;
- C2/C3 E7/combined-train readiness: 1942 H2 working close;
- C4 sea trial: 1943 working close;
- C5 combatant installation: Shimakaze branch;
- C6 1,000-1,500h fleet proof: ongoing into 1944;
- C7 full standardization: still a gate.

Follow-on direction:
- a small advanced batch (order-of-four scale working concept) before full standardization;
- ultimate standard DD converges Yugumo endurance/torpedo ability + Akizuki AA/fire control + Shimakaze combined machinery;
- approximate design study band ~2,900-3,200 t standard, ~36.5-37.5 kt, 10cm dual-purpose six-gun class, eight 61cm tubes, stronger ASW/electrical/command systems.

Matsu/Tachibana emergency line: **not generated**.

## Cruisers and larger surface combatants

- Agano four-ship class completes as last conventional destroyer-squadron flagship generation.
- Oyodo completes; second ship is not automatically revived because high-speed aerial reconnaissance reduces the special submarine-scout-cruiser niche.
- Ibuki completes as a cruiser rather than historical emergency carrier conversion; sister is not automatically completed because the surviving cruiser inventory is large.
- B-65 remains a plausible low-rate large-cruiser program because US Alaska-type threat may still exist; first generation remains steam rather than forcing immature large-scale GT machinery.

## Fleet auxiliaries

Fast replenishment/oiler ships are high-value candidates for early large-ship combined-propulsion scaling after destroyer proof because they have machinery-volume margin and can test long-duration fleet operation without gambling a carrier hull.

## Aircraft submarines

Do not delete the concept; normalize it:
- one-aircraft long-range cruiser submarines;
- improved one-aircraft ships;
- two-aircraft aviation cruisers as a natural main step;
- limited three-aircraft large boats as later strategic/scout growth.

The embarked aircraft is primarily a special submarine-carried float reconnaissance aircraft. Its performance may converge near historical M6A Seiran class, but strategic attack is a secondary use discovered from capability, not the initial overriding requirement.

## H7Y / long-range naval reconnaissance

H7Y is not a mass combat flying boat. V21 working close is a very small 2-4 aircraft ultra-long-range special-recon/navigation force. It conducts oceanic, weather, route, radio-environment and distant approach reconnaissance; routine peacetime penetration of US national airspace is not assumed.
