# V7 METHOD AND CAUSAL GUARD

## User intent
This worldline is to be physically and economically grounded. Materials, production, machine capacity, transport, manpower, ammunition, maintenance, incentives, institutions and command behavior constrain outcomes.

Do not reject an outcome merely because it feels “too much.” Reject or limit it only when the causal chain, capacity, geometry, time, logistics, organization or incentives do not support it.

## Difference-led history
Use the old branch as baseline and update only what is causally affected.

Preferred form:
**old value / historical state → causal delta → new value**.

Do not rebuild every army from zero at every gate unless needed.

## No ghosts
A destroyed or tactically dissolved unit cannot reappear later at full strength. A surviving unit cannot vanish merely because the narrative moved on.

Track:
- surviving personnel,
- wounded / separated / recoverable personnel,
- named formations versus local tactical groups,
- vehicles/guns in inventory versus operational-ready,
- equipment recovered after holding the battlefield,
- transferred units and exactly which saved CEQ follows the transferred subformation.

## Behavioral rule for retreat and resistance
A unit whose orders/threshold say “withdraw when X happens” generally withdraws when X happens, whether X is reached a little earlier or later. A unit ordered to hold or counterattack fights when the enemy arrives.

But “it fights” or “it retreats” is not the whole explanation. Timing still changes:
- warning time,
- command-message delivery,
- road congestion,
- rear-guard separation,
- artillery evacuation,
- casualty evacuation,
- bridge/rail demolition,
- whether a local unit is cut off,
- whether a reserve is committed early and loses rest time.

## Operational-level audit requirement
At every major gate, explicitly ask whether the delta crosses a threshold that changes:
- operational objective,
- main axis,
- landing point,
- reserve commitment,
- reinforcement/redeployment date,
- retreat decision,
- command relationship,
- logistical plan,
- use of aviation/artillery,
- or major calendar day.

Do not assume all effects are tactical.

## Enemy feedback
Japanese improvement often causes Chinese reaction earlier. This can partially or fully cancel positional gains while creating different costs (earlier reserve commitment, less rest, more abandoned supplies, etc.).

## Equipment stock-flow rule
Do not repeatedly subtract “a few percent of heavy equipment” at every retreat gate.

Track:
1. stock before the campaign,
2. combat destruction,
3. breakdown/abandonment,
4. deliberate disablement/demolition,
5. successful evacuation,
6. Japanese capture in repairable condition,
7. later replacement from domestic/foreign supply.

## CEQ caution
CEQ is only a bookkeeping shorthand for usable combat effectiveness, not literal headcount. When possible state whether a delta is personnel, trained manpower, organization, equipment, ammunition, readiness or positional advantage.
