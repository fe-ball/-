## Japanese year-end carryover
The Japanese Army is not carrying a huge extra arsenal. The main V7 effect is:
- surviving/trained usable CEQ at 1937 year-end: **about +1,700 to +3,600 versus old branch**;
- high-tempo vehicle-ready advantage that can be +3–6 pp during pursuit compresses to about **+2–4 pp during year-end rest/repair**;
- artillery-ready difference at rest is about **+1–3 pp**, with physical gun inventory much closer;
- general equipment inventory is only roughly **+1–4%** in many categories by late 1937; effects are larger for maintenance/recovery/communications/high-value heavy transport than for rifles or ordinary guns;
- saved trained NCOs, artillerymen, signalmen, drivers and mechanics remain a persistent difference after equipment readiness converges during rest.

Do not convert the +1,700–3,600 CEQ into an extra independent division. It is distributed through existing formations.

## Chinese year-end carryover
Early Shanghai/Nanking withdrawal decisions save some additional personnel and a small amount of mobile equipment, but do not restore the destroyed quality of the prewar Central Army.

Nanking final withdrawal V7 delta:
- additional people escaping: **about +900–1,800** versus old branch;
- immediate usable combat CEQ: **about +500–1,000**;
- after several weeks of reassembly, potentially **+700–1,300 CEQ**;
- by late Jan, roughly **+600–1,200 personnel** can plausibly be back in organized formations from this extra survivor pool;
- additional saved 75mm-class guns at the Nanking final exit are only **~1–3**, not dozens;
- more meaningful saved items are MGs, mortars, radios/telephone equipment and trained specialists.

A large fraction of Shanghai/Nanking survivors are used as cadre for rebuilding units with fresh recruits. They are not automatically “preserved” in quiet garrison service.

## Chinese rebuild quality
A nominal 7,000-man rebuilt division is not equivalent to a pre-Shanghai 7,000-man division. It can consist of exhausted veterans, reduced junior leadership, incomplete communications/transport/artillery and newly arrived recruits.

Heavy artillery is the hardest category to replace quickly. Rifles, MGs and mortars are more replaceable domestically or through later supply.

## Japanese post-Nanking posture
After Nanking/Hangchow, operational tempo shifts to occupation, repair, rail/road restoration and unit recovery. Better Asai-related readiness narrows during rest but reappears when high-tempo movement resumes.

Unit redeployment dates should not automatically move early because of better readiness: rail/ship allocation, orders and handover often dominate.
