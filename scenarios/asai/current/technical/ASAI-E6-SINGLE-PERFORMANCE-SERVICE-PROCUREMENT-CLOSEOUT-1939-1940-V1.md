## 5. Fuel, range and external-fuel closure

### Internal fuel
With ~0.73 t internal kerosene and real climb/combat/reserve accounting:
- cruise-equivalent clean distance ~0.9–1.0 thousand km;
- practical round-trip interception/high-cover/recon radius **~400–500 km**;
- high-speed dash/station time trades directly against that radius.

This is deliberately much shorter-legged than A6M-type naval long-range fighter doctrine and therefore does not replace it.

### External fuel
The central/rear engine plus cheek/side intake architecture permits one streamlined centerline tank or equivalent paired wing tanks.

1940 standard planning capacity:
- **~300–400 L external kerosene**;
- useful fuel mass roughly ~240–320 kg;
- normal combat use requires jettison before maximum-speed interception when fuel state permits;
- practical reach gain after tank drag, climb, reserve and jettison costs: **~+150–220 km**.

Working long-cover radius becomes roughly **~550–700 km class**, mission dependent.

A molded plant-FRP shell is a natural 1940 production implementation, but tank material does not change the fundamental range arithmetic.

## 8. Industrial and procurement verdict

### Why serial production is rational
The old rule correctly said E6 Single should not become a military product merely because the technology exists. By late 1939 the missing requirement test is now answered:

- **one engine:** YES;
- **lower cost than Twin:** YES, roughly half;
- **smaller airframe:** YES;
- **sufficient range:** YES for land interception/high cover, ~400–500 km internal;
- **large performance gain:** YES, ~750 km/h at 6 km vs Ki-42 ~570–580;
- **engine ecosystem:** already paid by E5/E6 Twin;
- **rear-engine/side-inlet architecture:** already paid by E5 future-form and 1939 E6 Single test work;
- **production bottleneck:** pure-J avoids the JF free-turbine/fan/governor T-AUX bottleneck.

Therefore a service requirement does arise naturally.

## 9. Why conventional fighters still survive

E6 Single does not make A6M/Ki-43/Ki-44/J2M logic disappear.

It pays for its speed with:
- prepared-runway dependence;
- turbine-qualified maintenance and kerosene logistics;
- one-engine failure = aircraft loss rather than Twin-style return;
- shorter range than long-range naval fighter doctrine;
- higher unit cost than a conventional piston fighter;
- turbine throttle/restart characteristics that remain less ideal for the cheapest front-line scramble role.

Thus the correct portfolio is:
- **E6 Single:** premium land-based interceptor / high-cover / high-threat fighter;
- **E6 Twin:** deep/high-threat recon, long cover, multi-mission, two-engine operations;
- **A6M/Ki-43:** long-range / conventional fighter mass roles;
- **Ki-44/J2M:** conventional-fuel/base rapid-interceptor hedge and alternative production ecology.
