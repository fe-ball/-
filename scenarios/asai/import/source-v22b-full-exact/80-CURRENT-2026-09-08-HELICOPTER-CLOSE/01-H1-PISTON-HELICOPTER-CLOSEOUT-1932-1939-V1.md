# H1 PISTON HELICOPTER CLOSEOUT — 1932-1939

`H1` is a V13 audit label, not a historical service designation.

## 1. Development chronology

- late 1932: preliminary helicopter/rotorcraft work;
- 1933: Asai formal in-house helicopter program;
- 1934: scale and full-size rotor, hub and transmission rigs;
- 1935: tethered lift and short hops; Asai 180-200 hp aviation-gasoline engine work runs in parallel;
- 1936: free flight;
- 1937 H1-summer: Asai-engined prototype work on cyclic/collective control, anti-torque, autorotation, rotor head, freewheel, main reducer, vibration and durability;
- 1938-Apr1939: repeatability, wear, maintenance, ground-resonance diagnosis and repeated-sortie maturation.

The line is not reset to `can it hover?` after 1937.

## 3. H1 performance band

| item | V13 close |
|---|---:|
| maximum level speed | **105-115 km/h** |
| normal cruise | **80-90 km/h** |
| practical endurance | **1.6-1.9 h** |
| practical range | **130-160 km** |
| climb, light | **~1.2-1.6 m/s** |
| climb, two-person | **~0.6-1.0 m/s** |
| useful forward-flight altitude | **~1,800-2,200 m class** |
| armament | none |

Hover bookkeeping at sea-level standard conditions is closed only as an engineering check: ~169 hp engine-side at ~900 kg, ~177 hp at ~930 kg and ~186 hp at ~960 kg under the adopted early-machine efficiency assumptions. Therefore 190 hp normal is sufficient for a useful research machine but gives little hot/high margin at upper weight.

## 4. Controls and instrumentation

Flight control remains deliberately conventional:
- mechanical cyclic;
- mechanical collective;
- mechanical pedal/tail-rotor pitch;
- mechanical swashplate linkage;
- mechanical freewheel/overrunning clutch;
- manual throttle, with simple mechanical correlation assistance permitted;
- no standard hydraulic power controls;
- no electronic stabilization;
- no autopilot.

Asai's mechanical/electrical strength is used primarily for measurement and diagnosis: rotor and engine rpm, vibration phase/amplitude, bearing and gearbox temperature, oil pressure, tracking, shaft/strain measurement and test logging. This accelerates fault isolation but does not create an anachronistic flight-control system.

## 5. Safety maturity

- autorotation is repeatable enough to be a formal test item;
- the complete height-velocity diagram is **not** known in advance;
- low-altitude/low-speed operation remains a mapped danger region under test;
- ground resonance remains a real design hazard handled by damping, gear/leg symmetry, tracking, joint-clearance control and prohibited rpm bands rather than pilot skill alone.

## 6. Physical inventory

On 1939-04-20 retain exactly **two H1-family development airframes**:
1. primary flight/endurance/control article;
2. alternate/rebuild/instrumented reserve article.

Army/Navy evaluation funding does not create additional H1 production aircraft in V13.
