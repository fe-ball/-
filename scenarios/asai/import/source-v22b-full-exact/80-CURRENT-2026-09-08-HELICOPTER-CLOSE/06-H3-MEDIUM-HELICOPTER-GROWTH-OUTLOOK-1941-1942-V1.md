# H3 MEDIUM HELICOPTER GROWTH OUTLOOK — 1941-1942

`H3` is an audit/growth label, not a service designation.

The military is shown a scale ladder from H1 to H2 and a credible medium-helicopter growth path. V13 closes only the **design outlook**, not a 1941 aircraft.

## 1. 1941 design point

| item | V13 outlook |
|---|---:|
| gross mass | **~3.0-3.3 t; center ~3.2 t** |
| main rotor | **~15 m class** |
| power | **~900 shp normal / ~1,000 shp short class**, E6-derived shaft product flat-rated for the rotorcraft development ladder |
| crew/payload concept | 4-6 persons, or ~500-700 kg short-range useful load, or multiple litter configurations |
| missions | liaison, casualty evacuation, short-range cargo, deck/rescue trials, visual maritime patrol |

At ~3.2 t and ~15 m rotor diameter, the engine-side sea-level hover requirement is expected to be roughly 700-750 shp under early-medium-helicopter assumptions, leaving useful but not unlimited margin at 900 shp.

## 2. 1941 state

By 1941 year-end:
- no H3 flight airframe exists;
- design studies, rotor-head/main-mast/gearbox scaling and full-scale component rigs may be funded;
- engine power availability alone does not close the aircraft;
- rotor-head loads, blade bending/fatigue, main transmission durability, control loads and ground resonance remain the primary scale gates.

Earliest flight-airframe work belongs to **1942+**, not the 1941 OOB.

## 3. Prohibited backflow
Do not infer a 4-5.5 t / 1,500 shp medium service helicopter in 1941 merely because E6 shaft power exists. H3 must pay the helicopter-specific scale/maturity ladder.
