## 6. Soviet adaptation during June

By mid/late June the Soviet side does adapt, but in layers.

Aviation:
- A2 -> partial A3:
  - high-speed twin reconnaissance / light attack nature broadly understood
  - same-type high-cover recognized after repeated encounters
  - exact mission-kit flexibility remains incompletely understood
- selected high-altitude CAP and alert posture increase around important airfields / routes

Working cost in directly threatened periods:
- roughly **6–12 fighters** are more often committed to high/early CAP than under the old worldline
- this is an allocation cost, not a permanent loss of aircraft
- fuel / engine-hour / pilot-fatigue burden rises modestly

Ground / logistics:
- A1 -> A2/A3 around repeatedly photographed nodes
- larger vehicle parks and fuel / maintenance concentrations begin to disperse
- camouflage and night movement increase

Working local handling cost:
- **~3–5% throughput / handling penalty** at the most repeatedly exposed nodes while dispersion procedures are active
- not a theater-wide Soviet logistics penalty

These countermeasures reduce later direct E5 kill opportunities but consume Soviet resources and time.
