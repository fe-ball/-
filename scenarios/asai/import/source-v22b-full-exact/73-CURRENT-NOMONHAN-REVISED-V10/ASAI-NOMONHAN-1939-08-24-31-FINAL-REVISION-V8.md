## 2. Soviet operational choice

The Soviet wings are available earlier than in OLD because the old pocket contains less Japanese combat power.

Options:
1. secure the claimed / contested zone;
2. forceful reconnaissance / shallow pursuit eastward;
3. deep pursuit into Manchuria.

REVISED working probability:
- secure + stronger shallow probes: **dominant**
- limited 2–6 km local penetrations / reconnaissance-in-force: **plausible**
- deliberate deep Manchurian pursuit: **low**

Reasons:
- Soviet/Mongolian territorial objective is already being achieved;
- Japanese new line is visibly stronger than an unorganized rout;
- Soviet units have paid substantial armor / air / personnel losses;
- logistics and artillery need reorganization after the encirclement battle;
- higher political objectives do not require converting a border victory into a wider Soviet-Japanese war.

## 3. 24 Aug — earlier Soviet pressure on the new line

Because Soviet formations are freed earlier:
- reconnaissance-in-force begins earlier and somewhat more strongly than OLD;
- artillery probes likely Japanese reserve / tank-release routes;
- Japanese line receives more pressure than if the Soviet side had spent the day clearing a fuller pocket.

Japanese:
- no historical-style rescue attack;
- tanks remain defensive;
- E5 BDA / route reconnaissance supports local fire allocation;
- 7th Division battalion-group gives infantry density on the threatened shoulder.

REVISED:

- tanks counter only penetrations threatening routes / artillery / command depth;
- E5 attacks exposed pursuit support, not a deep interdiction campaign.

## 5. Soviet pursuit limit

By 26–27 Aug:
- Japanese withdrawal is clearly controlled;
- mobile reserve remains visible through battlefield contact;
- deeper pursuit would require a new operational objective and renewed logistics preparation.

Soviet command therefore shifts from “complete encirclement” to:
- secure the boundary claim;
- punish / probe;
- rebuild;
- prepare for a possible Japanese return offensive.

The earlier Japanese withdrawal does NOT automatically cause a Soviet invasion of Manchuria.

## 6. Air state, 24–27 Aug

Soviet adaptation is now approximately A5–A6 in key air sectors:
- E5 common-family mission flexibility broadly understood;
- higher CAP / dispersal / poor-weather movement are practiced;
- Japanese BDA habit is recognized.

Direct E5 yield therefore falls.

Revision delta vs OLD, 24–27:
- Soviet irreversible aircraft: **+2–3**
- damaged / mission-abort: **+3–5**
- Japanese irreversible aircraft: **~1 fewer**

The continuing cost to the Soviet side is more CAP hours / fuel / fatigue and dispersed handling, not dramatic aircraft destruction.

## 7. 27 Aug — artillery pressure and contemporary reporting

Heavy Soviet artillery pressure / local eastward action remains plausible.
Japanese field reporting may describe this as a dangerous Soviet advance.

REVISED judgment:
- real Soviet probes exist;
- claims of a major deep drive toward Hailar / Manchurian interior are not accepted without corroboration;
- E5 / ground reporting makes the distinction between shallow pressure and operational pursuit somewhat clearer than OLD.

No Japanese panic redeployment is generated.

## 8. Japanese counteroffensive planning

Because the revised Japanese force is stronger than OLD:
- First Tank Group remains materially healthier;
- more 23rd Division cadres / support personnel survived;
- E5 / Ki-42 remain an operating reconnaissance / high-cover system;
- the army has concrete lessons for artillery / AT / vehicle / reserve integration.

Kwantung / 6th Army therefore begins formal “return offensive” planning **earlier and more confidently**.

Working:
- 24–25 Aug: concept / requirements drafted;
- 26–27 Aug: artillery, ammunition, armor, air, engineer and reinforcement concentration demands formalized;
- earliest credible launch window remains early September, not immediate.

This is NOT an automatic offensive order.

## 9. Political environment — 23–29 Aug

External shock:
- German-Soviet nonaggression pact is concluded 23 Aug / signed early 24 Aug Moscow time;
- Japan interprets this as a collapse of the prior Anti-Comintern diplomatic premise;
- Japanese policy debate turns toward a “free and independent” European policy;
- Hiranuma Cabinet resigns 28 Aug.

The Soviet government also has reason to avoid turning a successful border operation into an unlimited Far Eastern war while European policy is shifting.

These facts are independent of Japanese battlefield casualty level.

## 10. Tokyo / IGHQ response

The revised battlefield outcome creates a paradox:

Compared with OLD:
- Kwantung Army has **more** physical ability and confidence to try again;
- therefore a September return-offensive plan becomes more credible;
- but because it is now a deliberate new operation rather than an emergency rescue, it is more clearly a national-policy decision.

Tokyo cannot rely on “your force is destroyed” as its argument.
Instead it must use:
- national war-risk authority;
- European strategic shock;
- China commitments;
- the danger of local command creating a general Soviet war.

Working branch probabilities:
- effective central prohibition / freeze by Aug29–31: **~60–65%**
- delayed stop Sep1–4 before launch: **~25–30%**
- limited September offensive actually launches: **~10–15%**

MAIN BRANCH:
- **30 Aug:** IGHQ / central authority issues an effective no-new-offensive / concentration-hold directive;
- planning and repair may continue;
- westward launch is frozen.
