## 2. Operation S — dawn 21 Aug

The long-range airfield raid still occurs:
- it was preplanned;
- aircraft and crews were already committed;
- cancelling it entirely overnight would waste a prepared counter-air opportunity.

But the 20 Aug ground situation changes allocation.

CURRENT OLD:
- ~85–90 fighters attack / escort
- ~10–15 retained for battlefield CAP / airfield defense

REVISED working allocation:
- **~75–82 fighters attack / escort**
- **~20–28 conventional fighters retained**
- E5 / Ki-42 provide additional high-cover / battlefield / corridor tasks

Result:
- the deep raid remains damaging;
- direct Soviet airfield losses are roughly similar to OLD, not dramatically larger;
- battlefield Japanese CAP / reconnaissance availability is materially better than OLD.

Revision air delta for 21 Aug:
- Soviet irreversible aircraft: **+1–2 vs OLD**
- Soviet damaged / mission-abort: **+2–4**
- Japanese irreversible aircraft: **~1 fewer**

The key revision value is ground support / corridor protection, not a larger Tamsag kill count.

## 3. 21 Aug morning — withdrawal trigger

The Soviet south continues to push after the 20 Aug delay.
North/Fui pressure also grows.

Because phase lines were predefined:
- **~09:30–11:00:** southern trigger condition is met;
- **~11:30–13:00:** northern / corridor-risk condition confirms that the situation is not a local penetration.

Revised command consequence:
- no new strategic debate is required;
- **~12:00:** first-stage line shortening becomes executable under the standing order;
- rear services, wounded, surplus ammunition / stores and vulnerable command/support elements already moving east are accelerated;
- exposed infantry positions prepare bounded withdrawal under registered fires.

This is roughly one day earlier in the command cycle than the CURRENT OLD branch’s Aug22 evening phased-withdrawal order.

## 5. 21 Aug evening — first actual movement of exposed infantry

This is NOT a whole-front rout.

Working sequence:
- southern / most exposed positions begin local pullback from ~15:00–17:00;
- north/Fui-related vulnerable elements begin staged eastward movement from ~17:00–20:00;
- stronger central positions remain in place to mask / cover the shortening;
- the new line is not yet fully occupied.

Japanese field culture still resists abandoning ground too early.
The standing conditional order makes movement possible, but local commanders continue to contest positions until their phase-line condition is met.

## 9. 23 Aug — Soviet wings close earlier on less

Because Japanese exposed positions have been vacated earlier:
- Soviet north/south elements can occupy / link through the old geometry **~8–14 h earlier than OLD**;
- but they close on rear guards, abandoned positions, wrecks and a smaller immobile residue.

This is the cost of early withdrawal:
- Soviet operational objective / ground occupation is achieved sooner;
- Soviet formations are freed sooner to probe the new line.

However:
- early link-up does not equal instant deep pursuit;
- Soviet units still require reorganization / route clearance;
- Japanese second line is stronger than the OLD branch because the 7th battalion-group and a larger tank reserve are already there.

## 11. Soviet response / interpretation

Soviet command sees:
- Japanese retreat began earlier and was more disciplined;
- Japanese tanks remain more numerous than expected after several days of battle;
- E5 reconnaissance / air cover continues to complicate pursuit.

Possible Soviet interpretations are not uniform:
- some field commanders read it as Japanese weakness / withdrawal;
- staff-level analysis increasingly recognizes controlled extraction.

Operational choice remains:
- secure the claimed / contested zone first;
- probe the new line;
- avoid immediately turning a successful encirclement operation into a deeper Manchurian campaign without higher-level purpose.

The probability of limited probing is somewhat higher than OLD because the wings link sooner.
