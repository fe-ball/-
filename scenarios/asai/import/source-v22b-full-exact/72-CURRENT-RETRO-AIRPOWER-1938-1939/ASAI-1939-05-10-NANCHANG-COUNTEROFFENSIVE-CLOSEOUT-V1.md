## 5. Worldline counteroffensive

### Apr 1–18 — concentration
Chinese night movement / camouflage prevents full disclosure.
Repeated E5 photography nevertheless identifies:
- growth in road traffic and bridging activity,
- artillery / supply-area changes,
- airfield activity,
- likely attack corridors.

Japanese estimate of the principal axes is available **~12–24 h earlier** than matched-history operational awareness, but exact attack timing remains uncertain.

Result:
- reserves and artillery ammunition are positioned somewhat better;
- route / bridge / airfield targets are pre-registered;
- no preemptive destruction of the Chinese offensive force.

### Apr 19–23 — opening attacks
Chinese attacks still achieve local gains.
Japanese positions are not uniformly strong enough to stop the offensive at the start.

Difference:
- fewer Japanese local units are caught while still reorganizing;
- E5 route attacks and BDA repeatedly hit crossing / assembly / supply points;
- Ki-42 / E5 high cover complicates Chinese fighter interception but does not create Japanese total air control.

Worldline offensive tempo becomes roughly **6–12 h behind the matched-history Chinese advance** by 23 April.

### Apr 24–28 — synchronization begins to fail
Chinese columns continue toward Nanchang, but:
- major daytime concentrations are dispersed;
- artillery ammunition / road movement becomes less synchronized;
- Japanese reserves are moved against the better-identified axes;
- 101st / 106th Division positions are somewhat stronger than matched history due earlier March preservation.

Chinese forces still threaten the outer Nanchang defense zone.
No large Chinese formation is annihilated.

### Apr 29–May 3 — Japanese local counterattack
Japanese counterattacks begin from a better prepared posture.

The Chinese offensive loses operational synchrony about **1 day earlier** than matched history.

Forward Chinese elements reach the western / southwestern outer approaches, but the worldline does **not** credit seizure of the Nanchang railway-station / airfield complex.

### May 4–8 — culmination and withdrawal
Historically dangerous pressure on the immediate urban perimeter does not fully materialize.

Chinese commanders preserve the main bodies rather than continue to feed troops into increasingly observed approach corridors.

Working close:
- counteroffensive effectively culminates on **May 6–7**
- organized withdrawal / termination begins
- by **May 8 evening**, the attempt to retake Nanchang is operationally over
- May 9–10 are rear-guard / local fighting, not a continuing realistic city-recapture attempt
