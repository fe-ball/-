# MARINE GT / COMBINED-CYCLE / CATAPULT GATES — V20

This file records engineering gates created during V20 discussion. Values are workbands, not guaranteed fleet test data.

## 1. Ground vs sea GT state

### E6-M marine generation

Existing installed-cycle work:
- simple-cycle installed shaft efficiency: ~22.5% center;
- BSFC ~0.372 kg/kWh center;
- marine continuous output is deliberately derated from the raw core peak for life and sea service.

Marine recuperation workband:
- early sea-capable recuperator effectiveness around ~0.60 class;
- installed efficiency ~26.9% workband;
- BSFC ~0.31 kg/kWh workband.

### E7 ground frontier, 1941-42 development line

For the latest ground core plus improved recuperation:
- pressure ratio ~8 class;
- ~900 C TIT class;
- advanced recuperator effectiveness ~0.70-0.75 workband;
- simple installed shaft efficiency ~mid-20% workband;
- recuperated installed shaft efficiency ~30.1-30.7% workband.

This is a ground-development capability. It does not mean a 1941 warship has a 30.7% E7 plant.

## 2. Residual-oxygen exhaust / steam combination

GT exhaust is not oxygen-rich compared with air; it is oxygen-rich **compared with normal furnace exhaust**, because GT combustors use large excess air.

E6-M center-point work produced a rough exhaust picture around:
- ~450-470 C class;
- mid-teens vol% residual O2 wet-basis workband;
- substantial sensible heat plus enough oxygen to support supplementary firing.

Therefore three distinct lower-cycle modes exist:
1. recuperator — use heat to preheat compressor discharge;
2. unfired exhaust boiler — make steam from waste heat only;
3. supplementary-fired exhaust boiler — use residual O2 plus extra fuel to make much more steam.

Do not count the same exhaust heat twice. Recuperator and exhaust boiler compete for heat unless bypass/split operation is designed.

Working combined bands:
- E6 + unfired exhaust steam: low/mid-30% combined efficiency is plausible if pressure losses and 1940s steam-cycle limits are paid;
- moderate supplementary firing: lower efficiency than the optimum unfired point but much higher output;
- maximum supplementary firing: a power-density mode, not an economy mode.

One E6 train was explored as a conceptual operating ladder:
- ~2.0-2.3k hp GT-only class;
- ~3.1-3.4k hp unfired economy combined class;
- ~4.5-5.5k hp moderate supplementary-fired class;
- ~8-9k hp high-fire combined class.

These are **design-study bands** requiring land and sea validation.

## 3. High-pressure steam is not a competitor that must lose

Historical Japanese experimentation (Amatsukaze, high-pressure boilers and associated land work) provides a natural bridge.

Worldline integration concept:
- practical first lower cycle: 40 kg/cm2 / 400 C class;
- advanced branch: 50/450 class if tube/circulation/material reliability passes;
- higher land research remains separate.

The combined plant can be politically/industrially attractive because it does not abolish existing boiler/steam-turbine industries; it adds the GT as an upper cycle.

## 4. Combined-cycle development gates

### C0 — concept/technology connection
**CLOSED.**

E6 sea work, 40/400 steam, E7 ground cores, reduction gears, measurement and residual-O2 exhaust make the concept credible enough for funded tests.

### C1 — exhaust-fired thermodynamic proof
**OPEN. Earliest test window 1941 Q3-Q4.**

Representative pass criteria:
- ~100 h accumulated integrated test;
- ~25 h continuous run;
- GT backpressure loss <= ~3% class;
- unfired combined efficiency >= ~31% class;
- stable supplementary firing;
- safe trip/bypass behavior.

### C2 — E7 marine-core readiness
**OPEN.**

Representative criteria:
- ground-qualified E7 core;
- marine free-power turbine/reduction/air-separation package;
- multiple core repeatability;
- ~300 h initial endurance without destructive failure.

### C3 — full combined train
**OPEN.**

Need GT-only, steam-only, unfired-combined and fired-combined modes plus emergency bypass.

### C4 — sea combined qualification
**OPEN.**

Requires a larger second experimental ship if C1-C3 justify procurement; 500 h-class sea work, salt/fouling/boiler-water/gear switching and safe return capability.

### C5 — combat-ship adoption
**OPEN.**

Need weight/volume/maintenance/survivability advantage that remains after full auxiliary and damage-control accounting.

### C6 — fleet trial
**OPEN.**

Prototype combat ship must accumulate ~1,000-1,500 h class and prove depot-away module exchange/long-range operation.

### C7 — mass production
**OPEN.**

Requires fleet statistics, gear life, spare-network, fuel doctrine and trained yards.

## 5. Ideal E7-combined leader study

Not a procurement decision.

Exploratory sweet spot:
- ~3,300-3,600 t standard;
- ~4,100-4,400 t full load;
- two shafts;
- multiple smaller cruise and larger boost E7 modules feeding common lower-cycle steam;
- ~85-90k shp battle maximum;
- ~36.5-37 kn rather than a 40-kn record chase;
- ~800-900 t fuel class;
- ~9,000-10,500 nm at 18 kn target band;
- heavy AA + strong torpedo + command/sensor role, conceptually a high-speed AA/torpedo leader.

The study demonstrates what the technology might buy in displacement/power/fuel trade. It is not a current ship.

## 6. Catapult gates

The first-generation carrier catapult concept is an **oil-pneumatic/hydraulic assist launcher**, recharged by GT/electric auxiliaries between launches. It is not a modern steam catapult and the GT does not deliver the launch pulse directly.

Historical/worldline design-shadow band:
- ~5.5-6 t initial launch mass class;
- ~30-35 m stroke;
- ~90-100 km/h deck-relative terminal assist class;
- ~1.0-1.25 g average gross acceleration class;
- initial cycle ~90 s, mature target ~45-60 s.

Gates:
- CAT0 concept/base technology — CLOSED;
- CAT1 funded/low-priority full-scale research — CLOSED;
- CAT2 full-scale land qualification — OPEN;
- CAT3 Taiho structural reservation — CLOSED;
- CAT4 actual Taiho fit — OPEN;
- CAT5 sea operational qualification — OPEN;
- CAT6 later-carrier standardization — OPEN.

No carrier-completion schedule should be held hostage to CAT2. If the launcher fails, reserved structure can be closed off and the ship completes conventionally.
