# GLOBAL WAR BALANCE — EUROPE / AFRICA / MIDDLE EAST / UNITED STATES

## 1. The key global divergence

Historical December 1941 merged the European and Pacific wars into a single global war. V20 does not.

At the current 1942-08-31 clock:
- Germany/Italy are at war with Britain/USSR;
- Japan is not at war with US/UK/NL/USSR;
- United States remains formally outside the war while materially supporting Britain/USSR;
- China and Japan are in branch peace/withdrawal, not renewed war.

The war is therefore global in economic and diplomatic reach, but not yet a single universal belligerency system.

## 2. British imperial balance without Japan war

Major differences:
- Singapore survives;
- Malaya survives;
- Burma is not a Japan war theater;
- Netherlands East Indies are not invaded/destroyed;
- Philippines remain US-held;
- Hong Kong remains British-held;
- Prince of Wales and Repulse survive the historical Force Z disaster;
- no Japanese Indian Ocean carrier raid;
- no emergency Australia/Singapore reinforcement drain on the same scale.

This leaves Britain with more Middle-East/Indian-Ocean shipping, aircraft and experienced formations than history.

## 3. North Africa

Up to early 1942, front position can remain broadly historical.

The divergence appears at Gazala/Tobruk:
- British operational/armored-command problems do not magically disappear, so Axis gains a tactical Gazala success;
- Australian 6th/7th Divisions were not withdrawn for the Japan war, and British 18th Division is not destroyed at Singapore;
- Britain therefore has materially more theater reserve and shipping flexibility;
- V20 working replay keeps Tobruk from falling in the historical June rush.

Consequence:
- Axis does not gain the same immediate Tobruk logistics payoff;
- the historical rapid drive to the El Alamein crisis does not auto-occur;
- Malta/Tobruk supply and a possible Herkules question remain central.

This is WORKING-REPLAY. Dedicated Africa OOB/logistics audit can refine it.

## 4. Malta / Madagascar / Torch

- Malta remains a key Axis logistics problem. A Herkules-type operation remains possible but not replayed as complete.
- Historical British Madagascar invasion loses its principal Japanese Indian-Ocean-base trigger; treat it as non-automatic/closed-leaning unless a new Axis/Japanese base trigger emerges.
- Historical-scale Operation Torch is unavailable while the US is formally nonbelligerent. Therefore Vichy North Africa and the Toulon-fleet/Anton chain are not automatically triggered on the historical November 1942 clock.

## 5. Middle East / Iran / Persian Corridor

1941 Iraq, Syria-Lebanon and Anglo-Soviet Iran events are largely retained because their main causes precede the Japan divergence.

The British strategic belt remains:
Egypt -> Palestine -> Syria -> Iraq -> Iran.

The Persian Corridor remains an important Soviet-aid route. With no Pacific/Indian-Ocean war, Allied shipping pressure is lower; however, US formal nonbelligerency may slow some direct US military administration compared with history.

## 6. United States

US status is peculiar:
- no Pearl Harbor;
- no Japanese declaration/attack;
- no historical German declaration of war;
- Lend-Lease to UK/USSR continues;
- Atlantic convoying/incidents and practical anti-German alignment continue;
- full wartime political mobilization and Europe deployment are not yet the same as historical 1942.

Do not simply apply historical US war-production/completion dates as fixed. Ships already authorized/laid down still matter, but wartime acceleration, priorities and manpower can diverge.

## 7. Pacific Lend-Lease / Vladivostok

Because Japan and US/USSR are not at war, the Pacific route is politically easier than history in some respects.

Japan's problem:
- it dislikes US military goods entering a port in the Soviet Far East;
- it lacks a lawful belligerent right to stop neutral US/Soviet traffic while itself neutral;
- stopping it risks US relations and southern oil access;
- permitting it strengthens the USSR it may later fight.

Current branch answer: monitor, seek political non-use assurances, do not blockade.

## 8. 1942 global-system consequence

Japan's nonbelligerence helps both sides in different ways:
- Axis benefit: US is not formally at war; some Soviet Far-East units are fixed by Japan.
- Allied benefit: British Empire does not fight a Pacific/Indian-Ocean catastrophe; southern resources/lines remain intact; USSR receives aid.

This is why neither “Japan neutral = Axis loss” nor “Japan neutral = Axis benefit” is sufficient. The balance is theater-specific.
