# NAVAL / AIRPOWER / SHIPBUILDING CLOSEOUT — V20

## 1. 1942 carrier force: no historical Pacific-war losses

By summer 1942 Japan has not lost the historical Pearl Harbor/Midway/Coral Sea carriers or aircrew.

Core fleet carriers physically available:
- Akagi
- Kaga
- Soryu
- Hiryu
- Shokaku
- Zuikaku

Light/converted carrier layer remains in being and is larger than historical after wartime losses are removed. Ryujo, Zuiho, Shoho and Taiyo remain available in their appropriate roles; Junyo enters service in May 1942 and Hiyo in summer 1942 on their basic completion clocks unless a dedicated yard audit changes them. Hosho remains primarily training-oriented.

Carrier strength must be measured by:
1. first-line usable flight decks;
2. operational aircraft, not maximum brochure stowage;
3. trained air groups/deck crews;
4. ability to concentrate in one theater;
5. 24-month construction shadow.

Working 1942 conclusion: Japan is not in an immediate carrier shortage. The concern is the US construction curve around **1944**, not a 1942 emergency.

## 2. Conversion-carrier ledger / gates

1941-07-02 state that remains useful for provenance:
- Zuiho: conversion complete/active;
- Shoho: conversion underway;
- Junyo/Hiyo: conversion/construction committed;
- Kasuga Maru -> Taiyo: conversion underway;
- close uncommitted candidates included Nitta Maru, Yawata Maru, Taigei, Chitose, Chiyoda.

Do not auto-convert every candidate. The conversion gates are:
- CV0 hull suitability;
- CV1 actual mission demand;
- CV2 opportunity cost of losing original merchant/seaplane/submarine-tender role;
- CV3 yard/material capacity;
- CV4 aircraft operation compatibility;
- CV5 air-group/deck-crew availability;
- CV6 fleet work-up;
- CV7 repeatability for series conversion.

No Pacific-war loss means CV1 pressure is much lower than history. Merchant hulls also have higher economic value because normal trade remains alive.

## 3. Taiho / medium standard / improved-Taiho policy

### Taiho
- construction continues;
- serves as armored-carrier prototype/Block 0;
- no mass copy order simply because it is the newest carrier;
- structural reservation for future catapult work is accepted, actual service launcher is not.

### No.800 medium standard carrier

Maru-5 800 is retained but reinterpreted as a **deliberate standard medium fleet carrier**, not an emergency simplified wartime Unryu copy.

Current working design band:
- ~21,000-22,500 t standard;
- ~27,000-29,000 t full load;
- ~235-242 m overall class;
- flight deck ~235-240 x 29-30 m workband;
- 4 shafts;
- 40 kg/cm2 400 C-class high-efficiency steam;
- ~155,000-160,000 shp;
- ~34.5-35 kn;
- ~10,000 nm at 18 kn workband;
- three large elevators;
- ~66-72 normal operational aircraft, higher maximum stowage not treated as normal combat strength;
- structural provision for two catapult tracks, actual fit gated;
- aviation fuel zoning for both gasoline and Grade-A/turbine fuel, fully segregated plumbing;
- strong fuel/fire/ventilation/drainage/electrical survivability but no Taiho-style full armored flight deck requirement;
- 10 cm/65 twin AA batteries as the baseline family, final mount count remains design-detail dependent.

Program clock:
- Apr 1942: construction execution authorized;
- Jun-Jul 1942: basic/detail design freeze process;
- Aug-Sep 1942: keel target, but actual keel date **OPEN at 1942-08-31**;
- Oct 1942: planned decision on two additional 800-family carriers, not yet current-history fact.

### Nos.801 / 802

Retained as improved-Taiho series, not replaced by mass No.800 construction. Their exact start dates depend on yards, Taiho findings and the wider fleet plan.

## 4. Carrier-balance judgment

Worldline planning judgment at 1942:
- 1942: safe/good carrier position;
- 1943: still acceptable if training and no war losses;
- 1944: current program alone enters the danger zone against the pre-existing US Essex build curve;
- therefore medium standard hulls must be laid down before any war makes the need obvious.

This is a planning judgment, not a guarantee of combat victory. US two-ocean allocation means total US carriers are not the same as immediate Pacific concentration.

## 5. Major-ship propulsion/hydrodynamic reinterpretation

Existing naval computation canon already supports integrated:
`resistance -> propeller -> shaft -> reduction -> prime mover matching`.

“No magic” means no arbitrary knot bonus. It does **not** mean historical-type ships are hydrodynamically identical to historical drawings.

Current working bands developed in V20 discussions:
- Shokaku class: ~1.0-1.8% same-speed shaft-power reduction workband; ~34.5-34.6 kn good-trial band; 18-kn range +~1.7-3% workband.
- Yamato class: already historically highly optimized; ~1.2-2.3% same-speed power reduction workband; ~27.6 kn center rather than a fantasy 30-kn ship; cruise economy is the more meaningful gain.
- Kongo/Nagato old hulls: much smaller headline-speed gain, more benefit in repeatable rated power, shaft/vibration health, cruise economy and squadron availability.
- later cruisers/destroyers benefit more because more of the design was done inside the mature worldline calculation/testing culture.

These remain working engineering closeouts unless superseded by a dedicated class-by-class naval performance layer.

## 6. 1941 high-speed aviation audit correction

The replay initially treated E6 as too sparse in Manchuria.

1941 procurement planning already has large new E6 receipts, not merely legacy stock. By Oct 1941 the northern high-speed/turbine layer can plausibly be on the order of ~230-280 E6/E5/Ki-42 aircraft assigned, inside an overall ~780-850-aircraft northern aviation picture.

Small evaluation assets can exist without becoming normal OOB:
- Ki-40 high-altitude reconnaissance evaluation;
- E6-B high-speed bomber evaluation;
- Ki-76 limited liaison/observation;
- H2 helicopter military test;
- A7M/A8N prototype/evaluation aircraft in naval test organizations;
- E7 remains a core/engine development frontier, not a 1941 fleet engine.

Do not convert prototypes into squadron strength.

## 7. Fifth Fleet / northern naval options

Fifth Fleet re-formation on 25 July 1941 is retained.

Northern naval tasks:
- Japan Sea/Soya/Tsugaru/Kuriles surveillance;
- minesweeping/ASW;
- Vladivostok blockade/containment planning;
- coastal air/recon support;
- North Sakhalin joint-operation planning;
- transports, landing craft, auxiliaries and seaplane/recon assets matter more than assigning battleships/carriers permanently north.

A direct opening amphibious assault on Vladivostok is not the preferred plan. Isolation from land plus blockade is the central logic.
