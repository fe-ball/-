# JAPAN ECONOMY — CHINA-PEACE RECOVERY UNDER KANTOKUEN

## 1. Character of the recovery

The 1941-42 Japanese economy is **not peacetime**. It is a heavily militarized economy recovering from the China Incident while maintaining a very large northern army and major naval/air construction.

Recovery sequence:
1. combat fuel burn falls;
2. shipping and transport return toward normal uses;
3. imports/foreign-exchange position improves;
4. stocks and industrial maintenance improve;
5. fiscal, labor and civilian-consumption relief appear later.

Kantokuen absorbs a substantial part of the peace dividend but costs less than continuing a major shooting war across China.

## 2. China Incident fiscal effect

Historical-scale China-Incident special-account expenditure provides the upper comparison, but the branch peace does not erase costs already incurred in the first half of 1941.

Worldline 1941 still pays for:
- first-half fighting;
- withdrawal/concentration/transport;
- veterans, wounds, pensions and families;
- repair/recovery of equipment;
- property/administrative settlement;
- the new northern mobilization.

Working 1941 China-incident-related expenditure band from the session:
- roughly **67-76 oku yen** versus historical ~94.9 oku-yen scale comparison.

Total government military spending falls less because Kantokuen, E6, armor/AT, airbases, fleet construction and northern logistics absorb savings.

A larger fiscal peace dividend should appear in 1942, the first full year without active China campaigning, if peace holds.

## 3. Shipping

This is one of the earliest and largest gains.

Historical Pacific-war mobilization seized a huge share of Japanese merchant tonnage. In this worldline there is no Pearl Harbor/Malaya/Philippines/DEI campaign and no associated mass military shipping commitment.

Working late-1941 shipping band:
- military/government use: ~1.8-2.4 million gross tons;
- civilian/normal trade: ~3.9-4.5 million gross tons.

These are strategic workbands, not a registry close.

Consequences:
- more oil can be imported;
- rice/food and coal can move;
- China/southern trade revives;
- domestic coastal transport is less strangled;
- merchant hulls do not incur historical Pacific-war sinkings.

## 4. Oil / middle distillates

No South-Indochina-triggered full asset freeze/oil embargo means oil becomes an import-consumption balance rather than a countdown-to-zero stock.

V16 physical doctrine remains:
- Grade A aviation turbine fuel;
- Grade B common military middle distillate;
- Grade C tolerant lower grade.

China peace reduces combat consumption. Kantokuen/E6/vehicles consume meaningful middle distillates, so stocks do not explode upward, but the late-1941 working band of ~0.85-1.00 million kL middle-distillate family is plausible from the session and should be dedicated-audited before exact use.

High-octane avgas/TEL/premium military-goods controls remain distinct from normal petroleum trade.

## 5. Foreign exchange / trade

No full asset freeze means Japan can still earn and spend dollar/sterling-area exchange within controls.

Key structure:
- normal oil can continue;
- premium aviation fuel/TEL and some strategic machine tools/metals remain controlled;
- DEI commercial oil remains available as long as Japan does not use military coercion;
- French Indochina/Thailand trade remains useful;
- China peace reopens ordinary trade but removes some occupation-era privileges.

Thus Japan can have **money and oil access while still being short of selected high-end capital goods/materials**.

This raises the strategic value of Asai-worldline domestic machine tools, bearings, gears, measuring systems, special steels and GT industries.

## 6. Labor / civilian life

Demobilization is slower than the fuel/shipping recovery because many China-returning formations are diverted to Manchuria.

Nevertheless occupation-security, temporary transport and administrative manpower can return gradually. Civilian relief should appear first as better availability rather than an immediate end to price/ration controls.

The state is likely to:
- keep core price/ration controls;
- raise civilian raw-material allotments gradually;
- relax selected low-military-value manufacturing bans;
- permit more maintenance/capital investment;
- avoid an abrupt free-price transition that would release accumulated inflation pressure.

## 7. Manchurian cost

The peace dividend is geographically uneven.

Manchuria experiences:
- army housing/barracks pressure;
- rail congestion;
- food/coal demand;
- vehicle/horse support burdens;
- military inflation;
- labor diversion.

The shift from 1941 emergency Kantokuen mobilization to a more sustainable 1942 standing posture returns some temporary transport/labor to the economy while preserving the combat core.

## 8. Strategic consequence

The central Japanese incentive becomes self-reinforcing:

no new war -> merchant ships survive -> imports and domestic transport improve -> fuel/stock position improves -> factories can maintain/expand -> fiscal pressure eases -> civilian consumption can recover -> Japan becomes stronger without conquest.

This is why the armed-neutrality branch remains competitive even when a northward opportunity appears.
