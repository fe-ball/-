# REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — 1941-06-01 THROUGH 1942-08-31

This chronology is the V20 replay spine. It is intentionally strategic rather than a day-by-day tactical history. Where exact unit identity/count is not closed, it says so.

## June 1941 — pre-Barbarossa and first reaction

### 1-21 June
- Kwantung branch base is ~13 divisions: historical 12 plus the 4th Division retained in Manchuria in this branch.
- China peace has begun releasing support troops, aviation, vehicles, staff capacity and transport, but no complete China-returning division is automatically counted Soviet-front ready by 21 June.
- Tokyo orders readiness improvement rather than general Soviet-war mobilization: fill units, repair vehicles, improve ammunition/fuel stocks, rail plans, artillery/AT training and airfield readiness.
- Soviet 16th Army westward movement is not retroactively cancelled; key orders predate the current replay gate.

### 22-30 June
- Germany invades USSR on 22 June.
- Japan does not immediately enter the war. Matsuoka presses for war; Army central, Navy and Konoe reject an instant decision.
- Finland remains formally outside on 22 June, then Soviet air attacks on 25 June become the political trigger for renewed war; state of war recognized 26 June.
- South Indochina military occupation is not selected. Normal southern commerce remains available.

## July 1941 — Kantokuen without southward reversal

- Early July state decision: Neutrality Pact remains in force; northward war remains an option; South Indochina advance is held.
- 7 July: worldline Kantokuen first stage begins. Priority is not only infantry count but artillery, AT, engineers, communications, rail, vehicles, workshops, aviation and supply.
- No historical 5 July southern-army order for an imminent South Indochina/Malaya campaign.
- German Smolensk successes raise the northward opportunity argument, but repeated Soviet regeneration weakens the “collapse in weeks” assumption.
- 16-17 July: cabinet reconstruction removes Matsuoka; Third Konoe Cabinet forms with Toyoda as foreign minister. Immediate-war diplomacy loses, conditional northward preparation remains.
- 25 July: Fifth Fleet re-formation in the north is retained; its importance is greater because Japan has no active southern war.

## August 1941 — first Soviet transfer divergence

- Kantokuen is not wound down on the historical southward timetable.
- By late August, working Kwantung base reaches ~15 ready divisions; 21st Division is arriving/redeploying from China, 33rd Division is in northward transfer.
- Finland recovers Viipuri and reaches the old-border zone. Karelian Isthmus advance stops near the old frontier; East-Karelian goals remain separately limited.
- Soviet transfer review begins to diverge:
  - 26th Rifle Division: GO west;
  - 21st Rifle Division: HOLD east.
- First concrete worldline “extra east” formation therefore appears by the end of August.

## September 1941 — 17-division Japan and second Soviet HOLD

- 21st Japanese Division integrates; 33rd arrives/integrates. Northern Japanese combat base approaches ~17 divisions.
- Tokyo orders another two formations kept within short-notice northward transfer ability but does not send them immediately.
- Finland conducts a limited East-Karelian advance but does not become an unrestricted Leningrad-assault partner.
- Soviet 114th Rifle Division can go west to the Finnish sector because Japan's western Manchurian posture is not the main offensive threat.
- 32nd Rifle Division on the Primorye/Ussuri axis is HOLD east rather than historically going to the Moscow sector.
- Germany wins at Kiev and prepares renewed Moscow offensive; Soviet state still does not collapse.
- Historical 6 September US/UK/NL-war deadline decision in Tokyo does not occur.

## October 1941 — Moscow crisis, but the season closes Japan's 1941 attack window

- Operation Typhoon causes the Vyazma/Bryansk catastrophe.
- Japan orders a third northern reinforcement wave: 56th Division from home and 41st Division from China/redeployment pool. At month end these are not both fully integrated, but a ~19-division northern posture is becoming physically possible.
- Soviet transfer choices:
  - 93rd Rifle Division: GO;
  - 82nd Motorized Division: GO;
  - 239th Rifle Division: GO;
  - 78th Rifle Division: HOLD;
  - 92nd Rifle Division: HOLD;
  - 58th Tank Division: HOLD pending/then retained.
- 21st and 32nd remain east from earlier decisions.
- Missing 32nd Rifle Division slightly worsens Mozhaisk/Borodino defense; local German progress improves, but mud, attrition, supply and encirclement-clearing still prevent a quick Moscow victory.
- Sorge is arrested on the historical domestic-security path; Moscow loses its best Tokyo-level source just as the northward question remains open.
- Tokyo closes a 1941 full-scale Soviet offensive except on extreme Soviet-state-collapse conditions. Kantokuen converts toward winterized option preservation.

## November 1941 — ~19 divisions, Moscow still holds

- Around 20 November the Japanese northern posture reaches approximately 19 divisions.
- Additional Soviet Far Eastern removals remain selective:
  - 65th/413th/415th-type newly formed or less exposed units can still go west as needed;
  - 58th Tank Division stays east as a key mobile reserve against Japanese armor.
- German final Moscow push is somewhat more favorable locally than history because several historical Far Eastern formations are absent, but German logistics, vehicles, fuel, cold-weather preparation and attrition remain binding.
- By late November German momentum visibly stalls. Tokyo's 1941 “Soviet collapse soon” thesis loses further credibility.

## December 1941 — global war does not merge

- Soviet counteroffensive begins 5-6 December. It is somewhat less dense in selected sectors than historical but succeeds in removing the immediate Moscow threat.
- 7/8 December: no Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, Malaya, Philippines, Hong Kong or Netherlands East Indies.
- 11 December: no historical German/Italian declaration of war on the United States.
- United States remains formally nonbelligerent; Lend-Lease and Atlantic confrontation remain.
- 16 December: Yamato commissions; unlike history it is not being rushed into a Pacific war.
- Third Konoe Cabinet remains; historical Tojo Cabinet does not exist.
- Japan holds ~19 divisions in the north but begins reducing the most economically disruptive temporary mobilization “fat” while retaining combat core, heavy support, armor and aviation.

## January-March 1942 — winter attrition, neutrality transactions

- Soviet winter offensives continue and inflict setbacks but do not destroy Army Group Center.
- Finland shifts increasingly to defensive war and exit-seeking rather than major new offensive aims.
- Japan turns Kantokuen from emergency mobilization toward a more sustainable reinforced standing posture.
- March 20: fisheries modus vivendi/extension with USSR is retained. North Sakhalin interests also favor practical neutrality.
- Pacific/Vladivostok Lend-Lease becomes a political issue: Japan monitors but does not blockade; it seeks political assurances that imported supplies are for anti-German use, not direct Far East buildup against Japan.
- 31 March spring review: Japan does not open a 1942 spring Soviet war. It waits for the German summer campaign while keeping north/south/neutrality options alive.

## April 1942 — German strategy turns south

- 5 April: Fuehrer Directive 41 confirms southern strategic emphasis: Don, Caucasus and oil rather than a renewed direct Moscow-first strategy.
- Tokyo understands the logic because oil is a core Japanese strategic concern. It does not treat the directive as proof of victory.
- No.800 medium standard carrier construction execution is authorized; detailed design proceeds.

## May-June 1942 — Soviet southern defeats and an African divergence

- Second Kharkov and Crimea remain major Soviet defeats.
- 28 June: Case Blue begins.
- Africa: Axis still wins tactically at Gazala because British command/armored problems remain. However, absence of the Japan war leaves additional Commonwealth/British reserve power in the Middle East. In V20 working replay, Tobruk does not collapse in the historical June rush; Axis advance into Egypt therefore does not follow the exact historical path.
- No Japanese Indian Ocean raid; no automatic Madagascar invasion trigger.

## July 1942 — Soviet transfer wave partly blocked

- 23 July: Rostov falls; German operational picture looks extremely strong.
- Historical Soviet July Far-East transfer wave is re-run against a much larger Japanese northern threat.
- Current working decisions:
  - 96th Rifle Division: GO west;
  - 204th Rifle Division: GO west;
  - 205th Rifle Division: GO west despite risk because southern emergency is acute;
  - 87th Rifle Division: HOLD east;
  - 98th Rifle Division: HOLD east;
  - 126th Rifle Division: HOLD east;
  - 208th Rifle Division: HOLD east;
  - 422nd Rifle Division: HOLD east.
- This preserves Soviet Far East combat quality but removes several historically important reinforcements from the Stalingrad approach.
- Japan's `N-OPP` rises to ORANGE but does not yet trigger war.

## August 1942 — northward opportunity turns RED

- Germany enters the Maikop region; oil facilities are wrecked and not immediately useful.
- German 6th Army reaches the Volga north of Stalingrad around the historical late-August window. Worldline Soviet defense is thinner because several historical Far Eastern reinforcements are absent.
- Local German conditions improve: the northern Volga corridor is harder to cut; southern pressure can be somewhat deeper. However, no automatic August fall of Stalingrad is replayed.
- 23-24 August: Tokyo conducts a full northward review.
- `N-OPP` becomes RED.
- Japan orders two additional China-withdrawal divisions into northward preparation/transfer on 24 August, aiming at ~21 divisions in September. Exact division identities remain OPEN.
- Fifth Fleet/North Sakhalin readiness, rail schedules, air/AT/armor stocks and Soviet-front intelligence receive higher priority.
- Japan simultaneously reaffirms the Neutrality Pact and avoids public war threats.
- Berlin is pressed for a clearer description of post-Soviet spheres/boundaries; “Germany wins” is no longer accepted as automatically synonymous with “Japan benefits.”
- 31 August 24:00: Japan has not attacked USSR; southern commercial policy remains active; no US/UK/NL resource strangulation has occurred.
