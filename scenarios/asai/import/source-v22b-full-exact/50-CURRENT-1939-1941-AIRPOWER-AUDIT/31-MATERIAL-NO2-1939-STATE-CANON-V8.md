# MATERIAL NO.2 — 1939-04-20 HIGH-q STRUCTURAL TEST STATE — V8 AIRPOWER9

> Current closure for Material No.2 at the Apr20 checkpoint. The old structural-testbed geometry remains useful; the old ~300 kgf E5-jet propulsion anchor is superseded by AIRPOWER6 E5-J. The answer is not to redesign the aircraft as a record machine, but to use the mature engine with a **staged thrust / dynamic-pressure release**.

## 1. Purpose

Material No.2 answers structural attribution questions under high dynamic pressure:

- sandwich/plant-fiber bonded skin stiffness;
- bonded-joint behavior;
- vibration/flutter;
- control-surface reversal margin;
- jet thermal boundary;
- repeated high-q damage and inspection.

It is not an HS-series Mach research vehicle and not a service fighter.

## 2. Retained airframe coordinate

Retain the useful old structural geometry unless flight construction forces a local correction:

- single seat;
- straight mid-wing;
- E5-J in rear fuselage;
- wing area ~9.5 m2;
- span ~7.6 m;
- length ~8.6 m;
- unusually large fuselage maximum diameter ~1.10 m because stiffness, not strength, drives the early sandwich shell;
- full weight ~1.48 t class;
- empty ~0.98 t old center before final instrumentation/rework accounting.

The central structural lesson remains: early low-modulus sandwich structure buys panel efficiency/finish/process benefits but needs a visibly fuller section to recover global bending stiffness.

## 3. Propulsion correction

The old ~300 kgf engine assumption is superseded.

Use a normal E5-J flight package:

- E5-J-Q ~425-430 kgf;
- E5-J-N ~450-455 kgf;
- selected short point near ~470 kgf;
- complete engine dry ~380-415 kg.

But Material No.2 does **not** open full thrust on the first flights. Test-card throttle stops deliberately reproduce and then exceed the old load environment.

## 4. Dynamic-pressure release ladder

Under the old constant-thrust/drag comparison, 300 kgf corresponded to ~568 km/h at sea level and q ~15.3 kPa; a 1.25-speed dive corresponded to ~23.8 kPa.

With the now-closed E5-J, the same airframe could theoretically be pushed much harder. Therefore the structural program uses q gates rather than a headline speed target:

1. **q <= 15 kPa** — correlation with old design proof;
2. **q <= 20 kPa** — first structural expansion;
3. **q <= 25 kPa** — beyond old level-flight envelope;
4. **q <= 30 kPa** — high-q qualification region;
5. **q ~35 kPa selected test** — only after flutter/joint/thermal evidence supports it.

At sea-level-equivalent density these correspond roughly to 564, 651, 727, 797 and 860 km/h respectively. Actual flights can choose altitude/speed to separate q from Mach as far as practicable.

This makes Material No.2 complementary to HS-2:

- Material No.2 emphasizes **q / stiffness / structural damage**;
- HS-2 emphasizes **Mach / compressibility / pressure-center / controls**.

## 5. 1939-04-20 physical state

Close the current state as:

- design and major structural definition complete;
- full-size representative panels/joints and structural companions already in static/fatigue/environmental work;
- one flight article in build, **~60-70% complete**;
- E5-J package allocated as the mature propulsion baseline;
- engine-bay/firewall/exhaust thermal isolation remains metal + insulated gap around the hot zone;
- no first-flight result yet.

First-flight gate remains **1939 H2 / early 1940 depending structural proof**. A working internal target in late summer/autumn 1939 is reasonable but is not canonized as an achieved date.

## 6. Why full E5-J thrust is not a problem

The propulsion upgrade does not invalidate the experiment because:

- thrust is throttle-schedulable;
- the new engine is more mature than the old 300 kgf assumption;
- higher available thrust gives a wider q map without forcing it on the first sortie;
- the aircraft can remain a deliberately blunt/fuller structural article rather than a speed-record configuration.

Thus the v46 engine re-audit strengthens the Material No.2 test program instead of turning it into an accidental HS aircraft.
