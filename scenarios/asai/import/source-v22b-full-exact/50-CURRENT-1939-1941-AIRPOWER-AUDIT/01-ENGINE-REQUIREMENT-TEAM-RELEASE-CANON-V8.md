# ENGINE / REQUIREMENT / TEAM-RELEASE CANON — V8

## Engine audit structure
Use:
- **EH** historical development state;
- **EC** computation / instrumentation gain;
- **EMQ** materials, machining, heat treatment, quality-control gain;
- **ES** supercharging, fuel, lubrication, exhaust and integration systems;
- **EI** integrated test maturity;
- **EA** actual engine available for aircraft/service at that date;
- **ET** engineering-team release into the next project.

## Common Asai diffusion to piston-engine industry
Do not transfer E-series compressor/turbine core IP wholesale. Plausible sales/licensing/transfer include:
- high-temperature exhaust-valve and exhaust-system materials;
- low-Ni/non-Ni high-strength steels where temperature regime permits;
- nitriding / heat treatment / surface processes;
- crank/gear/bearing machining and gauges;
- dynamic balancing;
- test-cell instrumentation and corrected engine maps;
- fuel/governor precision work;
- exhaust turbo as Asai product/black-box module;
- supercharger/cooling computation service;
- thrust-exhaust integration knowledge.
Fuel octane and lubricant quality remain independent hard gates.

### DB601 domestic: Ha-40 / Atsuta
This is one of the strongest direct V8 gains because historical problems map closely to Asai strengths: grinding, bearings, gauges, heat treatment, fuel-injection precision, water-leak inspection, QC, interchangeability.
- license timing historical unless later audit changes it.
- real schedule improvement **2-4 months** is plausible, and the larger effect is yield/serviceability.
- Ha-40 pilot accepted engines can plausibly appear in 1941H2, then series ramp 1942; no fantasy 1940 mass production.
- Atsuta pilot lot in 1940 can be healthier than historical, with 1941 ramp materially better.

### Homare
- do not complete in 1939.
- concept study target 1939Q3-Q4; formal design 1940Q1.
- first-running/durability clock only modestly earlier; 1942 main production window broadly maintained.
- primary divergence: production-engine quality, lubrication, vibration, temperature, tolerance control and prototype-to-series cliff reduced.
- low octane can still force derating.

### Ha-43 / MK9 class
- not a formal 1939 project.
- larger Mitsubishi 18-cylinder pre-study target 1940Q3; formal 1941Q1; first hardware late 1941-early 1942.
- advantage is extra pre-design and system maturity, not a magical year-early engine.

## Peripheral engine systems — CLOSED principles
- 3-blade constant-speed propellers are real historical technology already entering Japanese production; V8 improves accepted output, governor precision and integration, not invention.
- 4-blade high-power propeller pre-study 1939H2, rig/full-engine work in 1940-41.
- two-speed mechanical supercharging is the main near-term path; two-stage is a distinct harder branch.
- low-pressure fuel injection can be trialed earlier because Asai has metering/pump/nozzle experience; do not auto-replace all carburetors.
- DB601 direct injection remains a separate licensed technology path.
- design-origin thrust exhaust is evaluated on new high-speed aircraft, but never double-count where historical installations are already equivalent.
- exhaust turbo is mission-selective, especially Ki-46/high-altitude reconnaissance/interception/large bombers; no standard turbo on A6M/Ki-43/Ki-51/Ki-48/B5N/D3A.
- no double-counting of exhaust energy between turbo and thrust exhaust.

## Requirement-generation model — CLOSED
Aircraft requirements must be generated from:
1. **R0 Mission exists**;
2. **R1 Threat / operational urgency**;
3. **R2 Portfolio gap** in existing aircraft;
4. **R3 Technology demonstration** creating a credible new option;
5. **R4 Requirement translation** into speed/climb/range/armament/handling;
6. **R5 Industrial reality**: price, maintenance, basing, production, fuel, trained crews;
7. **R6 Revalidation at completion**: the world may have changed while the project was in development.

This permits a historically better aircraft to sell less in V8 because a competing technology has taken its seat by completion.

## E5/E6 effect on conventional fighters
- E5 Single does **not** erase J2M/Ki-44 demand. It proves high-speed energy-fighter feasibility and also proves that speed alone is not a quick-reaction interceptor.
- J2M becomes more explicitly a conventional, cheap, rapid-scramble base-defense interceptor. Its formal 14-Shi date stays Sep 1939.
- At completion J2M faces R6 against E6 single/JF economics, runway, TBO, fuel, maintenance and climb. Adoption likely; mass procurement remains open.
- Ki-44 survives more easily because Army front-line basing and logistics make turbine-only replacement unattractive.
