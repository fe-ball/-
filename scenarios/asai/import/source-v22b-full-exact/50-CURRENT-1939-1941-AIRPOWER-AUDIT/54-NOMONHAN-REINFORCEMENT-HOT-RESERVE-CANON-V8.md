# NOMONHAN REINFORCEMENT / HOT-RESERVE CANON — V8 NOMONHAN-A2

## Purpose
Correct the replay failure that treated the Aug20 battle only as a front-line closed system. Reinforcements, rear-area repaired units, aviation transfers, ammunition production, and forces outside the immediate Soviet artillery box must be represented by state and travel time.

## Readiness semantics
- **COLD**: rear base; rail/maintenance movement needed.
- **WARM**: in theater; generally 24-48 h from combat.
- **HOT**: fueled, armed, crewed, communications checked, able to move within hours.

HOT does not mean forward deployed or already under enemy fire.

## Reserve geometry
- 3rd Tank Regiment: inner reserve, roughly 15-25 km behind main resistance zone; protects road junctions, artillery rear, withdrawal corridor; Chi-Ha 4 form its faster local sub-reserve.
- 4th Tank Regiment: outer reserve, roughly 25-40 km behind / outside the immediate front; used against a mechanized envelopment axis after direction is confirmed.

These are working operational distances, not fixed map coordinates.

## Employment rule
A smaller Japanese armored reserve facing Soviet mass armor should not seek a symmetric tank duel. Preferred 1939 worldline use:
- force a marching Soviet column to deploy;
- strike reconnaissance/armored-car elements, motorized infantry, AT-gun towing elements and transport where accessible;
- use special AP as refusal fire against BT/BA armor;
- withdraw by bounds before artillery/air/armor mass fixes the Japanese regiment.

Operational delay may be more important than destroyed-vehicle count.
