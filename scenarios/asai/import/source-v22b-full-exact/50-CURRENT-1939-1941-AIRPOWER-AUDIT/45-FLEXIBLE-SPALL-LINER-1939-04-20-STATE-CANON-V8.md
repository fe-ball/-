# FLEXIBLE SPALL LINER — 1939-04-20 STATE CANON — V8 AIRPOWER13

> **Checkpoint:** 1939-04-20 24:00. This file closes the rollback gap between prewar plant-fiber/material research and the later Nomonhan limited vehicle trial. It does **not** back-propagate the July-August 1939 vehicle count or combat result.

## 1. Identity

The 1939 article is a **removable flexible fragment-catching liner**, not additional primary armor and not the later rigid GFRP/Doron-style composite-protection branch.

Its intended mechanism is deliberately simple:

- the main steel armor remains the penetration barrier;
- a flexible rear layer catches or decelerates small secondary fragments/spall after a non-penetrating or marginal hit;
- energy is dissipated by fiber stretch, pull-out, sliding and distributed rupture rather than by a thick hard plate.

Candidate fiber systems already exist in Asai's materials work: selected silk for high-performance comparison articles and lower-cost hemp/hair cloth, felt or mat families for scalable vehicle work. Full resin saturation is not the objective for the final flexible catching layer.

## 2. Why the idea does not begin at Nomonhan

By Apr1939 Asai has already paid the relevant upstream clocks:

- plant-fiber grading, drying, weave/roving and resin/process work;
- impact/fracture and sandwich-panel test practice;
- ballistic proof/range instrumentation from dense-core/special-ammunition work;
- vehicle interior/package and removable-panel fabrication capability.

With reincarnator foreknowledge, the fragment-catching mechanism and the distinction between a **flexible energy-absorbing back layer** and a later **rigid structural composite armor** are known before a battlefield demand appears. Nomonhan may create an urgent deployment/validation event; it does not invent the concept.
