## 2. Carrier-fighter gate
When the replay reaches the Zero-successor decision, resolve in this order:

### R0 threat / mission
What carrier fighter is actually required by the replayed strategic situation?
Record:
- expected enemy fighter/bomber speed and altitude;
- carrier deck/elevator footprint constraints;
- desired radius/endurance;
- escort vs fleet-defense weighting;
- armament/protection demand;
- acceptable runway/deck handling;
- required introduction urgency.

### R1 existing fleet / bridge
Evaluate the current A6M family and any Kinsei-powered bridge against the mission.
Do not order a new large fighter merely because the technology exists if an improved bridge closes the near-term gap cheaply enough.

### R2 candidate engines
Compare at the actual date:
- Homare available state and production maturity;
- Ha-43 available state and schedule risk;
- fuel/oil assumptions;
- propeller/cooling state;
- factory and maintenance implications.

### R3 candidate airframes
Use the persistent 1941 Reppu-equivalent predesign as a starting asset, but regenerate the actual specification from the worldline requirement.
Do not copy historical wing loading, climb, range or armament values automatically.

### R4 parallelism decision
Decide whether to:
- commit only to Homare;
- maintain Homare + Ha-43 parallel prototype installations;
- use Homare as bridge and Ha-43 as growth variant;
- delay a large new fighter in favor of A6M/J2M/N1K production.

### R5 industrial allocation
Explicitly price:
- engine factory/tooling seats;
- propeller family and VDM man-hours;
- Mitsubishi airframe design/manufacturing capacity;
- carrier production/availability;
- overlap with J2M/N1K/B7A and bombers.

### R6 revalidation
At prototype completion, rerun the requirement against the new combat state before serial commitment.

## 3. N1K procurement gate
N1K1-J technical first flight can occur before this procurement gate is fully closed.
The replay then decides:
- whether its measured performance fills a real land-fighter/interceptor gap;
- whether the long-gear/mid-wing penalties are tolerable for a short bridge run;
- whether N1K2 redesign deserves priority;
- how much Homare capacity it competes away from bombers/attack aircraft/carrier fighters.

Do not infer a large N1K1-J quantity solely because the prototype is better behaved in V8.

## 4. J2M procurement gate
J2M remains a rapid-scramble/interceptor answer with high-power radial integration maturity.
At its R6 gate compare:
- climb and scramble time;
- high-altitude power and future supercharging path;
- runway/basing and maintenance;
- A6M/N1K overlap;
- turbine/E-series competition where relevant;
- unit and engine cost.

A technically successful J2M need not imply historical-scale production.

## 5. B7A procurement gate
The B7A requirement lineage is early enough that prototype development can be treated as branch-neutral, but series procurement is not.
At its decision gate evaluate:
- actual carrier availability;
- torpedo/dive-bomber unification value;
- Homare allocation;
- deck handling and sortie cycle;
- competing land-based strike concepts;
- industrial cost of the large high-performance carrier airframe.

## 6. Army high-performance fighter gate
Do not pre-create a Ki-84-equivalent requirement on the historical calendar.
If replayed combat generates an equivalent need, the technical input is already favorable:
- Homare/four-blade propulsion is available enough to avoid a fresh technology program;
- the requirement can therefore be judged on Army mission, airfield, range, protection, weapons, production and team capacity rather than on whether a 1,800-2,000 hp propeller fighter is physically possible.

The prebranch piston-aircraft audit should stop rather than fabricate history when the next event requires one of:
- combat-derived threat assessment;
- fleet/carrier availability;

- wartime resource allocation;
