# ASAI HIGH-ALTITUDE GASOLINE ENGINE / TECHNOLOGY-TRANSFER CANON — V8

## Status
This file closes the **development mechanism and diffusion model**, not a single finished military engine.
It supersedes any older implication that Asai must either mass-produce complete gasoline aero-engines or remain irrelevant to piston-engine development.

## 1. Existing Asai technology topology — CLOSED
Asai's center of gravity is **high-speed rotating machinery and the development culture around it**, not a universal aircraft-engine bureau.

The existing topology is:

- core: high-speed rotors, bearings, balancing, thermal/mechanical measurement, corrected maps, failure analysis;
- upstream: special steels, heat treatment, surface engineering, precision grinding, gauges, gears, seals and lubrication materials;
- downstream: exhaust turbocharging, flight/high-altitude test, controls/actuation, heat exchangers and installation work;
- parallel: production engineering, lot traceability, acceptance testing, field-return analysis and supplier qualification.

Aviation gasoline-supercharging work grows out of this existing topology. It is a **research/application branch**, not a replacement for Nakajima, Mitsubishi, Aichi, Kawasaki, etc.

## 2. What Asai himself contributes — CLOSED
Do not write Asai as personally designing every combustion chamber, crankcase or aircraft installation.
His strongest cheat is **development mechanism / search-order knowledge**:

- identify the controlling variables before the industry has spent years discovering them;
- distinguish failure modes instead of treating all failures as one problem;
- skip known dead-end branches where the contemporary industrial base can already support the better branch;
- start long-lead precursor industries before demand arrives;
- force test results into reproducible specifications, gauges, maps, acceptance limits and supplier processes.

For high-output gasoline engines the key mindset is:

> knock, thermal load, mechanical load, lubrication and supercharging are separate limits; the service setting is the lowest of them.

Asai does **not** gain omniscience about the exact natural frequency, cooling hot spot, intake maldistribution or oil-starvation geometry of an engine that has not yet been built. Those remain engine-specific EI work.

## 3. What company engineers can develop without Asai's personal hand — CLOSED
Once the research framework exists, Asai engineers may independently advance:

- centrifugal compressor maps and diffuser work;
- wastegate/aneroid boost control;
- charge-temperature measurement and intercooler design;
- gasoline knock maps versus fuel grade / boost / ignition / mixture / intake temperature;
- water or water-methanol anti-detonant injection (ADI) rig work;
- fuel metering, pressure carburetion and low-pressure injection experiments;
- bearing material/surface/clearance rigs;
- oil aeration, pump-suction, filtration and oil-cooler studies;
- high-temperature exhaust systems and turbo hot-side materials;
- mechanical interlocks that prevent emergency boost unless the required ADI flow exists.

These are normal engineering programs once Asai has created the equipment, culture and initial problem framing.

## 4. Historical-boundedness rule — CLOSED
Japan historically already understood octane, knock, mechanical supercharging, mixture, cooling and high-output engine problems. V8 must not make ordinary Japanese engineers naive so Asai can look brilliant.

Asai's difference is earlier **integration and repeatable measurement**, not sole discovery of basic combustion science.

The correct contrast is:

- history: many of the right problems are recognized, but individual programs repeatedly rediscover interactions and suffer prototype-to-production degradation;
- V8: several interactions, test methods and component solutions are already on the domestic technology shelf when Nakajima/Mitsubishi begin their next engines.

## 5. Low-octane-sensitivity objective — CLOSED PRINCIPLE
Asai absolutely recognizes that high boost and high BMEP benefit from high anti-knock fuel. He does not claim that octane is unnecessary.

The Asai design objective is instead:

> **Do not make maximum fuel quality an all-or-nothing prerequisite for a useful engine. Reduce performance sensitivity to fuel quality as far as physics and weight permit.**

Primary methods:

1. higher compressor efficiency to reduce charge temperature for the same pressure ratio;
2. selective intercooling where its weight/drag is justified;
3. combustion-chamber geometry and ignition layout that shorten the vulnerable end-gas period;
4. tight ignition timing and compression-ratio production scatter;
5. worst-cylinder, not average-cylinder, mixture/temperature control;
6. pressure carburetion / low-pressure distributed injection research;
7. ADI for short high-output periods;
8. fuel-grade-specific boost limits and mechanical interlocks.

Thus 100-octane fuel remains highly desirable, but the engine has explicit 100 / standard / emergency maps rather than becoming an unknown derated engine when fuel quality changes.

## 6. Asai gasoline high-altitude research engine — CLOSED ROLE / OPEN EXACT MODEL
Asai may build and sell some complete gasoline engines, but this is not a mass aero-engine industry at Nakajima/Mitsubishi scale.

The important product is a **research engine / research-aircraft system**. A roughly 180–200 hp class Asai aviation gasoline engine already fits the V8 lineage and is used as a configurable measurement platform.

It is deliberately not a polished military production engine. Research configuration may include:

- dense instrumentation;
- replaceable pistons / compression ratios;
- adjustable ignition;
- manual and automatic wastegate configurations;
- oversized experimental intercooler/ducting;
- multiple fuel grades;
- swappable metering/nozzle layouts;
- water / water-alcohol injection;
- cylinder-by-cylinder temperature instrumentation;
- high-load bearing and oil-system variants.

The transferable result is not "200 hp becomes 2,000 hp by multiplying cylinder count". Scaling to an 18-cylinder military engine leaves crankshaft, master-rod, torsional, cooling, induction, reduction-gear and production problems to the engine maker.

## 7. Working research timeline — CLOSED AS DEVELOPMENT PATH
### 1931–33
- single-cylinder / small multi-cylinder gasoline-boost work;
- systematic knock mapping versus fuel, boost, ignition, mixture and intake temperature;
- bearing/oil failure classification introduced from existing high-speed machinery work.

### 1933–35
- compressor + combustion + lubrication treated as one high-BMEP research system;
- 180–200 hp class aviation gasoline research engine consolidates the work;
- charge cooling and early ADI rig experiments;
- high-load bearing comparisons and surface-engineering program.

### 1935–36
- research-aircraft/high-altitude operation becomes repeatable;
- approximately 3–5 km flat-rating demonstrations are plausible depending on installation;
- fuel-grade / boost operating maps become an explicit deliverable.

### 1936–37
- aneroid/wastegate work, ignition precision, mixture distribution and ADI flight testing deepen;
- approximately 5 km class high-altitude output retention is a credible technology demonstration, not a frontline fighter specification.

### 1937–38
- aviation gasoline exhaust-turbo A2-type trial branch;
- military/industrial observers and joint tests increase;
- still low-volume research and installation work.

### 1939
- Aero Turbo Pilot / Installation Cell becomes the productization point;
- output remains dozens-of-sets class, not national aero-engine mass production;
- Ki-46/high-altitude reconnaissance/interceptor comparison work is the natural military route.

## 8. Secret-patent / know-how diffusion — CLOSED CHANNEL
Prewar Japan's secret-patent system is a legitimate V8 domestic technology-transfer channel for military inventions. It does **not** mean a hidden patent gazette is automatically distributed to all industry.

Use three IP layers:

1. **ordinary patent/license** — civilian/peripheral technology intended for broad sale;
2. **secret patent + controlled military implementation** — technology desired across selected domestic military manufacturers but not foreign publication;
3. **Asai process know-how** — critical manufacturing/process/database details retained internally and supplied through components, tooling, inspectors or technical service.

A typical military transfer is:

Asai prototype/data -> secret patent or military specification -> Army/Navy controlled joint evaluation -> designated manufacturer implementation -> Asai supplies critical components/test gear/process support where appropriate.

## 9. High-load bearing cheat — CLOSED PATH, EXACT SPECIFICATION MAY EVOLVE
V8 accepts that Asai can independently converge early on a multilayer high-load plain-bearing solution rather than waiting for foreign capture or postwar knowledge.

The cheat is primarily search-order knowledge:

- separate structural fatigue strength from conformability / seizure resistance;
- use a strong backing/support layer plus a soft overlay rather than demand one alloy do everything;
- test corrosion, oil chemistry, shaft misalignment and surface finish as separate failure modes.

Japan already had domestic scientific awareness of indium-bearing ores by the early 1930s. Asai may therefore sponsor small-scale recovery from zinc/smelter residues before mass demand exists.

Working V8 path:
- 1931–33: copper-lead, silver and lead-overlay high-load comparisons;
- 1933–35: accelerated oil-corrosion screening including In-bearing solutions;
- 1934–36: Pb-In / indium-treated multilayer bearing process reaches industrial high-load test maturity;
- late 1930s: available to selected domestic military/high-output engine programs through secret-patent/specification channels.

This does **not** remove oil-starvation, shaft-deflection, wrong-clearance, contamination or engine-specific geometry failures. It mainly removes much of the historical search for a viable bearing material/surface system.

## 10. What is actually sold/transferred — CLOSED
The high-altitude gasoline/turbo offering is a package:

- turbo hardware where mission-selective use is justified;
- boost/wastegate controls;
- metering/ADI controls and selected components;
- bearing/surface/process specifications;
- compressor/boost/fuel/temperature operating maps;
- instrumentation and test procedures;
- installation engineering and joint flight-test support.

This creates a commercial incentive for Asai to make gasoline turbocharging easier to use on imperfect fuel: better engine-side integration expands the addressable market for Asai turbo hardware.

## 11. Military conversion boundary — CLOSED
An Asai research engine or research aircraft is not automatically military-service-ready.
Militaryization still requires the airframe/engine maker and Army/Navy to close:

- full multi-cylinder cooling and induction distribution;
- crankshaft/master-rod/torsional behavior;
- reduction gear and propeller match;
- cowl, oil cooler, fire protection and exhaust installation;
- cold start, field servicing and maintenance intervals;
- fuel/lubricant supply reality;
- production yield and interchangeability;
- military endurance and flight acceptance.

This joint-research absorption clock remains real even when the underlying Asai technology exists earlier.
