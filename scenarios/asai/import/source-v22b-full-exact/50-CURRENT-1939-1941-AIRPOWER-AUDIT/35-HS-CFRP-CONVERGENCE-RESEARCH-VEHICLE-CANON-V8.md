# HS / CFRP CONVERGENCE RESEARCH VEHICLE — V8 AIRPOWER10

> This file closes only the **technical concept and dependency chain**. It does not canonize a 1942/43 first-flight result from the 1939 checkpoint.

## 1. Purpose

The convergence vehicle is not Hakkou/Firen and not a service fighter prototype. It exists to answer one question:

**does a CFRP primary thin wing, built to Series-III allowables, behave in the real transonic shock/aeroelastic environment already mapped by the metal HS-series?**

It is the physical join between the high-speed and composite-material research ladders.

## 2. Why it is a separate aircraft

Series I-III deliberately keep propulsion and fuselage variables quiet while structural material is matured. HS-3 deliberately keeps the airframe metal while transonic physics is mapped. Combining both novelties before either is calibrated would destroy attribution.

The convergence vehicle begins only after:

1. HS-3 has useful M0.85+ pressure/control/load data;
2. Series III has a credible thin-wing stiffness/flutter model;
3. CFRP-metal interface, inspection and repair rules have usable allowables.

## 3. Working architecture

Planning coordinate only:

- single seat;
- metal fuselage, engine bay, landing-gear hard points and initial tail;
- E6-M-J as the initial known propulsion package;
- qualified E6-M reheat rear-end may be added later to control acceleration through the drag-rise region, but is not required for first structural flights;
- standard test weight roughly **2.2-2.5 t**;
- wing area roughly **10-11 m2**;
- initial CFRP wing thickness roughly **7-8%** after Series III earns the step;
- initial sweep **0-15 degrees**, followed by a 20-25 degree comparison wing only after the first CFRP transonic wing is understood;
- highly instrumented, no armament and no service-range requirement.

Do not force all-CFRP construction. Metal carry-through, root fittings, landing-gear structure and hot-zone structure remain entirely legitimate on the first vehicle.

## 4. Wing comparison doctrine

The strongest test is not CFRP against an imaginary perfect metal airplane but **same fuselage / same propulsion / same flight condition** with different wings.

At minimum plan:

- **Wing M** — metal baseline wing with matched external geometry/stiffness target where practical;
- **Wing C1** — straight or very lightly swept CFRP primary wing;
- **Wing C2** — later modest-sweep CFRP wing using the same design/inspection doctrine;
- optional **C3** — thinner/higher-Mach or different stiffness-tailoring article after C1/C2 evidence.

Ground twins are built with the same tooling/process lot for each structural wing family.

## 5. What is measured

- spanwise deflection and torsion under real shock-loaded pressure distribution;
- flutter mode/frequency/damping migration with Mach;
- divergence and control-reversal margin;
- shock location versus elastic twist;
- strain and bond-line state versus NDT after the sortie;
- metal/CFRP fitting load transfer;
- surface waviness/shape retention after thermal and humidity cycling;
- damage tolerance and repair effect on stiffness.

## 6. Flight-envelope concept

The sequence is deliberately conservative in **attribution**, not in capability:

- first correlate below M0.75;
- then M0.80-0.85;
- then M0.88-0.92 after flutter/strain correlation;
- M0.95+ / M1-near tests only after the HS-3 control solution and CFRP wing evidence support them.

No old M0.78 white-rainbow placard is a ceiling. Equally, no M1 result is awarded merely because the engine has thrust.

## 7. Timing shadow

From the Apr20 checkpoint, a technically coherent favorable sequence is:

- 1941-42: HS-3 and Series-III evidence mature enough to define the joint vehicle;
- 1942: full-scale metal/CFRP ground twins and vehicle build can proceed;
- **1942 H2-1943 H1:** earliest credible first-flight band if preceding gates pass;
- later 1943+: transonic expansion / modest-sweep comparison.

These are technical dependency shadows, not branch-independent historical achievements.

## 8. Relationship to the future practical aircraft

The convergence vehicle determines whether CFRP should be spent on:

- a transonic/low-supersonic multirole fighter retaining low wing loading and useful range;
- a high-altitude/long-range specialization;
- a lighter high-Mach fighter;
- or only selected composite primary structures on an otherwise metal aircraft.

Therefore the old Hakkou/Firen mission/specification remains **OPEN FOR REDESIGN** rather than automatically restored.
