# H1 / H2 ROTORCRAFT VISUAL CLOSEOUT V1

## H1
H1 is visually a late-1930s experimental helicopter rather than a shrunken modern utility helicopter. Fuselage/boom length is **8.35 m**; main rotor **10.80 m**, three articulated blades; tail rotor **1.75 m**, two blades on the port side. The compact two-place cabin is side-by-side, but a normal research flight may carry only the pilot.

The welded steel-tube primary body remains partly visible or lightly fabric/metal faired. The air-cooled piston engine and transmission live immediately aft of the cabin with removable louvered panels. The tail is a tapered truss/fabric boom, fixed wide main wheels and a tailwheel. Main rotor blade chord tapers from about 0.32 to 0.28 m. No hydraulic-control housings, modern sensor balls or weapon hardpoints are allowed.

## H2
H2 is the first practical enclosed Asai helicopter OML. Fuselage/boom length **9.15 m**, main rotor **11.20 m** three-blade articulated, tail rotor **2.00 m** two-blade port-side. Cabin width 1.45 m supports side-by-side crew and a rear third-person/litter bay; the starboard sliding door is about 0.95 x 1.25 m.

The E5-S-S installation is behind the cabin. Intake is screened at the dorsal/shoulder area; exhaust exits upward/aft so it does not wash the tail boom. The cabin and tail boom are substantially faired/semi-monocoque compared with H1. Fixed tricycle gear (2.55 m main track) is the canonical ground/deck-handling arrangement. Rescue winch, flotation and tie-down additions are removable Navy mission kits, not baseline OML.

The H1/H2 visual canon does not introduce electronic stabilization, autopilot or modern rotor-head fairings; visible pitch links, hinges, swashplate/control rods and service access are period-correct features.
