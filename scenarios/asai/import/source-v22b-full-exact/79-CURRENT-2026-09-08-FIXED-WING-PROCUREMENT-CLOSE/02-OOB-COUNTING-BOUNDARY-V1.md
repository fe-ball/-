# 1941 FIXED-WING OOB COUNTING BOUNDARY — V12

## Four separate states
For every aircraft family, track these states separately:
1. **factory accepted** — the manufacturer/acceptance authority has accepted the airframe;
2. **service released** — the type/article is permitted for normal service rather than restricted development;
3. **unit assigned** — an aircraft is physically charged to a normal operational unit;
4. **operationally available** — assigned aircraft that are actually ready for the mission/date in question.

No lower state may be inferred automatically from a higher one.

## Ordinary 1941 OOB eligible
Only these V12 branches can enter ordinary 1941 fixed-wing OOB without a new technical gate:
- E6 Twin serial aircraft;
- E6 Single serial aircraft;
- Ki-76 production aircraft that have passed the limited-service release gate.

Common E6 Twin mission/service fits remain subsets of the E6 Twin pool.

## Development / evaluation inventory — not ordinary OOB
The following remain outside ordinary 1941 OOB even if military crews fly them from operational airfields:
- E6-B 1941 service-development/early evaluation aircraft;
- A7M Gaifu prototypes/service-evaluation aircraft;
- A8N Sakufu prototypes;
- Ki-40 TP reconnaissance prototypes/evaluation aircraft;
- J2N (zero aircraft in 1941);
- E7 retrofit aircraft;
- E6 powered-lift demonstrator;
- P2 deck demonstrator;
- HS/material/other dedicated research airframes.

## Conversion accounting
If an existing serial E6 aircraft is transferred to E7 or powered-lift development, it is not counted twice. During the development period it must be removed from the ordinary available serial pool for any date-specific OOB.

## E6-B special boundary
An E6-B service-evaluation detachment may exist in 1941 and may fly realistic military missions for evaluation. This does not authorize normal bomber-unit OOB, routine combat availability, or theater assignment without a later replay/service gate.

## Ki-76 limited-service rule
Of roughly 18-24 1941 production acceptances, only roughly 12-18 may be normal service-released/unit-equipped by year end. The remainder can be acceptance reserve, school, modification or technical aircraft.

## Replay rule
When a 1941 or later combat replay begins, derive combat availability from:
`factory accepted -> service released -> unit assigned -> operationally available`.
Do not take the procurement ledger as a combat-order-of-battle ledger.
