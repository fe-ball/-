# NAVAL / MARINE CLOSEOUT HANDOFF — V14

Status: **CURRENT-HIGHEST for naval computation, ship-system integration, marine propulsion/test infrastructure and prewar adoption boundary through 1941-12-08.** Combat history is not advanced.

## Central verdict
The 1941 fleet is not visually transformed into a future fleet. Battleships, cruisers, destroyers and carriers remain predominantly historical-type hulls with steam propulsion. The largest differences are embedded in:
- computation and design iteration;
- weight/stability bookkeeping;
- shaft/reduction/propeller analysis;
- electrical/FCS integration and acceptance;
- machinery reliability/QC;
- faster engineering response when a new requirement appears;
- a much larger **option set** of technically mature but not-yet-procured systems.

## Asai scope
Asai is not Navy ruler, fleet designer or doctrine owner. Navy bureaus, arsenals, yards and primes retain requirements, procurement and operating decisions. Asai's direct authority is strongest in GT/turbo, materials, gears, measurement/QC, computation/electrical methods and joint test work. Farther domains receive **ripple effects**, not Asai command.

## 1941 high-level closures
- Surface combatants remain primarily steam through 1941.
- Yamato central propulsion remains all-steam; improved 13-go diesel engineering does not erase missing service history.
- No selected carrier/cruiser/destroyer GT propulsion or boost installation before 1941-12-08.
- Marine GT small-craft technology has crossed the threshold where, if Navy demand appears, it can move into prototype/acceptance/production engineering rather than fundamental research.
- A dedicated Yokosuka propulsion test ship is activated as long-horizon test infrastructure: 1938 requirement, 1939 design/order, 1940 launch/outfit, 1941 full trials.
- GT is not yet qualified as a sole primary large-warship heart in 1941. E6 is M1/boost-qualified territory and approaches M2 work; M3 primary-propulsion qualification remains future.
- Existing slow merchant conversions do **not** receive prewar GT boost by default. If powered-lift/STOL carrier aircraft qualify in 1942+, slow carriers gain value from aircraft-side low-speed performance rather than expensive retrofitted speed.
- Landing craft remain NA diesel main line; heavy landing craft and amphibious support are low-rate development branches.

## Strategic interpretation
Count both **inventory** and **option value**. A technically ready system with zero series inventory may still change strategy if the requirement-to-fielding lag has collapsed from years to months/roughly a year. This is particularly important for GT torpedo boats, radar integration provisions, heavy landing craft, advanced submarine studies and marine-GT propulsion.
