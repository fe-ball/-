# MARINE GT RATING / LIFE / QUALIFICATION CLOSEOUT — V14

## 1. The issue is no longer "can it make shaft power?"
Once the power turbine, reduction gear and propeller train work, ship speed follows ordinary installed shaft-power/hull physics. The decisive question is whether the GT system is reliable enough to be trusted as the heart of a ship.

The life ledger therefore separates:
- `L_core`: rated hot-section engineering life;
- `H_qual`: cumulative qualification/endurance hours;
- `TBO_prod/OH_prod`: installed product interval;
- starts/cycles, high-rating minutes, salt exposure, overtemperature/overspeed and module replacements.

## 2. Derating is a first-class marine design tool
E5 already demonstrates the worldline principle that moderate temperature derating can change life non-linearly: the non-Ni 800 C full-rating coordinate is far shorter-lived than the 760–770 C production/workhorse coordinate. Do not turn this into a universal fixed multiplier; local material, stress, geometry, starts and environment still matter.

Marine design may therefore choose a larger/more capable core and spend surplus capability on lower TIT/stress and longer life rather than maximum power. New generations also allow genuinely larger core scales, but scale-up is a new design and qualification problem, not free multiplication.

## 3. 1941 E6 marine state
E6-M working marine coordinates:
- short/sprint: ~2,300 hp;
- continuous: ~2,000–2,100 hp;
- long-life/transport-type derate coordinate: ~1,870 hp;
- cumulative marine qualification target: ~300 h;
- hot-section inspection: ~100 h class;
- product design-TBO target: ~500 h class, **not a fleet guarantee**.

This is enough for high-value boost/sprint and serious auxiliary experimentation. It is not yet sufficient evidence for sole primary propulsion of a capital/surface combatant.

## 4. Marine qualification ladder
### M1 — BOOST QUALIFIED
Salt/intake, restart, gearbox/shaft, clutch/freewheel, washing, repeatable sprint and catastrophic-failure prevention demonstrated. Several-hundred-hour qualification can be sufficient for a boost product whose full-power annual hours are low.

### M2 — CONTINUOUS / AUXILIARY QUALIFIED
Long continuous running, multi-module reproducibility, predictable inspection interval, reducer life, module exchange and shipboard maintenance are demonstrated. A product TBO around/above the thousand-hour class becomes the **target**, not an automatic 1941 value.

### M3 — PRIMARY-PROPULSION QUALIFIED
The ship may depend on this family to come home. Required evidence includes multi-lot reproducibility, several-thousand-hour-class life or an operationally equivalent module-swap availability system, reducer/shaft life, predictable salt management, voyage cycles, spares, yard overhaul and common-mode/battle-damage control.

**1941 E6 is not M3.**

## 5. Larger cores
E7+ large cores can make several-thousand shaft hp, but the marine value is not only more power. A core with substantially higher capability may be run at a lower fraction of its thermal/stress ceiling. That can make the same 3,000 hp less punishing than forcing a smaller architecture beyond its natural margin. The genuinely new large-core compressor/disk/casing/bearing/starting elements receive their own local qualification clock.
