# LOW-SPEED STOL CARRIER / CARRIER GT DECISION — V14

## 1. Prewar carrier GT retrofit decision
**No selected carrier GT boost installation before 1941-12-08.** Existing slow merchant conversions are not retrofitted merely to chase fleet-carrier speed.

The old idea "~21 kt merchant carrier + GT -> ~25 kt quasi-fleet carrier" is not the central branch. Speed does not repair deck length, elevators, hangar volume, aviation fuel, ammunition, maintenance throughput, damage control or sortie generation.

## 2. Aircraft-side solution changes the optimization
V12 fixed-wing work creates a 1941 powered-lift/STOL development path using cold bypass-flow blown high lift. A8N/Sakufu remains prototype-only in 1941 and carrier qualification is **1942+**; there is no 1941 hover/vertical-landing fighter.

If 1942 carrier trials demonstrate the short-deck approach/takeoff envelope, a slow merchant-conversion carrier can become more valuable **without** expensive propulsion retrofit. The problem shifts from "make the ship faster to create WOD" toward "use aircraft that need less WOD/deck run."

## 3. Resulting low-speed air-carrier concept
Potential missions after aircraft qualification:
- convoy CAP;
- reconnaissance/liaison;
- ASW/search support;
- rescue/rotorcraft support;
- amphibious-force air cover;
- limited strike depending on aircraft, deck handling and magazines.

This is not automatically a fleet-strike carrier. It is a distinct low-speed aviation-support/escort carrier concept.

## 4. Future new-build/conversion branch
GT is not dead for carriers. A future ship designed from the start around diesel+GT, steam+GT or GT-electric can avoid much retrofit tax. Such a ship requires marine M2/M3 qualification and its own yard/shaft/intake/exhaust design gate.

## 5. OOB guard
1941 powered-lift carrier fighter OOB = 0. Low-speed STOL-carrier force structure is a **1942+ policy/qualification option**, not a prewar combat fact.
