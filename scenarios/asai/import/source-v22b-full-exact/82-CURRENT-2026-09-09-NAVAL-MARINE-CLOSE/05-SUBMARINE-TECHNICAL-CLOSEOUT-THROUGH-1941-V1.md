# SUBMARINE TECHNICAL CLOSEOUT THROUGH 1941 — V14

## Principle
Do not turn Asai turbo/computation into magic surface speed or depth. The strongest prewar ripple is reliable use of existing power, electrical/battery reproducibility, rapid-dive bookkeeping and noise/shaft management.

## Main historical-type families
Kaida and Junsen A/B/C-type development remains broadly Navy-driven. Existing Navy engine/aircraft-reconnaissance mission choices are not replaced by Asai designs.

## Machinery and battery ripple
- two-stroke exhaust turbo work remains a compound-scavenging/test branch rather than blanket prewar fleet fit;
- engine failure maps, torsional vibration and acceptance become more systematic;
- battery separators, support, ventilation/H2 handling, motor thermal limits and high-current acceptance receive stronger QC;
- shaft/prop balancing and quiet/avoid-rpm maps become acceptance/operational data rather than anecdote.

## 1941 next-generation study
A non-aircraft, underwater-performance-focused attack-submarine research line is allowed to begin in 1941H2. Central engineering envelope remains roughly 1,800–1,850 t class, ~21 kt surface / ~12 kt underwater study, six tubes / twelve torpedoes, large conventional lead batteries and multiple electric motors. This is a **research/yard/electrical program, not a 1941 operational submarine**.

A ~15 kt underwater second generation is later research. Do not automatically open a wholly new high-output two-stroke diesel family merely because turbo knowledge exists; existing mature Navy engine families remain the first comparison.

Exact 1941–42 yard slots, battery/motor production accounting and fleet OOB remain intentional OPEN items.
