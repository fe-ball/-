# YOKOSUKA PROPULSION TEST SHIP CLOSEOUT — V14

## Decision
V14 activates the formerly optional dedicated propulsion-laboratory concept because the expected test queue is no longer one E5 experiment. By 1938–39 the foreseeable program includes E6-M rating/life work, larger marine cores, derating comparison, diesel comparison, recuperation, combining systems and GT-electric alternatives. Repeatedly converting unrelated old ships would consume more yard time and contaminate A/B data.

## Administrative identity
- owner/operator: **Yokosuka Naval Arsenal / Navy experimental service**;
- category: auxiliary miscellaneous/service vessel, **machinery experimental ship**;
- no prestigious combatant name is required;
- historical registry/public-number crosswalk remains OPEN; old working `Public Ship No. 4017` is **not locked**.

## Development clock
- 1938: requirement;
- 1939: design/order;
- 1940: launch/outfitting;
- 1941: full propulsion trials.

## Working ship envelope
- standard displacement: **700–900 t class**;
- full-load engineering band: roughly 900–1,100 t;
- length: roughly 70–80 m;
- beam: roughly 8.5–9.5 m;
- two main test shafts;
- initial test plant: **2 × E6-M**, aggregate roughly 4,000–4,400 hp depending on rating point;
- independent safe-return / harbor diesel machinery.

Exact principal dimensions, shaft rpm and detailed reducer geometry remain design-document details rather than history-critical constants.

## Why two shafts
The ship is an A/B laboratory, not a speed-record craft. Two substantially independent trains allow:
- normal vs derated E6;
- different intake/separator designs;
- different materials/coatings;
- E6 vs diesel;
- simple-cycle vs recuperated;
- later E6 vs larger core;
- mechanical vs electrical transmission studies.

## Design-for-experiment features
- exchangeable engine foundations/adaptor beds;
- replaceable intake and exhaust trunks;
- standardized alignment datums and couplings;
- permanent instrumentation room;
- large inspection/removal hatches and lifting access;
- workshop and spares space;
- separate safe-return plant so a failed experimental engine does not strand the ship.

## What it can and cannot prove
It can qualify marine environment, long-duration rating/life, salt management, gear/shaft endurance, maintenance and comparative architectures under standardized sea conditions.

It **cannot** by itself qualify a carrier/cruiser/destroyer installation. Each real class must still pay its own frame, trunk, reduction/combining gear, shaft, heat, damage-control and yard-installation gate.

OOB/combat status: **experimental/support only; combat OOB = 0.**
