# 1939-08-22 OUTSIDE-RELIEF / CORRIDOR LINK — V8 NOMONHAN-A3

## Historical anchor retained
Historical Aug21-22 Soviet southern forces bend the Japanese southern flank inward and place mobile elements across likely eastward escape routes. Historical summaries place a southern blocking position roughly 11 miles east of the river, while Soviet artillery/armor progressively reduce isolated Japanese strongpoints. Historical 2/28 intelligence on Aug22 estimates a much larger local enemy than on Aug16.

The worldline does not remove these forces. It changes their arrival geometry because Aug20-21 mobile delaying actions and early Japanese withdrawal mean the escape route is already defended and already in use.

## 0000-0600: withdrawal under pressure
The south/center withdrawal continues through the night. Workshop parties return two light-damage vehicles, giving a dawn immediate tank state of about **39**:
- 3rd Tank Regiment **~21**;
- 4th Tank Regiment **~18**.

Towable long-range artillery, wounded and signal/medical elements receive route priority. Short-range/horse-drawn/heavily damaged equipment has a much higher abandonment risk.

## 0600-1100: Soviet blocking attempt
Soviet armor/motorized infantry presses onto the likely eastward route and brings the main truck road under direct/indirect fire. The route is **fire-blocked for roughly 45-75 minutes** in the center branch; this is not a permanent physical sealing of the corridor.

3rd Tank Regiment, surviving AT guns and preregistered artillery fight an inner blocking action. A limited 3rd-Regiment counterstroke reopens movement without attempting to destroy the Soviet southern force.

The cost is material: Japanese armor/AT losses continue, and Soviet artillery/45-mm fire increasingly determines exposure time.

## 7th Division early outside relief group
The early reinforcement is now closed as a **1/27th Infantry reinforced battalion-group**, not a full regiment or division.

Working composition/limits:
- one infantry battalion main body;
- divisional/regimental liaison, signal and small engineer/transport detachments;
- **4 Type94 37 mm AT guns** as attached outer-shoulder AT element;
- organic battalion/regimental light guns only; no tank detachment and no modern motorized-battlegroup interpretation.

Timeline center:
- Aug20-21: Hailar concentration / road movement;
- Aug22 ~1400-1500: leading elements reach the eastern relief zone;
- ~1600-1700: main battalion-group establishes an **outer shoulder** on the withdrawal route and makes liaison/contact with the Japanese mobile screen.

The four newly arrived Type94 AT guns receive **72 AT-37 special rounds total (18/gun)** from existing 6th Army stock. This is redistribution, not new production.

## Corridor geometry after outside contact
The corridor becomes a two-shoulder system:
- inner shoulder: 3rd Tank Regiment + withdrawing 23rd Division heavy weapons/AT;
- outer mobile screen: reduced 4th Tank Regiment;
- outside shoulder: 1/27th reinforced battalion-group.

This does **not** make a safe highway. Soviet artillery, armor and air can still interdict it. It does prevent the historical-style transition from 'threatened route' to 'empty route suddenly occupied by Soviet blocking forces' from being automatic.

## Air reconnaissance / air support
Japanese fighters and bombers support the withdrawal where possible, but Soviet numerical/attritional air advantage remains. Ki40 performs a limited high-value strip/axis check rather than continuous battlefield observation. No real-time FAC/CAS system is inferred.

## 1600-2000: command closure
With the outside battalion-group in position and the Soviet blocking axis confirmed, 6th Army converts the prior 'line shortening' into an explicit **move to an eastern defensive line / preserve the mobile main body**. The planned Aug24 major counterattack is no longer merely dormant; it is canceled as a practical operation.

The purpose of the fresh outside force is to hold the **exit**, not to attack westward to restore the old line.

## Main-body withdrawal
By 24:00:
- the majority of mobile south/central 23rd Division remnants that began the Aug21 withdrawal have moved through or into the protected eastern corridor/new line;
- 2/28th Infantry has **>80% of its Aug21-night mobile remnant** east of the most dangerous closure line; center combat-effective strength is roughly **35-40% of original battalion strength** after another day of losses/fatigue;
- rear guards, damaged gun crews and scattered elements remain exposed and cannot all be extracted.

No claim is made that the whole 23rd Division is out or that the Soviet offensive has failed.

## Northern / Fui Heights decision
The same intelligence that drives the south/center withdrawal makes a prolonged isolated hold at Fui Heights strategically dangerous. Around evening Aug22, the northern defenders receive a **prepare-and-execute phased withdrawal on the night Aug22-23** order rather than a multi-day last-stand requirement.

Actual escape percentage and rear-guard losses remain the next gate; the order itself is CLOSED here.

## Operational result
At Aug22 24:00 the Soviet double-envelopment plan has been **disrupted in timing and denied a clean full-division closure**, but not defeated tactically. Soviet forces occupy abandoned ground, continue to form blocking positions and attack Japanese rear guards.

Japanese success is operational extraction at high cost, not a battlefield victory.
