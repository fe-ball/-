## 1. Historical core retained

The historical counteroffensive sequence retained as the anchor is:

- 27 April: the Japanese 101st Division main body counterattacks south/east of Nanchang under strong artillery and air support;
- late April into early May: repeated fighting over southern and southeastern suburbs/strongpoints;
- 1 May: Chiang Kai-shek orders Nanchang retaken by 5 May;
- 2-5 May: Chinese forces renew the assault and historically reach/contest the airfield, railway/station and eastern city approaches;
- 7 May: a northern penetration near Niuxing is too weak to cut the Nanchang-Nanxun connection;
- 9 May: exhausted Chinese forces withdraw; Japanese forces do not conduct a deep pursuit.

The historical sequence is used as an event skeleton, not as a forced identical tactical result.

## 2. Carry-in worldline state on 27 April

From REPLAY1:

- Chinese leading southern formations have reached the outer Nanchang approaches on a somewhat narrower frontage and roughly 6-12 h later than matched-history center;
- warned sectors have already paid roughly 5-10% extra early cohesion/loss cost;
- Japanese directly engaged sectors have taken roughly 5-10% less net gate-wide loss than matched history;
- Japanese artillery, local reserves and air tasking are already oriented toward the penetration;
- the Nanchang supply connection remains open;
- the 106th Division still carries most of its Apr20 +2,200 to +2,900 CEQ structural/cadre advantage;
- ordinary conventional-aircraft serviceability remains +3-5 percentage points where fuel/weather permit;
- effective found -> reported -> delivered -> acted air-ground pressure remains roughly +8-15% during usable windows.

The Apr20 early-warning advantage itself is now spent. From this point the advantage comes from posture, surviving cadre, maintenance, target folders and execution speed.

## 3. 27-30 April — Japanese counterstroke

The Japanese counterattack begins on the historical date.

The worldline difference is not a new division or new weapon. It is that the Japanese attack a somewhat narrower and more fatigued penetration while their own fire plan and reserves are in better order.

Ruling:

- the 101st Division main counterstroke still bears the main burden south/east of Nanchang;
- artillery preparation and air-ground tasking are more continuous and less frequently wasted on stale locations;
- Type 89 / Type 94 armored assets remain local road/junction assault-support tools; no Chi-Ha is introduced;
- the stronger 106th sector can sustain local counterpressure and security duties without stripping its cadre as severely;
- Chinese forward battalions resist hard and several villages/strongpoints change hands more than once.

Compared with matched history, the Japanese recover the first belt of key southern/eastern strongpoints roughly **12-24 h earlier in the center**, with local sectors near history where terrain/night movement reduces the information advantage.

This does not collapse the Chinese offensive. It forces it to re-form for the politically ordered final effort.

## 4. 1 May — political deadline remains

The worldline has not removed the command pressure that historically produced the order to capture Nanchang by 5 May.

Therefore the Chinese side does not simply terminate the operation because the first penetration is shallower. The renewed attack remains rational at the command level because:

- substantial forces are already committed;
- Nanchang remains operationally important;
- some forward units are still close enough to the city to make another effort appear possible;
- higher command has incomplete knowledge of the exact Japanese reserve/readiness margin.

No omniscient Chinese or Japanese headquarters is introduced.

## 5. 2-5 May — renewed Chinese assault

The renewed assault occurs, but from a worse tactical start than matched history.

### Southern/eastern approach

Chinese units again force their way toward the airfield, railway/station and eastern approaches. They achieve local penetrations and temporary fire/control over portions of the outer airfield/railway zone.

Worldline ruling:

- **airfield perimeter is breached/contested locally**, but a durable full-field recapture is not established;
- **railway/station approaches are reached and attacked**, but the station is not held long enough to interrupt the Nanchang supply system operationally;
- the Chinese attack reaches approximately **2-4 km less depth on the most successful axis** than the matched-history center at its high-water mark;
- several infiltrating/assault detachments get closer than the main body and are later forced back or cut off.

The difference comes from cumulative factors rather than a single decisive weapon:

1. the Apr27-30 Japanese counterstroke restored more depth before the renewed attack;
2. surviving Japanese cadre/communications reduce local collapse propagation;
3. pre-existing air/photo target folders accelerate repeated attacks on routes and assembly points;
4. +3-5 pp serviceability yields only a modest sortie increase, but +8-15% effective air-ground pressure improves the value of the sorties that can be flown;
5. Chinese forces have already spent more cohesion reaching the city approaches.

## 6. 6-8 May — attack loses operational coherence

By 6 May the Chinese side can still launch local attacks, but the probability of taking and holding Nanchang has fallen sharply.

- artillery/ammunition shortage and accumulated infantry losses become increasingly binding;
- night movement and dispersion continue to reduce Japanese air effectiveness, preventing a rout from the air;
- Japanese local counterattacks recover road junctions and village strongpoints rather than attempt a deep encirclement;
- the northern effort remains insufficient to sever the Nanchang-Nanxun supply connection.

The worldline Chinese disengagement begins **during the night of 8/9 May**, several hours to roughly half a day earlier than the matched-history center. The general withdrawal is evident on 9 May.

## 7. 9 May — campaign closeout

Nanchang remains in Japanese hands.

Japanese forces restore the immediate security perimeter and pursue locally only far enough to prevent an immediate renewed assault. They do **not** conduct a deep pursuit into the retreating Chinese armies.

Reasons:

- the Japanese formations have also accumulated fatigue and ammunition/maintenance debt;
- terrain and road limits make a deep mechanized exploitation unrealistic;
- preserving the Nanchang position and supply line has higher value than a speculative pursuit;
- the worldline advantage is principally operational quality, not unlimited fresh mass.

The strategic result therefore remains the historical one: the Chinese counteroffensive fails to retake Nanchang.

## 9. Operational consequences

The Nanchang replay produces a **moderate**, not transformative, divergence:

- Japanese retention of Nanchang is more secure by the end of the counteroffensive;
- the Chinese high-water mark is shallower and more transient;
- Japanese direct losses are somewhat lower and organizational exhaustion is lower;
- Chinese assault formations leave the battle somewhat more depleted;
- no new strategic exploitation occurs;
- no armored/jet/advanced-aircraft miracle is imported into the campaign.

This is important for later 1939 China-theater accounting but is not large enough by itself to rewrite the national war balance.
