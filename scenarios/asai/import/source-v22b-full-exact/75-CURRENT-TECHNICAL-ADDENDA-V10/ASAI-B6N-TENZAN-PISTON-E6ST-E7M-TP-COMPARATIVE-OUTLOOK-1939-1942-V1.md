# ASAI B6N TENZAN PISTON / E6-S-T / E7-M-LF TURBOPROP COMPARATIVE OUTLOOK — 1939–1942 — V1

**Status:** CURRENT V10 FUTURE-OUTLOOK / H->A aircraft-design authority.  
**Clock guard:** China remains 1939-12-31 24:00. This file closes a design/procurement outlook only. It does not pre-record 1940–42 production, deployment, combat, carrier acceptance, or war-start date.  
**Parents:** B5NAUDIT1, TORPEDO41CLOSE1, NAVALJETTORP1, E7TPOUTLOOK1, high-power propeller/cooling canon, historical B6N 14-Shi geometry and carrier-envelope anchors.

## 1. Design problem

The B6N is the first carrier-attack aircraft for which the turboprop question is a genuine **new-build selection problem**, not a retrofit curiosity.

The historical 14-Shi requirement is retained as the geometric/mission anchor:

- three crew;
- low-wing all-metal carrier attack aircraft;
- same external 800 kg-class weapon family as B5N;
- materially higher speed and cruise speed than B5N;
- useful long-range carrier strike;
- compatibility with standard IJN carrier elevators, keeping overall length below roughly 11 m and using folding outer wings.

Historical B6N2 geometry remains the main airframe anchor:

- length ~10.865 m;
- span ~14.894 m;
- wing area ~37.2 m2;
- historical empty ~3.01 t;
- historical gross ~5.20 t;
- historical maximum takeoff ~5.65 t;
- four-blade propeller;
- 800 kg-class torpedo or bomb load.

The worldline does **not** enlarge the aircraft merely because more shaft power exists. Carrier elevator/deck geometry is the hard constraint. The main value of a compact TP is therefore to put more usable power and fuel/mission margin inside essentially the same external aircraft volume.

## 2. Competing propulsion paths

### 2.1 Conventional piston path

Use a ~1,750–1,850 hp-class high-power radial as the schedule/reference competitor. Exact production engine remains replay/design-freeze local rather than forcing the historical Mamoru outcome.

Historical comparison anchors:

- Mamoru 11: ~1,870 hp takeoff, ~1,600 hp at ~4.9 km, ~0.87–0.90 t dry, ~1.38 m diameter;
- Kasei 25-class: ~1,850 hp takeoff, ~1,540 hp at ~5.5 km, ~0.75–0.78 t dry, ~1.34 m diameter;
- historical B6N2: ~482 km/h at ~4.9 km.

Asai/worldline production can use earlier EX2-class exhaust and better prop/QC integration. Use **~485–492 km/h** as the piston B6N planning band rather than granting a new airframe.

### 2.2 E6-S-T fallback/test path

Current E6-S-T is only:

- ~1,300 shp standard;
- ~1,400 shp short/high-flow;
- small-core package.

Its smaller frontal installation and lower mass recover much of the lost power at maximum speed, so a clean B6N test installation can still screen around **~475–490 km/h** depending altitude and inlet/cowling detail. That does **not** make it a good carrier production engine:

- takeoff and wave-off power margin are inferior to the 1.8 khp-class piston competitor;
- torpedo-loaded acceleration is weaker;
- the B6N requirement is explicitly a high-performance replacement, not merely a kerosene conversion;
- E7-M-LF becomes available on the relevant late-development clock.

Verdict: retain E6-S-T only as a naval TP integration / schedule-fallback / systems-test path. Do not choose it as the normal B6N production engine.

### 2.3 E7-M lower-flow TP — main TP candidate

Use the E7 lower-medium product point from E7TPOUTLOOK1:

- gas-path scale ~10–11 kg/s;
- normal/flat-rated **~1,750–1,850 shp**;
- short **~1,900–2,000 shp**;
- near-rated BSFC screening ~0.34–0.36 kg/kWh;
- cruise/part-load ~0.39–0.46 kg/kWh provisional.

For B6N integration, close the following **H->A package screening envelope**:

- complete dry engine + free power turbine + reducer + accessories: **~0.56–0.64 t**, center ~0.60 t;
- main power-package diameter: ~0.80–0.90 m class;
- practical aerodynamic cowling maximum: **~0.95–1.05 m** after intake/accessory/duct allowance;
- propeller: **3.5–3.6 m four-blade constant-speed/feathering**, using the already-existing 1.8–2.2 khp high-power propeller school.

These are aircraft-screening values, not a certified E7 production engine drawing.

Relative to a 0.75–0.90 t, 1.34–1.38 m radial installation, the TP therefore buys both a meaningful nose-drag reduction and roughly **~0.15–0.30 t installed mass relief** after paying local mount/intake/reducer/kerosene-system differences.

## 3. E7-M-LF B6N airframe bookkeeping

Do not create a smaller wing or larger carrier aircraft. Retain the B6N geometry and Fowler-flap school.

Planning mass envelope:

| item | E7-M-LF B6N outlook |
|---|---:|
| empty/equipped | **~2.80–2.92 t** |
| ordinary torpedo/attack gross | **~5.05–5.22 t** |
| structural/deck MTOW target | **~5.60–5.70 t** |
| external weapon | Type 91 Mod1/Mod2 or ~800 kg-class bombs |
| internal kerosene allowance | aircraft-load-sheet dependent; mass relief may be spent on fuel rather than lower gross |

The mass saving is deliberately not converted entirely into lower takeoff weight. A carrier attack aircraft benefits more if part of the saving becomes fuel/reserve while preserving historical-like deck/elevator dimensions.

Wing loading at 5.1–5.2 t stays ~137–140 kg/m2, very close to historical B6N. Therefore the TP does **not** magically solve landing speed. Fowler flaps, hook loads, deck wind, brake/tyre quality and pilot technique remain carrier gates.

## 4. Performance H->A comparison

Historical B6N2 (~482 km/h at ~4.9 km with ~1.54–1.60 khp available in that altitude band) is the calibration anchor. The E7 TP case combines:

- ~1.75–1.85 khp normal power with stronger altitude flat-rating;
- a much smaller cowling/frontal package;
- ~0.1–0.2 t lower ordinary aircraft mass at equal mission load, or equal gross with more fuel.

Use the following planning bands pending a dedicated measured altitude/prop map:

| coordinate | piston B6N worldline | E6-S-T test B6N | **E7-M-LF TP B6N** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| max speed, 4–5 km | ~485–492 km/h | ~475–490 | **~520–535 normal; ~530–545 short** |
| high cruise | ~325–345 km/h | ~325–345 | **~350–370 km/h** |
| time to 5 km | ~9.5–10.5 min | ~9.5–11 | **~7.5–8.5 min** |
| service ceiling | ~9–9.5 km | ~8.5–9.5 | **~9.5–10.5 km** |
| loaded torpedo/attack radius | ~600–750 km planning | ~550–700 | **~650–800 km** |
| light/ferry range | ~3.0–3.3 thousand km class | ~2.8–3.2k | **~3.1–3.5k km screening** |

The E7 range advantage is intentionally moderate, not magical. Its cruise BSFC remains worse than the best mature piston part-load point. It recovers the difference through lighter installation, denser kerosene by volume, cleaner nose, and the ability to carry more fuel at the same gross/deck limit.

The main gain is therefore **speed + takeoff/go-around margin + post-release acceleration without surrendering carrier strike radius**.

## 5. Type 91 high-speed torpedo interface

TORPEDO41CLOSE1 remains controlling:

- 1941 Mod2 normal high-speed qualification: ~400–430 km/h;
- selected development: ~450 km/h;
- hit probability is not increased by assigning the same torpedo to a faster aircraft; the aircraft improves arrival, valid-release opportunity and survival.

The E7-M-LF B6N is designed around the 400–430 km/h release envelope from origin. It need not perform the very large deceleration demanded of G4As.

Typical logic:

1. approach the terminal area substantially faster than B5N;
2. descend/settle into ~400–430 km/h torpedo release rather than spending a long interval in low-speed attack formation;
3. release ~0.8–0.94 t weapon;
4. immediately exploit the resulting weight drop and ~1.9–2.0 khp short-rating margin for acceleration/wave-off/escape.

The E7 aircraft therefore improves **pre-release exposure and post-release survival**, while the final stable low-altitude straight run remains dangerous.

## 6. Carrier integration

### 6.1 Propeller/torque

A 1.9–2.0 khp four-blade propeller does not erase the historical B6N torque problem. The worldline advantage is that:

- high-power propeller/governor work is already mature;
- B5N TP carrier test articles have paid turbine-deck handling and reducer/governor lessons;
- Asai measurement makes torque/yaw mapping an early test item rather than a late surprise.

Retain an asymmetric fin/rudder trim or equivalent aerodynamic correction if flight test requires it. Do not assume the TP is torque-free merely because the turbine gas generator is smooth.

### 6.2 Elevator/deck geometry

The TP nose is slimmer, but overall aircraft length remains at the ~11 m carrier limit. Use the freed nose volume/CG freedom for:

- fuel/mission equipment placement;
- easier cowling/access;
- better forward visibility where local geometry permits;
- less cooling-exit drag.

Do not enlarge span, length or prop diameter until carrier-elevator/hangar clearance is requalified.

### 6.3 Landing and wave-off

Landing speed is driven mainly by wing loading and high-lift system, so it remains B6N-like. The TP's important deck benefit is **wave-off/go-around power response and power retention**, not a miraculous low stall speed.

## 7. Development and replacement schedule outlook

B5NAUDIT1 makes the B5N2 strong enough that the Navy does not need to rush an unreliable B6N merely to claim a replacement. This changes schedule strategy.

Recommended development ladder:

- **1939 Dec:** 14-Shi requirement opens; Type91 ~400–430 km/h future interface is already a design-origin requirement in this worldline.
- **1940 H1:** airframe/layout and carrier geometry; dual engine-mount/nose provisions are worth preserving while engine choice stays open.
- **1940 H2–1941 H1:** piston reference prototype and/or E6-S-T naval TP test installation can validate airframe, deck, torque, torpedo and systems before E7 flight release.
- **1941 H2:** E7 full core reaches ground-qualified development target; E7-M-LF reducer/prop/engine ground installation may close if the program remains healthy.
- **1941 Q4–1942 Q1:** earliest credible B6N E7-M-LF flight-release band.
- **1942:** carrier trials / service-development gate if engine TBO, gearbox, propeller, salt/FOD, hook/gear and Type91 release all pass.

This does **not** pre-record a 1942 service date. It closes the opportunity: if the E7-M-LF path passes, the Navy can avoid the historical long Mamoru reliability detour and begin useful B5N replacement materially earlier than the historical 1943 production/service ramp.

A **~6–12 month effective replacement opportunity** relative to the historical B6N ramp is a reasonable planning band, but actual acceptance remains replay/event dependent.

## 8. Procurement verdict

### Conventional piston

**A-/schedule hedge.** Mature, known fuel economy, existing industrial base. Still a valid production fallback if E7 misses TBO/reducer/carrier gates.

### E6-S-T

**C/test only.** Useful TP integration article, not enough power for the final 14-Shi aircraft.

### E7-M-LF TP

**A-/main-production candidate, conditional on 1941H2–1942Q1 flight/reliability closure.**

It wins because the B6N is volume-limited by the carrier, not because the TP has infinitely better fuel economy. It provides roughly piston-class shaft power in a much smaller/lighter nose while inheriting years of Asai turbine, propeller, kerosene and Type91 high-speed-release development.

If it passes reliability and carrier qualification, a serial TP Tenzan is rational. If it does not, the piston B6N proceeds without waiting.

## 9. Guardrails

This outlook does not:

- make B6N operational before replay closes it;
- erase B5N2 before replacement scale exists;
- turn E7-M-LF capability into certified service power before reducer/prop/TBO tests;
- grant Type91 a higher terminal hit rate merely because the carrier aircraft is faster;
- solve carrier landing speed through engine power;
- require every future carrier aircraft to use kerosene/TP;
- lock B6N model-number sequencing before the winning engine path is known.

## 10. Current conclusion

The B6N is the first naval carrier attack aircraft in which the E7 TP is not a curiosity but a credible preferred production branch.

The important comparison is no longer `B5N piston vs E5 TP`. It is:

**mature B5N2 bridge -> B6N airframe -> piston schedule hedge OR E7-M-LF ~1.8 khp TP main branch.**

The E7 branch is expected to buy ~30–50 km/h more maximum speed than a worldline piston B6N, materially better climb/acceleration/wave-off, and at least comparable useful torpedo radius while keeping the same carrier-sized airframe.

D4YAUDIT1 does not reduce this verdict. D4Y uses the E7 **small** TP while B6N uses the **lower-medium** product; they are not a one-for-one core allocation choice. More importantly, the D4Y comparison shows why the B6N TP business case is stronger in headline aircraft performance: the B6N TP replaces a bulky radial, while the D4Y TP competes with an already-clean Atsuta inline installation.
