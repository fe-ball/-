# G4M1 TYPE 1 LAND-BASED ATTACK AIRCRAFT — PISTON / TP / LONG-RANGE ATTACK COMPARATIVE CLOSEOUT — 1939-1941 — V1 G4MAUDIT1

**Status:** CURRENT TECHNICAL CLOSEOUT / no combat-clock advance.  
**Purpose:** close the conventional Type 1 land-attack branch against G3M2 and G4As, test turboprop substitution, define 1941 long-range torpedo/level-bombing roles, and expose replacement-tempo consequences.

## 1. Historical-like physical anchor

Use early G4M1 Model 11 class:

- crew: **~7**;
- length: **~20.0 m**;
- span: **~25.0 m**;
- wing area: **~78.1 m2**;
- empty mass: **~6.8 t**;
- maximum takeoff mass: **~12.5 t class**;
- engines: **2 x Kasei 11**, ~1,530 hp takeoff, ~1,410 hp at 2 km, ~1,340 hp at 4 km;
- historical early maximum speed: **~428 km/h at ~4.2 km** comparison anchor;
- cruise: **~320-325 km/h class**;
- maximum/ferry reach: **~6,000 km class** comparison anchor;
- fuel: very large unprotected/integral wing fuel system;
- offensive load: **one ~800 kg Type91-class torpedo OR ~800 kg-class ordinary bomb mission**; local overload/mission variants remain separate;
- normal defensive fit includes tail 20 mm plus light machine-gun positions.

The design requirement itself demanded exceptional loaded reach. This is the central reason the airframe remains valuable after G4As appears.

## 2. Current worldline piston G4M1

Current exhaust authority treats early historical G4M1 as EX0 and permits worldline production to incorporate EX2/EX3 much earlier.

Use physical exhaust gain **~+5.2 to +7.9 km/h** where lot/state supports it.

Therefore a current early-production EX2/QC G4M1 uses:

- maximum speed: **~433-436 km/h class at ~4.2 km**;
- cruise: **~320-330 km/h class**, with part of exhaust margin optionally spent on temperature/fuel margin rather than headline speed;
- no generic range multiplier beyond the historical-like fuel architecture.

Engine/propeller matching, cooling, wiring, instrumentation, fuel-line fittings, leak inspection, navigation, bombing/torpedo tables and service acceptance are more consistent than historical production.

### Protection guard

This does **not** create:

- self-sealing integral wing tanks;
- crew armor;
- a protected fuel-cell architecture.

The central G4M1 survivability trade remains. Better fuel plumbing/shutoff/fire-extinguisher inspection can reduce avoidable failures and ground/service fires, but a penetrating incendiary/cannon hit into the large unprotected wing fuel volume remains dangerous.

## 3. Torpedo mission radius

Historical design evidence requires at least ~3,700 km loaded range with an 800 kg torpedo/bomb class. Do not turn this directly into a 1,850 km combat radius: formation, climb, search, attack, weather diversion and reserve all consume margin.

For current replay planning use:

**ordinary Type91 torpedo radius ~900-1,150 km class**

with favorable long-range planning potentially extending toward **~1,200-1,300 km** only when search/reserve/alternate-base conditions permit.

This is materially longer than current G3M2 ordinary torpedo radius (~650-800 km) and far longer than G4As (~300-400 km initial / ~350-450 favorable growth), while remaining much slower in terminal attack than G4As.

Therefore the branches are complementary:

- **G3M2:** mature existing long-range force, lower cost and already fielded;
- **G4M1:** next conventional very-long-range gasoline land-attack main line;
- **G4As:** high-speed short/medium-radius torpedo/strike geometry specialist.

## 4. Level-bombing role

G4M1 is a useful long-range level bomber against:

- airfields;
- ports;
- oil/fuel/logistics installations;
- troop/vehicle concentrations;
- fixed industrial targets;
- ships at anchor or otherwise movement-constrained targets.

FCS1/FCS2 inheritance improves:

- calibrated bombsight and instruments;
- wind/heading cards;
- release-delay measurement;
- formation setting discipline;
- reconnaissance-derived target folders;
- post-sortie correction of systematic miss.

For a well-briefed daylight area target, retain the current **~+5-10% effective target-area bomb concentration** versus a matched-history package. This is not a universal direct-hit multiplier.

### Moving-warship guard

High-level level bombing against a freely maneuvering warship remains a poor primary kill method. Use it to:

- force maneuver;
- divide AA attention;
- create near-miss / suppression risk;
- attack ships constrained in harbor/formation;

but do not assign D3A-like direct-hit probability. Against a freely maneuvering major warship, ordinary high-level direct-hit probability should remain **low-single-digit per attacking aircraft/bomb pattern**, and torpedo/dive-bombing remain the principal precision anti-ship methods.

## 5. Type91 inheritance

By 1941 year-end, Type91 Mod2 normal high-speed qualification (~400-430 km/h) is already near or above ordinary G4M torpedo-release speed.

Thus, as with B5N, the torpedo no longer forces an artificially slow pre-release state. G4M can:

- approach faster than G3M;
- settle into stable low-altitude release geometry;
- release without a large torpedo-fragility speed penalty;
- accelerate away with ~0.9 t suddenly removed.

G4M still spends longer in the terminal threat zone than G4As because its airframe acceleration/top speed are much lower. The high-speed torpedo program improves G4M opportunity/survival but does not erase the G4As terminal-speed advantage.

## 6. E6-S-T G4M conversion study

### 6.1 Power mismatch

Current E6-S-T is approximately:

- ~1,300 hp standard;
- ~1,400 hp short/high-flow.

Kasei 11 is ~1,530 hp takeoff and retains ~1,340 hp around 4 km. Therefore direct E6-S-T substitution is **not** a power upgrade at the altitude that sets G4M performance.

### 6.2 Installation advantages

A TP conversion could provide:

- smaller frontal nacelles;
- lower vibration;
- kerosene commonality;
- some installed-mass / cooling cleanup;
- potentially ~4-7% parasite-drag reduction depending nacelle/wing interference.

But the huge wing/fuselage/fuel architecture dominates the aircraft, and the conversion cannot erase the cruise-SFC penalty already established by B5N/G3M screening.

### 6.3 Performance screening

Without inventing an E6-S-T altitude-power map, use:

- low-altitude maximum speed: **~400-420 km/h class**;
- ~4 km maximum-speed screening: **~390-425 km/h class**;
- ordinary torpedo radius: **~650-850 km class**.

This is worse than piston G4M in the mission that justifies the airframe, while still far slower than G4As.

### 6.4 Decision

**Reject serial E6-S-T G4M conversion.**

No physical conversion is required beyond paper/installation rigs because B5N TP test aircraft, D3A TP test aircraft and purpose-built E5/E6 naval aircraft already supply carrier/turbine/weapon integration data.

## 7. E6-M-T / higher-power TP option

The ~2,050-2,100 shp E6-M-T class is large enough to create a genuinely different high-power twin. Installing two on G4M would require major:

- nacelle/wing structural redesign;
- fuel architecture review;
- propeller/gear clearance changes;
- landing-gear/brake/weight review;
- empennage and flutter requalification.

That is a **new aircraft**, not a G4M re-engine program. Keep it for a later purpose-built TP patrol/attack/transport study rather than disguising it as a G4M variant.

## 8. Replacement and production-tempo implication

Historical-like first flight remains an October 1939 anchor unless replay later changes it. The worldline advantage is in test closure and production health:

- better structural calculation and instrumentation reduce iteration;
- Kasei production/QC is healthier;
- exhaust/prop/cooling states can be frozen earlier;
- long-range navigation, Type91 and naval field-maintenance schools already exist through G3M/E5.

Therefore use a **~2-4 month earlier effective series/operational-ramp opportunity** versus historical experience, without declaring combat use before replay reaches it.

If the factory reaches the same mature monthly rate, this timing advantage can produce roughly **~20-50 additional accepted airframes by late 1941** versus a matched historical ramp. This is a capacity/planning implication, not a pre-recorded 1941 inventory.

G3M should not be stopped the instant G4M appears. Early transition is overlapping:

- G3M fills existing units and long-range capacity;
- G4M takes new-build first-line long-range allocations as reliability/crew conversion close;
- G4As remains a separate high-speed specialist.

The result is a faster *replacement slope*, not an instantaneous type swap.

## 9. Force-structure consequence by late 1941

The Navy now has three materially distinct land-torpedo solutions:

| platform | primary virtue | ordinary torpedo radius | terminal speed/survival character |
|---|---|---:|---|
| G3M2 | mature reach / existing numbers | ~650-800 km | slowest / longest exposure |
| G4M1 | very long reach / heavier next-generation conventional line | **~900-1,150 km** | faster than G3M but still conventional |
| G4As1 | rapid concentration / high ingress-egress survival | ~300-400 km initial; ~350-450 favorable | by far fastest / shortest terminal exposure |

A scarce Type91 is therefore allocated by geometry and base position, not by aircraft modernity alone.

## 10. Guards

- no automatic self-sealing/armor on 1941 G4M1;
- no 6,000 km torpedo combat radius;
- no serial E6-S-T G4M;
- no high-level moving-ship bombing accuracy comparable to dive bombing;
- no automatic G3M retirement;
- no pre-recorded 1941 production count or combat result;
- no combat-clock advance.

2. **D4Y Suisei replacement audit**, because D3AAUDIT1 exposed a real 1941 replacement-tempo opportunity;
