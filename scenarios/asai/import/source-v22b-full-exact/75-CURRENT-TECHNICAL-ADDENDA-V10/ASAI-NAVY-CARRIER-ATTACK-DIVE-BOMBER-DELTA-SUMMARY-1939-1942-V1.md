# NAVY CARRIER ATTACK / DIVE-BOMBER DELTA SUMMARY — 1939-1942 — V1 NAVALJETTORP1

**Purpose:** compact current answer to "what already changed in the carrier attack / carrier bomber lines?"  
**No combat-clock advance.**

## B5N2 — Type 97 Carrier Attack Aircraft

Current delta is evolutionary, not a new airframe:
- historical-like basic airframe and carrier handling retained;
- later production can progressively adopt EX2 thrust exhaust; physical EX0->EX2 comparison is ~+4-6 km/h when the actual lot state supports it;
- engine/prop/cooling/vibration/QC/serviceability are better controlled;
- Type 91 torpedo sight, heading/gyro setting, airspeed/altitude instrumentation and release-envelope tables benefit from FCS1; FCS2 mechanical lead aid remains date/lot gated;
- Type 91 air-to-water entry stabilization/depth-settling/QC are a major worldline lever.

B5NAUDIT1 now adds the full piston/TP comparison. Applicable later EX2/QC piston lots use ~382-384 km/h as the working maximum-speed class and retain ~980-1,020 km-class normal mission range. An E5-S-T conversion is allowed only as a **2-4-aircraft technical test program**: its kerosene/lower-vibration/smaller-nose advantages do not repay the medium-altitude and range penalty for serial fleet conversion. The 1941 high-speed Type 91 standard mainly gives B5N2 more release margin and consistency; by then the B5N airframe, not the torpedo, is the ordinary attack-speed limiter.

## D3A1 — Type 99 Carrier Bomber

D3AAUDIT1 now closes the detailed comparison. The historical-like fixed-gear D3A1 remains the serial piston baseline; applicable later EX2/QC lots are **~391-393 km/h class at ~3 km** and keep roughly **~1,430-1,500 km-class** mission range depending load/deck condition. Instrumented testing closes dive-brake vibration/stability and release-system scatter earlier.

For 1941 planning, dive bombing is layered rather than assigned a magic aircraft hit-rate. Ordinary trained attacks use ~45-60 degree dives, ~400-440 km/h controlled dive speed and ~450-650 m ship-target release. After a valid release, a maneuvering large warship uses a **~18-28% direct-hit planning band**, with higher favorable/elite and lower smoke/evasive bands.

An E5-S-T D3A conversion is physically credible but rejected for serial service. Fixed-gear drag dominates; a narrower turbine nose only gives ~2-4% parasite-drag cleanup, the 3 km speed is bracketed around ~350-378 km/h and mission range falls to ~1,000-1,180 km. Limit the program to **2 aircraft (+ optional third)** for carrier turbine/dive/store-separation data.

The important worldline gain is replacement readiness: better dive-test practice plus healthier Atsuta scheduling gives the future D4Y audit a **~3-6 month earlier effective replacement opportunity** to test. Do not close an early D4Y service date until that airframe is audited.

Current detailed authority: `ASAI-D3A1-PISTON-TP-DIVE-BOMBING-COMPARATIVE-CLOSEOUT-1939-1941-V1.md`.


## D4Y Suisei — clean piston baseline, E7-S-T is a close competition rather than an automatic win

D4YAUDIT1 closes the future comparison. The D4Y's liquid-cooled Atsuta installation is already exceptionally narrow, so an E7-S-T does **not** receive the large frontal-area benefit seen when replacing a B6N-class radial. A healthy worldline ~1,400 hp piston D4Y screens around **~583-592 km/h**, while E7-S-T screens around **~585-598 km/h normal / ~595-607 short**. The TP's stronger arguments are climb/wave-off, cooling simplification, low vibration and carrier-reconnaissance utility; attack/recon range remains slightly better on the piston branch.

Current planning therefore keeps piston D4Y as the schedule baseline and rates E7-S-T **B+ to A- conditional**, with a limited reconnaissance/fast-strike series as the most natural first naval E7-S application if 1941H2-42Q1 TBO/reducer/carrier gates pass. Worldline high-q/Material No.2/HS/D3A instrumentation can plausibly compress the historical flutter-delay, giving a **1941H2-1942H1 service-development opportunity** without pre-recording service.

Current detailed authority: `ASAI-D4Y-SUISEI-PISTON-E7ST-TP-COMPARATIVE-OUTLOOK-1940-1942-V1.md`.

## B6N Tenzan — E7 lower-medium TP becomes a real production competitor

The 14-Shi carrier-attack requirement starts in Dec 1939. The aircraft remains carrier-elevator constrained at roughly historical B6N geometry and inherits the Type91 **~400-430 km/h** release interface from design origin.

B6NAUDIT1 now closes the engine comparison as an outlook:
- conventional ~1.75-1.85 khp piston: valid schedule hedge, ~485-492 km/h-class worldline performance;
- E6-S-T 1.3/1.4 khp: test/fallback only; insufficient production power margin;
- **E7-M lower-flow TP ~1.75-1.85k shp normal / ~1.9-2.0k short: main-production candidate if 1941H2-1942Q1 reducer/prop/TBO/flight gates pass.**

The E7 TP keeps essentially the same carrier-sized airframe while screening around **~520-535 km/h normal**, ~7.5-8.5 min to 5 km and ~650-800 km ordinary torpedo radius. Its main benefit is speed, takeoff/wave-off and post-release acceleration without surrendering the useful carrier strike radius. A ~6-12 month earlier effective B5N replacement opportunity versus the historical B6N ramp is plausible if the engine path closes; no service date is pre-recorded.

Current detailed authority: `ASAI-B6N-TENZAN-PISTON-E6ST-E7M-TP-COMPARATIVE-OUTLOOK-1939-1942-V1.md`.

## B7A Ryusei — later unification becomes more credible

B7A stays an independent Aichi design. By its 1941-42 gate the Navy can combine:
- B6N/G4As high-speed torpedo release experience;
- D3A/D4Y dive-brake and dive-release experience;
- Homare + large four-blade carrier integration.

Thus "torpedo bomber + dive bomber in one aircraft" has a much better technical foundation than a paper requirement alone.
