# ASAI AIRCRAFT / ENGINE GENERATION FAMILY & ARMY-NAVY SERVICE MATRIX — 1937-1942 — V1 EFAMILY1

**Status:** CURRENT V10 family-architecture closure / outlook crosswalk.  
**Clock:** China remains 1939-12-31 24:00; no 1940 combat or future procurement result is pre-recorded.  
**Purpose:** make the propulsion family visible as a hierarchy rather than treating every airframe as a one-off type.

> **NAMEGUARD1:** service names/codes added during the 2026-09-07 wash (notably the new Japanese long-form titles and `R2As`/`G4As` shorthands) are **working aliases** unless independently attested in earlier current material. This matrix closes family relationships, not final nomenclature. Older labels may be restored at a later naming gate without reopening hardware lineage.

## 0. Reading rule — five independent axes

Every Asai-powered aircraft is to be read through five independent axes:

1. **E-generation** — E5 / E6 / E7 etc. = technology-front coordinate, not a single engine model and not a calendar-only type number.
2. **core scale** — **S / M / L** where locally defined. Different scale cores of the same E-generation can mature into different aircraft classes in parallel.
3. **backend / output package** — **J** pure jet, **JF** aft-fan, **T** shaft/turboprop, or reheat/other development backend. Backend is not the generation.
4. **airframe family** — one propulsion product may serve multiple independent airframes; conversely one airframe family may accept more than one backend.
5. **service fit** — Army / Navy / company / research mission and formal service designation. A service name does not define the engine family.

Use the shorthand `E-generation / core scale / backend / airframe family / service fit` in future design discussions. This is an internal bookkeeping syntax, **not** a Japanese military designation system.

A later E-generation does not erase lower products. E7-era production may still build an E6-equivalent or E5-equivalent lower-rated product when that is cheaper, longer-lived or better matched to the aircraft.

## 1. Generation flow at a glance

### E5 — first broad operational family, 1937-39

E5 is the generation where the technology stops being a collection of experimental engines and becomes a **multi-product aircraft ecosystem**.

- **E5-S-T**: mature practical turboprop product. P1/P2 and material/utility work live here.
- **E5-J**: ~430-455 kgf normal product family, selected ~470 kgf short; powers Ki-42/E5 Single and pure-J Twin comparison aircraft.
- **E5-JF**: ~515-545 kgf installed practical-mission band; becomes the dominant E5 Twin operational backend.

Service consequence:
- Army: E5 Single/Ki-42 high-speed fighter/high-cover + E5 Twin **九八式司令部偵察機**.
- Navy: same Twin family becomes **九八式高速陸上偵察機 / R1As** and supports high-speed light-strike/torpedo development.
- Company/utility: P1/P2 carry the shaft/turboprop branch into ordinary practical service.

E5 therefore establishes the family principle: **same technology generation, different core/output packages, multiple aircraft roles and services.**

### E6 — 1939-41 mass-service family and deliberate specialization

E6 separates scale and mission more clearly.

- **E6-S-J**: ~475-505 kgf standard-flow / ~500-530 kgf high-flow short small-core research package; HS-2 belongs here.
- **E6-S-T**: ~1,300 shp normal / ~1,400 shp short; useful as a test/fallback carrier-TP comparator, not the final answer to every 1941-42 aircraft.
- **E6-M-J**: ~850 kgf normal / ~900 kgf selected short; main E6 Single engine and specialized pure-J Twin backend.
- **E6-M-JF**: ~984 kgf long-life production center, >1,000 kgf capability at higher rating; the main E6 Twin family engine.
- **E6-M-T**: ~2,050-2,100 shp transport/large-aircraft shaft product coordinate.
- **E6-M + reheat**: research branch for HS-3/high-speed work rather than normal 1940 fleet propulsion.

E6 produces two different kinds of family growth:

1. **common-airframe service variants** — E6 Twin -> Army common multi-role, Navy R2As reconnaissance, Navy G4As torpedo/land attack;
2. **distinct airframes sharing the same engine product** — E6 Single and E6-B fast heavy bomber both draw from the E6-M ecosystem, while the larger E6-B uses the same E6-M-JF pair as the common Twin in a different airframe.

### E7 — 1940-41 research closure, 1941-42 retrofit/new-build divergence

E7 is already an active research front during 1940:

- 1940H1 full compressor/hot-section rigs;
- 1940H2 integrated gas-generator demonstrator;
- 1941H1 full-core ground development;
- 1941H2 ground-qualified development core;
- inherited aft-fan limited flight-release candidate can exist in 1941H2 if release/endurance gates pass;
- earliest E7-dedicated new-aircraft first-flight target remains 1942Q1-Q2.

E7 must be treated by **scale**, not as one magic engine:

| E7 scale/product | working output | natural aircraft class |
|---|---:|---|
| E7-S-T | ~1,425-1,500 shp normal / ~1,525-1,575 short | D4Y-size small carrier/fast-recon aircraft |
| E7-M pure-J | ~970-1,000 kgf capability | E6 Single retrofit / new high-speed single |
| E7-M inherited JF | ~1,100-1,150 kgf capability | E6 Twin / E6-B retrofit and new twin family |
| E7-M lower T (10-11 kg/s) | ~1,750-1,850 shp / ~1,900-2,000 short | B6N-size carrier attack sweet spot |
| E7-M central T | ~2,250-2,450 shp normal | larger tactical / transport / AT-3 growth |
| E7-L-T | ~3,500-4,000 shp first aircraft flat-rate | large transport/patrol; very-large propeller gated |

The central planning rule is: **re-open engine choice at project start, detail freeze and pre-first-flight.** A 1940 design is not permanently chained to an E6 engine if an E7 product closes before the airframe does.

## 3. E6 family — one propulsion ecosystem, several aircraft classes

### 3.1 Small-core branch

| propulsion | airframe | service | role | family verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E6-S-J | HS-2 | company research | compressibility / high-speed calibration | dedicated research; not E6 Single |
| E6-S-T | B6N test/fallback study | Navy future | carrier TP experience / schedule hedge | test comparator only after B6NAUDIT1 |

The old confusion `E6 Single = E6-S-J` is explicitly forbidden. E6 Single uses the **M-core**.

### 3.2 Medium pure-J branch

| propulsion | airframe | service | role | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E6-M-J | **E6 Single** | Army lead; Navy land-fighter interest plausible | interceptor / high-cover / CAP / sweep / tactical recon | 1940 serial-production line; ~96 acceptance center planning |
| E6-M-J | E6 Twin J | Army/Navy specialist | high-speed/high-altitude comparator and specialist service | minority ~10-15% Twin backend |
| E6-M-J + reheat | HS-3 | company research | transonic / reheat / nozzle research | development outlook |

E6 Single and E6 Twin J share the gas-generator family but are **different airframes**. Single uses one core; Twin uses two.

### 3.3 Medium aft-fan branch — the main 1940 twin ecosystem

The **E6-M-JF** pair is the most important example of one engine product supporting multiple aircraft/service identities.

| airframe / service fit | structural relationship | main role | 1940 status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Army E6 Twin common | common compact Twin | recon / command / high-cover / 0.4 t light attack / courier | main service-production family |
| Navy **R2As / 零式高速陸上偵察機** | same common Twin service fit | high-speed land reconnaissance / contact confirmation | 1940 service lineage |
| Navy **G4As1 / 零式陸上攻撃機一一型** | structurally specialized common-Twin derivative | one Type91 torpedo OR ~0.35-0.40 t bomb mission | high-speed land-attack branch |
| **E6-B fast heavy bomber** | **distinct larger airframe** sharing 2x E6-M-JF | ~1.0-1.2 t internal fast bombing; ~1.5 t overload target | prototype/outlook branch |

This distinction matters: R2As/G4As are very close members of the common Twin family, while E6-B is a **different aircraft family using the same two engines and much of the same systems school**.

### 3.4 Medium shaft branch

| propulsion | airframe | service | role | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4x E6-M-T | Material No.3 / AT-3 initial concept | company / future military transport | ~52-55 t heavy transport baseline | initial technical envelope |

E6-M-T is not a reason to convert G3M/G4M. The current audits explicitly reject serial TP conversions of mature long-range gasoline land-attack aircraft when conversion destroys their range advantage.

## 4. E7 family — retrofit first, dedicated airframes second

### 4.1 Jet / aft-fan retrofit family

| E7 product | recipient | service implication | payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| E7-M pure-J ~970-1,000 kgf | E6 Single | Army/Navy land fighter development | ~785-800 km/h @6km, ~4.8-5.3 min to 6km; airframe compressibility becomes limiter |
| E7-M inherited JF ~1,100-1,150 kgf | common E6 Twin / R2As / G4As | both services | only modest raw speed gain; prefer fuel/payload/protection/high-altitude margin |
| same E7-M JF | E6-B | Army-led fast bomber | ~715-740 km/h same-airframe OR ~7.3-7.6 t heavy growth / 1.3-1.5 t normal bomb load |

The practical difference is deliberate:
- **Single feels the generation change as speed/climb.**
- **Twin feels it as load/fuel/protection margin because the straight wing already hits compressibility.**

### 4.2 Turboprop branches

| E7 product | aircraft | service | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E7-S-T** | D4Y Suisei TP branch | Navy | small-core recon/fast-strike competitor; not automatic replacement of healthy Atsuta |
| **E7-M lower T** | B6N Tenzan | Navy | conditional main-production TP candidate; best match for ~1.8k shp carrier attack requirement |
| **E7-M central T** | AT-3 growth | transport | preferred 4-engine growth: ~9,200-9,800 shp aggregate |
| **E7-L-T** | future large transport/patrol | Army/Navy/company possible | airframe not yet fixed; first product flat-rated by propeller/reducer limits |

D4Y and B6N therefore do **not** directly compete for the same core. They occupy different E7 scale products. They do compete for naval reducer/propeller engineering, carrier qualification, turbine maintenance capacity and procurement priority.

### 4.3 Dedicated E7 airframes

The E6 retrofit program is a bridge, not the end state.

- **E7 dedicated single:** earliest 1942Q1-Q2 first-flight target; thin/light-swept wing becomes rational once E6 Single proves the engine has outrun the straight-wing airframe.
- **E7 dedicated twin:** same calendar; new thin/light-swept wing and inlet/nacelle layout can convert E7 thrust into speed instead of merely payload growth.
- **E7 bomber:** compare continued E6-B growth against a clean-sheet thin/light-swept bomber rather than enlarging the E6 straight-wing derivative indefinitely.

Exact military designations and procurement remain OPEN until their adoption/replay gates.

## 5. Army service-family view

### 1938-39

**High-speed fighter line**  
E5 Single / Ki-42 — E5-J — specialist high-cover/interception.

**Recon/command/light-strike line**  
E5 Twin J/JF — formal Army reconnaissance identity **九八式司令部偵察機** — JF becomes mission-workhorse backend.

### 1940

**Tactical fighter / interceptor family**  
E6 Single — E6-M-J — no longer an experimental curiosity; serial line, with interception as acquisition justification but CAP/sweep/escort/tactical-recon capability inside its radius.

**Common fast twin family**  
E6 Twin — mostly E6-M-JF, minority J — command/photo/BDA/high-cover/light strike/courier.

**Dedicated fast heavy-bomber family**  
E6-B — distinct larger 3-crew airframe, 2x E6-M-JF, 1.0-1.2 t normal internal bombs. Army is the cleaner initial sponsor because it fills the gap between common E6 attack and conventional heavy-bomber work.

### 1941-42 outlook

- E6 Single selected E7-M-J retrofit -> speed/climb bridge -> E7 dedicated fighter.
- E6 Twin selected E7-M-JF retrofit -> heavier mission state and systems margin -> E7 dedicated twin.
- E6-B E7 growth -> either heavier 1.3-1.5 t standard bomb aircraft or clean-sheet E7 bomber.
- AT-3 heavy transport remains a separate shaft-powered family; E7-M-T growth is preferred over blindly jumping to two huge E7-L engines.

## 6. Navy service-family view

### Land-based Asai turbine branch

**E5 Twin / R1As**  
九八式高速陸上偵察機 — E5-JF-biased recon branch, with E5 torpedo/light-strike testing creating the later attack school.

**E6 Twin / R2As**  
零式高速陸上偵察機 — E6-M-JF — high-speed land recon/contact confirmation.

**E6 Twin derivative / G4As1**  
零式陸上攻撃機一一型 — same E6-M-JF core family and much of the Twin hardware, specialized for one Type91 torpedo / short-medium high-speed land attack.

**E6 Single Navy interest**  
Same E6-M-J engine as the Army-led single line. A Navy land-based fighter/interceptor fit is plausible, but formal service designation and allocation remain OPEN rather than being invented here.

### Carrier branch — turbine technology selectively enters conventional airframes

The Navy does **not** convert every carrier aircraft to turbine propulsion.

- B5N2: piston serial main line; E5-S-T conversions are 2-4 test aircraft only.
- D3A1: piston serial main line; E5-S-T conversions are 2 + optional third technical aircraft only.
- D4Y: healthy Atsuta remains strong; **E7-S-T** is a parallel fast-recon/strike competitor.
- B6N: piston remains schedule hedge; **E7-M lower-flow TP** is the conditional main TP candidate.
- B7A future: inherits both the G4As/B6N high-speed torpedo school and D3A/D4Y dive/high-speed-store school; engine selection must be reopened at its own detail-freeze gate.

### Conventional gasoline land-attack branch remains intentional

- G3M2: long-range gasoline line; ordinary torpedo radius ~650-800 km.
- G4M1: very-long-range gasoline line; ordinary torpedo radius ~900-1,150 km.
- G4As: much shorter radius but radically higher attack-positioning and escape speed.

This is an intentional **avgas + kerosene mixed fleet**, not a transitional error.

## 7. Same-engine / multiple-airframe examples — mandatory mental model

### E6-M-J
One core product supports:
- E6 Single service fighter;
- E6 Twin pure-J specialist variant;
- HS-3/reheat research branch after backend modification.

### E6-M-JF
One core/backend product supports:
- Army common E6 Twin;
- Navy R2As service fit;
- Navy G4As structural derivative;
- larger, separate E6-B fast-heavy-bomber airframe.

### E7-M family
The same generation/scale band can become:
- J/JF retrofit engines for E6 Single/Twin/Bomber;
- lower-flow T product for B6N;
- central T product for AT-3 growth.

They are **not the same finished engine package** merely because they share E7-M core technology.

### E7-S family
E7-S-T naturally maps to D4Y-size TP work. An E7-S jet product is not automatically declared simply because the scale exists; product backend still requires a local program and qualification.

## 8. Portfolio guard — what this matrix does NOT mean

- It does not make `E7` a single universal engine model.
- It does not make R2As, G4As and E6-B the same airframe; only R2As/G4As are close common-Twin relatives, while E6-B is a larger separate aircraft.
- It does not force every E5/E6 aircraft to re-engine when E7 appears.
- It does not cancel piston B5N/D3A/G3M/G4M/Atsuta-D4Y branches.
- It does not pre-record 1941-42 procurement, deployment or combat.
- It does not promote H->A E7 output bands to measured service ratings before the relevant engine package flies and passes endurance/installation gates.

## 9. Planning consequences

From this point onward, aircraft development reviews should answer in this order:

1. What E-generation is available at **project start**?
2. What S/M/L core scale matches the airframe?
3. Which backend — J / JF / T / reheat — matches the mission?
4. Is this a **service variant of an existing airframe**, a **structural derivative**, or a **new airframe sharing the same engine**?
5. At **detail freeze** and **pre-first-flight**, has the E-front advanced enough to justify reopening the engine choice?
6. Does the service want a special high-end product, or an E6/E5-equivalent cheaper long-life product made with later-generation process control?
