## 2. Worldline piston D4Y

### 2.1 Atsuta is not historically doomed

Current engine canon gives the domestic DB601/Atsuta line:

- better material/process/tolerance/QC discipline;

## 3. Why the D4Y flutter delay changes in this worldline

Historical D4Y development was badly delayed when dive testing exposed wing flutter / structural cracking. In the current worldline the technical ecosystem entering 1940 already contains:

- Material No.2 high-q structural work;
- HS-series flutter/brake/emergency test practice;
- D3A instrumented dive-brake vibration work;
- strain / NDT / repeatable test-data reduction;
- the explicit preflight rule that foreknowledge reduces wrong-branch search but does not invent coefficients.

Therefore D4Y does not get a free flutter solution, but the problem should be **found early, instrumented densely, and converged faster**.

Planning opportunity:

- first prototype remains late-1940 class; do not drag it unrealistically into early 1940;
- rigorous dive / structural envelope testing begins immediately rather than after an optimistic production commitment;
- limited reconnaissance use can precede full dive qualification;
- **~6–9 months earlier structural/dive closure than the historical bomber-service path is plausible**, overlapping the D3AAUDIT1 ~3–6 month effective replacement opportunity rather than simply adding both numbers.

Use **1941 H2–1942 H1** as the main future service-development opportunity band if tests pass. No service date is pre-recorded.

## 4. E7-S-T installation

### 4.1 Engine product point

Use E7TPOUTLOOK1 small-core product:

- normal/flat-rated **~1,425–1,500 shp**;
- short **~1,525–1,575 shp**;
- near-rated BSFC ~0.34–0.36 kg/kWh;
- part-load cruise ~0.39–0.46 kg/kWh provisional;
- E7 core ground qualification target 1941 H2; aircraft flight release 1941 Q4–1942 Q1 earliest if local reducer/prop/TBO gates close.

### 4.2 Correct frontal-area interpretation

Do **not** repeat the earlier loose statement that a TP necessarily makes D4Y's nose narrower.

Atsuta-30/31-class physical width is only ~0.71 m. A practical E7-S-T intake/accessory/cowling package screens around **~0.88–0.98 m aerodynamic maximum**. The TP is therefore likely *wider* than the liquid-cooled inline installation even though it removes the radiator/coolant system.

The installation trade is:

- TP pays larger local nose/prop frontal area;
- TP removes radiator inlet/duct/exit drag and coolant plumbing;
- TP shortens/simplifies thermal installation and reduces vibration;
- final high-speed drag ranking is an aircraft test result, not an automatic TP win.

### 4.3 Mass / propeller screening

For aircraft comparison use:

- E7-S-T engine + free turbine + reducer + accessories dry: **~0.48–0.55 t** H->A;
- full installed powerplant including prop/mount/intake/duct: **~0.62–0.70 t**, center ~0.66 t;
- propeller: **~3.25–3.35 m four-blade constant-speed/feathering**;
- compared with Atsuta + radiator/coolant/duct/prop system, net installed aircraft relief: **~0.12–0.22 t** after local structure and prop changes.

This mass band is a design-screening value, not an E7 certified drawing.

## 5. TP D4Y mass and performance outlook

Retain the D4Y wing, bomb bay and carrier envelope. Do not enlarge the aircraft merely because E7 exists.

|---|---:|---:|

The headline result is deliberately modest: **TP D4Y is not 30–50 km/h faster than a healthy 1,400 hp worldline Atsuta D4Y.** The inline piston installation is already very clean.

The TP wins more clearly in:

- low-altitude acceleration;
- climb / wave-off;
- reduced cooling-system vulnerability and maintenance burden;
- low vibration for reconnaissance and bombing instrumentation;
- kerosene commonality at turbine-equipped carriers/bases.

The piston wins or remains highly competitive in:

- part-load cruise economy;
- maximum long-range mission endurance;
- mature industrial cost until E7 production scale grows.

## 6. Dive bombing and attack realization

D4Y inherits D3AAUDIT1's layered bombing logic. Engine choice does not create a magic hit-rate.

Planning dive envelope after structural qualification:

- setup altitude: ~3–5.5 km ordinary;
- dive angle: **~50–65 degrees**;
- controlled dive speed: **~430–480 km/h class** after improved brake qualification;
- ordinary large-ship release: **~450–650 m**;
- 250 kg / 500 kg internal carriage remains the main aerodynamic advantage over D3A.

Per valid release, retain approximately:

- maneuvering large ship: **~18–30% direct hit**;
- favorable/elite geometry: **~25–38%**;
- smoke/heavy evasion/disruption: **~8–16%**.

The D4Y advantage is more aircraft reaching a usable attack setup and more aircraft leaving the target area, not a doubled post-release ballistic accuracy.

For moderate 1941-class opposition use future screening only:

- piston D4Y valid-release completion after reaching target area: **~0.82–0.92**;
- E7-S-T D4Y: **~0.84–0.93**;
- post-release escape / return advantage is expected to be modest but real for TP because of lower attack weight and stronger low-altitude power response.

Exact combat survival stays replay-local.

## 7. Reconnaissance branch

D4Y is naturally valuable as a carrier reconnaissance aircraft even before dive qualification.

Worldline advantages:

- E5/E6 photo-calibration and vibration practice already exists;
- D4Y internal bay can accept an auxiliary fuel / camera mission set when not bombing;
- clean airframe and two-seat crew make it a natural fast carrier scout.

The E7-S-T version is especially attractive here because low vibration and cooling simplification are valuable while the modest range penalty can be partly recovered by using the bomb-bay volume for fuel/camera mission equipment.

Therefore the **first serial/limited E7-S-T D4Y application may rationally be reconnaissance**, even if the main dive-bomber production line initially remains piston.

## 8. Carrier integration

The ~1.5 khp E7-S-T needs a high-power propeller but stays below B6N's ~2 khp propeller problem.

- 3.25–3.35 m four-blade is inside the existing high-power propeller school;
- ground clearance / landing-gear compression still requires local carrier qualification;
- TP does not lower stall/landing speed automatically;
- wave-off and bolter recovery are improved by low-altitude power response;
- D3A TP test aircraft provide dive/reducer/governor/store-separation inheritance.

## 9. Procurement / replacement verdict

### Piston D4Y1 / D4Y2-style branch

**A / schedule baseline.** The healthy worldline Atsuta removes the historical engine-collapse argument against the liquid-cooled branch. Piston D4Y is fast, long-legged and already matches the aerodynamic concept extremely well.

### E7-S-T D4Y

**B+ to A- conditional.** It is the most natural small-core E7 carrier-aircraft application, but not an automatic full replacement for the piston D4Y.

Preferred planning:

1. piston prototype / early reconnaissance and structural-test aircraft fly first;
2. E7-S-T installation enters late-1941/early-1942 flight competition;
3. if E7 TBO/reducer/carrier maintenance gates pass, use a **limited reconnaissance / fast-strike series first**;
4. switch the main dive-bomber line only if measured sortie generation / maintenance / acceleration benefits outweigh the piston range and cost advantage.

A future model-number split is left OPEN. Do not force historical D4Y2 numbering onto the TP branch before adoption.

## 10. Relationship to B6N

D4Y and B6N do **not** directly compete for the same E7 core scale:

- D4Y: **E7-S-T**, ~7.5–8 kg/s / ~1.5 khp;
- B6N: **E7-M lower-flow**, ~10–11 kg/s / ~1.8–2.0 khp.

The real shared bottlenecks are:

- naval reducers;
- propeller/governor production;
- carrier turbine maintenance qualification;
- hot-section manufacturing capacity;
- deck crews / kerosene logistics.

Therefore both programs can rationally continue in parallel.

However their TP business cases differ:

- **B6N TP:** larger performance gain because the TP replaces a bulky 1.8 khp radial while preserving carrier envelope;
- **D4Y TP:** smaller headline speed gain because the Atsuta inline installation is already exceptionally clean; value shifts toward climb, wave-off, maintenance, reconnaissance vibration and kerosene commonality.

This preserves B6NAUDIT1's E7-M-LF main-candidate verdict while making D4Y the natural **small-core** naval E7 test/limited-production branch.
