# ASAI G3M2 PISTON / TURBOPROP COMPARATIVE CLOSEOUT — 1939-1941 — V10 G3MAUDIT1

**Status:** CURRENT TECHNICAL ADDENDUM / H->A comparative closure  
**Clock effect:** NONE. 1940 combat has not started.

## Executive closure

The G3M2 / Type 96 Land-based Attack Aircraft remains a **high-value gasoline long-range land-attack baseline**. Its strongest attribute is not speed but the combination of low wing loading, very large internal fuel volume, 800 kg torpedo/bomb carriage and mature long-range navigation/attack practice. Asai exhaust/QC improvements should be applied without erasing that architecture.

Current closure:

- 1940 comparison anchor is G3M2 Model 22 class: ~16.45 m length, 25.0 m span, ~75 m2 wing, ~4.97-5.15 t empty, ~8.0 t loaded, two ~1,075 hp Kinsei 45-class radials, one 800 kg Type91-class torpedo or ~800 kg bombs.
- historical maximum-speed anchor ~373-375 km/h around 4.2 km; applicable worldline EX2 new-build/major-overhaul aircraft use **~377-381 km/h class**.
- historical maximum/ferry reach remains roughly **~4,400 km class**; no generic Asai range multiplier.
- H->A torpedo-sortie radius is **~650-800 km class** after allowing an ~800 kg torpedo, crew/mission equipment, reserve/search/attack fuel and the 8 t weight class. This is a working mission band, not a historical placard figure.
- E5-S-T direct conversion is rejected for serial production: same ~1,000 hp class, modest nacelle/weight improvement, but no meaningful low-level speed gain and a major cruise-SFC/radius penalty.
- E6-S-T 1,300/1,400 hp can make a faster experimental derivative, but still sacrifices the central long-range virtue and overlaps the much faster G4As. No serial G3M-TP branch is justified.
- E6-M-T is too large/powerful to call a G3M retrofit; any such aircraft is a new design and belongs in a later TP land-attack study.
- Result: keep gasoline G3M2/G3M3 development alive alongside kerosene G4As. Fuel-system coexistence is deliberate, not a temporary anomaly.

## Current piston worldline state

Current exhaust authority permits EX2 on 1939-40 new-build / major-overhaul G3M2 aircraft, with a physical **+4 to +6 km/h** speed delta. Therefore use **~377-381 km/h** for applicable later lots. Cruise/fuel margin may absorb part of the gain; do not add a separate range multiplier.

FCS/QC inheritance improves engine/prop/cooling/vibration consistency, navigation, torpedo/bomb release tables, instruments and serviceability. It does not create armor, self-sealing tanks or a generic hit-rate scalar.

## Torpedo mission radius

The published ~4,400 km figure is a maximum-range anchor and must not be used as an 800 kg-torpedo combat radius. With the 8 t aircraft mass ceiling, an 800 kg torpedo and seven crew, only part of the very large tank volume can be filled.

A current drag/power screening at ~260-280 km/h cruise gives roughly ~0.9-1.05 khp total shaft demand at 3-4 km. With mature piston cruise economy and realistic reserve/search/attack allowance, use **~650-800 km ordinary torpedo radius** as the H->A planning band. Longer one-way/ferry/bomb-load missions remain possible; exact sortie load sheets stay replay-local.

This is already roughly twice the initial G4As torpedo radius. G4As buys speed and survivability; G3M buys reach.

## E5-S-T direct conversion

Two E5-S-T engines provide roughly the same total headline shaft power as the Kinsei installation. Turbine nacelles can be smaller and perhaps save ~0.2-0.3 t total installed/airframe mass after inlet/exhaust/firewall/access changes. A ~5-8% parasite-drag reduction is plausible.

However, a back-calculation calibrated to the historical 373 km/h / 4.2 km point shows the decisive fact: at sea level the piston G3M2 is already around the low-330 km/h class in clean-power screening. Two 1,000 hp E5-S-T engines plus lower drag remain in essentially the **same low-330 km/h class**. At 3-4 km the supercharged Kinsei retains power better, so the E5-S-T aircraft is worse unless an unclosed altitude map is invented.

Cruise is the larger penalty. E5-S-T part-load fuel economy is materially worse than a mature piston installation. Kerosene density and modest weight/drag savings do not compensate. Working torpedo-radius screening falls to approximately **~450-600 km class**.

**Verdict:** no physical conversion program is required. Paper/bench installation work is enough; B5N TP test aircraft and existing E5/E6 land aircraft already supply turbine naval/torpedo data.

## E6-S-T derivative

The 1,300 hp standard / 1,400 hp short E6-S-T can create a more energetic G3M-like derivative. Low-altitude clean speed can move into roughly the **360-375 km/h class** depending installation, with better takeoff/acceleration. But:

- medium-altitude power remains map-dependent;
- cruise BSFC remains worse than the piston long-range architecture;
- torpedo radius remains below the piston G3M, screening roughly **~550-700 km class**;
- by the time this product is attractive, G4As already provides much greater high-speed terminal performance, while piston G3M/G4M branches provide range.

Therefore no serial E6-S-T G3M branch is opened. The useful inheritance is design data for future purpose-built TP patrol/attack/transport aircraft.

## Fuel and force-structure consequence

The Navy should expect mixed aviation fuels for years:

- gasoline: B5N, D3A, G3M, G4M, A6M and other conventional piston fleets;
- kerosene: E5/E6 J/JF, G4As/R2As and any selected TP products.

This is rational. Converting a long-range piston aircraft to turbine power merely to simplify fuel type can increase total fuel mass/mission and consume scarce GT hardware.

## Type91 opportunity cost

The G3M reinforces the B5N result. A scarce Type91 can be assigned to:

- B5N: carrier-mobile mature launch platform;
- G3M: long-range land-based launch opportunity;
- G4As: much faster but shorter-radius land attack;
- later B6N: faster carrier attack after production ramps.

No one platform automatically dominates. Later torpedo allocation must be mission-based.

- no automatic G3M retirement when G4As appears;
- no 4,400 km torpedo combat radius;
- no free armor/self-sealing-tank retrofit;
- no combat clock advance.
