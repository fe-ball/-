## 0. Core rule

This closeout does **not** create:
- amphibious Chi-Ha;
- snorkel/deep-wading tanks;
- unlimited landing craft;
- extra bridges;
- blanket Japanese motorization;
- immunity to flood, mud, current or destroyed roads.

It closes a different advantage:

**wet-weather reliability + post-ford readiness + heavy-load mobility + engineer/boat uptime.**

The main effect is:
- fewer vehicles / radios / generators become unusable after rain / splash / shallow ford;
- heavy vehicles retain more useful pull in mud / grades;
- engineers keep boats, pumps, power and repair equipment working more continuously;
- Chi-Ha uses its existing 200/220 hp worldline power margin where terrain is bad.

- **200 hp continuous**;
- **220 hp short-duration**;
- 220 hp explicitly intended for:
  - grade,
  - mud,
  - speed recovery,
  - hot/high conditions,
  not headline top-speed inflation.

Worldline power/weight:
- continuous: **~12.8–13.1 hp/t**
- short-duration: **~14.1–14.4 hp/t**

Historical reference:
- ~11.2–11.3 hp/t

Thus:
- continuous power margin is roughly **+14–17%**
- short-duration available power margin is roughly **+25–29%**
relative to the historical 170 hp / ~15 t reference.

This does not mean +15–30% tactical speed.
At low speed, traction, mud, gearing and ground pressure can dominate.

## 2.2 Chi-Ha WET-1 service configuration

**Working designation:** `CHIHA-WET1`

Standard / production-service measures:
- better sealed starter/generator terminals and cable entries;
- impregnated electrical windings;
- gasket / boot improvement on vulnerable electrical boxes;
- protected radio-power and lighting connectors where fitted;
- improved drain paths in boxes / compartments that cannot be made watertight;
- better grease / seal discipline at selected bearings and hubs;
- protected transmission/final-drive breather routing where practical;
- canvas/rubber temporary covers for selected exposed service openings during river preparation;
- explicit post-ford inspection / drain / restart checklist.

This is **weatherproofing / splashproofing**, not hull watertightness.

## 2.3 Fording limit

Routine Type 97 Chi-Ha fording depth remains:

**~1.0 m class**

No new deep-wading rating is credited.

Why:
- engine air intake / ventilation geometry;
- hull openings;
- turret / vision / weapon apertures;
- exhaust;
- transmission / final drive;
- crew safety

still impose a physical limit.

WET1 therefore improves:
- reliability at the rated ford;
- tolerance to splash / short wet exposure;
- speed of return to combat-ready condition after crossing.

It does **not** authorize:
- 1.5–2 m unsupported deep wading;
- submerged engine operation;
- long underwater movement.

# 3. Chi-Ha mobility effect in mud / bank exits

The 200/220 hp worldline engine matters most after the vehicle has crossed or on the approaches.

Use cases:
- climbing a muddy exit bank;
- pulling out of soft ground;
- recovering speed after obstacle / ford;
- towing / assisting another vehicle for short distances;
- moving with heavier mud drag;
- avoiding an unnecessary downshift / stall on a grade.

Working combat interpretation:

### Good road
- headline speed difference: small
- no generic speed multiplier

### Wet road / moderate mud / grade
- movement clock advantage can be **~5–10%** for a mixed armored column where engine power is a real limiter

### Short severe mud / bank-exit segment
- individual Chi-Ha can show a much larger local advantage in stall avoidance / recovery
- do not convert this into a whole-column +20% speed claim

### Physical impassability
If:
- bridge is gone,
- bank cannot support 15.5 t,
- water is too deep,
- current is too strong,

power advantage = **zero until engineers solve the geometry**.

# 8. Critical tank-ferry constraint

Not all Japanese landing / motor craft can carry Chi-Ha.

Historical craft examples:
- ~14 m Daihatsu class: roughly 10–12 t cargo / one ~7.5 t light tank class;
- larger ~17 m landing-craft family: about **15.6 t class** capacity in relevant variants.

A worldline Chi-Ha is **15.3–15.6 t**.

Therefore:

**ordinary light landing craft / assault boats cannot automatically ferry Chi-Ha.**

To cross a river with Chi-Ha, one needs:
- a bridge rated for the load;
- heavy ferry / pontoon;
- sufficiently large landing craft;
- or another specifically qualified crossing method.

This is a major gate for First Changsha.

The engine's extra 30–50 hp does not help while the tank is waiting on the wrong bank.
