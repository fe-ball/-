# ASAI WORLDLINE — 1939 WINTER GT / COLD-START / HEAT-SUPPORT CLOSEOUT V1
## 1939-10-10 through 1939-12-31

**Status:** TECH / PRODUCT / SERVICE-PRACTICE / FIELD-ALLOCATION CLOSED
**Purpose:** Close the first winter in which mobile-GT exhaust heat becomes a measurable historical differential rather than a summer-side utility.

---

## 1. Existing anchors carried forward

By 30 Jun 1939:
- small mobile-GT operational/evaluation population: **32–45 sets**
- Nomonhan/Hailar allocation: **5–7**
- **2–3** Nomonhan sets already had simple exhaust-to-air / exhaust-to-water heat recovery
- validated summer roles:
  - workshop/recovery power
  - signals/HQ backup
  - airfield maintenance/lighting
  - pumps/water/purification
  - engineering/medical utility
  - hot water / drying / oil warming

Post-Nomonhan:
- mobile GT becomes **LIMITED-STANDARD SUPPORT EQUIPMENT / FIELD-VALIDATED**
- September H2 decision standardizes:
  - mounting/interface
  - generator output
  - pump/workshop connections
  - exhaust/heat-recovery interface
  - filters/test points
  - training / life-card / exchange-core practice

Winter capability is therefore not invented in October 1939.

## 2. Three-layer winterization

### Layer 1 — no new GT core required
Army/customer procedure:
- temperature-banded lubricants
- battery charging/storage discipline
- fuel/water separation and filter checks
- cold-start sequencing
- post-start warm-up limits
- engine/gearbox fault classification
- spare-battery / cable practice
- drying and condensation control

This can spread widely through supported formations.

### Layer 2 — retrofit heat package
Existing mobile utility set receives:
- exhaust-to-air heat exchanger
- exhaust-to-water heat exchanger
- insulated hose / duct kit
- oil-pan / gearbox warming mats or hot-air shrouds where practical
- hot-water cans / service manifolds
- drying tent / component-drying connection

No new turbine core is required.

### Layer 3 — new mobile GT utility set
New generator/pump/workshop module produced and accepted.

This remains the slowest layer because:
- generator/switchgear
- vehicle/tow integration
- field training
- spares
must all exist.

## 6. Temperature-effect bands

These apply to **supported clusters only**.

### +5 to +10 C
Main value:
- drying
- battery condition
- workshop comfort/process
- oil handling

Morning immediate-ready improvement:
**~+1–3 percentage points**

Typical start/prep reduction:
**~5–15 min**

### 0 to +5 C
- oil viscosity begins to matter
- damp electrical faults increase
- battery power degrades

Immediate-ready:
**~+3–6 pp**

Start/prep:
**~10–25 min shorter**

### -10 to 0 C
Strong cold-start band.

Immediate-ready:
**~+6–12 pp**

Start/prep:
**~20–50 min shorter**

Routine cold-start mechanical/electrical failures reaching workshop:
**~15–25% lower** in supported equipment families

### below -10 C
GT heat has high value, but distribution capacity becomes the limit.

Immediate-ready:
**~+10–18 pp locally**

Start/prep:
**~30–90 min shorter**

No universal formation-wide bonus:
many engines will still be outside the supported heating cluster.

## 7. Vehicle applications

Priority:
1. artillery tractors
2. heavy ammunition/fuel/water trucks
3. recovery/workshop vehicles
4. engineer vehicles
5. tanks/mobile reserve
6. ordinary light trucks only when capacity remains

Heat is applied to:
- engine oil / sump
- gearbox/final-drive oil
- batteries/electrical gear
- fuel filters/lines where needed
- workshop components

### Chi-Ha
WET1 + improved diesel service procedure helps cold start.

But:
- no special mass Chi-Ha heater is invented
- a GT-supported tank cluster receives preheated oil/battery/gearbox service

Supported winter Chi-Ha morning ready advantage:
- 0 to -10 C: **~+5–9 pp**
- below -10 C: **~+8–14 pp**

The engine's 200/220 hp rating does not increase because it is cold.

---

## 8. Aviation applications

### Conventional piston aircraft
Highest cold-start payoff:
- oil preheat
- battery/ground power
- plug/component drying
- lighting/maintenance
- heated workshop

Supported flight-line effect in real winter:
- first-wave launch delay reduced
- fewer cold-soak no-starts
- higher dawn serviceability

### E5 / Ki-42 turbine family
GT aircraft are not immune to cold.
Relevant support:
- bearing/oil temperature
- batteries/start system
- fuel-control condition
- ground electrical power
- instrument/airframe drying

However their benefit from external warming is generally **smaller than for large piston engines**.

Do not give E5/Ki-42 a double “GT aircraft + GT ground heater” winter multiplier.

---

## 9. Communications / optical / medical applications

Cold and condensation affect:
- batteries
- connectors
- telephone/radio power
- optics
- instruments

Heat/drying gives:
- faster restoration
- lower condensation fault rate
- better battery condition

Medical / sanitation:
- hot water
- drying
- limited heated work/aid space

No army-wide warm barracks advantage is credited.

## 11. Important historical-delta rule

Winter creates a larger differential because historical cold-start friction is nonlinear.

But:
- supported equipment != all equipment
- preheat capacity is finite
- roads/snow/ice can remain the dominant problem
- enemy action can destroy heating points
- units moving away from their GT/support node lose the advantage

Therefore model:
**morning readiness / first-wave timing / repair load**

Do NOT model:
**+X% division combat power all day**

### Manchuria / Inner Mongolia
**major winter effect**

### Shanxi / Hebei / Henan / Suiyuan
**strong effect**, especially nights/dawn

### Wuhan / northern Hubei / north Hunan
**moderate cold-damp effect**

### Lower Yangtze
**moderate drying/electrical effect; mild engine-preheat effect**

### Guangdong / Guangxi coast / Nanning
**minimal cold-start effect**

GT remains useful there for power/pumps/workshop, not warmth.
