# ASAI B5N2 PISTON / TURBOPROP COMPARATIVE CLOSEOUT — 1939-1941 — V10 B5NAUDIT1

**Status:** CURRENT TECHNICAL ADDENDUM / H->A comparative closure  
**Clock effect:** NONE. 1939-12-31 24:00 remains closed; 1940 combat has not started.  
**Purpose:** put the Type 97 Carrier Attack Aircraft (B5N2) on the same performance/fuel/propulsion/weapon-inheritance basis used for Asai turbine aircraft before deciding whether a turboprop retrofit, B6N acceleration, or torpedo reallocation is rational.

## 0. Executive closure

The B5N2 is **better than the old worldline shorthand implied**. It is a light, low-wing-loading, mature carrier torpedo aircraft whose historical Sakae-powered performance remains competitive enough that Asai improvements should be applied as evolutionary gains rather than as justification for an automatic turbine replacement.

Current closure:

- Historical airframe anchor: ~10.3 m length / ~15.52 m span / 37.7 m2 wing / 2.279 t empty / ~3.8 t normal loaded / ~4.1 t maximum takeoff.
- Historical Sakae 11 power anchor: ~1,000 hp takeoff, ~970-980 hp around 3 km.
- Historical maximum-speed anchor: ~378 km/h around 3.6 km.
- Later worldline EX2/QC B5N2 lots: **~382-384 km/h class** at the historical maximum-speed altitude; normal cruise change is deliberately small.
- Worldline normal mission range remains **~980-1,020 km class**, not a free large multiplier.
- The 1941 worldline Type 91 Mod 2 normal high-speed air-drop envelope (~400-430 km/h) is above the practical B5N low-altitude attack speed. By that point the torpedo is no longer the primary ordinary-sea-attack speed limiter for the B5N2; the airframe is.
- An E5-S-T conversion is technically plausible and useful as a **2-4 aircraft test/qualification sub-program**, but **not** as a serial B5N re-engine program.
- Main E5-S-T advantages are kerosene use, lower vibration, a smaller frontal package, turbine carrier-operation experience, and a clean test path into B6N/naval TP work.
- Main penalties are inferior part-load specific fuel consumption, weaker medium-altitude power retention than the supercharged Sakae, two-fuel carrier logistics, naval turbine intake/salt/FOD/start/maintenance burden, and GT opportunity cost.
- The B5N2 therefore remains a high-value carrier torpedo platform into the B6N transition. A Type 91 torpedo assigned to a B5N2 has non-trivial opportunity value because the carrier can move the launch point while the aircraft itself is already mature and numerous.

## 2. Current worldline piston B5N2

### 2.1 Propulsion / exhaust

Current V8/V10 piston authority retains the Sakae-powered B5N2. The worldline improvement is **not** an early total engine replacement.

Applicable later production / overhaul lots progressively receive EX2-class exhaust-energy recovery and Asai-influenced matching/QC. Current exhaust authority gives a physical EX0->EX2 maximum-speed delta of roughly **+4 to +6 km/h** for B5N2-class installation.

Therefore use:

- historical-like early B5N2: ~378 km/h class at the historical maximum-speed altitude;
- applicable later worldline EX2 lots: **~382-384 km/h class**;
- normal cruise: **~260-264 km/h working band**, with much of the exhaust/QC margin allowed to buy thermal/fuel/service margin rather than headline cruise speed.

No additional generic speed scalar is allowed.

### 2.2 Range / carrier utility

Do not multiply historical range because of Asai QC. The airframe and wing-tank architecture remain historical-like.

Use **~980-1,020 km class normal mission range** as the working worldline band until a mission-specific fuel/load calculation supersedes it.

The B5N2's strategic advantage is not land-based radius alone. A carrier moves the launch point. Therefore a mature B5N2 plus a scarce Type 91 torpedo can create attack opportunity in areas where a faster land-based G4As still requires a suitable airfield within its own torpedo radius.

That is why torpedo allocation must later be solved at fleet/mission level rather than by assigning the newest torpedo automatically to the newest aircraft.

### 2.3 Fire-control / torpedo system

Current FCS authority already gives B5N2:

- torpedo-sight calibration;
- gyro / heading-setting discipline;
- airspeed and altitude instrument QC;
- Type 91 release-envelope tables;
- reduced systematic setting error;
- later mechanical lead aid only when separately date/lot qualified.

No fixed hit-rate multiplier is created by these improvements.

## 3. 1941 Type 91 high-speed inheritance changes the B5N2 in an asymmetric way

NAVALJETTORP1 closes the 1941 Type 91 Mod 2 normal air-drop qualification around **400-430 km/h**, with selected development growth around **450 km/h**.

For G4As and B6N this is an enabling high-speed weapon technology. For B5N2 it does something different:

1. the B5N2 cannot exploit the whole speed envelope because its own low-altitude torpedo-attack speed is lower;
2. the stronger / more repeatable torpedo air-entry envelope removes torpedo fragility as a major ordinary-sea-attack speed constraint;
3. the same mature aircraft can therefore attack closer to its own practical airframe limit with more repeatable release handling;
4. rougher sea-state / minor height/speed-setting error costs can reduce because the torpedo has more entry margin;
5. this benefit arrives without paying for a new B5N airframe.

Thus the G4As-driven torpedo program indirectly **extends the useful life and opportunity value of the piston B5N2**.

Do not interpret this as a claim that historical Type 91 could never be released fast. Historical high-speed release existed in some conditions. The worldline difference is **broader, more repeatable, better-instrumented qualification**, not invention of aerial high-speed torpedo release from zero.

## 4. E5-S-T B5N2 conversion study

### 4.1 Why the conversion is technically credible

Current E5-S-T is a mature ~1,000 hp-class shaft/turboprop product:

- quantity/continuous coordinate ~952 hp;
- common normal rating ~1,000 hp;
- selected high-end ~1,050-1,060 hp;
- engine + free turbine + reducer + accessories ~390-460 kg;
- complete installed package including prop/mount/duct allowance ~510-640 kg, center ~575 kg;
- approximately 3.2-3.3 m three-blade constant-speed propeller is adequate at this power level.

Therefore the B5N does **not** need a new long landing gear merely to accept a much larger propeller. The historical ~3.2 m propeller/gear geometry is already in the right class.

The conversion is a packaging and mission-economics problem, not a raw-power impossibility.

### 4.2 Working conversion mass

Removing the radial engine, circular cowling and some associated hardware saves mass and frontal area. The turbine conversion then returns mass through:

- inlet/duct installation;
- exhaust;
- reducer/free turbine accessories;
- starter / electrical changes;
- firewall / hot-zone changes;
- naval salt/FOD protection and access provisions.

Working estimated empty weight:

**~2,170-2,210 kg, center ~2,190 kg.**

This is only ~90 kg below the historical empty B5N2, not a fantasy half-tonne saving.

### 4.3 Drag

A narrower turbine nose removes part of the radial-cowling parasite drag but introduces inlet/exhaust/local-interference drag.

Use a working **4-7% reduction in parasite drag**, center ~5%, for the conversion aircraft.

A historical-airframe power/drag back-calculation calibrated to 378 km/h @ 3.6 km gives a model coordinate Cd0 ~0.0283. This is only an internal comparative coordinate, not a claimed wind-tunnel measurement.

### 4.4 Speed

The key problem is altitude power.

The historical Sakae 11 retains roughly 970-980 hp near 3 km through supercharging. No current E5-S-T aircraft altitude-power map is closed that justifies assuming the turbine still has 1,000 hp at 3-4 km.

Therefore bracket, rather than invent, the E5-S-T altitude map.

Working clean-speed result:

| altitude | B5N2 E5-S-T working band |
|---:|---:|
| sea level | ~350-353 km/h center ~351 |
| 1,000 m | ~347-354 km/h |
| 3,000 m | ~339-354 km/h center ~346 |
| 3,600 m | ~335-354 km/h center ~345 |

Interpretation:

- the TP conversion can be **slightly better at low altitude** than the historical piston airframe because of cleaner frontal geometry and good low-altitude shaft power;
- it is **worse at the historical 3-4 km maximum-speed altitude** unless a later compressor/control development closes much stronger altitude power retention;
- EX2 piston B5N2 remains faster in the altitude regime that sets its historical headline maximum speed.

This is a very important non-result: turbine availability does not automatically make the aircraft faster everywhere.

### 4.5 Fuel consumption / range

Current E5-S-T full-power SFC is around:

- ~0.435 kg/kWh at the selected Ni 1,000 hp coordinate;
- ~0.447 kg/kWh at the non-Ni 800 C coordinate.

At partial cruise power, do **not** assume a miraculous improvement. A ~0.47-0.55 kg/kWh working part-load band is appropriate until a closed aircraft cruise map exists.

The mature piston B5N/Sakae cruise installation is substantially more economical in the relevant long-endurance regime. A ~0.29-0.33 kg/kWh effective piston-cruise comparison band is adequate for H->A screening.

Kerosene's greater fuel density helps when tank volume is limiting, and the TP airframe is modestly lighter / cleaner. Those gains do not erase the SFC penalty.

Therefore, for the same basic B5N wing-tank architecture and mission class, use:

- TP normal heavy-mission range: **~680-780 km class**;
- TP ferry range: **~1,350-1,600 km class**.

These are H->A screening bands, not placard figures.

The penalty against the piston B5N is large enough that a serial conversion undermines one of the aircraft's strongest carrier-strike qualities: useful radius/endurance from a mobile deck.

### 4.6 Deck operation

The TP conversion can improve:

- vibration;
- throttle response / low-altitude power consistency after governor tuning;
- nose visibility from smaller frontal packaging;
- kerosene commonality with E5/E6 naval turbine units;
- turbine training / maintenance familiarity.

But it does not magically transform takeoff or landing because:

- wing area is unchanged;
- maximum aircraft mass remains in the same class;
- propeller diameter and gross shaft power are in the same class;
- carrier approach requirements still dominate.

Expect **small improvement or no major change** in practical carrier takeoff/landing distance rather than a dramatic STOL gain.

## 5. Procurement decision

### CLOSED

**Do not serially re-engine B5N2 with E5-S-T.**

Authorize **2-4 conversion/test aircraft** in the late-1939/1940 technical program for:

- carrier turbine starting and deck-handling qualification;
- salt/FOD/intake durability;
- torpedo separation / release testing;
- low-vibration sight/camera trials;
- kerosene deck-fuel procedure;
- propeller/reducer/governor naval acceptance;
- direct data feed into B6N and later naval TP designs.

Do not count these aircraft as a new frontline B5N variant unless later replay explicitly assigns them.

The serial fleet remains Sakae-powered.

## 6. Why this matters to B6N

The B5N result makes the B6N requirement **harder, not easier**.

The successor cannot justify itself merely by being newer or by using a turbine. It must provide a meaningful combination of:

- materially higher speed;
- high-speed Type 91 release by design origin;
- useful torpedo radius at carrier gross weight;
- better climb / acceleration;
- acceptable deck footprint / folding geometry;
- adequate protection / damage tolerance growth;
- production and fuel economics that justify replacing a very mature B5N system.

The E6-S-T 1,300 hp normal / 1,400 hp short candidate must therefore be evaluated against both:

1. a good worldline B5N2 piston baseline; and
2. the competing historical-like high-power piston B6N path.

No automatic turboprop B6N selection is allowed.

## 7. Torpedo opportunity-cost consequence

By 1941, high-speed Type 91 production is a scarce weapon-production decision, not just an aircraft accessory decision.

A torpedo allocated to B5N2 buys:

- a mature carrier-deck aircraft;
- a mobile launch platform because the carrier moves;
- well-developed crew doctrine and maintenance;
- no requirement for a special turbine base.

A torpedo allocated to G4As buys:

- much greater aircraft speed and ingress/egress survivability;
- rapid multi-axis positioning;
- prepared-land-base dependence;
- shorter/medium torpedo radius relative to long-range piston land attack branches.

A torpedo allocated to B6N later buys a faster carrier attack platform, but only after B6N production/deck conversion reaches useful scale.

Therefore **do not allocate high-speed Type 91s by aircraft prestige**. A later fleet-level ledger should compare launch opportunity, aircraft availability, target distance, survivability, and torpedo stock.

## 8. Guards

This closeout does **not**:

- create frontline B5N-TP squadrons;
- replace Sakae B5N2 with E5-S-T;
- give B5N2 400+ km/h aircraft speed;
- give a generic torpedo hit-rate multiplier;
- pre-record any 1940 combat result;
- remove B6N development;
- imply all carriers should carry kerosene-only air groups;
- claim E5-S-T altitude power that has not been closed by a current altitude map.
