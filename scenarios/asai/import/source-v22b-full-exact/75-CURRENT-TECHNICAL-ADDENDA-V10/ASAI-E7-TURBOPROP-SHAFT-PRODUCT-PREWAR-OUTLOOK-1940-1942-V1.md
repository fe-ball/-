## 1. Why the TP outlook must be reopened at E7

Earlier B5N/D3A/G3M/G4M comparisons correctly rejected **E5-S-T or E6-S-T retrofit** where those products were the engines actually available for the 1939–41 comparison. They do **not** imply that future 1941–42 aircraft should be frozen to E6-S-T.

The E-generation and the core scale are independent axes. Current scale bands are:

- small: ~5–8 kg/s;
- medium: ~10–15 kg/s;
- large: ~20–40 kg/s.

E7 raises the specific-work/efficiency front while the Navy and Army already possess mature free-power-turbine, reducer, governor, constant-speed/feathering-propeller and kerosene-service experience. The important design move is therefore often **not** `E6-S -> E7-S`, but moving an aircraft requirement to the **lower edge of the E7 medium core** if its power demand has outgrown the small-core band.

## 2. Screening method

Upstream E7 coordinate:

- PR ~8;
- TIT ~900 C class;
- gross specific-work comparison ~173 kJ/kg;
- organization/Asai E7 large-core shaft capability already closes around ~4,650/~4,780 hp at ~24 kg/s.

The E6 installed shaft audit shows gross-to-installed shaft retention of ~84% before the final turboprop output transmission debit. Applying an **84–86% installed-work screening band** and ~97.5% output-transmission efficiency to E7 is a consistent H->A extrapolation. It is not an installed-product certification.

This reproduces the existing E7 ~24 kg/s large-core capability and gives the following scale-sensitive screening values:

| E7 flow coordinate | calculated flange capability | interpretation |
|---:|---:|---|
| 7.5 kg/s | ~1,425–1,460 hp | small-core upper practical neighborhood |
| 8.0 kg/s | ~1,520–1,555 hp | **small-core ceiling neighborhood** |
| 10.0 kg/s | ~1,900–1,945 hp | **lower-medium / carrier-attack sweet spot** |
| 11.0 kg/s | ~2,090–2,140 hp | lower-medium growth |
| 13.2 kg/s | ~2,510–2,570 hp | medium central product capability |
| 14.0 kg/s | ~2,660–2,725 hp | medium upper trim |
| 24 kg/s | ~4,560–4,670 hp | agrees with upstream E7 large-core band before Asai concentrated upper edge |

Do not read capability as normal aircraft rating. Gearbox life, propeller absorption, hot-day margin, deterioration, TBO and production scatter require flat-rating below the thermodynamic ceiling.

## 3. E7 shaft / TP product points for aircraft planning

### 3.1 E7 small TP

A small-core E7 TP does **not** jump to 1,900 hp. The ~8 kg/s scale ceiling prevents that.

Use as H->A product outlook:

- normal/flat-rated: **~1,425–1,500 shp**;
- short/high-flow: **~1,525–1,575 shp**;
- full-power BSFC screening: **~0.34–0.36 kg/kWh**;
- part-load aircraft cruise: keep **~0.39–0.46 kg/kWh** open band until a product map is measured.

This is a useful ~10–20% improvement over E6-S-T 1,300/1,400 hp, but it is not enough to turn every 1,700–1,900 hp piston-aircraft requirement into a small-core TP.

### 3.2 E7 lower-medium TP — the important new product

At the lower edge of the medium band, ~10–11 kg/s, E7 naturally produces the power class that carrier-attack and medium tactical aircraft begin to demand.

Planning product point:

- normal/flat-rated: **~1,750–1,850 shp**;
- short: **~1,900–2,000 shp**;
- capability ceiling around ~10 kg/s: ~1,900–1,950 hp before service flat-rating;
- lower-medium dry/installed mass, diameter, altitude map and TBO remain **OPEN**, to be closed in the B6N/product audit.

This should be described as an **E7-M lower-flow product point**, not as an impossible enlarged E7-S. Scale semantics stay intact.

### 3.3 E7 medium central TP

At ~12.5–14 kg/s:

- shaft capability: **~2,400–2,725 hp**;
- first service product target: **~2,250–2,450 shp normal**, ~2,500–2,650 short only where reducer/propeller qualification permits;
- early-aircraft practical prop absorption may cap useful installed power before the core does.

Current 3.3–3.6 m four-blade high-power propeller work is already strong around 1,800–2,200 hp. Sustained 2,400–2,600 hp on a compact aircraft pushes toward larger diameter, more blade area, five-blade work, lower prop rpm or a purpose-designed installation. Therefore the medium core can thermodynamically outrun the 1941–42 propeller package.

### 3.4 E7 large TP

Upstream E7 large central ~24 kg/s is already:

- shaft capability ~4,650–4,780 hp;
- practical prop absorption roughly ~3,600–4,750 hp depending organization/Asai development level.

For 1942 planning, do **not** assume a 4,700 shp serial aircraft engine merely because the core can produce it. A first large-aircraft product should be flat-rated around **~3,500–4,000 shp** unless a suitable very-large propeller/reducer has independently qualified.

This is a heavy transport / patrol / future large-aircraft product, not a normal carrier-engine answer.

## 4. Fuel economy outlook

E7's generation coordinate (~28% cycle comparison) should improve full-power installed shaft efficiency over E6, but the same accounting rules apply.

Use:

- full-power/near-rated BSFC screening: **~0.34–0.36 kg/kWh**;
- long-life derated high-power point: **~0.35–0.38 kg/kWh**;
- part-load cruise: **~0.39–0.46 kg/kWh provisional**, strongly installation/control dependent.

This materially narrows the E5-S-T part-load penalty (~0.47–0.55), but it does **not** automatically beat a mature supercharged piston engine at long-endurance cruise. Therefore gasoline long-range branches can remain rational even after E7 TP becomes available.

The value split remains:

- TP: kerosene, low vibration, power density, low-altitude response, cooling simplification, altitude flat-rating potential;
- piston: excellent mature part-load economy, existing mass production, simple service at non-turbine bases.

## 5. Aircraft consequences

### 5.1 B5N2 / Type 97 carrier attack

No serial E7 retrofit is recommended. By the time E7 lower-medium is ready, B5N2 is a mature older airframe and a 1,750–1,850 shp installation would force propeller, mount, landing-gear, torque, deck-handling and structural work large enough to approach a new aircraft.

Use B5N TP test articles as technology feeders only. **The E7 lower-medium product belongs in B6N/new-build competition.**

### 5.2 B6N Tenzan

This is the largest correction to the previous future outlook.

Do not compare B6N only against E6-S-T 1,300/1,400 hp. At detail-design freeze and pre-first-flight engine selection, include:

- conventional piston ~1,700–1,850 hp class;
- E6-S-T mature 1,300/1,400 hp fallback/test path;
- **E7-M lower-flow ~1,750–1,850 normal / ~1,900–2,000 short** if its 1941H2/1942Q1 flight-release and naval reducer/propeller gates close.

E7-M-LF is therefore a credible **main-production candidate**, not merely an experimental TP. B6NAUDIT1 now supplies the dedicated H->A aircraft outlook: E7-M-LF screens at ~0.56-0.64 t dry package, 3.5-3.6 m four-blade prop, ~520-535 km/h normal max-speed band, ~650-800 km torpedo radius and ~7.5-8.5 min to 5 km. Treat these as future-outlook values pending measured E7 altitude/TBO/carrier qualification.

### 5.3 D3A1 / D4Y branch

No E7-D3A re-engine program. Fixed-gear D3A cannot exploit the power economically.

D4YAUDIT1 now closes the D4Y comparison. The clean Atsuta inline installation is a much harder comparator than a B6N-class radial: the TP is not automatically narrower, and a healthy ~1,400 hp worldline piston D4Y remains ~583-592 km/h class. E7-S-T ~1,425-1,500 shp screens at ~585-598 km/h normal / ~595-607 short, with slightly weaker mission range but better climb/wave-off, cooling simplicity and low vibration.

Therefore E7-S-T is **B+ to A- conditional**, especially attractive as an early fast carrier-reconnaissance / limited fast-strike application, but it does not automatically displace the piston D4Y dive-bomber line. D4Y and B6N use different E7 scale bands; shared naval reducers/props/carrier qualification are the real competition.

### 5.4 G3M / G4M

Earlier no-conversion verdicts stand. An E7 medium retrofit would be a major redesign and still trades against the piston aircraft's core virtue: long cruise endurance.

Purpose-built E7-M/L patrol/attack aircraft remain possible after 1942, but they are a new branch and must compete with:

- piston G4M very-long-range economy;
- G4As high-speed terminal survivability;
- E6/E7 jet/fan reconnaissance and strike systems.

### 5.5 AT-3 / Material No.3 heavy transport

The current E6-M-T first-generation design remains coherent:

- 4 x 2,050–2,100 shp;
- aggregate ~8,200–8,400 shp;
- 52–55 t class.

E7 changes the growth path:

**Option A — four E7 medium engines:**
- flat-rated ~2,300–2,450 shp each;
- aggregate ~9,200–9,800 shp;
- ~10–19% power increase without jumping to the large-core scale;
- preferred for hot/high field performance, 55–60 t growth, or retaining runway targets with more payload.

**Option B — two E7 large engines:**
- aggregate power can match/exceed four E6-M engines, but engine-out asymmetry, huge propeller diameter/blade-area, gearbox and ground-clearance problems make it **not** the preferred first transport application.

Therefore the natural path is **E6-M-T prototype/early aircraft -> E7-M-T growth production**, not an automatic twin-engine E7-L redesign.

### 5.6 P1/P2 and utility aircraft

No upgrade merely because E7 exists. P2 already closes around mature E5-S-T 952–1,000 hp, low wing loading and STOL utility. E7-equivalent manufacturing methods can later improve cost/life, but service-class power remains appropriate.

### 5.7 Rotorcraft

The small helicopter remains E5-S-S-derived 330/360 shp in its own prewar development line. Post-1942 medium rotorcraft already has an E6-S-S derated ~900/1,000 shp path. E7 does not justify skipping the transmission/rotor-head maturity ladder.

## 6. Propeller is now a first-order gate

From E7 onward, the gas generator can exceed the practical aircraft propeller package more easily than before.

Use the following planning rule:

1. close core shaft capability;
2. close reducer torque/TBO;
3. close propeller diameter/blade-area/rpm/ground-clearance;
4. only then publish aircraft shaft rating.

For 1941–42:

- ~1,500 shp: mature 3–4 blade solutions are straightforward;
- ~1,800–2,000 shp: high-power four-blade class is credible and especially suitable for B6N-size aircraft;
- ~2,200 shp: already covered by current high-power propeller school with careful qualification;
- ~2,400–2,650 shp: propeller integration becomes a principal program, not a footnote;
- ~3,500–4,000 shp: large-aircraft-only until very-large/high-blade-count prop systems qualify.

CFRP blade research can contribute fatigue/vibration/weight knowledge but is **not** assumed to magically solve the 1941–42 high-power propeller gate.

## 7. Prewar schedule

Use the same E7 core clock as E7OUTLOOK1, with shaft-product lag separated:

- **1940 H1:** E7 compressor/hot-section rigs; E6 reducer/free-turbine/prop school supplies backend design data;
- **1940 H2:** integrated gas-generator demonstrator; E7-S/E7-M shaft-product layout, reducer and prop matching can be designed in parallel;
- **1941 H1:** full-core ground development; lower-medium aircraft TP bench package and naval installation rigs are rational;
- **1941 H2:** core research/ground qualification target closes; E7 shaft packages may enter full bench/endurance and airframe ground-run qualification;
- **late 1941 / 1942 Q1 earliest:** limited E7 TP flight-release if reducer/prop/TBO gates close;
- **1942 Q1–Q2:** purpose-designed E7 aircraft first-flight window remains consistent.

Thus **E7 TP research can be substantially closed before a historical-date late-1941 Pacific-war expansion**, while serial aircraft adoption remains a 1942+ event unless an individual program earns an earlier flight/service gate.

## 8. Mandatory future TP selection rule

For every 1941–42 piston/TP aircraft decision, compare at least:

1. mature piston baseline;
2. mature lower-E TP/service-equivalent product;
3. newest E-generation product at the appropriate **scale band**, not merely the same old S/M label;
4. propeller/reducer maturity;
5. mission fuel mass at real cruise power;
6. carrier/base fuel logistics;
7. replacement tempo and production yield.

Do not select TP merely to unify fuel. Do not reject TP using the obsolete E5-S-T part-load penalty when an E7 product is the actual candidate.
