# ASAI WORLDLINE — E5-DERIVED E6 FAST HEAVY-BOMBER OUTLOOK — 1938-1941 — V1 E6BOMBER1

**Status:** CURRENT H->A DESIGN / PROCUREMENT OUTLOOK. Combat clock remains 1939-12-31 24:00. No 1940 production, deployment or combat result is pre-recorded.

## 1. Why a dedicated bomber requirement exists

The E5 Twin has already demonstrated that high-speed turbine attack is not a laboratory curiosity:

- by 1938-10-31: **178 attack sorties / ~71 t released**;
- by 1938-12-31: **~135 t cumulative 1938 release**;
- 1939 Q1 alone: **345 attack/fixed-target-interdiction sorties / ~138 t released**;
- Apr 1-May 10 Nanchang sector: **126 fixed-target/route attack sorties / ~50 t released**.

The common E5/E6 Twin is deliberately limited to a ~0.40 t internal mission bay because reconnaissance, command, fighter/high-cover, courier and attack functions share one compact airframe. Once repeated attack use proves valuable, a separate requirement naturally appears: retain turbine arrival/escape speed while trading away universal mission equipment for **Army-heavy-bomber-class internal bomb load**.

`heavy bomber` here follows the contemporary Japanese Army functional sense: a twin-engine bomber carrying roughly the Ki-21/Ki-49 bomb-load class or more. It is not a four-engine strategic bomber claim.

## 2. Requirement chronology — outlook, not future fact

- **1938 H2:** E5 attack sortie growth creates an informal `fast heavy bomber / fast interdiction bomber` requirement.
- **1938 Q4:** after ~135 t cumulative 1938 E5 release, Army technical staff can plausibly request a dedicated 0.8-1.2 t internal-bomb derivative study.
- **1939 Q1:** 345 E5 attack sorties / ~138 t in one quarter justify hardening the requirement around **~1.0-1.2 t normal internal load**, not merely a larger E5 rack.
- **1939 H1-H2:** E6-M propulsion maturity permits a purpose-designed larger twin rather than overloading the common E5/E6 mission airframe.
- **1940 Q1-Q2:** earliest ground article / prototype-completion opportunity if the project was funded in 1939.
- **1940 Q2-Q3:** earliest first-flight/service-development opportunity. This is a gate only; it is not pre-recorded history.
- **1941 H2:** E7 inherited-aft-fan retrofit/growth evaluation becomes plausible if E7 closes on schedule.

## 3. Architecture

Working technical name: **E6-B Twin / high-speed heavy-bomber branch**. Exact Army/Navy service designation remains OPEN until an actual adoption event.

The airframe is **not** a common E6 Twin with more bombs attached. It is a larger derivative using the same propulsion, systems and high-speed release school.

### H->A center architecture

- crew: **3** — pilot; navigator/bombardier; radio/observer;
- propulsion: **2 x E6-M-JF**, ~984 kgf/engine long-life production center;
- wing: straight thin metal wing, **27-29 m2**, center ~28 m2;
- normal loaded mass: **~6.5-6.9 t**, center ~6.7 t;
- heavy/overload planning ceiling: **~7.1-7.3 t** after local gear/brake/wing-root qualification;
- mission-ready zero-fuel center: **~4.15-4.35 t**;
- internal kerosene normal: **~1.3-1.6 t**;
- standard internal bomb load: **~1.0-1.2 t**;
- structural bomb-bay maximum target: **~1.5 t**;
- tricycle retractable gear; prepared-field aircraft;
- no large dorsal/ventral turret installation in the baseline;
- defensive armament kept light and low-drag; heavy forward battery is optional/local, not the normal bomber identity.

The design spends mass saved from the common Twin's fighter battery, interchangeable reconnaissance/courier equipment and broad mission wiring on:

- larger internal bomb bay;
- more internal fuel;
- stronger gear/brakes/wing center section;
- bombardier/navigation installation;
- local protection around crew and critical systems where weight permits.

## 4. Bomb-bay doctrine

Normal design center is **internal carriage**. External conventional bombs are avoided because drag destroys the high-speed-bomber premise.

Representative load families, exact Army/Navy bomb marks local:

- 4 x 250 kg class;
- 2 x 500 kg class where service stores permit;
- 1 x ~800 kg class + small secondary stores where geometry permits;
- mixed 50/100/250 kg classes for airfield, rail, depot and troop-area attack;
- structural overload toward ~1.5 t only with reduced fuel and explicit load sheet.

The existing E5/E6 high-speed release school is inherited. Larger 500-800 kg-class stores require their own release/separation ladder and are not automatically qualified at the common E6 50/100 kg high-speed envelope.

## 5. Performance screening — E6-M-JF baseline

These are H->A aircraft calculations, not flight-test measurements.

| item | working band |
|---|---:|
| sea-level maximum, normal mission | **~620-655 km/h** |
| 5-7 km maximum, normal mission | **~680-710 km/h** |
| light/clean development condition | **~700-720 km/h class** |
| climb to 6 km | **~7.0-8.5 min** |
| standard strike radius, 1.0-1.2 t bombs | **~550-700 km** |
| long-strike radius, ~0.8-1.0 t bombs / more fuel | **~700-850 km** |
| heavy 1.5 t overload radius | **~400-550 km** |
| ferry/light-load range | **~1,800-2,200 km class** |

At ~6.7 t, pair static thrust is ~1.97 tf, giving static T/W ~0.29. Wing loading at 28 m2 is ~239 kg/m2. The aircraft therefore remains a prepared-field high-speed bomber rather than a rough-field E5/P2 substitute.

The straight-wing Mach/compressibility wall is less severe than on the 790 km/h common E6 Twin because the larger bomber's parasite area holds it below that regime. Thus E6 thrust increase converts into useful bomber speed rather than immediately disappearing into M~0.70 drag rise.

## 6. What it is for

Best targets:

- airfields and parked-aircraft areas;
- rail yards, depots and road/rail concentration points;
- ports, warehouses and fuel/logistics installations;
- troop assembly and artillery/support areas;
- fixed operational headquarters and communications nodes.

It is **not** a precision bridge-killer merely because it is fast. Against small point targets or freely maneuvering ships, use dive/torpedo/specialized attack geometry. Level-bombing gains remain primarily target-area concentration, shorter exposure and rapid BDA/retasking.

The operational chain that creates the requirement is:

**E5/E6 reconnaissance -> target folder -> dedicated fast-bomber strike -> E5/E6 BDA -> rapid retask.**

A 12-aircraft E6-B sortie at 1.2 t each carries **~14.4 t**, equivalent in raw released mass to 36 common E5/E6 0.4 t attack sorties, before sortie-rate/survival differences.

## 7. Comparison with existing bomber branches

### vs common E6 Twin

Common E6 remains the better multi-role aircraft. E6-B buys roughly 2.5-3x normal bomb mass at the cost of:

- lower maximum speed;
- shorter role flexibility;
- larger prepared-field footprint;
- higher cost and lower sortie elasticity;
- reduced fighter/recon/courier utility.

### vs Ki-21/Ki-49-class Army heavy bombers

E6-B is the **fast short/medium-range partner**, not an automatic replacement:

- much faster ingress/egress;
- fewer crew and defensive positions;
- comparable or somewhat larger normal bomb load;
- less range/endurance and worse austere-field practicality;
- higher turbine-system cost.

### vs G3M/G4M

The conventional Navy land-attack aircraft remain far better for very-long-range work. E6-B is closer to a high-speed operational strike bomber. Navy interest is plausible, but Navy already has G4As for high-speed torpedo/strike work and G4M for reach; Army sponsorship is therefore the cleaner initial driver.

## 8. Pure-J branch

A pure-J bomber test is technically possible but is not the default. It gains some high-altitude/clean speed while paying range, takeoff and heavy-load acceleration. For a dedicated bomber, JF's low-speed thrust and mission economy dominate.

Treat pure-J E6-B as a development/high-speed comparison article unless a specific interception/penetration requirement appears.

## 9. E7 growth outlook

If E7 closes on the existing schedule, inherited-aft-fan thrust rises to roughly **1,100-1,150 kgf/engine**.

Two rational paths then exist:

### E7 retrofit, same basic airframe

- maximum at 5-7 km: **~715-740 km/h class**;
- 6 km climb: **~6-7 min class**;
- retain ~6.7 t weight and gain speed/climb, or;
- use the thrust margin for heavier protection/fuel/ordnance.

### E7 heavy-growth configuration

After local gear/wing/brake qualification:

- **~7.3-7.6 t** normal heavy design target;
- **~1.3-1.5 t standard bomb load**;
- more internal fuel/protection while retaining approximately E6-B baseline speed and field performance.

Beyond this point a new thin-/light-swept-wing E7 bomber should be compared with continued growth. Do not indefinitely enlarge the E6-derived straight-wing airframe.

## 10. Procurement logic

The requirement is strong enough to justify a prototype/competitive program before 1940, but not to pre-record mass procurement.

Reasonable late-1939 posture:

- dedicated prototype/engineering batch: **2-4 airframes**;
- production tooling study begins if structural/bomb-bay and E6-M-JF tests remain healthy;
- first operational block decision belongs to 1940 replay after flight performance, runway, bombing-system and cost trials.

The project should not consume the entire E6 common-Twin line. Its economic logic is **fewer aircraft delivering more bomb mass per strike while preserving high-speed survivability**, not replacement of every reconnaissance/fighter/courier E6.

## 11. Guards

- no pre-recorded 1940 adoption, production or combat;
- no 2-3 t bomb load on the E6-M pair without a new larger-airframe calculation;
- no claim that speed creates precision level bombing;
- no free combination of maximum bombs + maximum fuel + maximum protection;
- no automatic Navy replacement of G3M/G4M/G4As;
- no indefinite straight-wing growth after E7 makes the airframe the limiting factor.
