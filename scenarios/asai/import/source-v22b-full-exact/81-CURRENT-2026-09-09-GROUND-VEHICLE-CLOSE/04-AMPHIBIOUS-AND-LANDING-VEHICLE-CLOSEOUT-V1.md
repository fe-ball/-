# AMPHIBIOUS AND LANDING VEHICLE CLOSEOUT — V14

## Hard negative guard
- amphibious Chi-Ha: **0**;
- deep-wading Chi-Ha: **0**;
- Chi-Ha WET1 remains rain/splash/electrical/sealing/breather/post-ford reliability for ordinary ~1 m fording.

## Landing-craft doctrine
Normal Shohatsu/Daihatsu-type landing craft remain naturally aspirated diesel main-line products. No blanket turbo or GT conversion.

### Heavy Daihatsu growth
Working low-rate growth branch:
- length ~18–19 m;
- payload ~20–22 t;
- twin ~150–200 hp NA diesels, 300–400 hp total;
- full-load ~8–9 kt;
- reinforced ramp/bottom.

Development clock: 1939 study -> 1940 two prototype/engineering craft -> 1941 four to six pilot craft, for six to eight physical articles total by year-end. This is not yet a standard flotilla.

## Amphibious cargo transport
V14 does not resurrect the later Ka-Tsu name for an early vehicle. Existing Japanese F-B/swamp-vehicle experience is treated as the technical school for tracked soft-ground/water transport. An early cargo-first growth/trial branch may exist, but remains exercise/service-evaluation inventory rather than normal OOB.

For quantity logistics, a future wheeled amphibious-truck branch is preferred over creating a large new tracked fleet unless terrain specifically demands tracks. Asai contribution is sealing, shafts/gears, pumps, electrical waterproofing and QC.

## Ka-Mi
Keep the Navy amphibious-tank path close to the historical clock: design/development in 1941, production gate in 1942. Asai helps seals, welding/QC, pumps, corrosion, electrical and gearing; no automatic engine replacement and no Army Chi-Ha swimmer substitution.
