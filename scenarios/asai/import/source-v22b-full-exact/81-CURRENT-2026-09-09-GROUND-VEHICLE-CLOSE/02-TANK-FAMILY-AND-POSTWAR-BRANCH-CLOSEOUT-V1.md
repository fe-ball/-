# TANK FAMILY AND POST-OPENING BRANCH CLOSEOUT — V14

## Existing main line
### Type 97 Chi-Ha
1938 production-standard worldline baseline remains 15.3–15.6 t, Mitsubishi V12 + Asai low-pressure turbo/intake, 200 hp continuous / 220 hp short, 57 mm low-velocity gun, four crew, two-man turret. Road speed stays broadly ~39–40 km/h unless a separate gearing/rubber gate is paid.

### New-turret Chi-Ha
15.8–16.0 t, 47 mm high-velocity ~800–820 m/s, 200/220 hp, four crew, two-man turret, commander still loads, manual traverse. 1940 is prototype/pilot evaluation; 1941 is first serious series fielding.

### Chi-He
~17.5 t, 240 hp continuous, five crew, three-man turret, 47 mm, 50 mm main frontal hull, radio standard. 1941 is technical completion/development, not ordinary OOB. V14 closes 1941 year-end at two complete vehicles plus two running chassis as a development accounting coordinate.

## 75 mm branches
### Fixed-casemate 75 mm SPG
Chi-Ha second-generation chassis + Type 90 75 mm class, ~16.5 t design combat mass, five crew, ~60 rounds, ~50 mm front / ~25 mm sides, limited traverse. 1940 prototype -> 1941 limited/pilot production. It is **not** automatically counted as a tank regiment vehicle.

### Emergency 75 mm turret tank — 1942+ development branch
This is a fast firepower-upgrade branch using the Chi-He-family 240 hp and 50 mm-class infrastructure. Working envelope: 18.6–19.0 t, five crew, three-man turret, 75 mm ~680–720 m/s integration path. It is distinct from the next-medium program. Formal Army type/year name remains OPEN until the formal designation gate.

## Next-medium research -> development
V14 promotes the formerly conditional 20–22 t technical-test-vehicle branch into actual prewar research, but **not service procurement**.

Research pair through 1941:
- 20–22 t initial mass;
- ballast structure/drivetrain testing to 24–25 t;
- ~1.70 m turret ring;
- five crew / three-man-turret layout studies;
- 75 mm complete-round recoil/loading/integration;
- initial 240 hp vehicle testing, with 27 L V12 ~300 hp continuous / 320 hp short bench-development branch;
- 450 mm track starting point with ~500 mm comparison.

If a T-34/M4-class vehicle becomes a standard—not exceptional—enemy medium threat, this can become a 23–25 t production-development program with 55–60 mm main frontal protection, 30–35 mm sides and 75 mm ~770–800 m/s. Threat information and production date remain HISTORY/DEVELOPMENT gates.

## Heavy-threat responsibility split
KV-class or comparable special heavy armor does not automatically create a Japanese 30 t+ heavy-tank main line. The default response remains responsibility sharing among high-velocity SPG/AT gun, artillery, flank engagement and engineers. A heavy-tank program requires evidence that heavy armor has become a common standard threat.

## GT vehicle branch
The existing ~15 t direct-GT demonstrator remains a research branch. Pacific-war conditions do not justify a normal GT tank main line because dust, part-load economy and fuel/support burdens remain strong. Re-open only for a distinct cold-weather/rapid-start requirement.
