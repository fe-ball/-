### N1K Kyofu

Central 1942 water-fighter concept:

- Kasei 14/15 class.
- **2x2-blade CRP retained**.
- fixed center float + low-drag fixed auxiliary floats.
- retractable auxiliary-float mechanism is not inherited from E15K for the fighter role.
- dedicated water-fighter exhaust/cooling takeoff mode.
- takeoff-specific flap / propeller / hull-attitude integration.

Working performance:

- normal combat mass roughly **3.58–3.62 t**.
- top speed **500–510 km/h**, center about 505 km/h.
- 5 km climb about **5:15–5:30**.
- standard armament initially 20 mm x2 + 7.7 mm x2; later armament evolution remains date-gated.

Water takeoff integration:

- CRP and EX3 are not free speed bonuses; their highest value is low-speed power absorption, torque cancellation, thermal margin and repeatable acceleration through the hump.
- high wing loading means Kyofu may require a longer takeoff distance than the A6M float fighter despite higher power; it reaches the required speed faster and with larger control/thermal margin.
- working standard optimized takeoff distance shadow after flap/attitude/prop/EX3 integration: about **405–430 m** in good still-water conditions; environment dominates exact value.
- wing incidence should be reduced from an overly large fixed incidence toward roughly **3.0–3.25 deg**, with a takeoff flap position around **7–8 deg** as the central concept.
- more aggressive 10–12 deg takeoff flap can be tested for heavy/no-wind cases but should not be standard without qualification.

### Float-fighter exhaust / EX3 rule

A new separate exhaust technology stage is **not** created. Existing EX3 already includes nozzle area, back pressure, jet direction and cooling-outlet integration.

For water aircraft, mission-specific EX3 implementation is allowed:

- low back pressure remains primary at takeoff; sacrificing shaft power to maximize exhaust-jet velocity is usually wrong at low speed.
- exhaust-ejector cooling can be used to sustain takeoff power and reduce dependence on wide-open cooling exits at low ram speed.
- fixed hot exhaust nozzles + variable secondary cooling/ejector flow is preferred over many hot movable nozzle mechanisms.
- fail-safe should bias toward cooling/takeoff-safe state, accepting small top-speed loss on failure.

A6M-derived float fighter should use the simpler version; Kyofu may justify a two-position integrated ejector/cooling system.

### A6M-derived float fighter naming

Historical A6M2-N is "Type 2 Float Fighter". In this worldline, if the current working schedule reaches formal service acceptance in 1941, use **Type 1 Float Fighter** as the worldline designation. Do not mechanically import the historical Type 2 name.

Working speed remains roughly **445–450 km/h**. This was rechecked against the A6M2 -> A6M2-N historical speed ratio and remains plausible.

## 3. Flying-boat consolidation

### H8K1 / H8K2

Do not make H8K a fantasy-speed aircraft. Main worldline effect is water-operation repeatability and usable heavy-weight envelope.

H8K1:

- first flight remains near historical timing (late 1940 / early 1941).
- historical hull spray/porpoising problems still appear; they are identified and iterated faster through instrumented water runs, high-speed imaging and hull/trim/flap data.
- worldline top speed only modestly above historical: roughly **435–440 km/h**.
- standard takeoff procedure likely uses small flap through hump, then larger flap in the late planing regime while holding about a 5-degree hull attitude.
- large gain is lower dispersion of takeoff result, not a miraculous 30 sec -> 15 sec conversion.

H8K2:

- Kasei 22-class high power.
- keep official max takeoff weight near **32.5 t**; do not automatically raise structural max because power increased.
- use excess power to make 32.5 t practical over a wider range of temperature, wind, sea state and payload combinations.
- top-speed shadow about **467–472 km/h**.
- 4-blade quick-feathering propeller is strongly recommended from H8K2 generation; main gain is engine-out drag/survivability rather than headline speed.
- worldline advantage is mission completion, bad-condition takeoff, engine-out return and usable fuel/payload, not inflated bomb load.

### H8K3

Treat as a limited high-speed reconnaissance branch, not universal H8K2 replacement.

- retractable wingtip floats.
- smoothed side gun positions / reduced external drag.
- retractable/low-drag upper gun position may be justified for the reconnaissance branch, but not necessarily for all patrol/attack aircraft.
- top-speed working band **479–485 km/h**; clean reconnaissance upper shadow around **482–487 km/h**.
- 500 km/h on the same Kasei 22 power is not central; it would require implausibly large whole-airframe drag reduction.
- H8K2 remains superior for ordinary patrol/attack/transport/rescue because it avoids the extra mechanisms/weight.

### H9A

Raise importance from "trainer" to **flying-boat test/training system**.

It can cheaply develop:

- takeoff/landing measurement method.
- staged flap procedures.
- attitude cues.
- crew coordination.
- engine-synchronization and EX3 procedures.
- H8K pilot/engineer/navigator/radio-team training.

Do not claim H9A directly scales H8K hull hydrodynamics.

### H10H 14-Shi medium flying boat

Historical plan is four-engine, not twin-engine.

Technical result:

- approximately 18 t, 28 m span, four Kinsei 50-class engines.
- original roughly 472 km/h design value is not obviously fantasy; worldline prototype after refinement can plausibly center around **468–476 km/h**, with 472 km/h a good center.
- 481 km/h is an upper design/test target, not a guaranteed service value.
- high wing loading makes high-speed water run stability the major issue; power-to-weight is strong enough that simple power shortage is not the dominant problem.
- worldline likely completes a prototype that history cancelled at about 85% design completion.

Procurement remains OPEN: prototype success does not imply mass production. Limited high-speed patrol/recon procurement (order-of-tens) is possible but competes with H8K, land patrol aircraft and manpower.

### H7Y

Worldline can technically rescue the aircraft better than history through vibration/structural-mode analysis, improved takeoff-power diesel development and water-run integration.

However:

- extreme range mission remains the fundamental requirement driver.
- worldline result is a viable special ultra-long-range reconnaissance/experiment aircraft, not a normal mass-production combat flying boat.
- central expectation: a few special aircraft, not a fleet type.

## 7. M6A Seiran technical closeout candidate

### 7.1 Common airframe requirement

Keep the strong historical geometry:

- about 3.5 m cylindrical storage envelope.
- about 12.25 m unfolded span / 27 m2 wing class.
- two crew.
- folding/rotating wings and folding tail surfaces.
- removable twin floats.
- 800 kg bomb / roughly 850 kg Type 91 torpedo class.
- compatible with both AM two-aircraft boats and I-400 three-aircraft boats.

No normal in-flight float-jettison mechanism is required for the central production aircraft.

Mission modes:

1. reusable/recovery: floats fitted.
2. aircraft-discard return: no floats; return near submarine and ditch for crew recovery.
3. pure one-way attack: no floats; aircraft recovery not required.

### 7.2 Engine comparison and result

Central engine selection candidate: **E7-M lower-flow turboprop + 3.20–3.25 m high-activity 4-blade constant-speed/feathering propeller**.

Why:

- roughly 1,750–1,850 shp normal, 1,900–2,000 shp short working band.
- ordinary 3.2–3.25 m 4-blade can plausibly absorb the power within the 3.5 m package; CRP is therefore not mandatory.
- turboprop packaging can recover weight from liquid-cooling hardware and reinvest it into folding-joint high-dynamic-pressure strength, naval salt package and reliability.
- kerosene-class aviation fuel is safer in submarine service than aviation gasoline, but still requires a dedicated clean aviation grade/day system; do not mix casually with submarine diesel fuel.
- fast/reliable starting and lower vibration are highly valuable in submarine launch operations.

Fallback hierarchy:

- Atsuta 32: strongest schedule hedge and potentially best cruise economy.
- E7-S-T: small turboprop fallback if E7-M reduction/naval qualification is late but smaller TP package is mature.
- E7-M + small-diameter CRP: comparison test only; promote only if the normal 4-blade fails vibration/power-absorption/diameter gates.

### 7.3 Weight/performance closeout shadow

Central E7-M 4-blade Seiran:

- equipped empty roughly **3.18–3.28 t**.
- standard catapult launch mass **4.60–4.70 t**.
- proof catapult capacity candidate **4.9–5.0 t**.
- floats fitted top speed **510–522 km/h**, center roughly 516–518.
- floats removed top speed **598–612 km/h**, center roughly 605; this is a technical performance shadow, not immunity from late-war fighters.

Heavy-weapon endurance/range must be interpreted carefully:

- earlier 1,150–1,250 km figures should be treated as **total mission range**, not 1,200 km combat radius.
- floats fitted + 800/850 kg class heavy weapon: total mission range roughly **950–1,150 km** working band; practical attack radius roughly **350–450 km**, mission-dependent.
- no-float aircraft-discard return: attack radius roughly **550–700 km** working band.
- pure one-way no-float mode: **1,200–1,600 km** attack reach shadow.

### 7.4 Attack profiles / accuracy working bands

These are planning/Monte-Carlo bands, not guaranteed hit rates.

- 250 kg true dive attack vs large moving ship: roughly **14–25% effective direct-hit per attacking aircraft** after valid attack setup.
- 250 kg vs large stationary target / moored ship: roughly **27–42%**.
- 800 kg limited-dive vs large moving ship: roughly **9–17%**.
- 800 kg limited-dive vs large fixed facility / moored large ship: roughly **19–33%**.
- 800 kg limited-dive vs narrow point target (lock gate / drydock gate class): roughly **9–17% direct hit**, with direct + damaging near-miss band roughly **14–28%**.
- shallow/horizontal 800 kg attack vs narrow point target: roughly **2–6% effective direct hit**.
- Type 91 aerial torpedo vs warned freely maneuvering large ship, single-direction attack: roughly **7–14% effective hit per attacking aircraft**.
- good multi-axis torpedo attack: roughly **13–22%**.
- surprise / anchored / maneuver-constrained target: roughly **38–56%**.

Use a two-level error model in replay: formation/common navigation/weather/target-ID error first, then individual release error. Do not use simple independent binomial math as final combat resolution.
