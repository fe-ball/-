# FORCE / EQUIPMENT STOCK-FLOW AND RECOVERY — V7

## Why this file exists
Earlier dialogue repeatedly used phrases like “heavy-equipment preservation -2–5 pp” at successive Chinese withdrawals. That treatment is **SUPERSEDED** for cumulative accounting. It would overcount the same shrinking equipment stock.

Use category stock-flow instead.

## Chinese equipment: structural facts
Chinese 75mm+ artillery is scarce and difficult to replace quickly. Many divisions have little/no proper divisional artillery; Shanghai concentrated an unusually rich share of independent artillery. Domestic production can replace rifles/MGs/mortars much more readily than modern field/heavy artillery.

Therefore 10 lost 75mm guns can matter greatly even if the number looks small relative to Japanese divisional artillery.

Do not sum these bands with old gate-by-gate “-x pp” numbers.

## Chinese retreat behavior by equipment class
Early withdrawal warning tends to help **artillery, headquarters, hospitals, signal and transport units** more than a walking rifleman, because these units need preparation time to hook tractors/horses, organize ammunition, cross bridges and choose roads.

Thus V7 late-Shanghai re-evaluation is:
- 75/77mm+ guns: early retreat may save **~2–6 more pieces overall** than the older branch, rather than lose extra dozens;
- of this, only ~1–3 plausibly reach the Nanking defense allocation;
- 20/37mm/infantry gun category is close to cancellation, perhaps -3 to +5 pieces versus old branch depending route/air attack;
- MG/mortar category likely modestly better saved with personnel;
- trucks are closer to same or slightly worse because breakdown, congestion and air attack remain;
- large ammunition/supply dumps can be worse preserved because they cannot all move and are more easily identified/abandoned/demolished.

## Capture is not equal to enemy loss
Chinese equipment can be:
1. destroyed in combat;
2. broken and abandoned;
3. deliberately disabled (breech/sight removed, explosive damage);
4. captured repairable by Japan;
5. evacuated without ammunition;
6. gun saved but ammunition/draft animals lost.

Only a fraction of Chinese losses become immediately usable Japanese weapons. Different ammunition standards further reduce the value of captured artillery to Japanese front-line batteries. Vehicles, horses, wagons, fuel, food, engineering material and signal wire can be more immediately useful than a foreign-pattern gun.

## Chinese personnel reuse
Survivors are not automatically preserved in quiet roles. Central Army remnants are commonly used as cadre, mixed with recruits, and reinserted into combat. Specialists are especially valuable as specialists rather than generic riflemen.

Nanking extra escape V7 working:
- actual people: +900–1,800 versus old branch;
- immediate usable CEQ +500–1,000;
- after weeks of collection/reorganization +700–1,300;
- by late Jan ~600–1,200 people plausibly back in organized formations.

## Japanese equipment accounting
Japanese equipment loss does **not** track personnel casualties one-for-one. When Japan holds the battlefield, dead/wounded soldiers' rifles and many damaged guns/vehicles can be recovered. A gun can survive while its trained crew is depleted; a damaged tank can return after recovery; a vehicle that is “out today” is not necessarily permanently lost.

Model usable equipment as:
**inventory × operational readiness × operator/crew availability × ammunition/fuel support × single-item performance**.

Do not stack all percentages multiplicatively across the whole army. Effects are concentrated by mission and bottleneck.

## High-tempo versus rest
During pursuit, better recovery/workshop/turbo transport can produce high-value vehicle-ready +3–6 pp and selected transport/tractor daily work +8–15% or more.

During weeks of rest, the historical army also repairs equipment, so readiness differences compress to ~+2–4 pp while trained-manpower survival persists.
