# Submarine technical and procurement consolidation

## Common principle

Asai-worldline gains are dominated by machinery maturity, repeatability, dive transition measurement, battery service life, noise mapping and maintenance quality. Do not convert these into arbitrary extra knots or crush depth.

## Development sequence

- 1927–29 KD III/IV and Junsen I: essentially historical; only later refit/maintenance/QC benefit.
- 1930–33: torsional vibration, damper tuning, dangerous-rpm logging and failure records improve.
- 1934–37 KD VI: frame weld/piston-rod/corrosion and related machinery problems converge earlier; 23 kt becomes routinely usable by late 1936–37 rather than a fragile trial achievement.
- 1938–41: dive is managed as a measured trajectory; battery separator/support/ventilation/QC improve; own-noise data becomes quiet/avoid-rpm maps on newer boats.
- 1941: underwater-speed next-generation research begins without an operational production boat.

## Internal engineering indices (working, not direct combat multipliers)

Historical same-type/same-time baseline = 100 where applicable.

- HPI High-Power Practicality Index: higher is better.
- MBI Machinery Burden Index: lower is better.
- DTI Dive Transition Index: lower is better.
- BCL battery cycle life.
- ANI Acoustic/Noise Integration: 0–4.

Representative workbands:

- KD III/IV: HPI 100–103; MBI 95–100; DTI 97–100; BCL ~250; ANI 0→1.
- KD V: HPI 102–105; MBI 90–95; DTI 95–98.
- KD VIa/b: early-maturity HPI 115–125; MBI 70–80; DTI about 92–98. Historical practice catches up later; this is not a permanent +25% combat bonus.
- I-6: HPI 108–115; MBI 80–90; DTI 94–98.
- I-7/I-8: HPI 110–120; MBI 75–85; DTI 92–97.
- early A/B/C: HPI 108–115; MBI 80–90; DTI 80–90.
- KD VII: HPI 112–120; MBI 75–85; DTI 85–90; BCL 350–450; ANI 3–4.

## Battery

Historical-style wood separator anchor remains about 250 cycles. Worldline microporous separator + glass mat + QC target is roughly 350–450 cycles. This is a service-life improvement, not a chemistry-capacity miracle.

## Important individual decisions

- I-67 loss on 1940-08-29 remains central if replay reaches that date; Asai does not erase human/training accidents. The event becomes a strong trigger for positive-closure/dive-procedure dissemination.
- Type 2 10-cylinder sea experience begins with I-16 completion in March 1940; do not delay all practical data until I-15.
- **I-38/I-39:** worldline procurement divergence to early B2-like standardization using ~11,000–11,200 hp Type 1A 10-cylinder family rather than automatically repeating 12,400 hp Type 2 arrangement; surface target about 23.0–23.5 kt. Purpose is standardization and machinery burden reduction, not efficiency magic.
- KD VII is the first normal mass-production submarine where worldline design culture is deeply embedded while public headline specs remain close to historical.

## 1941 next-generation underwater-speed research

Working target only:

- ~1,800–1,850 t;
- under 100 m;
- ~21 kt surface / ~12 kt submerged;
- 6 bow tubes / 12 torpedoes;
- no aircraft and no deck gun;
- 360 battery cells;
- 4 × ~1,000 hp electric motors.

Only 1–2 hull-equivalent electrical/yard test sets in 1941–43. No operational 1941 advanced boat.

## Submarine aircraft

- E6Y: almost historical; too early for large Asai effect.
- E9W: already highly optimized historically; worldline gains are fixtures/connectors/corrosion/repeatability.
- E14Y: meaningful difference in weight budget, storage envelope, launcher load, float stability and environmental sealing. Initial launch prep 8–12 min, mature 5–7 min working band; recovery remains slow/dangerous.
