# Submarine Type 95 / fire-control closeout

## Weapon body

Type 95 remains historically extraordinary. Do not increase headline speed/range/warhead merely because Asai exists. Worldline body gains are common-component repeatability: gyro calibration, depth control, steering, oxygen-system cleanliness, launch reliability and lot variance.

Historical-performance anchor remains approximately:

- Model 1 warhead ~405 kg;
- ~49–51 kt / 9 km;
- ~45–47 kt / 12 km.

## Japanese historical fire-control anchor

Large Japanese submarines already possessed an angle-solving system accepting torpedo speed, target speed, spread, target angle, range, own speed, relative bearing and time. Periscope bearing and gyro-setting transmission could be electrical. The important limitation is that the system did not continuously integrate target position in the US TDC sense.

## Worldline FCS tiers

### FCS1
Calibration/tables/alignment improvements only. No position keeper.

### FCS2
Standardized plot, transmission, spread control and rapid reset. Better solution workflow but still not a continuous target-motion computer.

### FCS3
Manual range/speed/AOB input + electric bearing + mechanical continuous target-motion position keeper + existing gyro-angle solver. Simpler than US TDC and does not imply automatic fire.

I-38/I-39 are designed around FCS3 integration.

Working indices, not hit-rate multipliers:

- FCS1: GSI/SLI/TMI/FCI = historical baseline 100.
- FCS2: GSI 115–125; SLI 80–90 (lower better); TMI 110–120; FCI 115–130.
- FCS3: GSI 125–140; SLI 60–75; TMI 140–165; FCI 135–155.

## Practical firing range

- FCS1/2: 1.5–3 km main; 3–4 km normal; 4–6 km conditional.
- FCS3: 2–4 km high-quality; 4–6 km practical; 6–8 km conditional for large targets/salvo use.
- 9–12 km is not normal precision-shot doctrine merely because the torpedo can run that far.

At 9 km / 50 kt time of flight is about 5.8 min; at 12 km / 46 kt about 8.5 min. Small target-speed/heading errors become hundreds of meters at these times, so solution continuity and uncertainty-band salvo planning matter more than raw torpedo range.

## Hydrophone use

Hydrophone bearing / echo-ranging information can be manually fed into fire-control plotting. Worldline quiet-rpm maps make sound especially useful for maintaining continuity while the periscope is down. Do not turn this into routine blind acoustic torpedo firing before evidence supports it.
