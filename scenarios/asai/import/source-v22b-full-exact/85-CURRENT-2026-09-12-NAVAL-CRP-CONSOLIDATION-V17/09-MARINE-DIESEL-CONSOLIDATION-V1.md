# Marine diesel consolidation

## Industrial ownership

Japan already possesses serious large-marine-diesel expertise through Navy technical organizations and firms such as Mitsubishi, Kawasaki/MAN and Mitsui/B&W relationships. Asai does not teach Japan diesel fundamentals.

Asai contribution is narrower:

- exhaust turbo machinery / turbo-scavenge interface;
- thermal/vibration/combustion measurement;
- failure statistics;
- tolerances, lot tracking and gauges;
- materials/QC;
- fuel ignition/water/sulfur/wax characterization.

Large two-stroke diesels require scavenging integration; do not model turbocharging as a bolt-on +20% power switch.

## Taigei / Type 11

Historical initial machinery trouble remains an anchor. Worldline convergence is earlier through ring/liner/combustion/fastener/cooling diagnosis. Central 1936–37 practical band for four engines remains roughly 16–18k hp. Hull/welding/stability issues are separate and are not erased.

Because Taigei becomes a more useful diesel submarine tender, later conversion to Ryuho has a somewhat larger opportunity cost. Original diesel machinery remains underpowered for a useful carrier, so steam replacement remains plausible/correct.

## Type 13 / Yamato / Nisshin

Historical Type 13 no.6 test is not treated as a failed concept: roughly 4,800 PS, 350 rpm, 144-hour continuous trial, with issues such as smoke, ring failure and wear but also a real successful endurance run.

Worldline QC/measurement reduces those technical issues and Type 11 stigma, but cannot create thousands of hours of sea service before they occur.

Therefore Yamato remains all-steam: diesel is judged one ship too early for a nation-critical battleship, not fundamentally impossible.

Nisshin is the natural qualification ship for the Type 13 family; its main purpose becomes accumulating trustworthy sea hours.

## Taiho

No current canon requires an individual diesel-Taiho branch. Full-diesel Taiho remains unattractive because carrier-scale ~160k shp would require too many medium engines and excessive machinery/exhaust/cooling complexity. Mixed diesel+steam is technically more plausible than once thought but the first Taiho remains steam. Later modified Taiho/G-15 designs can reconsider after Nisshin sea service.
