## Major V17 closures

### Submarine system

- Prewar headline displacement/speed/depth numbers remain largely historical.
- Asai-derived effects are reliability, repeatability, maintenance, dive transition, battery life/QC and acoustic/noise mapping rather than magical speed/depth.
- KD VI machinery maturity converges earlier; Japanese submarine design culture becomes more instrumented and acceptance-test driven.
- Type A/B/C retain historical basic architecture; Type A = command/air recon, Type B = mass long-range air recon, Type C = pure attack tendency.
- **I-38 / I-39 are the principal procurement divergence:** early B2-like boats using the lower-output, more standardized Type 1A 10-cylinder family rather than simply repeating the B1 Type 2 10-cylinder arrangement.
- 1941 next-generation underwater-speed work is research/prototype infrastructure only; no operational Type-XXI-like boat appears.

### Torpedo employment system

- Type 93, Type 95 and Type 91 headline speed/range/warhead are not boosted merely because Asai exists.
- Common improvement is gyro/depth/steering repeatability, lot/QC discipline and weapon-employment integration.
- Submarine fire control is divided into FCS1 / FCS2 / FCS3; FCS3 adds a mechanical continuous target-motion position keeper to existing Japanese angle/gyro solution practice but remains simpler than the US TDC.
- Type 91 aerial torpedo high-speed qualification remains the V10 baseline; G4As high-speed torpedo attack is a platform/release-envelope result, not a new torpedo-range miracle.
- Surface Type 93 effectiveness is treated as torpedo + launcher/reload + fire control + night optics/comms + formation synchronization, not as a body-performance uplift.

### Naval fleet architecture

- Existing large combatants retain historical plant type unless a specific conversion exists in current canon.
- No automatic extra front-line hull count from Asai computation/productivity gains.
- Low-speed merchant carrier GT up-power remains rejected pre-1941-12-08.
- Shadow-carrier value is preserved through historical conversion options and aviation-logistics roles, not by turning slow ships into fleet carriers.
- The strongest remaining fleet-wide leverage is in auxiliaries, repair, aviation support and replenishment rather than headline combatant speed.

### Auxiliary and support ships

- **Akashi:** high-value forward industrial/QC node. Same basic ship/equipment; improved diagnosis, measurement, repair repeatability and reduced rework. No automatic second Akashi.
- **Mizuho:** 11-go-family chronic machinery trouble is materially reduced by already-closed diesel maturity. Initial defects remain possible; prolonged historical-type speed restriction is not the central branch.
- **Underway replenishment:** hardware integration is substantially easier; doctrine/crew experience remains a separate gate. Better pumps/hoses/valves do not create trained station-keeping crews automatically.
- **Nisshin:** Type 13 machinery risk is lower, but completion timing remains gated by outfitting/role changes including midget-submarine support; no automatic 1941 commission.
- **Taiho:** first ship remains steam. A dedicated aviation-fuel / ventilation / damage-control architecture audit is now high priority; do not pre-record survival in any later battle.
- **Oyodo/E15K:** requirement and aircraft integration must be reconciled after the CRP common audit; no automatic simplification of E15K.

### Contra-rotating propeller (CRP) common technology

- CRP is reopened as a common Japanese high-power propeller technology, not an E15K-only oddity.
- Asai is **not** the ruler of the national propeller technology pool. Sumitomo, engine makers, airframe firms and Navy technical organizations remain primary product/acceptance actors.
- Asai is a strong cross-domain contributor because its own turboprop roadmap needs future high-power propeller architectures and because it is unusually strong in gears, bearings, lubrication, torsional measurement, hydraulics, balancing, gauges and failure classification.
- Asai research begins as a turboprop-backend option study before E15K. The 1939 14-Shi E15K requirement is the first major aircraft project that gives that research an immediate customer and drives integration with Sumitomo/Mitsubishi/Kawanishi/Navy actors.
- CRP benefits are not a giant free top-speed multiplier. Principal benefits are torque cancellation, greater power absorption at constrained diameter/tip speed, and partial swirl recovery.
- CRP-specific system risks remain: coaxial shafts, reversing gears, high-load bearings, seals, oil supply/aeration, dual pitch control, overspeed, torsional modes, blade matching and salt-water maintenance.
- Existing V8 rule that E15K CRP likely falls away and N1K starts biased toward single rotation is **reopened**. New rule: E15K CRP qualification outcome gates N1K adoption.
