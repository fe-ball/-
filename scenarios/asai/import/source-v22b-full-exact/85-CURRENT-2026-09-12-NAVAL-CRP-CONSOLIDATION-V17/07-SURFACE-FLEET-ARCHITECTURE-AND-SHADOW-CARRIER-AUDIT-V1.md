# Surface fleet architecture and shadow-carrier audit

## General fleet rule

The largest V17 conclusion is negative: do not create a visibly futuristic 1940 fleet. Existing hull histories, major rebuilds, plant types and yard bottlenecks remain close to historical unless an explicit current file changes them.

### Battleships

Kongo/Nagato/Fuso/Ise modernization history remains largely historical. Asai effects sit in machinery reliability, QC, electrical/fire-control integration and acceptance rather than new speed/armor architecture. Yamato remains steam. Better Type 13 diesel prospects make the decision closer, but do not manufacture sea-service history.

### Fleet carriers

Akagi/Kaga/Soryu/Hiryu and Shokaku/Zuikaku retain basic historical hull/steam architecture. The main worldline effect is machinery/electrical/aviation-logistics readiness rather than extra hull count. No prewar fleet-carrier GT retrofit.

### Heavy cruisers

Hull/refit history remains historical; later rebuilds show cleaner electrical/FCS/data integration. Yard congestion remains binding.

### Light cruisers

Most old cruisers remain old. 1941 Oi/Kitakami conversions are real refit windows. Agano/Oyodo are more interesting because their design phase falls inside mature worldline integration; dedicated audit still required where not closed.

### Destroyers

Fubuki → Hatsuharu/Tomozuru/Fourth Fleet → Shiratsuyu → Asashio → Kagero → Yugumo historical design-learning sequence remains. Worldline computation does not magically advance completion dates. Later Akizuki and Shimakaze are high-sensitivity design-generation candidates, not 1940 combat additions.

## Shadow-carrier layers

### Military auxiliaries convertible to carriers

- Takasaki → Zuiho: conversion already underway; near-term real carrier rather than speculative shadow hull.
- Tsurugizaki → Shoho: next conversion line.
- Taigei → Ryuho: later; higher opportunity cost in worldline because submarine-tender/diesel utility is greater.
- Chitose/Chiyoda: conversion options remain but their seaplane/special-support roles are themselves more valuable, so no automatic early carrier conversion.

### Merchant liners with conversion option

- Kashiwara Maru / Izumo Maru → Junyo/Hiyo: retain at least historical requisition/conversion timing.
- Nitta Maru class → Taiyo/Unyo/Chuyo: low-speed aviation logistics/training/escort role is important; do not treat as fleet carriers.

### Aircraft transports / special merchant ships

Aircraft/engine/personnel transport capacity forms a shadow aviation-logistics fleet even without flight decks. Worldline high-performance aviation makes this layer more valuable.

## Explicit rejected branch

Old pre-V14 ideas that GT-up-powered low-speed merchant carriers and inflated offensive-carrier counts are not restored. V14 rejection remains binding.
