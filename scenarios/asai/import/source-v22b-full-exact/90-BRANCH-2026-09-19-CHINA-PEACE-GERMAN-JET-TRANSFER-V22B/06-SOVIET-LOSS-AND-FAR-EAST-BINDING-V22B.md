# V22B — SOVIET LOSSES AND FAR EAST BINDING

## 1. Threat-perception mechanism

The Soviet Far East effect is not simply "Japan looks scary, add X divisions."

The intelligence pattern is cumulative:

- 1939: Nomonhan-era E5 Twin sightings/records;
- 1940–41: evidence of German interest in/acquisition of Japanese aviation technology;
- 1941–43: unexpectedly rapid German jet maturity;
- ~1942: public Japanese government/civil turbine transports derived from aircraft families visibly related to large military twins;
- continued sightings of newer-engine Japanese variants in/around Manchuria.

The key Soviet inference is:

> technology seen as secret and exceptional in 1939 may have become a public/civil generation by 1942; therefore the current Japanese military frontier is higher and poorly bounded.

## 2. What remains in the Far East

The branch effect should be modeled more through **quality retention** than a gigantic raw headcount increase.

Hard-to-release categories include:

- modern fighter units;
- AA artillery;
- radar/early-warning/communications;
- airfield engineers;
- bridge/rail engineers;
- mobile armored/mechanized reserves;
- skilled maintainers;
- rail/security units protecting Trans-Siberian vulnerability.

Working branch year-end 1944 Far East strength can sit roughly in the high-800k to high-900k range, but the more important delta is that better units and aircraft cannot be stripped westward.

## 3. Westward opportunity cost

Relative to the V21 center branch, cumulative additional westward denial by 1944 can be thought of as roughly:

- several division-equivalents, growing toward ~5–8 division-equivalents in late-1944 planning terms;
- ~250–450 modern aircraft-equivalent retention in the Far East in the stronger-warning periods;
- meaningful AA, communications, engineering and air-base resources.

Do not assume every withheld division would otherwise have gone to Bagration. The effect is distributed across Stavka reserve, Ukraine, central front, replacement pools and air defense.

## 4. Casualty semantics

A stronger Germany changes losses in both directions:

- successful German mobile defense raises Soviet losses;
- avoided German encirclements reduce German irreversible losses;
- early German withdrawals can also reduce Soviet losses because the Red Army does not have to destroy a trapped formation;
- therefore "Germany stronger = USSR always loses more in every battle" is false.

The long-run branch effect is nevertheless adverse to Soviet manpower quality because repeated high-cost advances consume:

- veteran infantry;
- tank crews;
- company/battalion commanders;
- artillery observers;
- combat engineers;
- pilots and flight leaders;
- maintainers and signals personnel.

Weapons can be replaced faster than experience.

## 5. 1944 year-end cumulative working delta

Relative to historical USSR, after all branch interactions through 1944:

- additional Soviet irreversible loss: roughly +1.1 to +1.55 million working band;
- additional death/war-wound-death component: roughly +0.7 to +1.0 million class;
- additional total wounded/sick/temporary loss events: roughly +2.0 to +3.0 million class;
- additional tank/SPG loss: roughly +6,000 to +8,000 class;
- additional combat-aircraft loss: roughly +4,000 to +5,500 class.

These are scenario accounting bands, not archival statistics.

The corresponding 1945 Soviet front-line shortage is smaller than the cumulative casualty number because the USSR mobilizes replacements. The deeper cost is lower cadre/crew quality and a thinner second echelon.

## 6. German survival effect

Relative to history, German irreversible losses avoided by 1944 year-end can plausibly reach roughly ~1.2–1.6 million in the broad branch environment, but **do not attribute all of this to E5/E6 transfer**. It includes:

- V21 no-Normandy/no-US-bombing effects;
- earlier/cleaner withdrawals;
- smaller Korsun/Hube/Crimea disasters;
- reduced Bagration encirclement;
- healthier Axis political/military situation.

V22B adds a meaningful share through reconnaissance, airpower maturity and improved service integration.
