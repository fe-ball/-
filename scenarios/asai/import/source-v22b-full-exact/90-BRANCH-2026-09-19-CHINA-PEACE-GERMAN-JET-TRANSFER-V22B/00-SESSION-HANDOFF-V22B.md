# V22B SESSION HANDOFF — CHINA-PEACE / GERMAN-JET-TRANSFER BRANCH

**Status:** BRANCH WORKING-CLOSED THROUGH 1944 YEAR-END  
**Parent:** V21 China-peace / no-Pacific-War line  
**Next clock:** 1945 entry; German strategic behavior and Soviet response OPEN.

## 1. Why this branch exists

The prior V21 branch had already made Germany materially stronger than history because:

- no US-German war;
- no Normandy;
- no US daylight strategic bombing campaign;
- no Big Week;
- no historical Oil Campaign;
- Ploiesti and German transport/industrial systems are materially healthier;
- Germany avoids several historical political/military cascades in Italy and the Balkans;
- Japan remains an armed-neutral great power and continues to pin Soviet Far East forces.

V22B adds a new upstream cause that had previously been under-modeled: **serious German acquisition of Japanese mature turbine-aircraft technology beginning in 1940**.

The key correction is that Germany would not rationally buy only an engine as a curiosity. If German decision-makers conclude that the Japanese E5 Twin and its engine family are reproducible industrial products, the natural acquisition target is a complete service/production package.

## 2. Accepted branch decisions

### 2.1 German recognition timeline

- 1938 H2–1939 H1: fragmentary reports of Japanese operational turbine twins.
- 1939 spring–summer: Luftwaffe attache Wolfgang von Gronau and technical channels verify that E5 is not a one-off experiment but a repeatedly operated military aircraft family.
- 1939 H2: German technical review increasingly concludes that Japan has solved an industrialization/maintenance problem Germany is still solving.
- Aug 1939 German-Soviet pact temporarily damages political trust at exactly the moment E6 becomes visible.
- late 1939–1940 H1: RLM/Udet/Milch/Goring-level acquisition pressure grows.
- 1940 spring–summer: German perception changes from "interesting foreign jet" to "state-level acquisition worth paying for."
- 1940 autumn: formal state-to-state package negotiation becomes natural under the political cover/institutional framework of renewed German-Japanese cooperation.

### 2.2 Asai position

Asai Iwao does not obstruct the state decision:

> If the government judges transfer to be in the national interest, Asai will cooperate. However, the assets being transferred are company property, production know-how, engineer labor, tooling and intellectual capital; therefore the Japanese government must compensate Asai domestically.

This makes the arrangement easier, not harder. The preferred structure is:

`Asai -> Japanese government -> German government / designated German firms`

rather than a sensitive direct private sale.

### 2.3 1940 first agreement — E5 FULL PACKAGE

Branch-local working decision: Germany obtains a full E5 production/service license package, including:

- E5-J and E5-JF production rights;
- E5 Twin airframe production rights;
- manufacturing drawings;
- process specifications;
- tooling/gauges;

- balance/rotordynamics/NDT/inspection methods;

Initial physical transfer before the Soviet route closes can plausibly include:

- 4–6 Japanese reference aircraft;

- 20–30 E5-J/JF engines;
- tooling, gauges, samples, records and a Japanese technical detachment.

Exact legal text and exact shipment serial numbers remain OPEN.

These are functional assignments, not a claim that every named company receives unrestricted access to all Japanese secrets.

## 3. E5-D — Germanized E5 Twin

### 3.1 Character

E5-D is not an inferior bootleg and not an instantly superior German remake.

The first German-built machines combine:

- Japanese mature E5 architecture;
- German instruments, FuG radio, oxygen, weapons and optical/photo equipment;
- German production/material substitutions;
- initially conservative overhaul limits.

Performance remains broadly comparable to late Japanese E5 service aircraft. German equipment may improve mission capability while weight/material substitutions slightly degrade raw margins.

### 3.2 Engine-life semantics

Japanese E5 quantity/low-temperature product has mature core-life coordinates in the hundreds of hours. German production initially does not inherit the full statistical confidence of Japanese factories.

Therefore:

- early German forced-removal/OH intervals: roughly 100–150 h class;
- later 1942 maturation: roughly 180–250 h class;
- mature German E5-D line can approach roughly 250–350 h service-removal intervals depending duty.

This is not "the engine dies at 100 h." Germany deliberately removes engines early, tears them down, learns its local failure distributions, and widens intervals. This becomes a major institutional preparation for later Jumo 004 operation.

### 3.3 Mission differentiation

By 1942 the German E5-D family plausibly separates into:

- `E5-D/R`: high-speed reconnaissance;
- `E5-D/K`: command, courier, conversion/training and high-value rapid transport;
- `E5-D/N`: radar/night-fighter and all-weather development;
- daytime heavy-fighter trials exist but do not become the dominant production purpose.

By 1944 E5-D is no longer a first-line high-speed reconnaissance aircraft. It transitions toward training, rear-area reconnaissance, liaison, experimental and secondary night/all-weather tasks.

## 4. Feedback loop and E6-D

German mass/service operation generates real field feedback:

- landing gear/brake durability;
- mud/snow/ice protection;
- cold starting;
- canopy and camera-window anti-fog/heat;
- rapid engine/nacelle disconnection;
- wiring/fuel-line standardization;
- access-panel placement;
- heavy radio/radar electrical load;
- photo-mount vibration and heating;
- high-sortie maintenance practices.

These observations travel back to Japan through engineers, documents and later submarine/high-value communication channels. Japan incorporates useful lessons into later E6 service blocks where technically justified.

When Germany later receives E6 technology, the result is therefore a **back-crossed derivative**:

`Japanese E5 -> German E5-D -> returned field lessons -> later Japanese E6 block -> German E6-D`

E6-D is not merely an E6 with German markings.

Working mission identities:

- `E6-D/R`: long-range high-speed reconnaissance;
- `E6-D/N`: two-seat radar/night/all-weather fighter;
- command/high-speed transport/mission variants as needed.

German military equipment generally pushes mass upward, while aerodynamic/detail integration recovers part of the penalty. A branch-local ~780–800 km/h class for mission-equipped E6-D high-speed variants is a reasonable working band, not a universal placard speed.

## 5. Indigenous German jets remain German

Do not convert German history into "all German jets are Japanese copies."

- Me 262 remains a German design whose pre-existing project predates this transfer.
- Ar 234 remains a German design.
- Jumo 004/BMW 003 remain German programs.

Japanese transfer accelerates **maturity and service integration**, especially:

- compressor/rotor vibration diagnosis;
- hot-section cooling/material trade logic;
- balancing;
- bearings/seals;
- inspection/NDT;
- product-life accounting;
- production QC;
- fuel/start/fire/ground-service practice;
- engine exchange and scheduled removal;
- pilot/maintainer jet conversion;
- reconnaissance-system integration.

Thus the branch effect is larger than merely moving a first-flight date left.

- 1944: E6-D enters meaningful serial production; E5-D shifts increasingly to secondary/training roles; Me 262/Ar 234/E6-D form a multi-family jet ecosystem.

## 7. 1944 production working bands

- Me 262 family: ~1,700–2,300 produced in 1944;

These numbers only work because this branch simultaneously retains:

- healthier factories;

- no historical Big Week;
- no historical Oil Campaign;
- healthier training/fuel availability;
- several years of jet-service institutional experience.

## 8. Japan public-visibility / Soviet threat reassessment

By ~1942 Japan is plausibly publicizing government/high-speed civil turbine transports derived from larger military families. The exact airline naming is flavor, not strategic canon. The important intelligence signal is:

> a technology observed as unusual military equipment at Nomonhan in 1939 is already appearing in government/civil transport service by 1942.

Combined with German acquisition and rapid German jet maturation, this allows Soviet intelligence to infer that the E5-era system is not Japan's current frontier.

Consequences:

- Soviet Far East retention remains quantitatively large and becomes qualitatively more modern;
- modern fighters, AA, communications, engineers, airfield units, and mobile reserves are harder to strip westward;
- the cost to the Western Front is larger than the raw headcount difference.

## 9. Japan entry logic

Japanese participation in the anti-Soviet war becomes more plausible than in the original V21 because Japan is now a real stakeholder in German survival. However, Japanese armed neutrality still has enormous strategic value.

The branch center through 1944 remains **non-entry** because:

- the neutrality treaty remains valuable;
- breaking it damages Japanese diplomatic credibility after the China peace;
- war risks trade with the US/UK/Netherlands;
- Japan already pins Soviet forces without taking combat losses;
- Soviet Far East defenses remain non-trivial;
- Germany is strong, but not clearly about to destroy the USSR.

Japan nevertheless raises real northern preparedness in 1943–44 and keeps entry as a live gate rather than a rhetorical option.

## 10. Current handoff point

By 1944 year-end, the dominant open question is no longer simply whether Germany can keep retreating successfully.

Germany now possesses:

- materially more surviving manpower/cadres;
- larger usable armored reserves;
- much healthier Luftwaffe, including mature multi-family jet operations;
- healthier fuel and transport;
- no historical western strategic-bombing destruction;
- no Normandy-scale western ground drain;
- a front that is farther east than history;
- Soviet forces that have suffered additional losses and remain burdened by Japan in the Far East.

The next replay must therefore ask whether Germany continues pure elastic retreat in 1945, or attempts a limited operational/strategic offensive when Soviet posture and local force ratios permit.
