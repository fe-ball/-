# V22B CHRONOLOGY — E5/E6 TRANSFER AND GERMAN JET SYSTEM, 1940–1944

## 1940

### Spring–summer
German technical and air-attache reporting converges on three judgments:

1. Japanese turbine flight is physically credible and reproducible, not propaganda.
2. E5 is already beyond prototype status; the key lead is service maturity, not merely thrust.
3. E6 implies that E5 may already be a transferable old generation rather than Japan's frontier.

### Autumn
State-level acquisition negotiations begin. German demand naturally opens high (E6), while Japan protects current-front technology and offers E5 as a complete licensed generation.

Working settlement: E5 full package for a broad German counter-transfer package in submarine, radar, optics, torpedo/fire-control, chemistry, machine tools and technical access.

## 1941

### H1
- reference E5 aircraft/engines and technical material arrive before the Soviet route closes;
- German production/integration work begins;
- first German-assembled / Germanized E5-D flights occur;
- German Jumo/BMW groups study not only hardware but life, failure, inspection and production records.

### H2
- Barbarossa does not receive a large combat-jet shock; E5-D numbers are still small;
- E5-D/R begins limited operational reconnaissance and E5-D/K command/courier activity;
- first Eastern winter produces valuable cold/mud/runway/maintenance feedback;
- Jumo 004/BMW 003 development benefits mainly through faster diagnosis and fewer sequential dead ends.

## 1942

### H1
- E5-D becomes a recurring specialist system, not an experimental novelty;
- German engine-removal/OH intervals remain more conservative than mature Japanese intervals, but aircraft availability can still be strong because swap/repair practice is institutionalized;
- Me 262 full-jet testing moves earlier; exact branch flight date remains local;
- Japanese field-feedback loop receives German E5-D experience.

### H2
- E5-D/R improves Case Blue reconnaissance but does not cure German strategic overreach;
- Uranus is not prevented; German warning quality and local preparations improve modestly;
- Winter Storm / breakout branch performs better than V21 base: roughly 90–120k 6th Army personnel escaping is a working branch range, not a locked exact count;
- Germany pushes for E6 access;
- Japan's public military/civil turbine visibility becomes a Soviet threat-assessment accelerant.

## 1943

### H1
- Me 262 moves through practical service testing earlier than history;
- Ar 234-type dedicated jet reconnaissance moves into useful trials earlier;
- E5-D is by now a mature training/recon/liaison platform;
- high-value submarine/technical exchange replaces the lost trans-Siberian route.

### Summer — Kursk
- E5-D/Ar 234 reconnaissance and early Me 262 employment improve German local air and information conditions;
- German southern wing performs better and holds Prokhorovka locally for several days;
- Citadel still fails strategically because the northern wing, Soviet depth, and Kutuzov remain decisive;
- the important branch result is lower German mobile-force attrition and higher Soviet cost, not a German strategic victory.

### August–September
- German counterattacks around Bogodukhov become more effective because reconnaissance + intact mobile reserves + better air cover interact;
- Soviet armored spearheads can suffer temporary semi-envelopment and higher vehicle abandonment;
- Kharkov is still abandoned, but later and more deliberately;
- Tokyo raises northern readiness but does not enter the war.

### H2 — E6-D
- E6 technology arrives in enough detail for German integration work;
- German E6-D first-flight / integration-test center is around Q3;
- Q4 service evaluation begins;
- E6-D reflects two-way evolution: Japanese E6 lineage plus some returned German E5-D field lessons plus German equipment/production practice.

### Autumn–winter — Dnieper/Kiev
- Soviet crossings persist; Germany cannot seal the entire Dnieper;
- better reconnaissance materially reduces surprise around Lyutezh;
- Kiev does not fall on 6 Nov 1943;
- repeated Soviet pressure ultimately forces planned German evacuation around early Jan 1944.

## 1944

### Q1 — Korsun / Hube
- because Kiev fell later and German withdrawals occur earlier, the historical Korsun pocket is sharply reduced or never forms at historical scale;
- temporary small enclosures/corridor battles can occur, but several German divisions are not destroyed as in history;
- Hube/1st Panzer Army crisis is reduced toward semi-envelopment with earlier withdrawal, preserving heavy equipment and cadres.

### Q1/Q2 — Crimea
- branch learning makes a planned evacuation more politically achievable;
- nonessential personnel and equipment are withdrawn earlier;
- a large share of German/Romanian manpower escapes before the historical final disaster;
- the USSR also frees Crimea forces earlier, preventing this from being a pure Axis gain.

### June–August — Bagration
- Soviet first blow remains extremely powerful;
- German front lines are broken and Belarus is lost;
- unlike history, AG Center possesses meaningful mobile reserves and much better air reconnaissance;
- staged withdrawal begins earlier; Vitebsk/Orsha/Mogilev/Bobruisk losses occur but mass encirclement of the army group is avoided;
- Minsk is abandoned around end-Jun / early-Jul working window;
- subsequent Soviet pursuit meets a coherent mobile defense rather than open space.

### July–October
- Soviet approach to Warsaw is delayed; historical 1 Aug Warsaw Uprising chain is no longer automatic;
- Lvov falls, but Soviet armored exploitation toward the Vistula is slower and more costly;
- Baranow/Sandomierz bridgehead forms but remains smaller and is later compressed;
- Baltic land corridor lasts longer; more German formations escape before Courland closes;
- Romania does not suffer historical Aug 1944 state collapse; Axis line contracts toward Focsani/Carpathian defenses.

### Year-end
The USSR has won large territories, but Germany has repeatedly traded land for army survival. The strategic question for 1945 is whether that remains optimal when Germany now possesses substantial manpower, armor, fuel, airpower and mature jets while Soviet forces are more worn and Japan still pins the Far East.
