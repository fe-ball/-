# V22B — E5-D / E6-D / GERMAN JET FAMILY AND 1944 OOB WORKING BANDS

## 1. Family roles by 1944

| Family | 1944 role | Branch interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| E5-D/R/K/N | training, rear reconnaissance, liaison, experimental/night secondary work | mature first-generation licensed family; first-line speed no longer exceptional |
| E6-D/R | long-range high-speed reconnaissance | branch-specific day-to-day operational ISR jet |
| E6-D/N | two-seat radar/night/all-weather fighter | uses twin layout, electrical capacity and mission volume |
| Me 262 | high-speed fighter/interceptor | indigenous German combat jet whose maturation is accelerated by E5-derived methods/service culture |
| Ar 234 | specialized high-speed reconnaissance/light-bomber family | indigenous German design; earlier and more practical because engine/service bottlenecks are reduced |
| Bf 109 / Fw 190 | numerical fighter backbone | still produces most routine sorties and kills |

## 2. 1944 annual German jet production — branch working bands

- Me 262 family: 1,700–2,300;
- E6-D family: 500–800;
- Ar 234 family: 250–450;
- E5-D new/rebuilt: 100–180;
- other jet prototypes/low-rate types: limited, do not inflate into mass Luft '46.

## 3. Bagration-area immediate airpower, 22 Jun 1944 — working bands

Directly supportable / immediately available around AG Center, not total German inventory:

- Me 262: 80–130 combat-ready;
- E6-D: 35–60;
- Ar 234: 15–30;
- E5-D and secondary jets: 20–30;
- Bf 109: ~300–400;
- Fw 190 A/F/G: ~250–350;
- Ju 87G / Hs 129 and dedicated antitank types: ~60–100;
- Ju 88 and other bomber/recon/night assets: ~120–180.

Total immediately usable/supportable aviation is much larger than the jet subtotal. Jets modify the ecosystem; they do not replace conventional aviation.

## 4. Jet effect by role

### Me 262
Direct value:
- intercept Pe-2, reconnaissance and high-value Soviet formations;
- force Soviet fighter escorts upward/away from Il-2 support;
- create short local windows of air denial.

Indirect value:
- allows Bf 109/Fw 190 to concentrate on conventional fighter/attack tasks.

### E6-D / Ar 234
Direct kills are not the metric.

Value:
- repeated high-speed photography;
- detect mobile-group axes;
- track bridge/rail/road throughput;
- shorten delay from observation to corps/army decision;
- improve timing of artillery fires and mobile reserve movement.

## 5. Ground armored inventory around AG Center, Jun 1944 — branch working bands

Immediate operational AFVs roughly 950–1,150, with another 400–600 class available in nearby reserve/reinforcement windows.

Illustrative immediate mix:

- Panther A/G: ~220–300;
- Panzer IV H/J: ~220–280;
- Tiger I: ~50–80;
- StuG III/IV: ~300–380;
- Jagdpanzer IV: ~50–80;
- Marder/older self-propelled antitank: ~70–100;
- Tiger II: none to very few at opening; not a normal Bagration battlefield weapon.

## 6. What actually destroys Soviet armor

Do not assign the majority of AFV losses to German jets.

Branch operational decomposition for a high-loss Soviet summer offensive can reasonably be thought of as:

- artillery/Pak/mines: ~40–50%;
- tanks/StuG/Jagdpanzer: ~25–35%;
- air attack: ~5–10%;
- mechanical/fuel/abandonment/other: ~10–20%.

These are modeling shares, not archival statistics.

## 7. Late-1944 Eastern Front branch-center German inventory

Working year-end centers/ranges:

- German Eastern Front manpower: ~3.1–3.4 million;
- organized east-facing Axis including allies: ~4.2–4.6 million;
- operational German tanks/assault guns east: ~5,000–6,000 class;
- operational Luftwaffe east: ~2,800–3,500;
- operational jets east: ~400–650;
  - Me 262 ~250–350;
  - E6-D ~100–170;
  - Ar 234 ~50–80;
  - residual E5-D/other: balance.

Treat these as branch working ranges to be replayed against exact front allocation, not as immutable census figures.
