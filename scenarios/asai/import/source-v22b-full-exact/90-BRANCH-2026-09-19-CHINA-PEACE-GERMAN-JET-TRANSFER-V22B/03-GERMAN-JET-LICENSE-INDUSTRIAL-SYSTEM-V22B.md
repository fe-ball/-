# V22B — GERMAN JET LICENSE / INDUSTRIAL SYSTEM

## 1. Core logic

The acquisition target is not "a magic Japanese jet." German specialists already understand reaction propulsion. The high-value Japanese asset is the package that makes a turbine aircraft a repeatable military product:

- production drawings and tolerances;
- compressor/rotor dynamics practice;
- balancing;
- combustion patterning;
- hot-section cooling/material tradeoffs;
- bearings/seals;
- NDT and inspection;
- scheduled removal / teardown logic;
- fault taxonomy;
- service tooling;
- field maintenance;
- photo/radio integration;
- crew conversion;
- high-sortie operational routines.

## 2. Why Germany licenses the whole E5 system even if it prefers its own designs

German institutional pride does not eliminate the rational value of a proven benchmark.

The full package provides:

- a flying control/reference article;
- a jet conversion/training fleet;
- a service-maintenance school;
- a production comparator for Jumo/BMW programs;
- a reconnaissance aircraft before German dedicated jets mature;
- a way to test German subsystems on a known-good turbine platform.

Germany can simultaneously produce E5-D and continue Me 262/Jumo 004 development.

## 3. German improvement vs German degradation

### Likely improvements
- optics/photo equipment;
- radio and later radar integration;
- some mission-specific weapons;
- access/quick-change details after field learning;
- cold-weather and Eastern Front service modifications;
- German high-speed aerodynamic detail work in fairings, antennae, junctions and surfaces;
- mass production tooling at scale once process is understood.

### Likely degradations / conservative limits
- local materials do not reproduce every Japanese statistical property immediately;
- German substitutions alter bearings, seals, coatings, lubricants and heat-treatment confidence;
- early German engines use conservative forced-removal intervals;
- heavier German radios/radar/weapons can cost mass and drag;
- rough-field Eastern conditions expose landing gear/brake and intake/ground-service penalties.

Net branch judgment:

> Early German E5-D is roughly a late Japanese service E5 in useful performance, sometimes better equipped, but operated with shorter conservative OH intervals until German production statistics mature.

## 4. Why E5 makes Jumo 004 much more useful without turning it into an E5 copy

E5 does not provide Jumo 004's exact compressor map, blade shape or scale solution. It provides methods and known failure classes.

Thus Jumo 004 still has to solve:

- its own all-axial compressor;
- German-specific vibration modes;
- thrust-scale thermal loading;
- German materials and production constraints;
- Me 262/Ar 234-specific integration.

But it wastes less time rediscovering categories of solution already demonstrated by the licensed Japanese system.

## 5. German jet service culture

By 1943–44, Luftwaffe jet operations benefit from three years of E5-D institutional experience:

- turbine fuel/fire discipline;
- runway and brake rules;
- engine-hour bookkeeping;
- scheduled module removal;
- ground-start procedures;
- maintainers trained on real turbine aircraft;
- pilot conversion culture;
- two-engine failure/return procedures;
- jet reconnaissance mission planning.

This greatly increases the ratio of produced jets that become usable combat assets.

## 6. Submarine link after June 1941

Once the trans-Siberian route closes, technology exchange becomes low-volume/high-value.

Because Japan remains formally at peace with Britain and the United States, branch-center exchange initially avoids unnecessarily public Japanese military entry into German Atlantic ports when possible. A plausible pattern is:

- pre-Barbarossa: heavy machinery, tooling, full-size equipment, engineers over land where possible;
- post-Barbarossa: ocean rendezvous, microfilm, small precision instruments, material samples, specialist personnel and very high-value components;
- later, as strategic value rises, some larger Yanagi-style voyages can occur despite diplomatic risk.

The center branch assumes the link survives well enough to carry E6 technology, but not at the throughput of a normal rail/sea commercial route.

## 7. German return package to Japan

German counter-transfer naturally emphasizes fields where Germany has high-value depth:

- submarines, underwater endurance, batteries, diesel/electric integration;
- sonar/listening/ESM;
- torpedoes and fire control;
- radar/high-frequency technology;
- optics;
- precision machine tools;
- high-pressure chemistry, synthetic fuel/rubber and catalysts;
- later guided weapons and rocket/control work;
- Type XXI/Schnorchel-era information as it matures.

This makes the exchange a continuing state bargain rather than a one-off engine purchase.
