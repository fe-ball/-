## User's unresolved question
During the huge Chinese withdrawals/evacuations of late 1937, did Japanese aviation actually have the concept and practice of bombing/strafing retreating military columns, rail movement, bridges, road nodes, dumps and mass evacuation routes?

If yes:
- how mature was the operational concept;
- how often was it done;
- which Army/Navy aircraft could reach the relevant nodes;
- were the right aircraft based close enough at the right time;
- what bombs/attack profiles were practical;
- how much was target acquisition/intelligence rather than aircraft count the bottleneck;
- how much would V7 photo reconnaissance, radio/EMI, metadata/indexing, serviceability and faster reporting improve the effective interdiction cycle?

If no or weak:
- was the limitation doctrine, command priorities, basing, aircraft range, bomb load, weather, target identification, sortie availability, inter-service coordination, or simply that aircraft were already tasked with CAS/airfield/strategic targets?
