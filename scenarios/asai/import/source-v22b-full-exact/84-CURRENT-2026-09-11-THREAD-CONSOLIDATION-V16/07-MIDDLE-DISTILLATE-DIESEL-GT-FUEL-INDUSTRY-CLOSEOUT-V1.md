# MIDDLE-DISTILLATE / DIESEL / GT FUEL-INDUSTRY CLOSEOUT — THREAD V16

## Core doctrine

The worldline does not create a separate “jet-fuel island.” It builds a military middle-distillate pool serving diesel and GT users with graded quality.

### Grade A
Aviation GT fuel. Tight low-temperature, water, cleanliness and thermal-stability control.

### Grade B
Standard military middle distillate. Fast diesel, tanks/trucks/tractors, generators, ground GT, selected marine GT and GT advanced trainers.

### Grade C
Broader / lower-grade middle distillate for tolerant diesel, stationary and selected marine use.

Higher grade can be down-used. Lower grade is up-used only under approved derated schedules / inspection penalties.

## Fuel-property effects on GTs

Low-grade-fuel problems are not summarized as “lower heating value.” Main effects:

- viscosity / volatility → atomization, ignition, low-load stability
- aromatics / low H:C → soot, radiant liner heating
- thermal stability → nozzle/heat-exchanger coking
- wax/freezing → filter blockage in aviation
- sulfur + sodium → hot corrosion
- vanadium / sodium / ash / residual contamination → severe turbine deposit/corrosion risk

### E5/E6 working restrictions

- clean straight-run kerosene / gas-oil can be used broadly.
- ordinary diesel/gas-oil on E6 JF: derated ~810–825 C class.
- ordinary diesel/gas-oil on E6 pure-J: ~820–835 C ceiling class.
- 920 C selected-Ni high rating is prohibited on low-grade fuel.
- inspection interval shortens before TBO is automatically redefined.

### E7 outlook

E7 can be designed for broader-distillate combustion, but fuel impurity tolerance is not the same as combustion tolerance. Aviation E7 does **not** burn residual/heavy fuel. Treated residual fuel remains a stationary/marine derated research path with heating, separation, washing, filtration, inhibitors and short hot-section inspection.

## Palembang / South Sumatra interpretation

Strategic value is not “raw crude can be burned in E7.” It is:

- desalting/dewatering + atmospheric distillation quickly yields useful kerosene/gas-oil cuts;
- many South-Sumatran oils are relatively low-sulfur but can be waxy;
- ground diesel / ground GT can often use the middle cut with modest finishing;
- aviation Grade A may require tighter cut selection, dewax/blending, water control and selective mild HDS.

Thus captured/restored refining capacity matters as much as the oilfield itself.

## Prewar source-screening program

From roughly 1932–35 onward in this worldline, Asai-related fuel work systematically screens imported crude/product samples for:

- middle-distillate yield
- sulfur
- wax/pour/cloud behavior
- water/salt
- ash / residual contaminants
- aromatic / smoke tendency
- diesel ignition quality
- upgrade cost to Grade A

State oil ledgers therefore distinguish crude quantity from military Grade-B equivalent and Grade-A potential.
