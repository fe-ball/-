## Foreign assessment pattern

A plausible 1940 failure mode is not ignorance but coherent misinterpretation:

- China / Chennault-type observers get local speed / climb / repeated-sortie behavior relatively right, but lack national production visibility.
- Soviet analysts take the new technology seriously after Nomonhan but may still underpredict production scale.
- U.S./British technical analysts may discount extreme observed values as measurement error, special prototypes or short-life engines.
- Dutch commercial/intelligence channels may notice unusual Japanese middle-distillate specifications without automatically linking them to large-scale aviation GT deployment.
- German propulsion specialists are less likely to deny jet/reaction propulsion in principle, but may find the reported service life / production maturity least believable.

## “Laminar aircraft” hypothesis

One plausible foreign analytical hypothesis is that E6 performance comes from an extreme low-drag / laminar-flow-type wing and very clean airframe combined with a special propulsion unit of only moderate “piston-equivalent” power.

Why it is attractive:

- NACA laminar-flow work is contemporary and plausible.
- E6 appears clean and avoids prolonged low-speed dogfight.
- analysts can rationalize implausible speed reports by crediting airframe drag rather than accepting mature high-thrust GT technology.

Why it is incomplete:

- jet thrust power does not map to one constant horsepower number.
- sustained field operation, damage tolerance and production growth eventually contradict “fragile special laminar prototype” assumptions.

This hypothesis remains a side analytical option only.
