# AIRPOWER SUSTAINMENT / TRAINING / AIRFIELD CLOSEOUT — THREAD V16

## GT life and circulation

### E6-M-JF
- Normal 1940–41 long-life product point: non-Ni / ~835 C class.
- Scheduled removal / OH_prod: **450–600 h; center 525 h**.
- Product is deliberately not driven to the highest temperature for routine fleet service.

### E6-M-J
- Normal product point ~870 C class.
- Scheduled removal / OH_prod: **250–350 h; center 300 h**.
- selected-Ni high-temperature branch remains premium / shorter-life and is not the fleet baseline.

### Spares
- Flight-grade spare / acceptance float: **20–25%, center 22.5%**.
- This is a circulating pool for removal, overhaul, transport and acceptance, not annual discard.

## T-AUX reading

- 1940H1 aviation JF is a local scheduling pinch, especially during E5→E6 transition.
- Annual 1940/41 JF backend capacity remains above ordinary aviation demand.
- 1941 aviation JF does not justify pulling T-5 forward solely to rescue E6 production.
- Marine reducer/clutch/shaft and future turboprop reduction/propeller backends stay distinct bottlenecks.

## 1941 manpower / training working closure

- Pilot graduates above historical-type pipeline: **+500/year**.
- Observer/navigation/mission crew: **+300/year**.
- Direct + depot aviation technical personnel in Asai-series system: **~7,500**.
- Total additional Asai aviation organization including fuel/armament/radio/base/admin: **~14–16k, center 15k**.
- E6 combat release: pilot total flight experience **300 h+** working minimum.
- New full unit: 6–9 months initial capability / 9–12 months mature.
- Cadre-derived E6 unit: 8–12 weeks initial / 12–16 weeks normal capability.
- E5/Ki-42 donor units pay temporary readiness/training cost when cadre is pulled.

## GT advanced training

Mature 1941 planning state:

- 40–50 twin-seat GT advanced trainers.
- ~20,000 flight-hours/year training capacity.
- 35–45 h GT advanced phase per student.
- Training rating ~760–790 C class.
- Grade B middle distillate is acceptable as normal trainer fuel.
- Combat-grade rejection does not automatically equal scrap: structurally qualified lower-performance cores can be training-grade.

This shifts high-end training away from expensive high-octane piston operation without replacing elementary/intermediate piston training.

## Airfield classes

- **HA:** high-altitude piston / E5 class; prepared/drained surface, oxygen, radio/weather, power, instruments/fuel support.
- **E6-F:** Single / light Twin forward operations; HA + FOD/dust control, A/B fuel, external power, core handling.
- **E6-M:** Twin main base; ~1.2–1.5 km prepared runway class, hardstand/taxi, oxygen, filtration, line shop.
- **DEPOT:** E6-M + hot-section, precision measurement/balance, NDT, parts repair/core exchange.

1941 planning network:

- principal HA/E6 bases: **24–30**
- support packages: **30–36**
- deep GT aviation depots: **5–7**

No blanket heavy-concrete requirement. Surface strength, drainage, dust/FOD, tyres/brakes and support matter more than extreme runway length.

## High-altitude physiology / oxygen

- 1940 normal high-altitude combat band: ~8–9.5 km class.
- 1941 limited positive-pressure / improved oxygen permits time-limited ~9.5–11 km practical operations.
- 12 km+ is not treated as ordinary combat altitude; pressure-suit capability is not granted.

## Warning / readiness

Primary 1940–41 improvement is operational, not radar-revolution:

- observer posts
- direct telephone/radio reporting
- plotting rooms
- standardized report format
- readiness states

Working states:

- Ready I: **2–3 min launch**
- Ready II: **5–7 min launch**
- Ready III: ordinary 15–20 min class

Radar remains limited supplementary early-warning equipment; no RAF-style precision fighter-direction network is imported early.
