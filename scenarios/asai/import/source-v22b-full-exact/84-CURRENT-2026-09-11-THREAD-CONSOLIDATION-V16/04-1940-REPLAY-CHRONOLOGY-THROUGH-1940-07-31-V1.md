# 1940 CHINA / STRATEGY REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — THROUGH 1940-07-31

This file records only the replay path actually advanced in the thread. Side calculations and illustrative air-combat scenes are excluded.

## 1940-01-01 opening

- E5 military physical extant center 130; Ki-42 physical extant 69.
- E6 ordinary combat allocation 0.
- Kwantung Army retains stronger-than-history rebuild posture after revised Nomonhan outcome.
- No automatic Chinese-front reinforcement from Manchuria beyond already closed reallocations.

## 1940-01-01 to 01-15

- Central-China winter offensive largely absorbed; 6th Division sector remains active.
- 3rd Division local winter actions around Xinyang/Changtaikuan improve slightly from better reconnaissance and support; no strategic breakthrough.
- North-China Changzhi fighting continues; winter support improves machine start/readiness but does not solve mountain control.
- Nanning remains Japanese; Kunlun Pass remains Chinese at opening; Japan reorganizes rather than immediately reattacking.

## 1940-01-16 to 01-31

- Central-China winter offensive ends on essentially historical timetable.
- Suiyuan/Wuyuan Japanese counteroffensive preparation begins with somewhat better warning and winterized mobility.
- South Guangxi: Japanese counteroffensive begins 1940-01-28 on historical army-level timing. By 01-31 Japanese forces have regained Kunlun Pass major positions / are completing the recapture, but Chinese main forces remain organized.
- Yonai cabinet forms 01-16; U.S.–Japan commercial treaty expires 01-26.

## 1940-02-01 to 02-15

- Binyang/Pinyang occupied temporarily by Japan around 02-02 during the South Guangxi thrust.
- Japanese forces withdraw after the operation; by ~02-11/13 Chinese forces re-enter Binyang. Nanning remains Japanese.
- Wuyuan captured by Japan around 02-02, followed by westward short-range pursuit / destruction rather than permanent expansion; main Japanese force begins withdrawing by mid-month.

## 1940-02-16 to 03-31

- Wuyuan: Chinese attack 03-21, temporary Chinese seizure; Japanese counterattack retakes principal area ~03-25, then Japanese main force withdraws ~03-29; Chinese hold the area again at month end.
- Central China: Tang Enbo / 5th War Area concentrations around Zaoyang become increasingly apparent. Japanese staff work for a spring offensive begins earlier because repeated E5/E6-style reconnaissance shortens information age.
- Wang Jingwei Nanjing regime formally established 03-30. No mass Chinese army defection or international recognition collapse occurs.
- E6 Q1 acceptance reaches roughly 21 Twin / 15 Single central planning. Central-China service-proving allocation begins.
- Winter War ends 03-13. Thread uses a somewhat less adverse Finnish peace as a working political result; exact frontier intentionally open.

## 1940-04-01 to 04-15 — E6 combat-system proving

- E6 Twin normal-unit release opens 04-01. Central-China evaluation group begins limited normal missions.
- First regular E6 operational recon/BDA sortie placed on 04-05 in this replay.
- Main lessons: E6 performance is not a linear E5 improvement; same-altitude pursuit is often meaningless; high-altitude/high-speed dive limits and compressibility symptoms matter; photo procedures must adapt to short target dwell time.
- Chinese fighters learn that rear-chase interception is poor; predictive / ahead-of-route positioning is the correct early counter.
- No irreversible E6 loss in this period.

## 1940-04-16 to 04-30 — Yaoyi preparation

- Central-China E6 Twin reinforced to about 12 assigned; E6 Single evaluation group to about 6 assigned by late April.
- E6 employment begins shifting from single premium reconnaissance to multi-aircraft armed-recon / BDA / sector-cover packages.
- 04-21/23 historical 11th Army offensive decision/tactical scheme retained. E6 shortens information age but does not remove concentration, road, artillery or bridge constraints.
- 5th War Area concentration around Zaoyang is assessed with higher confidence; Japanese plan becomes more adaptive to observed enemy movement.

## 1940-05-01 to 05-10 — Yaoyi first phase

- E6 Single normal service release opens 05-01.
- 3rd, 13th, 39th Divisions plus Ishimoto/Ikeda detachments form the principal historical operational framework; earlier accidental reference to 16th Division as a main participant is corrected.
- Zaoyang taken on 05-06; Tanghe principal traffic points held around 05-08 after contested approach.
- Chinese counterattacks still penetrate / destabilize the Tanghe area but are recognized earlier and do not cut Japanese traffic for as long.
- Working branch delta for this sub-period: Japanese losses ~250–400 lower than historical-type; Chinese additional loss/capture/dispersal ~1,000–1,800; Japanese noncombat vehicle/tow loss lower.

## 1940-05-11 to 05-20 — pursuit, Zhang Zizhong, Japanese reversal

- Chinese forces pursue the Japanese contraction in large numbers.
- E6/E5 + existing SIGINT allow Japanese command to recognize major concentration earlier and identify higher-command activity in the Zhang Zizhong sector sooner.
- Zhang Zizhong still remains with his troops and is killed on 05-16; his survival is not granted merely because the worldline is different.
- Japanese all-line counterattack begins about one day earlier than historical-type timing because enemy pursuit posture is recognized sooner.
- First E6 Twin irreversible loss: one aircraft written off after low-altitude armed-recon damage / landing damage; crew survives.
- Working branch delta for 05-11/20: Japanese losses ~500–800 lower; Chinese additional loss/capture/dispersal ~2,000–3,500.
- This is treated as an **operational nonlinear event**: information changed the timing and posture of the ground battle, rather than E6 directly causing all losses.

## 1940-05-21 to 06-01 06:00 — reset and Han River crossing

- Past deltas are absorbed into current unit condition; no separate historical bonus multiplier is stacked.
- 11th Army stops first-phase pursuit rather than overextending.
- 05-23/30: repair, replenishment, bridge/river reconnaissance and deception preparation.
- E6 employment shifts to crossing-site intelligence, deception and rear-route interdiction.
- Night 05-31/06-01: Han River crossing succeeds on roughly historical timing, but bridgeheads contain somewhat more heavy weapons / communications / support by dawn due better readiness and transport.

## 1940-06-01 to 06-06

- Xiangyang/Yicheng/Nanzhang fighting develops; Chinese local counterattack succeeds at Nanzhang on ~06-04. This result is preserved.
- Japanese units withdraw from the threatened salient somewhat more deliberately because the counterattack is recognized earlier; fewer heavy weapons are abandoned.
- Southern axis does better: Shayang/Jingmen line reached on near-historical timing; Jingmen Japanese-held by 06-06.
- Working branch delta: Japanese ~180–300 losses avoided; Chinese additional ~600–1,000 plus somewhat more equipment abandonment.

## 1940-06-07 to 06-12 — Yichang approach

- Dangyang/Yuan’an taken on 06-10; Yichang occupied officially on 06-12, preserving the historical date.
- E6 role is strongest on retreat routes, bridges, traffic nodes and immediate BDA, not urban close combat.
- Chinese forces increasingly save manpower by night/mountain withdrawal while abandoning more heavy equipment.
- Working branch delta: Japanese ~250–400 losses avoided; Chinese additional ~900–1,500.
- Yaoyi campaign working total branch delta through 06-12: Japanese losses avoided ~1.3–2.1k; Chinese additional loss/capture/dispersal ~4.8–8.4k.

## 1940-06-13 to 06-18 — Yichang retention decision

- Historical temporary withdrawal/reoccupation confusion is avoided because Yichang’s aviation value is recognized earlier and command delivery is more redundant.
- 13th Division remains to hold Yichang.
- South-bank heights remain Chinese-held and threaten the airfield; Yichang is not yet a safe E6 main base.
- 4th Division transfer from Manchuria is treated as an upcoming strategic allocation decision, not automatic history.

## 1940-06-19 to 06-30 — south-bank heights / Indochina pressure

- South-bank height assault preparation accelerated modestly due higher airfield priority and absence of the historical retreat/reoccupation delay.
- Main heights taken around 06-28/29; nearby fighting continues briefly.
- Yichang airfield becomes a genuine forward operating base; Hankou remains deep-maintenance / main-support hub.
- Small E6 forward detachment begins permanent/regular use (working center: Twin ~4, Single ~4 plus a few E5), not the whole Central-China fleet.
- France collapses; Japan increases pressure on French Indochina and China-supply routes.
- **4th Division remains in Manchuria** rather than making the historical 1940-07-01 transfer to 11th Army.

## 1940-07-01 to 07-15

- Central-China E6 reaches Q2 anchor class: Twin ~18 assigned, Single ~12 assigned across Hankou/Yichang; smaller ready fractions.
- Yichang forward-base routine becomes practical; repeated Sichuan-edge weather/recon/traffic missions begin.
- A6M small-scale China operational trial becomes eligible from 07-01; no major air-to-air battle is pre-recorded.
- Indochina supply route effectively suppressed; Burma Road closure pressure reaches crisis level.
- Chinese 5th War Area rebuilds rather than launching a new major offensive; local infiltration/sabotage continues around Yichang.

## 1940-07-16 to 07-31

- Yonai cabinet resigns 07-16; second Konoe cabinet forms 07-22.
- Burma Road temporarily closed; U.S. export control pressure tightens.
- Japan increasingly believes coercive diplomacy can close China’s external supply routes without immediate general war.
- Central China does not launch a new army-scale offensive. Local / air pressure continues.
- Eighth Route Army begins preparations for a major North-China transport offensive; Japanese security intelligence sees only diffuse activity, not the coming operational scale.
- 4th Division remains in Manchuria; the China Expeditionary Army therefore has better regeneration but one fewer historical reinforcement division.
