## 1. Historical operating frame

The East Shanxi operation is a large multi-axis North China Area Army offensive.

Historical/Japanese monograph working frame:
- Chinese force that had moved into the Changzhi / southeast-Shanxi operating zone:
  - Nationalist: ~80,000
  - Communist: ~20,000
- Japanese attack begins 3 July from several axes with roughly four divisions.
- Operation ultimately runs to 21 August.

First-phase historical anchors:
- 4 Jul: Liaoxian occupied by part of 4th Independent Mixed Brigade
- 5 Jul: Wuxiang and Qin County occupied by 109th Division
- 6 Jul: 385th Brigade recaptures Liaoxian
- 8 Jul: Yushe occupied by Japanese cooperation of 4th IMB / 36th / 109th elements
- 8 Jul: Qinshui falls to 20th Division, after which heavy rain restricts vehicle movement
- 8 Jul: She County occupied on Handan–Lu'an axis; bridges are washed out by heavy rain
- 9 Jul: 108th Division task-force line reaches Fucheng / Liangma / Qinyuan / Zhangdian family
- 11 Jul: 108th / 109th resume the next phase and take Tunliu / Xiangyuan; Communist forces recover some vacated/reduced-garrison towns behind them

The operation is a sweep-and-occupation campaign, not one continuous encirclement battle.

## 4. “Better eyes” doctrine effect

The new reconnaissance system changes caution as well as speed.

Japanese staff increasingly distinguishes:
- **enemy not observed**
from
- **enemy absent**.

Therefore:
- mountain valleys / wooded routes with stale imagery receive more flank/rear security;
- columns are less willing to convert a local Chinese withdrawal into an unsupported deep pursuit;
- E5 daylight trend maps are used to compare road / village / bivouac state;
- bad-weather gaps are treated as uncertainty, not proof of enemy disappearance.

This sometimes costs minutes/hours of maximum advance speed.
The payoff is fewer ambush / isolation losses.

## 5. 3–6 July — Liaoxian / Wuxiang / Qin County

### Liaoxian
Historical:
- Japanese small force occupies 4 Jul.
- 385th Brigade retakes 6 Jul.

Revised:
- repeated E5 / ground-report comparison detects increasing movement toward the Liaoxian sector late on 5 Jul, but terrain / night movement prevent exact tactical localization.
- the local Japanese force is too small to hold every approach.

Result:
- **Liaoxian is still recovered by the Chinese on 6 Jul.**
- Japanese nonessential stores / vehicles are pulled out earlier.
- rear/security elements avoid being caught as deeply.

Differential:
- Japanese casualties / missing: **~15–30 fewer**
- Chinese casualties: **~20–40 additional**
- abandoned Japanese stores: modestly lower

This is not a retained-town nonlinear flip.

### Wuxiang / Qin County
- occupied 5 Jul broadly on historical clock.
- better radio / route information cleans up battalion synchronization by roughly **1–2 h**.
- no major Chinese main-force trapping.

## 6. 5–9 July — southern Qinshui front

The 20th Division still encounters strong prepared resistance from Chinese 14th / 15th Army formations.

Worldline effects:
- E5 photography helps distinguish occupied hill systems from already-vacated positions.
- artillery target handoff is somewhat fresher.
- BDA reduces repeated missions against positions already suppressed / abandoned.
- vehicle recovery is better on approach roads.

Limits:
- steep terrain and close infantry fighting dominate.
- Chinese units can move at night / under terrain cover.
- rain increasingly degrades air and vehicle value.

Qinshui:
- still falls on **8 Jul**.
- local clock improvement: roughly **2–4 h**, not a day.

Differential through 9 Jul:
- Japanese casualties: **~45–75 fewer**
- Chinese casualties / captured / scattered: **~100–170 additional**
- Japanese vehicle / support-equipment breakdown losses: **~5–10 fewer unit-equivalents**

## 7. 5–11 July — Jincheng / Jiaozuo front

The 35th Division still meets stubborn resistance from the Chinese 40th Army.

Historical hard-fighting pattern remains:
- small forward positions are overrun;
- Changping / Mengliangzhai / Dakou-Xiaokou family involves severe close combat;
- Chinese formations can trade ground for time.

Worldline differences:
- Japanese artillery / air strikes are less often wasted on empty reverse-slope or already-abandoned points.
- rear-route reconnaissance reduces some surprise flanking moves.
- better communications make relief / reserve movement less piecemeal.

Result by 11 Jul:
- Japanese tactical progress is **hours cleaner**, not operationally transformed.
- no early capture of Jincheng is credited.
- no collapse of the Chinese 40th Army.

Differential:
- Japanese casualties: **~40–70 fewer**
- Chinese casualties / captured / scattered: **~110–190 additional**
- Chinese heavy/support weapons lost: **~3–6 additional**

## 8. Handan–Lu'an axis — rain gate

- heavy rain washes away bridges and halts further movement.

Revised:

- bridge / road reconnaissance and engineer-tool readiness are better.

Working engineering effect:

- no immediate operational breakthrough beyond the historical weather gate

The later Handan–Lu'an road-opening clock is therefore **not yet moved** at this checkpoint.

## 9. 10–11 July transition

As Japanese main formations shift forward:
- the Eighth Route Army still exploits reduced garrisons / exposed rear areas.
- Yushe / Wuxiang-type local recoveries remain possible.

Better Japanese reconnaissance identifies this behavior somewhat faster, but:
**eyes do not create garrison battalions.**

11 Jul:
- 108th / 109th resume major movement.
- Tunliu / Xiangyuan are taken on the same calendar day.
- local occupation timing can be **~2–5 h earlier**.

No attempt is made to hold every previously swept town simultaneously.

## 10. Chemical-warfare accounting guard

Contemporary Chinese sources contain repeated allegations of Japanese chemical-shell use during this operation.

For this alternate-history delta:
- no additional chemical-war capability, doctrine, stock, or effectiveness is attributed to Asai developments;
- any historical chemical-warfare effect remains inside the matched-history baseline;
- no worldline casualty gain is credited from it.

This prevents an unrelated historical factor from being mistaken for an Asai-derived improvement.
