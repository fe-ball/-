## 1. Historical anchors

Working matched-history sequence:
- 10–13 Aug: 20th Division sweeps the Chinese 85th Division northwest of Jincheng, inflicts heavy casualties, and nearly overruns the divisional headquarters.
- Chinese 39th Division sends battalion-size forces to attack Japanese troops / vehicles around Jincheng and Changzhi in support.
- 10 Aug: First Army orders the 20th Division to leave Jincheng gradually and move toward Lu'an / Qin County between 16 and 18 Aug.
- Chinese counter-move:
  - 85th Division moves from the Qinshui side;
  - Eighth Route Army detachment pressures the city;
  - 117th Brigade + 14th Cavalry Brigade try to cut the Japanese northward withdrawal;
  - 115th Brigade attacks Jincheng.
- Chinese cutting forces historically fail to catch the main 20th Division rearguard; only a straggling Japanese sergeant-major is captured at Bagong.
- 21 Aug: 115th Brigade recovers Jincheng.
- 21 Aug: Taniguchi Unit finally opens the Handan–Lu'an road.
- 23 Aug: Gaoping is subsequently retaken by Chinese forces.

The East Shanxi operation effectively closes on 21 Aug, though local combat continues.

Effects:
- Chinese forward units are forced to displace;
- some staff vehicles / pack animals / signal equipment are left behind or destroyed;
- headquarters fragments before the Japanese can close the full pocket.

### 13 Aug — headquarters gate

**CLOSED revised result:**
- the 85th Division headquarters itself escapes;
- divisional command is NOT decapitated;
- however, a **partial headquarters / rear-echelon overrun** occurs.

Working Chinese losses attributable to the HQ-system hit:

- documents / maps / signal material: useful partial capture
- radios / pack / command-support items: several lost
- temporary command disruption: **~6–12 h**

This is the main new nonlinear event of the gate.

Why it stops here:
- Chinese headquarters is already using temporary / dispersed sites;
- night / terrain break continuous Japanese contact;
- Japanese force is preparing to leave Jincheng and does not turn the sweep into an open-ended chase.

## 7. Jincheng recovery gate

Question:
Does the healthier Japanese force leave a larger permanent garrison?

**No.**

Reason:
- the same First Army concentration logic still applies;
- a larger garrison would recreate the manpower-dispersion problem elsewhere;
- the Chinese 85th / 39th / local forces remain active;
- Japan cannot turn every swept county into a permanent strongpoint.

### Revised result

After the last Japanese rear guard leaves:
- Chinese reconnaissance / local troops re-enter the outer Jincheng area;
- 115th Brigade still conducts the main recovery action.

**Jincheng is recovered on 21 Aug, same calendar date as matched history.**

Clock difference:
- because the Japanese rear guard is better organized and leaves behind fewer confused / isolated positions, Chinese entry into the central town is **~3–8 h later within 21 Aug** than matched-history center.

This is not a strategic hold.
The county-city still returns to Chinese control.
