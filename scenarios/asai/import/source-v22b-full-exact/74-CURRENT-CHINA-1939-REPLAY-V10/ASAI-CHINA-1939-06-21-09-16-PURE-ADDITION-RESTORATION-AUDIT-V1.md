# 2. What was missing

Because the E5 / Ki-42 fleets are near-pure additions versus the OLD branch, some missions historically flown by conventional aircraft are now performed by the Asai fleets.

That creates a real second-order capacity release.

Examples:

### E5
When E5 handles:
- deep route photography,
- repeated BDA,
- long command reconnaissance,
- same-type distant high cover,

some conventional reconnaissance / liaison aircraft can shift toward:
- lower / closer tactical route checks,
- rear / flank observation,
- artillery / column-support reporting,
- shorter-frequency battlefield updates.

### Ki-42
When Ki-42 handles:
- high cover,
- fast interception,
- distant bomber / E5 escort,

some Ki-27 capacity can shift toward:
- lower / medium-altitude CAP,
- closer escort,
- airfield / column protection,
- short local fighter sweeps.

### E5 strike
A minority of E5 attack sorties can release conventional light-bomber effort for:
- secondary road / supply targets,
- repeat attacks on targets confirmed by E5 BDA.

This is not automatic.
Only missions with a real backlog / alternate target are credited.

Therefore:
- no new generic “airfield congestion penalty” is subtracted;

# 15. First Changsha implication

This audit reinforces the already-corrected 17 Sep opening interpretation:

The operational effect of the revised fleet is not merely:
- E5 direct sorties
- Ki-42 direct sorties

It is:
1. those direct sorties,
2. plus selective release of conventional aircraft into other tasks.

At First Changsha, because Central China has:
- E5 30 assigned / ~24 ready
- Ki-42 20 assigned / ~16 ready
- plus historical conventional aviation,

this second-order effect will be substantially larger than it was in North China with only E5 8 / Ki-42 4.

Therefore First Changsha must explicitly carry a **mission-allocation ledger** for:
- E5,
- Ki-42,
- freed conventional recon,
- freed Ki-27,
- conventional bombers,
to prevent both omission and double count.
