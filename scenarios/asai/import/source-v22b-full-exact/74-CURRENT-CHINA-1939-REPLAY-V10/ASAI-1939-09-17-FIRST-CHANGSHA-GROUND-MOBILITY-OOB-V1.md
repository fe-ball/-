### 12 assigned / 11 serviceable

- 15.3–15.6 t
- 200 hp continuous
- 220 hp short-duration
- low-pressure turbocharged diesel
- WET1 wet-weather service treatment
- normal ~1.0 m fording rating
- no deep-wading/snorkel kit
- original 57 mm turret

### Company tactical use
Best roles:
- infantry strongpoint reduction;
- bridgehead exploitation after a tank-rated crossing exists;
- mud / wet-road movement where the extra torque/power margin matters;
- local mobile reserve.

Not best roles:
- unsupported river assault;
- long deep pursuit without engineer/logistic tail;
- assumed tank-vs-tank superiority.

## 14. Operational implications

The major worldline difference is not “tanks cross every river faster.”

It is a chain:

**better air reconnaissance**
→ crossing site / enemy-bank picture improves

**better engineer control / boat uptime**
→ infantry/heavy weapons cross with less technical delay

**waterproof communications**
→ control survives rain/splash more often

**heavy turbo transport**
→ guns, ammunition, bridge material and recovery vehicles are less likely to stall on approaches

**Chi-Ha 200/220 hp WET1**
→ once a tank-rated crossing exists, the modern tank company is more likely to arrive and remain combat-ready in mud/bank-exit conditions.

The physical river still sets the gate.
