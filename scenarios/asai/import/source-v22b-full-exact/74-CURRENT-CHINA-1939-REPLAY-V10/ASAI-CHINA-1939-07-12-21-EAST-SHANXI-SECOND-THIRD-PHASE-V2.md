## 4. 15–18 July — decision for the Jincheng convergence

The First Army still decides to concentrate toward Jincheng.

Why the decision remains:
- northern Chinese resistance appears weaker than on the southern front;
- Jincheng is the operational convergence point;
- heavy rain has reduced the value of dispersed pursuit;
- linking the 20th / 35th / 108th / 109th family of forces is still sound.

### New intelligence state
E5 / ground reports show:
- Chinese forces are not generally collapsing;
- the 14th Army remains active south / southwest of Dongfeng;
- 93rd Army movement indicates counter-concentration rather than withdrawal;
- road / village occupancy around Yangcheng changes repeatedly.

This does **not** reveal exact night movement or every hill position.

### Operational consequence
The 20th Division is more cautious with the 39th Brigade.

Instead of treating westward Chinese movement around Dongfeng as a retreat to be pressed indefinitely:
- advance objectives are limited by phase line;
- one battalion-class element / equivalent local reserve is kept closer to the Yangcheng / Qinshui route system;
- artillery and vehicles are not pushed as far forward before the flank picture is clearer.

Cost:
- the 39th Brigade's maximum westward penetration is slightly shallower than matched history.

Benefit:
- less exposure to the 14th / 93rd Army counter-concentration.

This is a real doctrinal consequence of “better eyes -> more respect for the unseen gap.”

## 8. 21 July Chinese counterattack southwest of Yangcheng

This is the main nonlinear gate in the period.

Matched-history:
- 14th Army and 93rd Army attack Japanese positions southwest of Yangcheng.
- the Japanese 39th Brigade is exposed after its Dongfeng fighting.

Revised precondition:
- Japanese command already expects a counter-concentration.
- 39th Brigade is less deeply extended.
- one local reserve / route-defense layer is closer to Yangcheng.
- artillery target folders include likely approach valleys / road junctions.

### Chinese behavior
Chinese commanders still attack.

Why:
- they observe Japanese forces converging on Jincheng;
- they have a real opportunity against the 20th Division's stretched western elements;
- Japanese restraint is not interpreted perfectly as a prepared trap.

### Battle result
The Chinese attack achieves local pressure and forces limited Japanese positional adjustments, but:
- it does **not** dislocate the Yangcheng route system as strongly as matched history;
- the 39th Brigade does not begin the next phase from as exposed a posture.

Working 21 Jul delta:
- Japanese casualties: **~30–50 fewer**
- Chinese casualties / scattered: **~70–120 additional**
- Chinese local penetration depth reduced by roughly **0.5–1.5 km** on the best-prepared approaches
- no destruction of a Chinese division or army headquarters

### Qualitative closure
**NEW local nonlinear difference:**
the historical-style Chinese counterpressure still develops, but the 39th Brigade is not pulled as far into the exposed Dongfeng/Yangcheng geometry.

This sets up a different 22–27 Jul withdrawal / recapture race.

## 9. Chinese adaptation

The Chinese side is also learning.

By 18–21 Jul:
- daylight road columns are broken into smaller packets;
- more movement is shifted to night / rain;
- bivouacs are dispersed away from obvious villages / road junctions;
- false / temporary positions are used more often.

Effect on E5:
- direct daylight target yield falls.
- the value shifts toward trend comparison and confirming **where not to over-pursue**.

Cost to Chinese forces:
- concentration is slower;
- artillery / supply coordination is somewhat harder;
- night march / dispersed handling imposes fatigue.

No theater-wide Chinese logistics penalty is credited.
