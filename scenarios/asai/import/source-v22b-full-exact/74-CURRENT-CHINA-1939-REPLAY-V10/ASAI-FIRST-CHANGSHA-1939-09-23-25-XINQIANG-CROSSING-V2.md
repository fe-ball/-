- bridge-material traffic;
- artillery / ammunition follow-through.

- infantry / assault engineers begin on historical schedule.

- waterproof radios / telephone-control points fail less often;

- selected turbo-heavy vehicles keep bridge material and ammunition moving through soft approaches.

### Chinese line effect
The Chinese forward position still fights hard.

Revised center:

- no entire Chinese corps collapses.

### Heavy support lag
Compared with matched history:
- machine-gun / infantry-gun support: ~0.5–1.5 h closer to infantry clock
- first useful artillery/ammunition crossing: **~2–3 h earlier**

This is one of the direct payoffs from the wet/engineer system.

## 4. 23 Sep — Uemura landing

Historical landing remains under Chinese fire.

Worldline systems:
- boat mechanical availability + maintenance;
- Navy E5 route/BDA;
- radio traffic control;
- mobile power / pump / lighting support;
- conventional naval aviation remains the major fire-support mass.

### Landing-clock effect
The detachment does not sail earlier into daylight risk.

At dawn:
- first-wave landing timing improves by roughly **~15–30 min**
- follow-on boat cycle is more orderly

### Craft losses / disabled craft
Enemy artillery / mortar fire remains the main danger.

Better maintenance cannot stop shells.

However:
- fewer mechanical stragglers;
- better dispersion / route control;
- somewhat faster suppression / BDA of firing points

reduce the number of craft becoming unavailable.

Working differential:
- **~3–6 fewer landing/river craft put out of action or stranded** than the matched-history equivalent
- not all are saved from direct fire; part of the gain is mechanical / recovery

### Ground result
Uemura still establishes the landing area on 23 Sep.

No immediate encirclement of a Chinese corps.

## 8. 25 Sep — 6th Division at Ku Shui

Historical anchor:
- by morning 25 Sep the 6th Division reaches the left bank of the Ku Shui and prepares to attack the next Chinese line.

Revised:
- improved heavy-support follow-through and earlier Xinqiang break make the leading elements arrive **~5–8 h earlier** in the center case.

Practical interpretation:
- advanced infantry / reconnaissance can reach the Ku Shui sector during late 24 Sep / very early 25 Sep;
- full organized attack preparation still belongs to **25 Sep**.

Why no immediate night crossing:
- information after dark is stale;
- a new river line must be reconnoitered;
- Chinese rear guards remain active;
- the worldline's better-information doctrine discourages blind night pursuit.

Thus the calendar remains 25 Sep, but preparation begins earlier.
