## 1. Starting operational picture

By 25 Sep:
- Chinese first-line formations have been pushed south;
- Uemura is in contact with retreating and newly arrived Chinese troops;
- 6th Division is at the Ku Shui line;
- Nara is advancing toward Tzuchiang;
- 33rd Division is fighting through the eastern hill-road system;
- Chinese forces are attempting to rebuild a second line and preserve outer wings.

Revised Japanese intelligence:
- E5 trend reconnaissance more clearly shows that the retreat is **organized redistribution**, not general collapse;
- conventional close recon, freed by the E5 layer, increases revisit rate on roads / valleys;
- Japanese staff therefore has more confidence in the need to cut retreat routes rather than chase empty ground.

This reinforces the historical eastward-turn logic.

---

## 2. 26 Sep — command decision

Historical Army decision:
- concentrate around Hsinshih;
- destroy forces before they complete second-line organization;
- redirect Uemura / 6th / Nara.

Revised:
- the same operational conclusion is reached **~6–10 h earlier** within 26 Sep.

Why not a full day earlier:
- Chinese night movement remains difficult to observe;
- the Japanese need 25 Sep ground reports to confirm the second-line concentration;
- a premature turn based only on aerial trend would risk chasing a false axis.

### Consequence
Orders / route changes begin circulating during 26 Sep with:
- better radio continuity;
- E5 courier / command movement;
- pre-marked alternate roads.

Leading elements can begin the eastward reorientation earlier, but the principal movement still belongs to 27 Sep.

## 4. Uemura Detachment — surrounded contact / breakout

Historical:

- breaks through / destroys forces near Taniangchiao on 27 Sep.

Revised advantages:
- better boat / river-route logistics;
- Navy E5 reconnaissance/BDA;
- faster command reports;
- more accurate identification of the main Chinese concentration;
- less mechanical loss among support craft.

Chinese advantages:
- reinforcement from un-attacked sectors still arrives;
- terrain / night movement remains effective;
- Uemura is still a relatively small detachment.

### Revised result
The breakout / local destruction occurs:
**late 26 Sep to early 27 Sep**, center about **8–14 h earlier** than matched-history center.

This is a small nonlinear clock change.

It does NOT:
- trap the Chinese reinforcement mass;
- create a corps pocket;
- make Uemura a mechanized exploitation force.

Additional local Chinese losses rise because the Japanese strike is better synchronized with the actual retreat route.

## 10. Chinese adaptation

Chinese command increasingly recognizes:
- daylight regrouping is expensive;
- repeat use of obvious road villages draws BDA/restrike;
- Japanese high cover makes opportunistic fighter interception more difficult.

Adaptation:
- more night movement;
- smaller packets;
- greater use of hill tracks;
- command/supply dispersion;
- deliberate rear-guard action to buy time.

Cost:
- slower reassembly;
- higher march fatigue;
- more difficult artillery/supply synchronization.

Benefit:
- Japanese reconnaissance never becomes omniscient.
