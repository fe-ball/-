## 1. Historical frame

The East Shanxi operation has not ended on 31 July.

Important historical sequence:
- Japanese main forces continue local mopping-up / follow-on operations in early August.
- 7–13 Aug: 108th, 36th and 109th-Division family forces sweep around Qin County / Taiyue area and hit elements of the Chinese 42nd and 169th Divisions and Communist forces.
- 10–13 Aug: 20th Division mops up the Chinese 85th Division northwest of Jincheng and nearly overruns its divisional headquarters.
- 10 Aug: First Army orders 20th Division to begin gradual departure from the Jincheng area and advance toward Lu'an / Qin County between 16 and 18 Aug.
- Chinese forces exploit Japanese relocation and subsequently retake Jincheng on 21 Aug.

The key operational question is therefore:
**does a healthier 20th Division cause Japan to hold Jincheng longer, or does it execute the same redistribution more cleanly?**

## 5. Jincheng retention question

### Why “keep Jincheng because we are stronger” is rejected

The 20th Division is healthier, but:
- Jincheng is outside the preferred long-term concentration geometry;
- Japanese forces cannot permanently garrison every swept county;
- the First Army still needs strength around Lu'an / Qin County / Taiyue;
- Chinese forces can exploit a fixed Jincheng garrison while operating around it;
- keeping an extra battalion at Jincheng simply means one fewer battalion elsewhere.

Therefore:
**the 10 Aug decision to leave Jincheng between 16 and 18 Aug is retained.**

This is not a forced withdrawal decision.
It is an operational redistribution decision.

### Revised difference

Because the division is healthier:
- withdrawal preparation is more orderly;
- rear guard / route security can be stronger;
- 39th Brigade does not need emergency rest before movement;
- selected patrol / sweep activity can continue through 10–13 Aug without jeopardizing the later move.

## 8. Chinese adaptation

By early August the Chinese side has learned enough to impose real costs on Japanese reconnaissance.

Common adaptations:
- day movement broken into smaller groups;
- supply / headquarters elements dispersed;
- more night movement;
- bivouacs moved away from obvious village / road nodes;
- temporary command sites;
- local false tracks / decoys where practical.

Effect:
- E5 direct target yield falls.
- the main value shifts toward:
  - confirming repeated movement patterns;
  - identifying where a concentration is *probably* forming;
  - warning against unsupported pursuit.

Cost to China:
- slower concentration;
- more march fatigue;
- reduced efficiency of supply / staff movement.

No theater-wide logistics-collapse multiplier is applied.
