## Branch essence

The China-peace route remains in force. Japan does not enter war against the US, UK, Netherlands, or USSR through 1944 in the center branch. The major new divergence is that Germany recognizes the mature Japanese E5/E6 turbine-aircraft system early enough to negotiate a full technology package, not merely isolated engine samples.

- 1940: Japan and Germany agree to transfer the **E5 full package**: engine family, E5 Twin airframe, reconnaissance/communications integration, maintenance, inspection, tooling, training, and production method.
- 1941: Germany begins producing and operating Germanized E5-D aircraft. These become the Luftwaffe's practical jet school and high-speed reconnaissance/courier family.
- German E5-D field experience is returned to Japan and influences later E6 service blocks.
- 1942–43: Germany negotiates access to E6. The eventual German E6-D is therefore not a simple license copy but a **back-cross / hybrid derivative**: Japanese E6 lineage containing some returned German E5-D field lessons, then re-Germanized with German equipment and operational requirements.
- In parallel, indigenous German jets (Jumo 004/BMW 003, Me 262, Ar 234, etc.) mature earlier because the E5 package removes part of the failure-search, production, maintenance, and service-integration burden.
- By 1944 the Luftwaffe is not merely receiving a few jets; it has several years of institutional jet-operating experience.
- Public Japanese military/civil jet use, including government and high-speed transport derivatives, reinforces Soviet concern that the 1939 Nomonhan-era E5 was already an obsolete generation. This raises the quality and persistence of Soviet Far East force retention.

## Critical guard

Germany still has to solve German-specific materials, compressor, production, airframe, logistics, and combat problems.
