# LEADER PERCEPTION MODEL — 1944-12-31

Keep FACT / ESTIMATE / BELIEF / PLAN / PUBLIC-LINE separate.

## Japan

### Hirohito / Kido
BELIEF: war avoidance has produced tangible national gains, but Soviet postwar power could become the next existential problem.  
PLAN: preserve cabinet balance and avoid irreversible commitments until the European end-state is clearer.

### Konoe
BELIEF: the best Japanese outcome is continued multipolar exhaustion in Europe without Japanese belligerency.  
FEAR: Japan itself activating US total war, or Germany collapsing so rapidly that the USSR becomes the uncontested continental land power.  
ERROR RISK: overestimating how long diplomatic equilibrium can be managed.

### Toyoda
PLAN: keep Tripartite membership from functioning as automatic US-war machinery; preserve US trade; manage North Indochina normalization; keep Soviet treaty intent ambiguous until useful.

### Tojo
BELIEF: the main northern issue is no longer opportunistic partition of a collapsing USSR; it is the danger of a victorious USSR eventually freeing western armies for the Far East.  
PLAN: maintain credible northern options and deepen army quality while avoiding premature war.

### Sugiyama / Umezu
BELIEF: Japan can fight a serious Primorye campaign; it cannot assume that it can conquer the USSR by itself.  
ERROR RISK: extending Manchurian logistical performance too far into Soviet territory.

### Oikawa / Nagano / Koga / Yamamoto
Common point: do not trade the compounding value of peace for an unnecessary US war.  
Differences: Nagano watches the long US shipbuilding curve; Koga builds a genuine contingency fleet; Yamamoto explores asymmetric long-range systems and strategic reconnaissance without treating them as a reason to seek war.

## Germany

### Hitler
BELIEF: Soviet operational reserves can be exhausted; 1944-45 industry/fuel/new weapons permit restoration of initiative.  
PLAN: hold politically/economically valuable areas as long as possible while creating a mobile reserve for counterstroke.  
ERROR RISK: demanding both rigid territorial defense and reserve conservation.

### Manstein
BELIEF: the Soviet state is not on its last reserve, but each major offensive has a finite operational reserve.  
PLAN: trade space, preserve formations, allow Soviet mobile forces to extend, then destroy them by concentrated armored counterstroke.  
GOAL: restore strategic stalemate, not reconquer the 1942 line in one campaign.

### Speer
BELIEF: industry can still support the war under this branch; manpower becomes the harder ceiling.  
PLAN: maximize usable weapons, transport and fuel-supported operational capacity rather than prestige output.

### Guderian / OKH
PLAN: rebuild existing Panzer formations, avoid penny-packet commitment, preserve operational reserves.

## USSR

### Stalin
BELIEF: Japan has repeatedly declined opportunities to attack, making some Far-East release possible, but the Japanese threat cannot be dismissed.  
PLAN: continue western pressure while testing how much can be drawn from the Far East; use diplomacy to keep Japan neutral.  
FEAR: a German-Japanese timing coincidence while Soviet reserves are committed west.

### Zhukov / Vasilevsky
BELIEF: German fronts can still be broken, but German formations are harder to destroy than historical late-war expectation.  
PLAN: heavier preparation, deeper artillery/logistic concentration, more care with mobile-force commitment as cadre losses rise.

## Britain

### Churchill
BELIEF: Germany must be defeated, but a Soviet monopoly over Germany and central Europe is dangerous.  
PLAN: pull the US toward deeper European commitment, establish Greek/East-Med barriers, and develop a French western-entry capability.  
CONSTRAINT: no US continental army and insufficient British manpower for anti-Soviet continental war.

## United States

### Roosevelt / Hull
BELIEF: Germany must not dominate Europe; Japan should not be unnecessarily converted into an additional enemy.  
PLAN: support UK/USSR, manage Atlantic escalation, preserve leverage over Japan through trade/licensing rather than a self-defeating blanket embargo.  
OPEN: 1944 election outcome.

### Marshall / King / Stimson / Knox
BELIEF: Japan remains a heavily armed neutral, not a friendly state. Any European-war commitment must preserve enough Pacific deterrent to prevent Japanese opportunism.

## France

### de Gaulle
GOAL: France must be liberated and represented by French forces, then re-enter the great-power order; a Soviet-controlled Germany to the Rhine is unacceptable.  
CONSTRAINT: insufficient autonomous ground power.

### Petain / Laval / Darlan
Shared constraint: Germany may be losing strategically but can still punish an early defection.  
Split: Laval/pro-German elements prefer accommodation; Darlan-type power brokers want a survivable exit once military insurance is real; Petain prioritizes state continuity.

## Italy

### Mussolini
BELIEF: Soviet war should be stabilized/limited; bringing the US into the war is disastrous; Mediterranean interests matter more to Italy than deep eastern objectives.

### King / Ambrosio
BELIEF: a German collapse requires an exit plan, but an early defection without immediate Allied protection invites German occupation.
