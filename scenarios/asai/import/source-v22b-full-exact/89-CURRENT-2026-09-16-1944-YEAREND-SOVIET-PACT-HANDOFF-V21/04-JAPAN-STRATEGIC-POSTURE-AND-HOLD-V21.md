# JAPAN STRATEGIC POSTURE / HOLD — 1944 YEAR-END

## Core posture

`HOLD = ACTIVE` is an active armed-neutrality strategy, not inactivity.

Japan is using peace to compound:
- industrial capacity;
- shipping and oil stocks;
- pilot/crew and technical training;
- fleet construction;
- armored/artillery/logistics capability;
- information access through normal diplomacy with all major camps.

## North

Kwantung remains the visible coercive instrument. Periodic railway-mobilization exercises, air redeployment and large maneuvers prevent Moscow from fully treating Japan as harmless.

The objective is not to simulate permanent imminent attack at maximum cost. It is to keep enough uncertainty that Soviet Far-East release remains limited.

`N-OPP` (opportunistic entry into German victory): **YELLOW/AMBER** by 1944 year-end.

`N-PREV` (preventive-security logic if Soviet victory frees a large Red Army): **HIGH-YELLOW**.

`N-PREV-LONG`: ACTIVE — the threat model now includes the post-European-war disposition of the Red Army, not merely current Far-East forces.

## Soviet treaty diplomacy

Tokyo deliberately avoids giving Moscow an early unconditional promise of automatic long-term neutrality. Practical relations can remain calm while the legal 1945 denunciation deadline stays open.

This ambiguity is part of HOLD because early reassurance may release additional Soviet forces westward.

## United States

Active objective: `US-JPN-NONBELLIGERENCY`.

Japan seeks a tacit understanding that a European-origin US-German war does not automatically produce a US-Japan war, in exchange for continued Japanese non-expansion and restrictions on re-export of sensitive US-origin goods to the European Axis.

General petroleum trade is kept conceptually separate from high-grade aviation fuel, TEL, advanced machine tools, electronics, bearings and special-alloy controls.

## North Indochina

`NFI-NORM = ACTIVE`.

With the China war over, the residual military rationale for special northern-Indochina privileges weakens. V21 direction is gradual military normalization and replacement by commercial, port, aviation and consular arrangements before a restored French government can turn the issue into a major anti-Japanese case.

## China

`CHINA-NORMALIZATION-II = ACTIVE`.

Japan avoids forcing immediate Chinese legal recognition of Manchukuo. Stability, border quiet, trade, shipping, finance and infrastructure access are treated as more valuable than a provocative recognition demand.

## Southern resource policy

Japan buys rather than seizes oil, rubber, tin and food. It is increasingly willing to accept end-use monitoring / anti-reexport controls when necessary to preserve trade.

The strategic objective is a commercial resource sphere that does not trigger US/UK/NL coalition war.

## Strategic stockpiles

Peace does not reduce stockpiling; it makes it rational. Petroleum, rubber, nonferrous metals, key spares, high-value catalysts/additives and industrial consumables are accumulated against future sanctions or war.

## Current strategic paradox

Japan benefits if a western European bloc prevents a Soviet monopoly over postwar Germany. But if Soviet forces are stopped farther east and occupation burdens are smaller, the USSR may be able to redeploy forces to the Far East earlier. `WESTERN-ENTRY-JPN = WATCH / MIXED`.
