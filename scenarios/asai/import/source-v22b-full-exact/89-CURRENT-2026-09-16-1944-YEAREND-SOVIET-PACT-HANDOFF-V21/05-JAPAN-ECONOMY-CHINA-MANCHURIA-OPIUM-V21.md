# JAPAN ECONOMY / CHINA / MANCHURIA / OPIUM — V21

## Economic structure

The central divergence is not an implausibly explosive GDP miracle. It is the quality of output and allocation:
- merchant shipping survives;
- imports continue;
- oil remains an economic flow problem rather than an imminent exhaustion clock;
- civilian transport does not collapse under Pacific logistics;
- skilled labor is not consumed at historical wartime rates;
- plant maintenance and capital renewal continue;
- armaments can expand without replacing vast combat losses.

V21 retains earlier working comparisons rather than claiming an exact GDP series. Japan proper, Korea/Taiwan and Manchukuo are healthier than historical 1943-44 counterparts, especially in civilian logistics, inventories, maintenance and investment.

## Manchukuo

Growth is biased toward coal, steel, electricity, chemicals, machine industry, rail/road logistics and military-support infrastructure because the Kwantung burden remains high.

The improved tax base reduces dependence on politically toxic monopoly revenue.

## Opium gradual prohibition — working close

By the 1943 close used in V21:
- registered habitual legal users: **~300,000-400,000**;
- legal opium flow: **~45-60% of 1938**;
- legal poppy area: **~35-50% of 1938**.

Policy:
- new legal registration largely stopped;
- lapsed/deceased licenses not reissued;
- legal purchase ceilings decline over time;
- urban opium dens are progressively replaced by registered distribution/medical facilities;
- legal cultivation is cut while alternative crops/finance are expanded;
- anti-smuggling becomes customs/railway-police/special-monopoly work.

The problem does not disappear. It changes from an overt urban monopoly economy to a smaller legal residue plus frontier/underground trafficking.

Target remains near-zero nonmedical legal sales around 1947-48, not a claim of zero illicit narcotics.

## China after peace

The National Government is stronger than historical 1944 because it recovers ports, customs, rail and tax bases and avoids years of continued Japanese operations. The CCP also survives but lacks part of the historical 1942-45 anti-Japanese expansion environment.

Japan-China relations remain cold and transactional:
- asset/debt settlement;
- shipping and port trade;
- machinery exports;
- finance and banking;
- transport normalization;
- Manchukuo legal dispute shelved rather than solved.
