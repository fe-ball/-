# V21 REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — 1942-09-01 THROUGH 1944-12-31

This file is the chronology spine for the replay developed after V20. It records accepted current replay and clearly marks major working ranges.

## 1942 Sep-Oct — Japanese card, Stalingrad, seasonal closure

- Japan continues diplomatic price discovery with Berlin while withholding a Soviet attack.
- Tokyo's core question shifts from "join the winner" to "is Germany actually going to win without Japanese intervention, and if Japanese intervention is decisive, what is its price?"
- Japanese northern combat capability is re-audited: E6 high-speed reconnaissance/attack, escort fighters and conventional bombers, improved artillery reconnaissance/fire control, 47mm armored forces, engineer/logistics improvements, and early rotorcraft all mean actual combat power is greater than passive border pressure reveals.
- Operational season matters. By late Sep/Oct the 1942 large northward offensive window is decaying even though limited Primorye operations remain possible.
- Germany pushes hard in Stalingrad factory districts but does not destroy the Soviet bridgehead.
- No historical Torch. No historical Pacific-war diversion of British Empire formations. North Africa follows a different track; this is later corrected to British capture of Libya by early 1943 while Vichy Tunisia remains neutral.

## 1942 Nov — Uranus

- Operation Uranus still occurs.
- Soviet Far-East retention does not mean five divisions simply vanish from Uranus. The direct high-impact absence is concentrated in formations such as 126th/422nd on the southern wing, while other retained formations affect earlier defensive attrition and later Winter Storm defense.
- Northern breakthrough remains powerful; southern breakthrough is slower and more costly.
- Encirclement of Sixth Army closes roughly 1-2 days later than historical working baseline.

## 1942 Dec — Winter Storm divergence

- No Torch means no Tunisia draw on German ground and transport-air assets; 10th Panzer is available for eastern use in the branch.
- Soviet 87th/98th Rifle Divisions remain in the Far East rather than reinforcing the Myshkova defense in the historical pattern.
- Winter Storm advances farther than historically. Sixth Army has somewhat better airlift and limited breakout fuel.
- A narrow land corridor is opened in the late-Dec working replay.
- Hitler initially insists on corridor supply / Stalingrad retention, then gradually permits extraction as Little Saturn threatens the wider southern position.
- **Working escape band:** roughly 70,000-100,000 Sixth Army personnel escape; the corridor is subsequently closed and a large residual pocket remains.

## 1943 Jan-Feb — southern withdrawal and Stalingrad end

- 1st Panzer Army exits the Caucasus in better order; 17th Army falls toward Kuban positions.
- Residual Stalingrad forces are destroyed/surrender in early Feb rather than surviving far into spring.
- German transport-aircraft and specialist-cadre losses are lower than historical branch.

## 1943 Feb-Mar — stronger Third Kharkov

- Soviet winter advance still overruns large areas and temporarily takes Kharkov.
- Manstein counterattack is stronger because of better preserved mobile forces, no Tunisia drain, healthier Luftwaffe and retained transport/logistics assets.
- Kharkov and Belgorod are retaken somewhat earlier/more firmly than historical baseline.
- Mud and exhaustion still prevent a drive to Kursk.

## 1943 Apr-Jun — Citadel preparation and US-production correction

- Historical US total-war production is not automatically imported. The US remains a heavily armed nonbelligerent: prewar naval expansion and Anglo-Soviet support continue, but no Pearl Harbor-driven total mobilization.
- Soviet Lend-Lease remains high relative to historical deliveries, especially trucks, food, industrial materials, rail and general logistics, because US domestic military absorption is lower even though total production is lower.
- Germany has no US daylight strategic-bombing campaign, no Torch/Italy front and no Normandy threat at historical scale.
- Japan conducts strong northern exercises/demonstrations; limited Soviet Far-East release is possible but coastal/Primorye core remains.

## 1943 Jul — Citadel

- 5 Jul attack date broadly retained.
- Model's northern pincer is stopped much as historically; Soviet Kutuzov offensive forces German northern withdrawal.
- Southern pincer performs materially better. Prokhorovka counterattack is more costly for Soviet armor; German mobile reserve is thicker and Luftwaffe local superiority more consistent.
- Prokhorovka is taken around 14-15 Jul in the working replay.
- Kursk is not captured; northern pincer failure kills the original encirclement concept.
- Southern limited offensive continues until about 18-20 Jul, then is deliberately reduced to preserve mobile reserve against Soviet counteroffensives.

## 1943 Aug-Sep — Rumyantsev and Kharkov

- Soviet Rumyantsev starts broadly on schedule, with 1st Tank Army carrying more of the deep role and 5th Guards Tank Army less recovered from prior losses.
- Belgorod falls.
- German armored counterblows around Bogodukhov are stronger. Soviet tank armies take heavier losses and lose deep exploitation momentum.
- Kharkov remains German through 23 Aug; it is later evacuated deliberately in early Sep as the wider Donbas/Dnieper withdrawal makes the salient unattractive.
- German southern formations reach the Dnieper in better order, but the river line is not a magically completed fortress.

## 1943 Oct-Nov — Dnieper / Kiev divergence

- Soviet bridgeheads are established but somewhat smaller/more contested.
- German mobile reserve is larger because Italy has not collapsed and no US-led Mediterranean land campaign exists.
- Historical 6 Nov Kiev loss does not occur. Lyutezh attack expands the bridgehead but German reserve prevents immediate operational breakthrough.
- German counterattack in mid/late Nov stops the deep Soviet thrust; Kiev remains German at 30 Nov.

## 1943 Dec — Kiev finally falls

- Soviet winter offensive is reshaped around isolating Kiev rather than exploiting a city already held.
- German command receives conditional withdrawal authority before encirclement becomes terminal.
- Kiev is evacuated and falls around 29 Dec in the current replay.
- Soviet westward exploitation starts later than historical Zhytomyr-Berdichev tempo.

## 1944 Jan-Mar — expensive Soviet advance, German cadre preservation

- Soviet forces continue west; Zhytomyr/Berdichev fall later than historical timing.
- Korsun encirclement occurs, but German relief/breakout conditions are better. Working result: roughly 70-80% of encircled personnel escape while much heavy equipment is lost.
- Nikopol / Krivoi Rog are eventually abandoned; Romania is not yet broken.
- Manstein remains in command at 31 Mar in this branch due stronger operational record.
- No historical Big Week; German fighter force, pilot pool and aviation industry are materially healthier.

## 1944 Apr-Jun — Crimea, Romania, Bagration opening

- Crimea is lost; evacuation is better and 17th Army manpower destruction smaller than historical branch.
- Soviet spring Romanian offensive fails to produce the historical late-summer political collapse.
- No Normandy, no Italy front, no US Oil Campaign. Germany shifts some western mobile divisions east while still retaining occupation/coastal defense forces.
- Bagration starts around 22 Jun. Soviet breakthrough succeeds, but German reserve and rail/air capacity are greater.
- Vitebsk/Bobruysk still cause serious losses; Army Group Centre is not yet annihilated.

## 1944 Jul — Minsk abandonment, Bagration becomes breakthrough rather than annihilation

- Model pushes for withdrawal to save armies rather than hold Minsk to destruction.
- Minsk is deliberately abandoned around 4-6 Jul.
- **Working AG Centre loss band by this phase:** about 170,000-230,000 personnel; roughly 10-14 divisions destroyed or needing deep rebuild.
- Soviet advance continues but with lower exploitation speed than historical branch.
- Lvov-Sandomierz still begins; Lvov falls around late Jul/early Aug.
- Historical Warsaw Uprising trigger conditions are absent on 1 Aug; no uprising is launched.

## 1944 Aug-Sep — Vistula, Romania, Finland

- Soviet Vistula bridgeheads form, including Sandomierz, but are smaller and under heavier German counterpressure.
- Warsaw remains German; Home Army retains main strength.
- A renewed Soviet Romanian offensive gains ground but does not produce the historical two-day Army Group collapse.
- Historical 23 Aug Romanian coup does not trigger. Antonescu survives; royal/opposition and government peace channels remain active.
- Finland does not close the historical Sept armistice; de facto quiet and secret peace exploration grow while German northern withdrawal planning develops.
- Me 262 / Ar 234 are more useful than historical branch; Type XXI enters limited initial operational use; V-weapons impose psychological/resource pressure but do not reverse the war.

## 1944 Oct-Dec — German mobile reserve spent at Sandomierz

- Germany chooses the Sandomierz bridgehead as the best operational target for its accumulated late-1944 mobile reserve.
- Romania remains under pressure but not yet broken; Finland remains politically on the way out but formally unresolved.
- A 6-8 mobile-division-class main striking group, supported by infantry, assault guns, artillery and healthier Luftwaffe, attacks the bridgehead after ground conditions harden.
- German forces regain substantial ground and retake Sandomierz city in the current working replay.
- The Soviet west-bank lodgment is compressed toward southern/Baranow sectors but not eliminated.
- Germany spends a large portion (working estimate roughly 40-50%) of the mobile reserve it had accumulated for a major counterstroke.
- Historical-form Jan 1945 Vistula-Oder offensive is therefore not assumed.
- Warsaw remains German-held; no 1944 Warsaw uprising; AK remains a major unresolved political/military factor.
- Romania remains Axis; Ploiesti remains an Axis fuel source.
- US-Germany formal war still has not begun.
