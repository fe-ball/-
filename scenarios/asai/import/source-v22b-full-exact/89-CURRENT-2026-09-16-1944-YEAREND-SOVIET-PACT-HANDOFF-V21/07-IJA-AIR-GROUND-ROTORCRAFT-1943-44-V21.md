# IJA / AIR / GROUND / ROTORCRAFT — 1943-44 V21 CONSOLIDATION

## Airpower layers

By 1943-44, the worldline no longer has a single "Asai jet miracle" standing above historical piston fleets. Multiple layers mature:

- E6 Single/Twin: mature high-speed reconnaissance / interception / special attack family;
- E7: test/evaluation generation; aerodynamic integration increasingly becomes the limiting technology rather than raw engine output;
- improved conventional fighters: Zero/Hayabusa/Shoki and next-generation high-output piston designs benefit from better engine/propeller/QC/maintenance conditions;
- large transports and long-range aviation continue without Pacific combat attrition.

## Air refueling / transport

Working close:
- early small transfer trials precede 1941;
- L5Si tanker kit creates practical top-up capability by 1943 working clock (~4.2-4.6 t transfer band inherited from prior notes);
- heavy one-off Shinzan transfer trials are experimental, not a tanker fleet;
- AT-3/Ki-92 tanker concept reaches ~12-14 t transfer class in the later technical shadow.

AT-3/Ki-92: V21 working close accepts a **4-6 aircraft initial/evaluation lot by late 1943**, without claiming mass fleet service.

## H2/H3 rotorcraft

- H2: no longer just four prototypes by 1943 working clock. Low-rate limited-service/evaluation fleet is appropriate; order-of-tens total rather than formation-scale mass use.
- principal army value: artillery observer/communications insertion, casualty evacuation, high-value liaison and terrain-independent movement of small teams.
- H3: medium 3.2 t / ~900-1000 shp class remains prototype/early-flight territory by 1943; not a mass combat air-assault aircraft.

## Armor

- 47mm new-turret Chi-Ha remains an important combat layer but is not a T-34 frontal solution.
- Chi-He: low-rate production/service introduction by the 1943 working clock is appropriate; three-man turret and standard radio matter as much as raw armor/gun.
- emergency 75mm turret branch: prototype/early low-rate direction; ~18.6-19 t class and ~680-720 m/s gun working band inherited from ground closeout.
- next 23-25 t medium tank branch: formally developed after T-34 is recognized as a standard enemy type; ~75mm 770-800 m/s and ~55-60mm frontal design band remains a development target, not 1943 mass OOB.
- 75mm fixed-fighting-compartment SPG remains a limited but useful direct-support / emergency anti-armor layer.

## Artillery / reconnaissance integration

The most important change is systemic:
- repeated aerial photography;
- stereo/mosaic interpretation and change detection;
- coordinate registration;
- survey and meteorology;
- muzzle-velocity/ammunition-lot correction;
- sound ranging and flash spotting;
- radio distribution;
- follow-up reconnaissance;
- H2 observer-team emplacement at selected terrain points.

This does not create modern real-time counterbattery radar. It does allow fixed/semi-fixed artillery, headquarters, rail yards, airfields and logistics sites to be attacked from much better precomputed data than 1939 Japanese artillery could use.
