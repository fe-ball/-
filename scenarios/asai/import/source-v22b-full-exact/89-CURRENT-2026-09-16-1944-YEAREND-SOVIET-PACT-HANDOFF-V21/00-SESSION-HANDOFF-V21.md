## 2. Worldline essence

Japan ended the China Incident in 1941, did not occupy South Indochina, did not trigger the historical US/UK/NL embargo cascade, did not attack Pearl Harbor or Southeast Asia, and remains formally at peace with the United States, Britain, the Netherlands and the USSR through the current clock.

Germany attacked the USSR historically in June 1941. Japan's large Kwantung posture retained more Soviet strength in the Far East than historically, but Japan did not cross the border. The eastern-front result is not a German victory: the Red Army still survives, counterattacks, takes Stalingrad, and advances west. The divergence is that Soviet victories are usually more expensive and German formations more often escape destruction.

The United States remains a formally nonbelligerent but highly armed supplier to Britain and the USSR. Its absence from the European ground war and daylight strategic bombing creates a much healthier late-war German military economy than historically.

## 4. Accepted 1942-44 replay headline

- Stalingrad: Uranus succeeds but with a somewhat weaker southern wing; encirclement completion is delayed. Winter Storm is materially stronger. A temporary ground corridor is opened; roughly **70,000-100,000** Sixth Army personnel escape in the current working range. Residual pocket surrenders in early Feb 1943.
- German Caucasus withdrawal succeeds better; 1st Panzer Army is recovered in relatively good order; 17th Army falls back to the Kuban before later strategic retreat.
- Third Kharkov: German counterstroke is stronger; Kharkov/Belgorod are retaken, but Germany does not regain 1942 strategic depth.
- Citadel 1943: northern pincer fails; southern pincer performs better, Prokhorovka is temporarily taken and Soviet armored reserves suffer heavier losses; Kursk is not captured.
- Soviet 1943 summer/autumn offensives still recover Orel, Belgorod, Kharkov and Donbas. German withdrawal to the Dnieper is more orderly.
- Kiev remains German through Nov 1943 and falls only in late Dec after a renewed Soviet winter offensive.
- 1944: German retreats continue but major pockets (Korsun, Crimea, Bagration) are less destructive in manpower than historically.
- Bagration remains a Soviet strategic victory but does not destroy Army Group Centre. Minsk is abandoned; German losses in the V21 working band are about **170,000-230,000 personnel** with roughly **10-14 divisions** destroyed or requiring deep rebuild, rather than historical-scale group annihilation.
- Warsaw Uprising does not fire on 1 Aug 1944 and does not occur in 1944; Warsaw and much of the Home Army remain intact at year end.
- Romania does not undergo the historical 23 Aug coup; Antonescu remains, while royal/opposition and even government channels continue covert peace exploration. Ploiești remains Axis-held.
- Finland does not yet close the historical Sept 1944 armistice; the front becomes increasingly quiet and peace contacts continue.
- Late 1944 German counterattack against the Sandomierz bridgehead succeeds operationally: Sandomierz city is retaken and the Soviet west-bank bridgehead is compressed but not eliminated. This spends a large share of Germany's accumulated mobile reserve and prevents a historical-form January 1945 Vistula-Oder offensive from being assumed.

## 5. Japan at year end

Japan's neutrality is active policy, not passivity. The government simultaneously:

- maintains strong Kwantung threat demonstrations;
- keeps Soviet treaty intentions deliberately ambiguous;
- explores a US-Japan nonbelligerency understanding in case of a US-German war;
- normalizes remaining North Indochina military privileges into commercial/aviation/port arrangements;
- deepens post-peace China trade/finance/transport normalization without forcing immediate Chinese recognition of Manchukuo;
- buys southern resources rather than conquering them and is increasingly willing to accept end-use / re-export controls to preserve trade;
- accumulates strategic stocks and technical/industrial capacity;
- continues naval, air, armored, artillery, rotorcraft and logistics modernization without combat attrition.
