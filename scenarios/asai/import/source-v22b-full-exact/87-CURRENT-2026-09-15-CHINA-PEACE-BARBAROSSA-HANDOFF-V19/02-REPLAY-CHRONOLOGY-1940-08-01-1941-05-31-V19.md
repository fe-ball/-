# REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — 1940-08-01 THROUGH 1941-05-31

This is the V19 chronology spine. It records replay results, not every technical audit performed in the sessions.

## August–September 1940 — Hundred Regiments Offensive

- Japanese command has improved reconnaissance, radio, engineering, recovery and mixed rail/road transport, but does not possess foreknowledge of the coordinated 20 August attack.
- Strategic surprise still succeeds. Rail, communications, bridges, stations and coal/transport infrastructure are attacked; initial destruction is not magically prevented.
- Japanese recognition of a coordinated multi-line offensive becomes faster and more coherent after the attack begins.
- Shinaoshan-type mountain positions do not vanish under technical superiority; initial Japanese counterattacks can still fail.
- Temporary E6 North-China deployment operates as rapid-response armed reconnaissance/fire brigade, not omniscient mountain search.
- Improved logistics allow rescue, repair, artillery, engineers and mobile reserves to keep acting deeper/longer.
- Eighth Route Army withdraws/disperses earlier where the cost of daylight concentration rises. Its organization survives.
- Do not use an automatic fixed casualty multiplier. The structural result is: deeper Japanese punitive pressure -> more Eighth Route manpower/rear-area/reconstitution damage; Japan may also take more absolute casualties because it fights farther and longer even at a favorable exchange ratio.

## September–November 1940 — counter-sweeps, Indochina, diplomacy

- The second Hundred-Regiments phase and later Japanese counter-sweeps diverge locally rather than reproducing every historical outcome.
- Japanese motor transport, tractors, engineers, recovery and aviation increase operational persistence along roads and supported axes; mountain/off-road limits remain.
- North French Indochina occupation/base use proceeds broadly along historical political-military lines. E6 presence grows from small test/support detachments toward a small routine support core; this is not a new independent fleet beyond production ledgers.
- Burma Road reopening restores a real external-supply path but does not erase trucking/spares/management bottlenecks.
- Nanning is abandoned/shrunk more orderly than history because Indochina reduces the reason to hold the Guangxi salient and improved logistics reduce abandonment loss.
- November counter-sweeps in Taihang/Taiyue continue to pressure Eighth Route rear areas. Guanjianao does not become a Japanese annihilation disaster; improved relief/communications slightly reduce the value of fighting extra hours, not a magical casualty collapse.

## November 1940 — peace gate reopened

Historical context already contained years of peace work; November is not the beginning of contact.

Branch turn:
- Chongqing has both reasons to test peace and reasons to wait for US/UK support.
- Japan is militarily more comfortable than history but still lacks a decisive military solution to the entire China problem.
- Verified representation/authorization prevents the channel from being dismissed as simply another unverified intelligence operation.
- **22–24 Nov:** representation and preliminary authority are authenticated enough to justify a real test.
- **25 Nov:** the planned Central Hubei offensive is held. This becomes the first proof that Tokyo/IGHQ can translate peace policy into operational orders.
- Chongqing’s Fifth War Zone also refrains from exploiting the pause with a major attack.
- **28 Nov:** the branch does not mechanically terminate negotiations and does not lock in the historical 30-Nov Nanjing-regime outcome.

## December 1940 — ceasefire becomes a process

- Limited ceasefire is extended rather than treated as one 72-hour anomaly.
- Japanese and KMT regular forces discover that command orders can suppress planned offensives even if patrol/fire incidents continue.
- Withdrawal principle, Manchuria deferral, Nanjing-personnel guarantees and non-permanent North-China garrison concept move into the negotiating space.
- Japanese deep bombing of Chongqing/Kunming and similar targets is curtailed as peace credibility becomes more valuable than sortie generation.
- CCP central behavior remains cautious: no clairvoyant “wreck peace at all costs” order; preserve arms/organization, avoid unauthorized major attacks, watch both Japanese and KMT movement.
- The New Fourth Army movement problem remains dangerous but is treated as a negotiated route/supply/control problem rather than pre-ordained civil-war battle.

## January 1941 — broader ceasefire, Wannan crisis defused

- Major Japanese regular-army offensive plans incompatible with the ceasefire are held.
- The historical South-Henan-type large offensive is not launched.
- New Fourth Army movement is handled with specified routes, liaison, partial supply and upper-level “do not independently attack” instructions.
- Local friction and shots are plausible; they do not escalate into the historical large-scale Wannan destruction event.
- New Fourth Army cadres/manpower are therefore preserved; CCP also does not gain the same historical propaganda event.
- A broader provisional regular-army ceasefire is treated as effective from mid-January.

## February 1941 — withdrawal becomes physical

- Japanese withdrawal planning becomes logistics/administration, not only diplomacy.
- Excess forward artillery/engineer/transport/support elements and selected formations begin rearward concentration and some theater exit.
- Chongqing creates reception/administrative teams so Japanese withdrawals do not become uncontrolled races, looting or local civil war.
- Local collaborationist personnel become a security problem; broad lower-level screening/guarantee becomes a military withdrawal tool, not only a humanitarian concession.
- US policy dilemma sharpens: reward peaceful behavior enough to sustain it, but do not simply refill Japanese war reserves without leverage.

## March 1941 — no Shanggao campaign; peace gains depth

- Historical large-scale Shanggao campaign is not launched.
- Repeated cancellation of planned campaigns changes front-line expectations: the mission has shifted from defeating KMT field armies to holding and withdrawing under policy control.
- Japanese support units and some personnel begin leaving the China theater, but do not count them all as immediately combat-ready in Manchuria.
- Yichang changes from a permanent forward position to an eventual handover asset; inventory/withdrawal preparation starts without immediate abandonment.
- 11 March US Lend-Lease law still exists; China’s future external option strengthens, so Chongqing has less reason to accept a humiliating settlement.
- 12 March Matsuoka departs for Europe; his wider Axis diplomacy does not automatically reopen already implemented China ceasefire decisions.

## April 1941 — neutrality pact + basic China peace agreement

- **13 Apr:** Soviet-Japanese Neutrality Pact occurs on the historical date.
- Nomonhan interpretation differs: Japan is cautious but not psychologically crushed; USSR sees a Japanese army that survived better and is becoming less tied down in China.
- China peace terms converge: staged full withdrawal from China proper, no permanent North-China anti-communist garrison, Manchuria deferred, Hainan later, Nanjing separate-government status ended, no state reparations, property/people handled separately.
- **28 Apr:** basic peace agreement is initialled/working close.

## May 1941 — formal peace and a new strategic problem

- Historical large-scale Zhongyuan/Chungyuan offensive is not launched.
- KMT forces that historically would have suffered another major campaign remain stronger entering the postwar internal competition.
- **15 May:** formal branch peace signed.
- **20 May:** ratification/entry into force.
- Japan’s domestic narrative can credibly claim that the punitive war forced a state-to-state settlement and ended Chongqing’s anti-Japanese war without Japanese battlefield collapse. Whether the result justified four years of cost is explicitly **not** answered by the treaty.
- China withdrawal now starts freeing force, transport and fuel consumption while also temporarily consuming lift for withdrawal.
- US/DEI oil policy becomes a first-order strategic branch variable.
- Current clock: **1941-05-31 24:00**.
