# FUEL / OIL / MIDDLE-DISTILLATE STRATEGIC HANDOFF — V19

## 1. Inherited physical doctrine

Authority remains V16 fuel close unless later technical layers explicitly supersede.

The worldline does not create one universal “jet fuel.” It creates a military middle-distillate pool:

- **Grade A:** aviation GT; tight low-temperature, cleanliness, water and thermal-stability control.
- **Grade B:** common military middle distillate; fast diesel, tanks/trucks/tractors, generators, ground GT, selected marine GT, GT advanced training.
- **Grade C:** broader/lower-grade middle distillate for tolerant diesel/stationary/selected marine use.

Higher grade may be down-used. Lower grade may only be up-used under approved derated schedules and inspection penalties.

E5/E6 restrictions retained:
- clean straight-run kerosene/gas-oil can be broadly usable;
- ordinary diesel/gas-oil on E6 JF -> derated ~810–825 C class;
- ordinary diesel/gas-oil on E6 pure-J -> ~820–835 C ceiling class;
- highest ~920 C selected-Ni rating is prohibited on low-grade fuel;
- lower grade primarily costs temperature/rating and inspection interval, not instantaneous impossibility of flight.

## 3. Why US avgas restrictions do not map cleanly to Japanese airpower

US high-octane aviation-gasoline/TEL controls directly constrain Japanese high-performance piston fleets.

But they do not remove:
- E5/E6 kerosene/middle-distillate turbine operations;
- diesel vehicle/tractor/generator demand;
- ability to shift premium avgas toward piston users that truly require it while turbine/diesel users consume lower-prestige fractions.

The strategic effect is a **fuel cascade**:
- high-octane gasoline -> high-performance piston aircraft;
- Grade A middle distillate -> front-line aviation GT where needed;
- Grade B -> GT training, ground mobility, generators and tolerant turbine users;
- Grade C -> tolerant stationary/marine/diesel users.

## 4. China peace compounds the effect

China peace reduces:
- combat sortie fuel;
- vehicle/air operational tempo;
- some convoy/forward-base consumption.

Production/acceptance/training does not instantly stop.

Therefore by mid-1941 it is plausible that:
- high-octane gasoline remains precious;
- middle-distillate stocks are comparatively comfortable or growing;
- E6/GT training can consume lower military grades without competing directly with premium avgas;
- diesel mobility remains less exposed to an avgas-specific restriction.

Do not convert this into “Japan has infinite fuel.” Navy heavy fuel, total crude/product imports, shipping, refining and strategic stocks remain real constraints.

## 5. US intelligence progression

Do not grant US analysts perfect internal Japanese grade tables at once.

Plausible observation path:
1. premium-avgas restrictions should reduce some Japanese aviation activity;
2. observed turbine/high-speed units and training fall less than expected;
3. Japanese kerosene/gas-oil demand appears to have military-air/ground significance beyond ordinary civilian use;
4. US asks whether middle-distillate licensing is partially bypassing the intended military-air pressure.

Policy problem:
- expanding controls to kerosene/gas-oil makes sanctions look less like “premium military goods” control and more like broad petroleum strangulation;
- doing so after China withdrawal risks destroying the political payoff of Japanese peaceful behavior;
- not doing so allows Japan to retain a meaningful military-energy base.

This tension should feed the US policy replay, not be resolved automatically.

## 6. Dutch East Indies relevance

South-Sumatran/Palembang-type oil remains strategically attractive because ordinary refining can yield useful middle distillates. V16 explicitly rejects the fantasy that raw crude is directly aviation-turbine fuel; refining/finishing capacity still matters.

If Japan buys Indies oil commercially:
- middle-distillate supply can support both diesel and GT users;
- the US must decide whether to tolerate normal commerce or coordinate broader strangulation.

If Japan seeks coercive quantities or military access:
- US/Dutch willingness to keep the commercial valve open falls rapidly.

## 7. Barbarossa connection

Fuel affects the northern option in two opposite ways:
- China peace lowers consumption and improves stock comfort, making a northern contingency more feasible;
- a northern war would consume enormous rail, vehicle, aviation and fuel resources and risks cutting off the trade normalization that created that comfort.

Therefore “more fuel available” increases the menu of options but does not mechanically select northward war.
