## 3. China battlefield loss logic retained

The late-1940 North-China counteroffensive follows a different loss logic than earlier Nationalist reactions.

Earlier in the war, better Japanese reconnaissance often prevents favorable Nationalist counterattacks from maturing into battles; this can lower losses on both sides because the battle never occurs.

Hundred Regiments aftermath is the reverse pattern:
- Japanese logistics/recon/vehicles/air allow pursuit and punitive pressure to go deeper and last longer;
- Eighth Route formations therefore pay more in personnel, rear-area facilities, supply, medical/organizational disruption and delayed recovery;
- Japan can still have a better exchange ratio while taking **more absolute casualties** than history because it fights more and farther.

Do not translate “stronger Japan” into “Japanese losses automatically lower.”

## 7. Fuel asymmetry that US policy must eventually notice

V16 fuel doctrine remains authority:
- Grade A = aviation GT fuel, tight cleanliness/cold-flow/thermal-stability control.
- Grade B = common military middle distillate for fast diesel, vehicles, generators, ground GT, selected marine and advanced GT training.
- Grade C = lower-quality middle distillate for tolerant users.
- higher grades can be down-used; lower grades only up-used under derating/inspection penalties.

1941-03-31 V16 working stock:
- kerosene-family ~380,000 kL
- light/gas-oil family ~400,000 kL
- combined ~780,000 kL
- finished aviation Grade-A reserve target ~40,000 kL, minimum ~30,000 kL
- 1941 aviation turbine liquid planning use ~64,000 kL including training, with advanced training mainly Grade B

Therefore high-octane avgas controls hit piston aviation strongly but do **not** proportionally suppress E5/E6 turbine flying or diesel mobility. China peace also reduces actual combat fuel burn while production/acceptance continues, so turbine/middle-distillate stocks can become increasingly comfortable.

US intelligence should not be given perfect fuel knowledge immediately. A plausible progression is noticing that Japanese high-speed turbine activity does not fall in proportion to avgas constraints, then asking whether kerosene/gas-oil imports have military-air value.
