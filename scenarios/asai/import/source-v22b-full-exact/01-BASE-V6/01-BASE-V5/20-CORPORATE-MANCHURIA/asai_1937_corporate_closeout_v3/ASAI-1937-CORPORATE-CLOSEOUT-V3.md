# Asai Works 1937 corporate / Manchuria closeout audit V3

## Bottom line

Recommended 1937 working company center:

- direct employees: ~10,000 (retain archive band 8,000–12,000)
- company sales: ~¥25.3m; defensible audit band ~¥20.6–30.5m
- paid-in capital: ~¥9m
- total assets/capital employed: ~¥23m
- equity ratio: ~61%
- EBIT: ~¥2.75m (~10.9%)
- net profit: ~¥1.85m (~7.3%)
- 1937 capex: ~¥3.6m, versus depreciation ~¥1.3m
- Greater-Manchurian sales: ~¥1.72m, about 6.8% of company revenue
- Manchukuo-proper direct sales: ~¥1.27m
- Greater-Manchurian staff: ~400

This keeps Asai materially larger and more technically diversified than Toyoda Automatic Loom Works in 1937 without turning it into a Mitsubishi-scale conglomerate.

## Historical calibration anchors

1. Toyoda Automatic Loom Works 1937 sales were ¥16.926m. Its reported profit money for 1937 first and second halves was ¥0.531m and ¥0.561m (about ¥1.092m combined). Source: Nagoya Gakuin University paper reproducing Toyoda 40-year history and Toyo Keizai company tables: https://www2.ngu.ac.jp/uri/syakai/pdf/syakai_vol5101_11.pdf
2. Toyoda increased capital from ¥3m to ¥6m in 1935; the later Koromo 2,000-vehicles/month project was estimated around ¥30m and exceeded the parent company's financing capacity. Source: Toyota 75 Years history: https://www.toyota-global.com/company/history_of_toyota/75years/text/taking_on_the_automotive_business/chapter2/section4/item1.html and item3.html
3. Japanese factory workers in September 1936 averaged ¥1.74/day overall and ¥1.98/day for men; grinding-machine workers, blacksmiths and pattern makers were roughly ¥2.9–3/day in 1938-era reporting. Sources: FRASER/BLS Monthly Labor Review 1937/1938.
4. Average Japanese domestic machine-tool price in 1937 was about ¥2,293/unit versus ¥6,698 imported. This supports keeping Asai's dedicated machine-tool external sales limited and treating gauges/jigs/general machinery separately. Source summarized from Toyo Keizai/Sawai in an interfirm machine-tool study.
5. Current quarantine cost workbook places E4/E5 low-rate/family-scale independent GT engine/module prices mostly around ¥18k–44k for E4/E5 stationary/marine products. This supports ~¥25k–35k realized central pricing on a mixed 1937 delivery portfolio; exact procurement contracts remain open.
6. Current computation provisional model uses factory cost ¥7,000–10,000 and monthly lease ¥300–420 for a 1933–35 standard card-machine set. This is retained only as a working commercial calibration, not a current-branch closed fact.

## Revenue structure

The important result is product mix, not the exact ¥25.3m point estimate.

Central sales mix is roughly:

- special steels / HSS / carbide / finished cutting tools: ¥9.0m (~36%)
- machine tools / gauges / jigs / general precision machinery: ¥3.7m (~15%)
- exhaust turbo / diesel auxiliaries / service: ¥3.1m (~12%)
- independent GT / power sets: ¥2.65m (~10%)
- computation: ¥1.25m (~5%)
- industrial turbomachinery/process machinery: ¥2.3m (~9%)
- legacy textile/general machinery: ¥1.2m (~5%)
- resins/composites/special supplies: ¥0.8m (~3%)
- prototypes/licenses/technical engineering: ¥1.3m (~5%)

Thus the company is still financially a materials/tool/precision-machinery firm. GT is technologically conspicuous, but does not have to carry the company economically in 1937.

## Turbo and GT reconciliation

The current production audit can survive with accounting semantics made explicit:

- exhaust turbo factory completions: ~1,300/year central
- end-user absorption: ~1,135/year central
- central segment revenue: ~¥3.1m/year including new units, spares, service and diesel-auxiliary work
- implied revenue per absorbed unit is ~¥2.7k, but this is only a portfolio average; army vehicle, stationary and large marine units differ substantially

Independent GT:

- factory completions: ~125 cores/year
- customer deliveries: ~88 cores/year central
- segment revenue: ~¥2.65m/year
- weighted realized unit revenue therefore sits around ¥30k, consistent with the quarantine E4/E5 cost/price envelope

Recommendation: elevate the *meaning* of the existing ~1,300 and ~125 numbers to `factory completions/run-rate`; do not label them pure customer sales.

## Workforce / wage sanity check

At 10,000 employees and average employer cash compensation/cost around ¥760/year, annual personnel cost is ~¥7.6m. This is ~30% of ¥25.3m revenue. Given 1936 male factory wages around ¥1.98/day, skilled metal trades around ¥3/day, and a mix of apprentices/women/clerks/engineers, this is demanding but plausible for a high-skill integrated company.

The workforce should not be assigned mostly to turbines. A useful central distribution is:

## Capital and cash flow

A useful central balance-sheet shape is ~¥23m total assets with ~¥14m equity and ~¥9m liabilities. Paid-in capital ~¥9m plus accumulated reserves ~¥5m gives an equity ratio ~61%, matching the archive's intended high-self-capital / independent-control posture.

A 1937 expansion cash-flow sanity check:

- net profit ~¥1.85m
- depreciation ~¥1.3m
- before-working-capital internal cash ~¥3.15m
- working-capital growth consumes roughly ¥0.6m
- capex ~¥3.6m
- modest new borrowing/customer advances of roughly ¥1.0–1.2m closes the funding gap

This is a much cleaner explanation of the 1936–38 turbine/plant expansion than assuming either limitless retained cash or major zaibatsu ownership.

## Manchuria branch economics

Greater-Manchurian central sales ~¥1.72m/year:

- Dairen/Kwantung ~¥0.45m
- Manchukuo proper ~¥1.27m
- headcount ~400
- capital employed ~¥1.25m
- branch EBIT ~¥0.11m (~6%)

The branch is therefore strategically important but not yet a huge profit center. Its real value is customer lock-in, code/form standardization, service infrastructure and access to industrial operating data.

A reasonable organizational split is approximately 140 Japanese/expatriate staff and 260 locally hired clerical, warehouse, service and technical workers. The exact nationality split should remain OPEN unless a personnel history is specifically needed.

## Link to macro uplift

At ¥25.3m central sales, Asai revenue is only about 0.11% of a ¥22.823bn Japan GNP baseline. A Japan-wide worldline uplift of 0.30% is about ¥68.5m, or ~2.7 times Asai annual sales.

This ratio is not a same-year sales multiplier. It includes accumulated pre-1937 stocks of tools, gauges, process know-how, installed computation, reliability practice and customer learning. Therefore ~0.30% remains defensible; ~0.35–0.45% should be treated as upper-side cases rather than the new center.

For Manchukuo, the prior central +0.42% national-income audit gives ~¥11.35m additional income. Direct Asai Manchukuo-proper sales are only ~¥1.27m, so the ratio is ~8.9x. This looks large if read as a current-year sales multiplier, but becomes plausible only if interpreted as a *stock/network effect* concentrated in existing SMR, mine, steel, power and government capital. Keep +0.42% as WORKING-AUDIT, not CLOSED.
