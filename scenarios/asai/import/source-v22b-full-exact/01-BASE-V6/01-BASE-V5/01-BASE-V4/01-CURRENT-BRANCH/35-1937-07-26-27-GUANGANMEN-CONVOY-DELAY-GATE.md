# 1937-07-26–27 — Guang'anmen convoy-delay / gate-firefight audit

## Verdict

**CLOSED-SAME through 27 July ~04:30, with one explicitly identified counterfactual sensitivity.** The historical Japanese battalion transfer remains more than an hour late because the best current source localizes the delay to **late departure from Fengtai**, not slow road movement, and no source ties that departure delay to truck breakdown, overheating, or poor mechanical readiness. Current-branch Asai heavy-vehicle improvements therefore do not justify moving the convoy onto the planned 16:00 schedule.

If the battalion had actually reached Guang'anmen at the first negotiated opening around 16:00, the exact incident sequence could plausibly have differed. That is a real sensitivity, not dismissed. But the branch does not receive the premise needed to activate it.

## Historical sequence

- 26 Jul: Tientsin Army sends Infantry 2nd Regiment, 2nd Battalion (minus one company in some records) toward Peiping for resident protection. The unit goes by rail to Fengtai, then is placed in **26 trucks** for the final move toward Guang'anmen.
- The Peiping-side arrangement is for the unit to pass the gate around **16:00**.
- **~15:20:** interpreter Saito, sent ahead from Fengtai, reaches Guang'anmen and finds the Chinese side closing the previously open gate and preparing weapons.
- **~16:00:** Sakurai arrives and negotiates with the local martial-law command. The Japanese party obtains an opening/permission for passage.
- The main body is not present on schedule. Iwatani's synthesis states specifically that **departure from Fengtai was late**, and that the unit reached Guang'anmen more than an hour behind plan.
- During that gap the gate is closed again under an order represented as coming from Chin Te-chun / the Peiping authorities, and the defenders' alert posture hardens.
- **~17:40:** after renewed negotiation, the local martial-law command again orders the gate opened.
- **~18:20:** the Japanese truck column begins entering.
- During the early portion of the column's passage, the gate is closed and firing breaks out from the wall/gate area; Japanese troops reply. Machine guns, rifles, grenades and mortars are reported.
- The exact number of vehicles already inside at first fire is **source-sensitive**. Iwatani's synthesis gives a sequence in which the third vehicle is at the gate and six vehicles ultimately enter before the remainder pulls back. Sakurai's field report instead recalls roughly ten leading vehicles already through when the first few shots were heard. The branch therefore closes the split-column fight but not the exact vehicle ordinal.
- Japanese and Chinese accounts dispute responsibility and intention. The Japanese on-scene report places the first shots on the Chinese-defended wall and describes local defenders as then joining in. The event itself is closed; absolute first-shot/command-intent attribution remains short of independent proof.
- Tientsin headquarters initially receives fragmentary reports severe enough to fear the battalion may have been annihilated. At **~22:20** it issues an order for a planned attack from noon 27 Jul.
- By **~04:30 on 27 Jul**, after fuller information, Matsui's intervention, the fact that the prior ultimatum still ran to noon 28 Jul, incomplete civilian evacuation, and recognition that the incident was smaller than first feared, the attack start is temporarily postponed.

## Convoy-delay causality audit

### 1. The recorded delay is a Fengtai-departure delay

This is the decisive wording. The modern source synthesis does not say that the 26-truck convoy left Fengtai on time and then lost an hour to road speed, overheating, breakdowns, mud, towing or traffic. It says **Fengtai departure itself was late**.

That means an Asai improvement in:

- engine thermal margin,
- heavy-truck reliability,
- drivetrain life,
- field repair speed,
- loaded-road speed,

is not, by itself, a cause for deleting the delay.

### 2. The exact 26 vehicles are not automatically Asai turbo trucks

Current branch canon deliberately avoids treating all Japanese Army trucks as Asai turbo-diesels. Asai military turbo installations are concentrated in selected heavy vehicles / tractors and support equipment, while ordinary gasoline trucks remain widespread. The exact make and powertrain of the 26 Guang'anmen vehicles are not closed.

Therefore the branch does not grant the entire convoy an Asai performance package by default.

### 3. Could better vehicle readiness still have caused an earlier Fengtai departure?

Possible in the abstract, but **not established here**. A battalion arriving by rail, detraining, assembling personnel and weapons, allocating 26 trucks, loading, forming march order and waiting on local command/liaison can depart late for many reasons unrelated to truck mechanical readiness.

No current source identifies vehicle failure, starting trouble, overheating, or truck shortage as the reason for the late Fengtai departure. Asai improvements therefore cannot be used to manufacture an earlier departure.

### 4. Punctual arrival is a genuine counterfactual sensitivity

This is not dismissed. The Japanese advance party had already obtained an opening around 16:00. If the main column had been physically present at that moment, passage might have begun before the later reclosure and before the defenders' alert state became still more severe. The exact historical firefight could then have changed or disappeared.

But the current branch preserves the late departure because its cause has not been linked to anything Asai changes. Therefore the sensitivity remains **counterfactual-only**, not an activated branch divergence.

## Asai battlefield crosswalk

- **Heavy vehicles / turbo:** no justified change to departure or arrival time. A few Asai-influenced support vehicles may exist in the wider force, but the 26 trucks are not auto-crosswalked.
- **Communications:** local telephone/liaison and repeated gate negotiation are functioning. The failure is not a terminal-equipment failure. No closed dense tactical-radio network gives the convoy a qualitatively different command picture.
- **Small arms / manufacturing QC:** can modestly improve Japanese stoppage/reliability behavior once the fight begins, but no documented Japanese weapon failure is the tactical hinge.
- **Artillery computation:** not relevant to this gate fight; this is a close-range gate/convoy engagement rather than a planned artillery fire-control problem.
- **Dense-core U/W ammunition:** irrelevant; no armored target drives the engagement.

## Policy consequence

Langfang had already pushed Tientsin Army away from confidence in local diplomatic control. Guang'anmen therefore does **not** create the first policy break from nothing; it hardens and operationalizes it. The temporary 22:20 attack order shows how strongly the event was read in the first hours, while the 04:30 postponement shows that even then the command had not yet abandoned timing/ultimatum/evacuation constraints.

Asai communications reliability does not erase this oscillation. The key problem is a chaotic split-column incident producing fragmentary tactical information, and the current branch has no closed portable-radio revolution that would instantly give Tientsin headquarters a complete picture.
