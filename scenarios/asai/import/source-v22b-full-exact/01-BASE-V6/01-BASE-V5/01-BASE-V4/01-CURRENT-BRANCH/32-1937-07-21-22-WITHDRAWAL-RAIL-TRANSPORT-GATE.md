# 1937-07-21–22 — Withdrawal / rail transport gate

## Verdict

**CLOSED-SAME.** The 20 July artillery clash is followed by a real renewed de-escalation on 21–22 July. Asai-worldline differences affect Japanese readiness margins but do not alter the Chinese withdrawal timetable, Chinese railway repair, or the Tientsin Army's political recommendation against committing the three home divisions.

## Historical sequence

- **21 Jul:** Soong Che-yuan again appeals for peace. Only minor firing is reported in the morning and around 20:00. Chinese troops at Lukouchiao and Yamenkou begin withdrawing rearward.
- **21 Jul:** the 132nd Division's 1st Brigade begins moving toward the Changhsintien concentration area; Central-government forces continue assembling farther south. Feng Chih-an nevertheless reiterates that defense is the principle and that Chinese forces are not to attack first.
- **21 Jul:** asked by Army central authorities for its view on employment of the three home divisions, the Tientsin Army answers that the 29th Army is progressively complying, current Japanese strength is sufficient, and pushing additional large forces into Peiping–Tientsin could instead complicate the situation.
- **21 Jul:** 37th Division troops around Paposhan fail initially to withdraw because the order has not been transmitted through the Chinese command chain. After Japanese inquiry, Chin Te-chun apologizes and orders withdrawal to begin by 20:00. At about 21:40 the troops assemble to move; several unexplained shots cause a temporary pause, after which withdrawal proceeds toward the Huangtsun area.
- **22 Jul:** repair of the Peiping–Hankow railway is completed sufficiently for the planned southward movement. Rail movement of 37th Division elements starts in the evening. A train scheduled for 15:00 actually begins departure at about 18:00 toward Chochow; subsequent units are to follow as they assemble.
- **22 Jul:** removal of the 37th Division from Peiping and the Marco Polo Bridge area, punishment/censure measures, dismissal of an anti-Japanese municipal official, and bans on anti-Japanese publications convince Japanese local authorities that the Hopei-Chahar side is making a substantial compliance effort. Contemporary foreign observers describe the day as exceptionally quiet.

## Asai crosswalk

### Railway repair and Chinese troop transport
The railway repair and the 37th Division's train movement are **Chinese-side railway operations**. No current-branch fact puts Asai Works in the maintenance, rolling stock, dispatching, or power supply of the Peiping–Hankow railway. The 20 July Japanese punitive artillery mission was also not redefined as a systematic railway interdiction mission. Therefore the repair scope and the roughly 18:00 first departure are preserved.

**No Asai acceleration is applied.** Better Japanese machine tools, electrical equipment, vehicles, or artillery computation do not create information or labor inside the Chinese railway organization.

### Tientsin Army view on home divisions
The current Japanese garrison/reinforcement force has a slightly better ready fraction in the Asai worldline because of heavy-vehicle, towing, maintenance and supply quality. This does not reverse the historical recommendation; if anything it marginally reinforces the existing argument that current strength is sufficient. The event remains the same: **Tientsin Army advises against committing the three home divisions at this point.**

### Paposhan order-transmission failure
This is again a Chinese command-chain failure, not a Japanese telephone-quality failure. Asai radio/electrical improvements cannot make an order appear in a foreign command net. The delay and renewed direct inquiry therefore remain.

## Closure

- 21–22 July renewed de-escalation: **CLOSED-SAME**
- Chinese forward withdrawals: **CLOSED-SAME**
- Tientsin Army opposition to using three home divisions: **CLOSED-SAME**
- Paposhan command-order failure and delayed withdrawal: **CLOSED-SAME**
- 22 July railway repair and evening rail transport: **CLOSED-SAME**
- Asai effect: **small Japanese readiness-confidence margin only; no event/timetable divergence**

## Next gate

**23–24 July:** withdrawal stalls, the 132nd Division enters Peiping in greater strength than the Japanese side believes was agreed, Hsiung Pin arrives from Nanking, and on 24 July Japanese negotiators discover that Soong Che-yuan has not understood the detailed implementation agreement in the same way as the negotiators who signed it. This is a command/political-information gate, not primarily an industrial one, but Japanese reinforcement information and mechanized transport begin to affect Soong's strategic calculation.
