# Gate 60 — 1937-11-01 to 1937-11-05
## Shansi: Hsinkou withdrawal / Taiyuan approach
## Central China: Hangchow Bay landing

Status: CLOSED at strategic/calendar level. Hour-scale and subunit casualty details remain WORKING/OPEN.

### Core ruling

#### Shansi
- The 109th Division's pack-horse shortage remains a hard constraint. Its mountain movement cannot be materially accelerated by Asai motorization.
- The Xinkou front begins a general withdrawal on 3 November after the eastern flank is compromised. This remains a command-level withdrawal, but heavily worn Chinese formations do not all retain enough cohesion to occupy the intended Taiyuan outer positions.
- By 5 November, the Japanese 5th Division has advanced toward Yangchu while the 20th Division's pursuit columns are pressing west; dates remain unchanged.
- Chinese unit attrition now affects force availability: several retreating formations remain on paper but contribute little to the immediate Taiyuan defense until reconstituted.
- Asai improves Japanese F/M/S locally, but ambushes, pack transport and mountain routes cap the effect. No one-day calendar shift.

#### Hangchow Bay
- The 10th Army lands in strength on the north shore of Hangchow Bay/Jinshanwei sector before dawn on 5 November.
- The historical landing date and basic geometry remain unchanged. Chinese coastal defenses are too thin for Asai improvements to change whether the landing succeeds.
- The 6th Division arrives from North China in slightly better equipment/readiness condition than the matched baseline because of lower maintenance debt and somewhat lower prior losses. The 18th/114th Divisions and Kunisaki Detachment are relatively fresh.
- Asai effects matter after landing: unloading-to-march readiness, artillery/vehicle availability, repair turnaround and supply classification. These can place the leading elements modestly ahead in hours, but not shift the landing date or create a 24-hour lead by the end of 5 November.
- Naval gunfire-control gains are usable where targets are known, but the landing already enjoys overwhelming naval support; marginal effect on the initial beach seizure is small.

### Attrition/readiness interpretation
P = personnel/junior leaders/fatigue
F = fire support availability
M = mobility/transport readiness
S = supply/maintenance depth

#### Japanese
- IJA 5th Division (Shansi): B-/C+ in P, stronger F/S; remains heavily worn from Xinkou.
- IJA 20th Division: B-/B; pursuit capable.
- IJA 109th Division: B-level personnel but M constrained by pack-horse shortage.
- IJA 6th Division entering 10th Army: baseline B+/A-, Asai A-/B+; meaningful difference mainly in F/M/S, not fresh-manpower count.
- IJA 18th and 114th Divisions: A-/A on arrival.
- Kunisaki Detachment: A-/B+ depending subcomponent; fresh relative to Shanghai veterans.

#### Chinese
- Xinkou/Niangzi retreating formations: C-/D mix. Many division headquarters survive, but battalion/regimental consolidation is common.
- Taiyuan immediate defense cannot count every retreating formation at nominal strength; a significant part is transit/reconstitution rather than usable line strength.
- Hangchow Bay coastal defense: thin and locally overwhelmed; not treated as the equivalent of a fresh field division.

### Calendar decision
- 3 Nov: Xinkou general retreat — SAME DATE.
- 5 Nov: Hangchow Bay landing — SAME DATE.
- 5 Nov: Japanese Shansi pursuit continues toward Taiyuan approaches — SAME DATE.
- Potential Asai divergence: local lead measured in hours and higher equipment readiness, not date.

### End-state
Clock: 1937-11-05 late evening.
- Shanghai main front: Chinese forces remain south/west of the Soochow Creek line under pressure.
- Hangchow Bay: Japanese 10th Army has established the southern landing front; the operational envelopment of Shanghai is now real.
- Shansi: Xinkou has been abandoned; retreating Chinese formations are converging toward/through Taiyuan with uneven cohesion. Japanese 5th and 20th Divisions are pressing toward the Taiyuan/Yutzu axis.
- The next gate (6–9 November) is the first strong candidate for an actual calendar divergence because the Hangchow Bay force begins a rapid inland drive while the Chinese Shanghai army must decide when to abandon the metropolitan front.
