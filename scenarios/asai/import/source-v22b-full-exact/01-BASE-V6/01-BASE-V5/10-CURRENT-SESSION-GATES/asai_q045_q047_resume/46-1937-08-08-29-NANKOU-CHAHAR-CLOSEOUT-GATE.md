# Q-045 — 1937-08-08 to 1937-08-29 — Nankou / Chahar Closeout Gate

Decision: CLOSED-SAME-CALENDAR / CLOSED-SAME-OPERATIONAL-OUTCOME / CLOSED-MINOR-ASAI-SUSTAINMENT / OPEN-DETAIL-LOCAL-TIMINGS

## Historical skeleton

Japanese operational records describe Suzuki Brigade, with attached artillery, attacking the Nankou area and initially bogging down because Chinese strength had increased, terrain was rugged, and weather was unfavorable. Advance elements of the 5th Division were committed progressively. After approximately ten days of hard fighting, Japanese forces broke through on 23 August, occupied Chuyungkuan/Juyongguan and advanced onto the Huailai plain.

Separate Kwantung Army support for the Chahar operation included three mixed brigades and seven infantry companies. Japanese operational records place a Kwantung Army penetration near Wanchuan on 27 August followed by pursuit.

August rains flooded North China rivers and turned low-lying Hopei areas, especially along the Tientsin–Pukou railway, into a sea of mud that greatly hindered movement.

## Worldline adjudication

The Asai worldline does not move the 23 August breakthrough date. The decisive constraints are mountain terrain, weather, pass geometry and the progressive arrival of 5th Division elements.

Asai differences do modestly improve:
- readiness of selected heavy trucks/tractors and recovery vehicles;
- maintenance turnaround for engines, artillery-support vehicles and workshop machinery;
- aircraft serviceability by a few percentage points;
- artillery-data/calibration consistency in units already exposed to the relevant process improvements.

These effects are most useful after the Japanese force emerges onto better roads and the Huailai plain. They do not make a truck climb where no road exists or make reinforcements arrive earlier.

## Force-balance audit at decisive phase

Direct Nankou axis working range:
- Japan: 18,000–28,000 interacting troops, center ~24,000;
- China: 45,000–70,000, center ~57,500;
- Japan 75mm+ artillery: 45–65; China 35–60;
- Japan AFV: 30–60 broadly available on the axis/adjacent support, but terrain sharply discounts their tactical value;
- daylight air-pressure index: Japan ~85 versus China ~20 center.

Campaign-wide strength figures are higher and should not be confused with the direct-axis force. Chinese campaign reconstructions commonly reach about 60,000; Japanese campaign-wide figures likewise include forces distributed across Chahar and supporting axes.

## Closeout

By 29 August the Nankou gate no longer needs tactical village-by-village tracking. The operational result is closed: Chinese forces have lost the pass system and are withdrawing/reorganizing; Japanese and Kwantung Army elements are pursuing west/northwest. The next strategically meaningful North China gate is the organization of the North China Area Army and preparation for the Central Hopei operation.
