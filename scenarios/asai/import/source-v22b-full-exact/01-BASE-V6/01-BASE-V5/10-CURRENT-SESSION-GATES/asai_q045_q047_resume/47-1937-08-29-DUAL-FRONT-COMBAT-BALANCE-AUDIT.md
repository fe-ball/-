# 1937-08-29 Dual-Front Combat Balance Audit

Status: WORKING-AUDIT, intended for worldline gate calculations. These are not claimed as exact historical strength returns. Historical anchors are separated from derived/model ranges.

## Method

The audit uses six independent axes rather than a single combat-power score:
1. personnel present and immediately usable;
2. 75mm+ land artillery actually available to the front;
3. armor/AFV operational availability;
4. daylight air-pressure index (sortie pressure, not inventory);
5. dedicated military motor transport immediately usable;
6. daily logistics-fulfilment index (100 = planned daily ammunition/fuel/food/repair demand can be met without accumulating new debt).

Asai effects are applied only to Japanese serviceability, maintenance turnaround, selected heavy turbo vehicles, computation/QC, and naval gunfire-control loops. They do not create additional divisions, landing craft, roads, rail capacity or mass motorization.

## Shanghai, 29 August 1937

Historical anchor: contemporary foreign observer estimate approximately 100,000 Chinese and 30,000 Japanese in the Shanghai field area. The same report judged Japanese naval guns and bombers to be major compensating factors and noted Chinese artillery weakness and increasingly clear Japanese air superiority.

Japanese organic divisional 75mm establishment gives a lower-bound framework: 3rd Division artillery regiment 27 x 75mm; 11th Division artillery regiment 36 x 75mm on the Shanghai Expeditionary Army OOB. Heavy artillery was attached, but not all heavy pieces or ammunition trains were immediately landed, so the 60–90 effective landed-gun band is deliberately below paper totals.

The Japanese 5th Tank Battalion is securely present in the OOB. Common secondary OOBs give approximately 47 tanks/tankettes before adding independent light armor; because counts conflict and serviceability/landing timing matter, the audit uses 30–45 operational AFVs rather than treating nominal strength as present-and-running strength.

Asai-specific subset estimate: 20–40 turbocharged heavy trucks/tractors/recovery vehicles in the Shanghai theater by late August. This remains a minority of Japanese transport and does not make the divisions motorized. No mobile GT is credited to Shanghai without a unit-level assignment.

Interpretation: China is infantry-rich and support-poor; Japan is manpower-poor but support-rich. Land-gun tube numbers need not be grossly unequal for Japan to have a large delivered-fire advantage because naval artillery and aircraft are an external fire-support pool. This asymmetry explains why 100,000 Chinese can fail to crush 30,000 Japanese while 30,000 Japanese still fail to produce an immediate operational breakthrough.

## Nankou / Chahar peak, approximately 18–23 August

This table refers to the direct Nankou breakthrough axis, not every Japanese unit operating throughout Chahar. Japanese operational records state that Suzuki Brigade initially bogged down because Chinese strength had increased and terrain was rugged; arriving 5th Division elements were committed progressively, and after roughly ten days of fighting in bad weather and rugged terrain the force broke through on 23 August.

The campaign-wide Chinese force was around 60,000 in several reconstructions; the direct-axis range is meant to capture the forces actually interacting at the decisive pass system. Japanese campaign-wide totals can be much higher than the direct-axis strength because units were distributed across Chahar and supporting axes.

Asai effect: modest reduction in vehicle/workshop maintenance debt and somewhat higher readiness of selected heavy vehicles and aircraft. Terrain and rain dominate mobility, so no calendar change is granted to the 23 August breakthrough.

## Central Hebei / North China, 29 August synchronization

Historical anchors: on 7 August the U.S. Military Attaché estimated about 130,000 Chinese troops already concentrated on the Paoting–Tsangchow front. Japanese reinforcements subsequently included the 5th, 6th and 10th Divisions, with their Tientsin-area concentration completed on 22 August; much of the 5th Division was committed on the Chahar/Nankou axis. Japanese operational records explicitly note that August rains flooded the Hopei plains and turned areas along the Tientsin–Pukou railway into a sea of mud.

The 150,000–220,000 Chinese late-August band is a growth-from-anchor audit range, not an exact archival strength return. A later Japanese intelligence estimate of roughly 400,000 in Central Hopei is retained only as a high contemporary estimate and is not used as the model center.

Japanese motor transport remains insufficient for wholesale motorization. A July reinforcement report already mentioned roughly 400 trucks plus three transport battalions; the 700–1,000 central-ready late-August estimate represents accumulated front transport, not trucks per division. Mud, bridges and railway repair remain stronger constraints than engine power.

Asai-specific North China subset: approximately 40–80 turbocharged heavy vehicles across the theater by late August, concentrated disproportionately in artillery tractors, heavy 6x4 trucks, recovery/support vehicles and special vehicles. The subset matters because its mission value is higher than its numerical share, but it is far too small to make the theater mechanized.

| Effective fire support | ~2.4 | ~1.5–2.0 | Japanese advantage from naval/air support and ammunition/control |
| Armor | ~3.2 | ~5.0 | Japanese advantage, terrain-sensitive |

| Logistics fulfilment, Asai worldline | 1.38 | 1.41 | Slightly stronger Japanese advantage; not a category change |

Do not combine these numbers into one scalar combat-power score. Shanghai canals/urbanization, Nankou mountains, and Hopei mud multiply or suppress different axes differently.

## Canonical recommendation

Close the late-August state as follows:
- Shanghai: China ~100k versus Japan ~30k; Japanese support-power superiority but operational stall persists.
- Nankou/Chahar: same 23 August breakthrough and late-August pursuit; Asai produces minor sustainment margin only.
- Central Hebei: China retains roughly 2–3:1 manpower advantage in the immediately relevant field force, while Japan has major air/armor and moderate logistics advantages.
- Asai delta remains a 2–5% readiness/sustainment effect in high-value Japanese systems, not a force-generation effect.
