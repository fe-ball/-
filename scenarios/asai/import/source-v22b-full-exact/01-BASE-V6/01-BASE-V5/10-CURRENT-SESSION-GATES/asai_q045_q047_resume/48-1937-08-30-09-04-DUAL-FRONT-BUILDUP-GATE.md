# Q-046 — 1937-08-30 to 1937-09-04 — Dual-Front Buildup Gate

Decision: CLOSED-SAME-STRATEGIC-BUILDUP / CLOSED-SAME-SHANGHAI-STALL / CLOSED-MINOR-ASAI-SUSTAINMENT / OPEN-NEXT-OFFENSIVE

Clock at close: 1937-09-04 approximately 18:00 local operational time.

## North China

31 August: the Order of Battle of the North China Area Army, including 1st and 2nd Armies, is published. The former China Garrison Army is integrated into the new structure, its commander becoming commander of the 1st Army.

3 September: advance echelons of the 14th Division land at Tangku and move toward their assigned area west of Peiping. The division's main concentration is not completed until 12 September, so the worldline does not credit a full additional division on 3–4 September.

4 September: General Terauchi reaches Tientsin and formulates the coming Central Hopei operation. The intended decisive effort is along the Peiping–Hankou railway, with preparations to strengthen lines of communication, airfield capacity and transport links as additional forces arrive.

Asai effect in this gate is principally depot/repair/readiness: Tangku and Tientsin handling, heavy-vehicle serviceability, workshop throughput and computation/standardization reduce small amounts of logistics debt. Weather, railway repair, river crossings and division arrival schedules remain dominant. No day-scale calendar shift is granted.

## Shanghai

30 August–4 September: the Liuho–Woosung front remains operationally stalled. A 3 September contemporary U.S. report explicitly states that the Japanese offensive has not yet begun and appears to be waiting for additional troops and artillery.

1 September: Japanese air operations are moving toward broader strikes on Chinese bases, airfields and lines of communication. By this stage Japanese daylight air superiority around Shanghai is already well established relative to late-August Chinese activity.

3 September: Chinese artillery in Pudong fires against Japanese shipping/consular targets; the Japanese flagship conducts a heavy bombardment of Pudong for approximately six hours, damaging/destroying waterfront structures. This episode demonstrates both the value and the political/geographical constraints of naval gunfire in the dense Shanghai environment.

Asai naval-fire-control influence improves repeat-fire setup, calibration and fire-mission cycling on influenced ships, but it does not eliminate line-of-fire restrictions, neutral-settlement risk, target identification problems or the basic shortage/delay of landed Army artillery.

## Worldline result

North China is transitioning from improvised July/August fighting to a formal area-army campaign system. Shanghai remains a costly operational stall while Japan waits for more troops and artillery. The Asai worldline enters September with slightly lower maintenance debt and somewhat higher equipment readiness, but the operational calendars remain historical-equivalent through 4 September.

Next open gate: the first large Central-Hopei offensive preparations/commencement and the next Shanghai reinforcement-heavy-artillery phase.
