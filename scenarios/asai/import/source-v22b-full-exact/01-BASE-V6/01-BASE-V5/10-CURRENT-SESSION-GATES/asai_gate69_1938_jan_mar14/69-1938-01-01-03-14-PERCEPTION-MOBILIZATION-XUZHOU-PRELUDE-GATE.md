# Gate 69 — 1938-01-01 to 1938-03-14
## 「早く逃げた」情報効果 / 対手トセズ / 徐州前夜 / 浅井航空研究の1938年第1四半期

Status: strategic dates CLOSED through the opening of the Tengxian fighting; Taierzhuang outcome remains OPEN.

## 1. Shanghai withdrawal creates a real information signal

The slightly earlier-within-day Shanghai disengagement saved some Chinese personnel, but it was visible.

This produces two simultaneous Japanese readings:

### Optimistic / field-command reading
- Chinese troops left when the encirclement threat became serious.
- Therefore another hard flank threat may cause another withdrawal.
- The Shanghai/Nanking sequence appears to validate the idea that operational pressure can break Chinese positional resistance.

### Professional operations/intelligence reading
- The Chinese main army was not annihilated.
- It escaped before the ring fully closed.
- Therefore future operations must close the encirclement faster if the objective is destruction rather than territorial occupation.

These are not contradictory.
Together they strengthen the future Xuzhou idea:
**“the enemy will retreat under pressure, so this time close the ring before he escapes.”**

Chinese headquarters receives the inverse lesson:
- early withdrawal preserves men;
- but it has a visible political/propaganda cost.
This creates a mild incentive to make later defensive stands more conspicuous before ordering withdrawal.

No major January date is changed by this perception delta.

## 2. January political gate

10 January: Japanese forces enter Tsingtao/Qingdao on the historical date.

14 January: China asks for clarification of the Japanese peace terms.

16 January: Japan terminates the Trautmann exchanges and issues the “cease to deal with the Nationalist Government” statement.

The worldline Shanghai signal slightly strengthens the hard-line argument that the Nationalist Government is militarily brittle and that a friendly replacement regime might be built after further pressure.

However, the historical drivers are already strong enough that:
**16 January remains CLOSED-SAME-DATE.**

The more important consequence is conceptual:
after 16 January, the “the war may end in a few weeks through negotiation” assumption becomes substantially weaker inside technical/economic planning.

## 3. Strategic contradiction: non-expansion vs decisive-battle optimism

After Nanking, central Army planning historically attempts to avoid new major operations until summer 1938 while consolidating occupied areas.

The North China Army and other field commands remain much more interested in further advance and in a decisive blow near Xuzhou.

The Asai worldline does not remove this split.

It makes the field side slightly more confident:
- equipment readiness is a little better;
- Shanghai seemed to show that flank pressure produces withdrawal;
- yet the enemy escaped, creating a strong desire to achieve a true encirclement next time.

Therefore:
- formal central non-expansion policy: SAME;
- field pressure for a Xuzhou operation: MODESTLY STRONGER;
- operation authorization date: not moved at this gate.

## 4. February-March mobilization environment

19 February: the National Mobilization Bill is introduced on the historical date.

This matters to Asai because an aviation-turbine capacity proposal no longer looks like an isolated company fantasy; it can be framed as one item in a wider industrial-mobilization problem.

Still, there is no service aircraft requiring hundreds of E5 engines.

Thus the military moves only one stage:
**E5 aviation GT from prototype technical evaluation toward a controlled pilot/evaluation manufacture.**

## 5. Aviation GT pilot lot

By February, a central working order of **24 flight-grade E5 cores for the first half of 1938** is authorized.
Working band: 18–30.

Purpose:
- Army/Navy bench and flight evaluation;
- E5 twin/single research;
- alternate nacelle/aft-fan work;
- spare cores for failure investigation.

This is A2→A2/A3.
It is NOT a mass operational-engine order.

No dedicated service fighter is ordered.

The existing and expanded turbine plant remains under capacity study, with a future target envelope of ~40–60 flight-grade cores/month only after new equipment, skilled labor and qualification are in place.

## 6. E5 twin

No.1 remains the principal flight-research aircraft.

By 14 March:
- basic flight envelope: ~90%;
- engine/nacelle integration: ~85%;
- single-engine and mission-system work: ~65–75%;
- endurance/repeat sortie/TBO: ~50–60%.

It is increasingly clear that the main military value of the program is not another speed demonstration but:
- reliability,
- mission radius,
- rapid turnaround,
- single-engine return,
- camera/radio integration.

No.2:
- authorized at year end;
- by mid-March structure/fabrication is roughly 25–40% complete as a working estimate.
- no military service designation.

## 7. E5 single

The late-1937 first flight is now behind it.

By 14 March:
- roughly 25–40 flight hours working;
- basic envelope around 50–65%;
- intake/CG/rear-engine architecture is substantially validated;
- still far from service qualification.

The program confirms the value of the architecture without changing the prior ruling:
**E6 remains the first credible operational single-engine interceptor/recon/fighter gate.**

The deleted separate small early-GT fighter branch stays deleted.

## 8. aft-fan

No 1938-Q1 flight yet.

Ground and nacelle work continues.
The program must close static thrust / TSFC / installed mass / thermal and failure behavior before flight authorization.

Twin No.2 remains the preferred first flight carrier if the audit succeeds.

## 9. Small TP / STOL cultivation

The existing tiny fleet remains around three aircraft.

- rough/short strips;
- liaison;
- photographic/radio carriage;
- turnaround;
- maintenance by non-Asai personnel.

This is not frontline combat issue and not formal adoption.

The old archived STOL name/specification/adoption history remains excluded.

## 11. Shandong and the coming trap

Late February / early March, contemporary foreign observers report large Japanese reinforcements and heavy artillery flowing through the Shanghai area, with more than half estimated for the southern Tsinpu front.

9 March: heavy fighting is underway around Linyi.

14 March: Tengxian fighting opens.

The Asai worldline enters these battles with:
- somewhat healthier Japanese F/M/S;
- no extra division magically created;
- new/reinforcing formations having less accumulated Asai advantage than old exposed units;
- a dangerous perception bias that the Chinese will withdraw when sufficiently pressured.

This is the key setup for Taierzhuang.

## 12. Numerical delta at 14 March 1938

Working center:
- Japanese cumulative battle casualties avoided since July 1937: ~4,200;
- serviceable vehicles at a high-tempo theater moment: ~+130;
- mission-capable aircraft on an active day: ~+16;
- immediately usable artillery: ~+36;

Major event-date divergence remains zero.

The important new divergence is **decision psychology**:
Japanese local commands are slightly more operationally capable and slightly more willing to over-read Chinese withdrawal behavior.
