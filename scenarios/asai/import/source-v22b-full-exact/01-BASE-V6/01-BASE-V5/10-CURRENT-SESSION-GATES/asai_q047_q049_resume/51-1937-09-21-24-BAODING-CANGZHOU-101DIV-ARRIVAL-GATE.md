# Q-049 — 1937-09-21 to 1937-09-24 — Baoding/Cangzhou and Shanghai 101st Division Arrival Gate

Decision: CLOSED-SAME-DATE-MAJOR-OBJECTIVES / CLOSED-FIRST-HOUR-SCALE-ASAI-CALENDAR-MARGIN / CLOSED-SAME-SHANGHAI-NO-BREAKTHROUGH

Clock at close: 1937-09-24 approximately 18:00 local operational time.

## North China

On 21 September the 2d Army began attacking Chinese positions around Tsanghsien (Cangzhou). On the 1st Army front, the 6th Division continued toward the Chingyuan/Paoting defensive area while the 14th and 20th Divisions worked toward the flanks and retreat routes.

The historical-equivalent record gives an unusually useful bottleneck. The 6th Division's attack was first delayed by artillery preparations. On 23 September it penetrated the outer positions around 1000, but the advance of artillery units was delayed and necessary artillery support did not begin until dusk; the occupation was explicitly described as correspondingly delayed. At dawn on 24 September the attack resumed under artillery cover and the city was occupied during the morning. On the same day the 2d Army captured Tsanghsien.

This is the first point at which the Asai delta directly intersects a documented limiting variable. Asai-influenced heavy tractors/vehicles, repair practice, gauges, replacement-part control and artillery computation do not eliminate road and bridge constraints, but they plausibly reduce mechanical/organizational delay inside the artillery movement itself.

Worldline ruling: the capture remains on 24 September. However, artillery support is allowed an hour-scale improvement and the final occupation may occur roughly 1-3 hours earlier within the same morning. This is a WORKING tactical calendar margin, not a change of date. The fall of both Paoting/Chingyuan and Cangzhou on 24 September remains CLOSED-SAME-DATE.

## Shanghai

The 101st Division, the first of the major new reinforcement divisions ordered after the September fighting demonstrated the inadequacy of existing forces, landed between Woosung and Shanghai on 22 September. Contemporary U.S. reporting indicates intense transport use of Hongkew wharves from 22-24 September, including roughly 4,000 troops landed on one of those days.

Asai logistics improve sorting, repair, parts availability and the fraction of landed vehicles/weapons that can be used quickly. They do not create additional transports, wharf frontage, landing craft or roads. The 101st Division therefore arrives on the same date and does not create an immediate operational breakthrough by 24 September.

## Combat balance at close

Shanghai: Japanese manpower is rising toward a less unfavorable ratio, but China still retains a large infantry mass and prepared defensive depth. Japanese superiority remains strongest in air, naval gunfire, artillery concentration and sustainment.

North China: the Japanese operational advantage is now converting into pursuit. Asai effects become more useful as the campaign moves onto roads/railways and repeated artillery displacement, but bridge destruction and weather remain hard constraints.

## Casualty trend through 24 September

Relative to historical-equivalent, cumulative Japanese combat casualties are estimated approximately 4-6 percent lower by this point; Chinese military casualties are approximately unchanged to 0-2 percent higher. The exchange ratio has improved modestly for Japan, but force preservation is still measured in hundreds or low thousands, not a transformed campaign.

## Next open gate

25 September onward: pursuit from Paoting/Cangzhou toward Chengting/Shihchiachuang, while Shanghai receives further major reinforcements (9th Division landing 27 September, 13th Division 1 October). This is the first interval in which Japan's manpower deficit in Shanghai begins to narrow materially while North China becomes a sustained pursuit/logistics campaign.
