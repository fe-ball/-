Clock at close: 1937-09-20 approximately 23:00 local operational time.

## North China — Chohsien-Chingyuan operation

On 14 September the 14th Division found the enemy thin on its front and began moving at noon. The 6th Division attacked at approximately 1440. The 20th Division entered its attack on 15 September after its preparations were completed. These timings are driven principally by reconnaissance, enemy movement and Army command decisions; the Asai worldline does not move them to an earlier date.

By 17 September the 14th Division had advanced south of Chohsien and severed the Peiping-Hankou railway, while the 20th Division reached the area west of Chohsien and the 6th Division reached the Taching Ho sector.

From 18-20 September physical logistics again dominate. Torrential rain, unsatisfactory rail transport, unrepaired railway bridges, marshy ground, bad roads and an impassable river constrain the pursuit. The historical-equivalent 1st Army temporarily suspended the advance of heavy artillery because supply transport was becoming inadequate. Asai heavy-vehicle reliability and workshop throughput reduce breakdown loss inside the motor transport available, but cannot replace a destroyed bridge or make an impassable river passable.

Local columns may recover tens of minutes to a few hours through better serviceability, but no day-scale calendar shift is granted through 20 September.

## Shanghai

The Japanese attacks of 11-14 September forced an orderly Chinese withdrawal to the first defense line running from the Lotien area toward North Station/Chapei. This remains a controlled withdrawal rather than a rout. On 20 September, after weather improved, contemporary reporting recorded renewed strong Japanese pressure and substantial gains around Lotien, while Chapei and Kiangwan continued to be shelled without a major infantry breakthrough there.

Asai naval gunfire control, artillery readiness and aircraft serviceability make the Japanese pressure somewhat more consistent. They do not turn the first-line withdrawal into a collapse or advance the operational front by a full day.

## Loss-exchange tendency

For this phase, Japanese combat casualties are modeled locally around 4-7 percent below historical-equivalent in fire-support-heavy actions, while Chinese losses are approximately unchanged to 1-3 percent higher. Campaign-wide cumulative differences remain smaller because many casualties arise in terrain and infantry fighting where Asai effects are weak.

## Next gate

21-24 September contains the first documented logistics bottleneck where delayed Japanese artillery directly delayed the capture of the Chingyuan/Paoting position. This is the first strong candidate for an hour-scale calendar delta.
