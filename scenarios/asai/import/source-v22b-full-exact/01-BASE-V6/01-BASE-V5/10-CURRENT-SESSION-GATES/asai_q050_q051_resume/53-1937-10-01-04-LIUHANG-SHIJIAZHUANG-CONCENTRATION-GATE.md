# Q-051 — 1937-10-01 to 1937-10-04
## Shanghai Liuhang breakthrough / North China Shijiazhuang concentration

Status: CLOSED-SAME-CALENDAR / CLOSED-MINOR-ASAI-HOURLY-ACCELERATION

### Historical frame
- 1 Oct: Japanese 13th Division lands between Woosung and Shanghai. Japanese reinforcement in Shanghai now comprises five army divisions (3rd, 9th, 11th, 13th, 101st) plus SNLF and supporting units, although newly landed formations are not instantly fully combat-ready.
- Early Oct: renewed Japanese attacks employ very heavy artillery and bombing.
- Night 3–4 Oct: Liuhang is captured and Chinese forces withdraw 5–7 miles to a new line extending toward Tachang; contemporary foreign reporting notes unusually heavy Japanese casualties over the preceding three days.
- North China: 20th, 14th and 6th Divisions begin southward movement from the Chuyang–Tinghsien line; 108th Division joins the operational concentration. On 4 Oct, 1st Army disseminates detailed orders for the Shijiazhuang battle.

### Shanghai balance audit, 4 Oct (working)
These are effective/front-and-direct-support estimates, not nominal order-of-battle totals.

- Japan: 80,000–95,000 effective personnel.
- China: 175,000–220,000 effective personnel.
- Army artillery ready for regular employment: Japan 220–300 pieces; China 100–160 pieces (LOW/MED confidence).
- Naval gunfire and daytime aviation give Japan an effective fire-delivery advantage well above the raw gun-count ratio.
- Military motor transport immediately usable: Japan 800–1,300 vehicles; China 250–600 (LOW confidence; includes rear/direct-support vehicles).
- ASAI high-value vehicle subset: roughly 45–75 in Shanghai theater.

### ASAI effects in the Liuhang fighting
The manpower bottleneck is materially eased by the arrival of 9th/13th/101st Divisions, allowing ASAI serviceability advantages to be converted into more continuous fire support. Nevertheless, prepared terrain and Chinese resistance dominate casualty production.

Working sensitivities:
- Known/fixed-target naval fire mission cycle: -5 to -10% on ASAI-influenced ships.
- Army artillery preparation/re-fire cycle: -5 to -10% where survey/data/communications are favorable.
- Aircraft mission-capable fraction: +3 to +5 percentage points locally.
- Heavy transport/towing readiness: +3 to +6 percentage points in ASAI-equipped subset; theater-wide vehicle readiness +1 to +3 points.
- Japanese personnel casualties in the 1–4 Oct intense phase: -5 to -7% versus historical-equivalent.
- Chinese personnel casualties: 0 to +3%.

Liuhang still falls during the night of 3–4 Oct. ASAI may move the local collapse by roughly 1–4 hours, but not to a different calendar day.

### North China balance, 4 Oct (working)
- Japanese 1st Army forces relevant to the Shijiazhuang axis: about 65,000–80,000 effective personnel across 20th, 14th, 6th, 108th Divisions and attachments, with not all units yet on the assault line.
- Chinese forces in the broad Hutuo River/Shijiazhuang defensive system: roughly 90,000–140,000 effective personnel (LOW/MED confidence).
- Japan retains strong advantage in artillery concentration, aviation, motorized high-value support and repair organization.
- Rail/bridge restoration and river crossings remain hard physical constraints and are not canceled by ASAI technology.

### Closure
By 4 Oct, ASAI has begun to create hour-scale tactical acceleration under sustained combined-arms conditions, but no date-scale divergence is yet justified. The next hard test is the 6–10 Oct Zhengding–Hutuo River–Shijiazhuang operation, where heavy artillery deployment, crossings and pursuit can convert readiness margins into operational timing.
