## Historical baseline
Japanese operational accounts describe the Nanyuan attack as beginning early on 28 July. The 20th Division attacked from the south, the Kawabe group from the west, and the 2nd Infantry Regiment main force from the east, with temporary air support and northern/western pressure by the Sakai and Suzuki brigades. The Japanese postwar operational monograph describes hard fighting lasting through the day and the Kawabe tank element cutting a retreat route near sunset.

Chinese/local accounts commonly place the practical collapse of the Nanyuan position earlier, around midday to early afternoon, after hours of artillery/air attack, command disruption, deaths of senior commanders, and withdrawal fighting. Because these chronologies differ, this gate closes the strategic/tactical outcome but not a single exact "capture minute".

By late afternoon/evening, Nanyuan is irretrievably lost. Song Zheyuan's command decides to withdraw from Peiping after the Nanyuan disaster and the deaths of Tong Linge and Zhao Dengyu. Main Chinese forces begin moving south on the evening of 28 July; Japanese operational accounts place the broad evacuation from the Yongding left-bank area as essentially complete by 29 July.

## Asai-worldline delta
### 1. Artillery — strongest immediate effect
Existing Asai computation, surveying, firing-data, instrument-calibration, and QC improvements are now usable in a planned multi-axis attack rather than a limited punitive bombardment.

Working effect:
- well-prepared batteries: firing-data / registration workflow ~10–20% faster;
- selected local suppression missions can begin or transition to effective fire roughly **5–15 minutes earlier**;
- local assault timing may therefore gain **~10–30 minutes** where artillery observation and target data are good;
- effective concentration improves only by single-digit percentages after terrain, observation, ammunition, and map limitations are applied.

This does **not** move the whole battle hours earlier. It mainly reduces friction in the opening and follow-on fire plan.

### 2. Aviation — marginally higher serviceability
No aviation gas turbine or jet aircraft are introduced. The Japanese temporary air group remains historical in type and organization.

Asai-derived engine test/QC/maintenance practice gives only a few percentage points of serviceability improvement. For a major package of a few dozen aircraft, treat this as roughly **1–3 aircraft-equivalent additional usable sorties / fewer aborts**, not a larger force generation change.

### 3. Motor transport / artillery tractors
Newly releasable Manchurian vehicles cannot reach the dawn 28 July battle. Only Asai-equipped vehicles already present with North China/Japanese reinforcement formations count.

Working effect:
- exposed Asai heavy-vehicle subset: +3–7 percentage points readiness;
- whole relevant motor pool: +1–3 percentage points;
- primary benefit: artillery tractors, ammunition/supply vehicles, recovery/support vehicles arrive and remain usable slightly more reliably.

Japanese tanks are **not** automatically Asai-turbo-equipped and receive no magic speed/armor effect.

### 4. Communications
Japanese headquarters power, maintenance, records, and wired-network reliability are somewhat better. Chinese communications remain vulnerable to physical line cuts, bombardment, air attack, and command dislocation. Asai does not repair the Chinese command system.

## Battle effect
The Japanese attack is tactically cleaner, especially in artillery preparation and support-vehicle readiness. Some local objectives can be suppressed and assaulted tens of minutes sooner than in a historical-equivalent execution.

However, the dominant determinants of Nanyuan remain unchanged:
- Japanese multi-axis attack and air superiority;
- Chinese improvised defensive arrangement and weak prepared fortification;
- command/communications disruption;
- retreat-route interdiction;
- loss of senior commanders.

Therefore the battle remains a Japanese victory on **28 July**, within the same broad day-phase as history. No defensible basis exists to close an alternate capture hour one or two hours earlier.

## Casualty audit
Historical Chinese casualty figures are highly inconsistent across sources, ranging from roughly 2,500 to 5,000+ in different accounts. Absolute Japanese losses are also not sufficiently stable in the consulted sources for precise alternate accounting.

Canon decision:
- **Do not close absolute casualty totals.**
- Chinese losses remain the same order of magnitude, with directional pressure slightly upward or unchanged because withdrawal/interdiction geometry is preserved.
- Japanese losses are directionally slightly lower due to faster local suppression and reduced support-vehicle friction.
- Optional sensitivity only (not canon): Japanese losses roughly 2–5% below historical-equivalent; Chinese losses roughly 0–3% above historical-equivalent.

## Command outcomes
Tong Linge and Zhao Dengyu remain lost at event level. Their exact time/location is source-sensitive, but the route, command exposure, retreat crisis, and Japanese interdiction remain materially the same or marginally worse for their survival prospects.

Song Zheyuan still decides on withdrawal from Peiping during the afternoon/evening of 28 July. His departure is kept in a late-evening band (~22:00–23:00) rather than fixed to a minute. Zhang Zizhong remains behind to handle the Peiping political/transition situation.
