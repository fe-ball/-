# Gate 59 — 1937-10-27 to 1937-10-31
## Shanghai: Chapei withdrawal / Sihang Warehouse / Soochow Creek
## Shansi: Niangzi Pass exploitation / Pingting pursuit

Status: CLOSED at calendar and operational level. Detailed casualty and sub-unit-loss figures remain OPEN.

### Core rulings

#### Shanghai
- The general Chinese withdrawal from Chapei/Kiangwan after the fall of Dachang remains an ordered operational withdrawal, not a theater-wide rout.
- The Sihang Warehouse defense is treated as a deliberately retained reinforced battalion-sized rear guard (roughly 420 men), not as the remaining combat strength of the 88th Division.
- Sihang defense begins 27 October and the survivors withdraw into the International Settlement in the early hours of 31 October. Calendar unchanged.
- Japanese forces cross Soochow Creek west of the foreign defense lines on the afternoon of 31 October under heavy artillery cover. Calendar unchanged.
- Asai fire-control/readiness effects can improve repetition and availability of fire support, but foreign-settlement constraints, river crossing geometry, and the political sensitivity of fire near neutral zones cap the effect.
- Possible worldline delta: local fire missions / assault timing by tens of minutes to ~2 hours; no date shift.

#### Shansi
- After Niangzi Pass falls, the 20th Division begins relentless pursuit on the night of 26 October.
- Pingting is captured on 29 October after combat with an estimated Chinese force of about 1.5 divisions. Date unchanged.
- The 20th Division then masses in the Yangchuan/Pingting area and replenishes before the next phase.
- The 109th Division remains unable to depart before 1 November because of pack-horse shortages. Motor-vehicle advantages do not substitute on mountain tracks.
- Asai improves F/M/S of the 20th Division modestly, but the pursuit is still constrained by mountain routes, supply replenishment and enemy delaying actions. No calendar shift is granted.

### Attrition / readiness interpretation
- Shanghai Chinese old Central Army formations: many battalion/regimental remnants are D/reconstitution level, but the general withdrawal is still sufficiently controlled to establish positions south of Soochow Creek.
- 88th Division: C-/C as a formation-level effective force after prolonged battle; Sihang battalion is a selected rear guard and must not be counted as evidence that the whole division remains forward.
- Japanese Shanghai veteran formations: P remains low; Asai principally preserves F/M/S. They can continue pressure but are not restored to fresh exploitation status.
- IJA 20th Division: B-/B. Still capable of pursuit; human fatigue significant, but artillery/transport/repair depth slightly healthier in the Asai line.
- Chinese Niangzi-Pingting defense: retreating and locally fragmented; some subunits D, but not every division is annihilated.

### End-state
Clock: 1937-10-31 late evening.
- Shanghai: Chapei/Kiangwan has been abandoned by the main Chinese force; Sihang defenders have entered the International Settlement; Japanese troops have begun establishing bridgeheads south of Soochow Creek west of the foreign lines.
- Shansi: Pingting is in Japanese hands; the 20th Division is replenishing/concentrating for further movement toward the Yangchuan/Yutzu axis. The 109th Division remains delayed by pack transport.
- No one-day Asai calendar divergence yet. Divergence remains readiness, maintenance debt, and hour-scale tactical timing.
