# 1937-07-27–28 — Final ultimatum / Peiping offensive opening gate

Status: CLOSED-SAME-DECISION / CLOSED-MINOR-EXECUTION-DIVERGENCE
Branch time advanced to: 1937-07-28 dawn (~05:00–06:00 band; exact H-hour remains source-sensitive)

## Verdict

The Asai worldline does not alter the final political/operational decision to abandon local settlement, nor does it justify an earlier attack than the historical dawn opening on 28 July. The decisive constraints on 27 July are ultimatum deadlines, incomplete civilian evacuation, the Chinese 29th Army's non-withdrawal, continued local fighting, and the Japanese command's transition from negotiation to punitive operations.

Asai effects first become operationally non-trivial at the opening of the full offensive, but only as execution margin: somewhat higher heavy-vehicle/artillery-tractor readiness, fewer mechanical dropouts, slightly more reliable artillery fire preparation, and a small aviation serviceability margin. No mass GT aviation, no portable-radio revolution, and no instantaneous reinforcement from Manchuria are permitted.

## Historical sequence closed in this gate

- 27 Jul ~04:30: the attack ordered after the Guang'anmen incident remains temporarily postponed because the existing deadline ran to 28 Jul noon, civilian evacuation was incomplete, and fuller information showed Guang'anmen was smaller than first feared.
- 27 Jul morning: Japanese residents are ordered into the legation quarter. This remains a political/civilian-protection timing constraint.
- Chinese side had been required to withdraw 37th Division elements at Lugouqiao/Babaoshan by noon 27 Jul and city/Xiyuan elements across the Yongding River by noon 28 Jul.
- 27 Jul morning: fighting at Tongzhou and north of Peiping confirms that local fighting is widening. Song Zheyuan remains in Peiping and requests central reinforcements.
- 27 Jul ~13:00: some 37th Division/security elements remain at Babaoshan after the deadline; contemporary explanations include delayed/non-received movement orders. Asai communications do not repair Chinese command topology.
- 27 Jul ~14:00: fighting develops around Tuanhe between 132nd Division elements and Japanese 20th Division elements; by ~16:00 artillery is involved.
- 27 Jul ~15:00: the Jicha authorities publicly reject the Japanese final ultimatum. Local negotiations are effectively over.
- 27 Jul 11:50: Japanese central authorities decide renewed dispatch/mobilization and assign the Tientsin Army an explicit operational mission in the Peiping–Tientsin area.
- 28 Jul 00:00: Tientsin Army transmits a final notice that it will act independently and recommends immediate withdrawal of all Chinese troops from inside Peiping so the city itself will not be subjected to war damage.
- ~02:00: the notice is conveyed through the Peiping special-service channel to Qin Dechun and then Song Zheyuan.
- 28 Jul dawn: full Japanese offensive operations around Peiping begin. Exact minute is left OPEN-detail; the branch uses an H-hour band of roughly 05:00–06:00 pending unit-diary crosswalk.

## Asai crosswalk

### Motorized readiness

Do not inject the newly audited Manchurian releasable pool (20–40 initially, 30–60 under urgent draw) into the 28 Jul dawn attack. Vehicles newly released from Manchuria on the 27th cannot reach and integrate with the Nanyuan force overnight. Their effects belong to later gates.

For forces already in North China, Asai turbo adoption is concentrated in heavy 6x4 trucks, artillery tractors, recovery/support vehicles and selected heavy diesel equipment, not ordinary light trucks. A reasonable working interpretation at H-hour is:

- exposed heavy-vehicle subfleet readiness: +3 to +7 percentage points versus historical-equivalent condition;
- whole motor pool effect: roughly +1 to +3 percentage points, because most vehicles are not Asai-equipped;
- operational meaning: fewer tractors/heavy trucks missing at line-of-departure, less loaded-grade overheating/dropout, and a slightly better arrival-ready fraction for heavy artillery/support detachments.

Do not infer a whole brigade arriving hours earlier or a historical rail bottleneck disappearing.

### Artillery computation / survey / QC

This is the first gate where the mature Asai-influenced artillery-computation ecosystem can matter beyond a latent margin. The target set around Nanyuan/Xiyuan includes fixed barracks, defensive localities and communications/assembly points, making precomputed fire and rapid correction more useful than in the earlier punitive shelling of Wanping.

Working effect for the first planned missions:

- firing-data preparation / registration cycle: plausibly ~10–20% faster in well-surveyed batteries;
- initial data error / dispersion: modestly improved, not transformed; think single-digit-percent improvement in effective concentration, not precision artillery;
- ammunition expenditure for the same neutralization effect can be slightly lower, or the same ammunition can produce somewhat faster suppression.

Target observation, maps, weather, ammunition supply and command decisions remain dominant constraints. No exact casualty uplift is closed here.

### Aviation

Historical aircraft remain historical types. The Asai effect is engine/cooling/vibration test maturity, maintenance documentation, dimensional QC and fault classification.

At the opening of the offensive:

- sortie-capable fraction can be a few percentage points better;
- on a mission package of a few tens of aircraft this is normally an order-of-magnitude effect of roughly one or two extra serviceable aircraft / fewer aborts, not a new air arm;
- attack timing remains governed by orders, weather and airfield operations rather than Asai technology.

Mass-production aviation GT remains zero.

### Communications / mobile power

Existing fixed/field communications and a small number of mobile power sets can be more reliable, but there is no dense portable radio network. Chinese non-receipt or non-execution of withdrawal orders is not erased. Japanese command/control is a little more robust at headquarters and service points, not qualitatively different.

### Materials / small arms

Tooling, gauges, heat treatment and QA improve weapon reliability and replacement-part consistency at the margin. No documented weapon failure is the decision hinge in this gate, so this remains secondary.
