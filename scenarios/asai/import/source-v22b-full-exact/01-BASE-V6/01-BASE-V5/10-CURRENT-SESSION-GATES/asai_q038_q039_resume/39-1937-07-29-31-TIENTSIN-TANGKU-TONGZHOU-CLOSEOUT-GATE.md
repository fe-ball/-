## Historical baseline
Contemporary U.S. reporting preserves the correct transition better than a compressed “29 July dawn = battle over” narrative. Heavy fighting and Japanese bombing continued through 29 July. By 30 July organized Chinese resistance was collapsing/withdrawing, and by 31 July 10:00 the U.S. Consul General reported Tientsin quiet, with only nighttime firing as Japanese forces cleared remnants from the Chinese city.

Tangku/Taku belongs to the same closeout. Subsequent U.S. reporting describes Tangku as quiet and in Japanese hands, with Taku shelled and the navy yard reportedly destroyed, while Japanese troops and supplies were entering through the port route.

At Tongzhou, Japanese air action began on 29 July and was repeated on 30 July; contemporary Japanese reporting states that land reinforcement reached the area by the evening of 30 July. The mutinous force was then driven out / Japanese control restored. This closes the operational relief question without requiring an exact casualty chronology.
