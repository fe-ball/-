## Peiping: military control precedes formal ceremonial entry
Do not equate the 29–31 July political/military transition with a full Japanese army parade into the walled city. Contemporary U.S. reporting by 2 August nevertheless describes Japanese military control as established: some gates are under Japanese control, National Government-linked institutions are being pressured, and Japanese authorities are working to remove organized opposition and links to Nanking.

Thus the branch preserves a two-stage process:
1. **late Jul / early Aug:** practical military-political control and local order mechanism;
2. **later formal entry / municipal reorganization:** separate ceremonial-administrative milestones.

This prevents the timeline from inventing a large siege while also avoiding the false claim that nothing changed until a later parade.

## Maintenance-of-order committee
On 30 July Chinese notables met concerning a “committee for the maintenance of order”; six Japanese, including the local Assistant Military Attaché, attended. The 31 July U.S. Embassy report notes conflicting information on whether it had already been inaugurated and whether an intended 1 August inauguration might fail because reputable Chinese declined to serve.

Canon treatment:
- **the Japanese-sponsored local maintenance mechanism emerges by end Jul / early Aug: CLOSED-DIRECTION**;
- exact inauguration date and first membership roster: **OPEN-detail**;
- resistance/refusal by some respectable Chinese notables: **CLOSED-SAME-DIRECTION**;
- therefore no smooth, high-legitimacy puppet administration appears immediately.

Asai administrative/computation capability does not solve the legitimacy problem. Machines can process registers after an administration exists; they cannot make reluctant elites volunteer for it.

## Chinese national strategy
Chiang Kai-shek’s 30 July public statement explicitly rejects treating Peiping–Tientsin as a local matter and says no further local settlement is possible. On 1 August he reportedly explains that military preparations should be completed in roughly ten days, with the main northern front envisaged around **Paoting–Tsangchow**, and Central Government forces not expected to make an immediate drive north of Paoting.

Canon consequences:
- loss of Peiping/Tientsin is accepted as a serious tactical reverse but not the end of resistance;
- Chinese strategy shifts toward preserving/organizing forces on a southern Hopei line rather than an immediate counterstroke for Peiping;
- the conflict is now treated as national/wider war, even though formal declarations and subsequent theaters remain separate gates.

## Japanese strategic posture
On 30 July the Japanese Foreign Office spokesman publicly states that the military phase at Peiping is over and suggests that local peace-preservation bodies could develop toward autonomous administration. On 29 July the Diet had approved an additional 96.8 million yen for the North China incident, on top of a prior 10.1 million yen reserve allocation.

This closes the direction of travel:
- battlefield success is immediately converted into occupation/political engineering;
- Tokyo is funding a conflict larger than the original local garrison incident;
- nevertheless the exact architecture of a North China client regime is not yet fixed on 2 August.

### Wider-war preparation
The small Q-038 readiness carryover remains real. Japanese aviation/vehicle formations entering August have slightly less accumulated maintenance debt. This matters only as a small readiness margin for subsequent operations; it does not alter Chiang’s strategic front choice or generate an immediate Japanese advance to Paoting before orders, rail concentration and reinforcements are ready.
