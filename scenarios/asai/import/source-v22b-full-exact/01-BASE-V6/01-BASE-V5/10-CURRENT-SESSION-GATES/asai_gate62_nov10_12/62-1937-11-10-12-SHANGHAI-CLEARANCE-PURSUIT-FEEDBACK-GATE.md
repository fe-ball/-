# Gate 62 — 1937-11-10 to 1937-11-12
## End of immediate Shanghai fighting / pursuit toward Chingpu-Anting
## Retreat-preservation feedback

Status: CLOSED at operational/calendar level. Fine-grained capture hours and casualty totals remain OPEN.

### Core logic
This gate explicitly includes a negative feedback:
- Japanese Asai-enabled F/M/S can accelerate pursuit locally.
- But an earlier-within-day Chinese withdrawal order on 8 Nov can preserve some formations and reduce route congestion/encirclement losses.
- Therefore the divergence does not compound monotonically.

### Historical anchors
- 10 Nov: a politically motivated Chinese last stand continues in Nantao; fighting around Sungkiang remains heavy.
- 11 Nov: Japanese capture of Sungkiang is confirmed; Chingpu is reported fallen; Nantao is under combined land/naval/air pressure.
- 12 Nov: about 5,000 Nantao defenders have surrendered to the French; immediate Shanghai hostilities are ending. Japanese mechanized units continue rapid progress south/west; Chingpu and Anting are reported captured and Chinese forces withdraw from Nanhsiang.
- Japanese postwar operational record treats 12 Nov as the beginning of full-scale pursuit after the Shanghai front clears.
- The Japanese high command had already imposed an operational-area concept generally east of the Soochow–Chiahsing line; tactical readiness does not automatically erase command limits.

### Asai ruling
- Japanese mechanized leading elements and 6th Division-associated columns retain a small F/M/S advantage.
- Local pursuit/capture timings can advance by roughly 2–6 hours where roads and bridges permit.
- No clean one-day capture shift is granted for Sungkiang/Chingpu/Anting because contemporary reporting is too coarse and Chinese withdrawal timing has also improved marginally.
- Earlier Chinese disengagement preserves a small fraction of organized manpower in formations with intact command/route discipline.
- C-/D formations still lose heavy weapons, straggle, or fragment; the benefit is not uniform.
- Shanghai veteran Japanese divisions remain too worn in P to become fast exploitation formations; the fresher Hangchow Bay force carries much of the pursuit.

### Chinese retreat preservation effect (working)
- Well-controlled B-/C+ formations: modest reduction in trapped/straggler losses possible.
- C/C- formations: limited benefit; route congestion and communications remain severe.
- D-level subunits: little benefit; many still dissolve or abandon equipment.
- Net result: some personnel preservation, but high equipment loss and cohesion damage remain.
- Do not infer that Chinese operational strength increases versus history overall; treat this as a partial offset to Japanese tactical pressure.

### Command-limit effect
The Japanese tactical force can be somewhat more ready than history without immediately extending the strategic pursuit line. Orders, force reorganization and central decisions remain separate bottlenecks.

### End-state
Clock: 1937-11-12 late evening.
- Immediate organized Chinese resistance in Shanghai proper is essentially over, apart from local/political remnants.
- Japanese forces are driving west/southwest toward the next operational belt; Chingpu/Anting are in Japanese hands or being consolidated.
- Chinese forces are attempting to reconstruct a line toward the Soochow–Chiahsing / Wufu-line directions.
- Divergence remains hour-scale in movement and readiness, while early withdrawal feedback preserves a small amount of Chinese manpower. No calendar-day divergence is locked yet.
