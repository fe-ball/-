# 1937-11-12 Asai history / military-adoption rebase

## Main history correction
The stronger reading that late 1937 is already the point when Asai actively offers the Army/Navy a mass aviation-GT engine-production channel is superseded.

By autumn 1937 Asai already understands that an E5-family aviation turbine line could in principle create engine output without consuming the same assembly line as Mitsubishi/Nakajima piston engines. However, through 12 November 1937 this remains an internal contingency and military technical-study argument, not the company's main external sales pitch.

Three constraints dominate:
1. mature turbocharger, tool, steel, power and repair demand is already pulling on the same skilled labor, balancing, grinding, heat-treatment, bearings and inspection capacity;
2. military authorities still retain meaningful hopes for a short or decisive China settlement and have not fully internalized a multi-year aviation-engine mobilization problem;
3. E5 aviation applications remain at prototype/qualification stage, while piston-aircraft programs already have production organizations and immediate operational demand.

Therefore:
- Nov 1937: Asai answers capability inquiries and prepares a contingency production memorandum.
- Nov 1937: E5 aviation test cores continue; no series procurement.
- Late Dec 1937 to early 1938: only if the war clearly persists, the argument can be promoted into a formal additional-engine-production-channel proposal.
- Even then, dedicated facilities/material allocation must be funded; the existing turbo business is not free spare capacity.

## Product-history consequence
Autumn 1937 military/commercial activity is led by:
- exhaust-turbo replacements and new heavy-vehicle installations,
- special steels, carbide tools, gauges and repair tooling,
- fixed/mobile power,
- computation and logistics support,
not mass aviation GT.
