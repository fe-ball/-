# Gate 66 — Current-only aviation hardware and research-progress audit
Date: 1937-11-12

## Scope
This audit deliberately excludes archived aircraft names/adoption histories. It uses only:
- the current-session aviation branch cleanup,
- the current-session E4→E5 twin demonstrator canon patch,
- the current 1937 company history/product ledger conclusions.

## Main conclusion
Asai in late 1937 is not operating a hidden aircraft-production industry. It is operating a compact experimental aviation ecosystem.

The clean central physical picture is:

### Advanced GT fixed-wing research
- 1 E4 twin legacy systems testbed survives;
- 1 E5 twin high-speed mission demonstrator is the main flying research aircraft;
- 1 E4 clean single demonstrator survives as a legacy research asset;
- 1 E5 central/rear single-engine research airframe physically exists, but its exact first-flight state is kept WORKING. The preferred current reading is ground-integration / near-first-flight in November 1937, with first flight as a late-1937/early-1938 gate.

This means there is no need for an additional separate small early-GT service-fighter prototype.

### Practical small fixed-wing
- the small TP/short-field branch is already beyond one-off proof-of-flight;
- a 2–4 aircraft flyable population, center 3, is the best current working count;
- this can represent one company prototype plus a very small number of external-evaluation/small-sale aircraft;
- exact type name, customer list, sales count and old-STOL identity remain open.

### Rotorcraft
- two small helicopter airframes is the preferred working count:
  - one earlier lineage airframe associated with the imported-engine development period;
  - one improved airframe/configuration carrying the Asai 180–200 hp engine work.
- one is normally the active flight article while the other is reserve/rebuild/instrumentation support.
- this is still research hardware, not military procurement.

### Materials
- no dedicated Material No.1/No.2 flight aircraft exists yet in November 1937.
- the physical branch consists of coupons, joints, sandwich panels and a few full-scale substructure articles.
- dedicated material-aircraft construction begins in 1938.

### Test infrastructure
- one or two existing donor aircraft remain available for TP conversion/engine flight test;
- a mother/instrumentation aircraft for pod or support testing may be retained;
- these are infrastructure, not separate Asai original aircraft families.

## Research maturity

### E5 twin — program leader
The E5 twin has already crossed first flight and is in envelope/mission-system work:
- basic flight envelope: ~70–80%;
- engine/nacelle integration: ~65–75%;
- single-engine handling: ~45–60%;
- camera/radio/mission-bay integration: ~45–60%;
- endurance/TBO/service reliability: ~30–45%;
- live armament/strike qualification: only ~10–25%.

The main blocker is not aerodynamics. It is engine/service maturity: fuel, TBO, restart/throttle behavior, altitude thrust, repeat-sortie maintenance and mission usefulness.

### E5 single — architecture demonstrator, not service interceptor
The current clean reading should be:
- layout/design substantially fixed;
- one physical research airframe under final integration;
- first-flight status not retroactively assumed;
- military interceptor suitability deliberately held low until E6 installed thrust and mission requirements mature.

This is the key reason to delete the separate small early-GT fighter branch. The single-engine research need is already being served by this airframe.

### Small TP / short-field
This is the most practically mature Asai-original fixed-wing branch:
- basic flightworthiness ~85–95%;
- short-field operation ~70–85%;
- partial composite structure ~60–75%;
- repeatable small-series production only ~40–55%.

It is therefore plausible as a few-aircraft practical product/evaluation line even though exact old STOL specifications are not canon.

### Helicopter
The helicopter has passed the “can it fly?” question but not the “is it useful?” question:
- free-flight/control ~65–80%;
- Asai engine integration ~45–60%;
- autorotation ~40–55%;
- transmission/rotor-head durability ~30–45%;
- useful payload/endurance ~20–35%.

The 1938 task is durability and useful mission radius, not simply another first flight.

### Materials
Materials are ahead of dedicated aircraft:
- material/process coupons ~75–90%;
- full-scale substructure tests ~45–65%;
- dedicated flight-aircraft program ~0–10%.
This justifies Material No.1 in 1938 and Material No.2 later, without pretending those aircraft already exist in 1937.

## R&D organization implication
A working Asai experimental-aviation establishment around late 1937 can be kept modest:
- dedicated airframe/flight-test staff: roughly 120–220, center ~160;
- shared GT/nacelle/control engineering support: ~60–130 equivalent staff;
- experimental new-build/rebuild capacity: roughly 2–5 airframes/year, center ~3;
- only 2–5 aircraft can be under intensive instrumented flight test simultaneously.

This scale is enough to support the observed branches while remaining consistent with Asai being a diversified engineering company rather than a mass aircraft manufacturer.

## Recommended closure
CLOSE now:
- one flying E5 twin demonstrator by 1937 summer;
- one surviving E4 twin and one surviving E4 clean-single legacy test asset;
- no dedicated material flight aircraft before 1938;
- small TP practical branch has a few physical flyable aircraft;
- small helicopter program has more than a paper prototype and can reasonably be represented by two development airframes.

Keep WORKING:
- exact E5 single first-flight date;
- exact small-TP fleet/customer count within 2–4;
- exact helicopter architecture and which airframe receives which engine;
- second E5 twin airframe authorization.

Preferred next gate:
- late Nov 1937: decide E5 single first-flight status;
- Dec 1937–Jan 1938: decide whether the military funds a second E5 twin and/or uses the existing twin for aft-fan trials;
- 1938: begin Material No.1 physical airframe build and decide whether small TP moves from “few-unit practical product” to contract production.
