# ASAI 1939-Q1 AVIATION CLOSURE PATCH V3

**effective branch date:** 1939-03-31 24:00  
**supersedes:** `ASAI-1939-Q1-AVIATION-CLOSURE-PATCH-V2.md`  
**purpose:** Close the historical-source conflicts and branch-decision gaps that remained after V2. V2 system logic remains in force except where V3 explicitly changes it.

## V3 authority rule

- Historical chronology is closed when multiple independent/specialist references converge and the previous conflict is traceable to an outlier table.
- Future worldline events after 1939-03-31 are **not declared to have happened**. Where enough causal information exists, V3 closes the **target window / trigger rule** so the later event gate is deterministic unless new physical test evidence intervenes.
- A historical hardware feature that already provides the supposed Asai benefit receives **no automatic worldline performance bonus**.
- Exact performance remains test-derived. Closing a design choice is not the same as inventing an achieved speed.

# PART 0 — V3 NEW CLOSURES

## 0.1 A6M1 first-flight propeller — CLOSED-WORLDLINE

Historical A6M1 #1 first flew with a two-blade variable-pitch propeller and changed to a three-blade constant-speed unit during testing to cure vibration.

The current branch is materially different before first flight:

- initial engine is `Z-900N`, not the historical 780 hp Zuisen/Zuisei 13 branch;
- Mitsubishi and the Navy evaluation chain are already running structured engine/propeller matching;
- priority three-blade Hamilton-family hardware is available to a top-priority prototype.

Therefore close the **worldline first-flight A6M1 configuration** as:

**three-blade constant-speed metal propeller from first flight.**

This is a branch design decision, not a claim that history used it on 1 April 1939. It avoids deliberately starting a 900 hp prototype with a propeller installation already marginal on the historical lower-power prototype.

No extra km/h is assigned merely for this closure. The resulting vibration, climb and speed are Q2 flight-test outputs.

## 0.2 D3A1 formal order clock — TARGET-CLOSED

Historical anchor:

- first prototype 1938-01;
- extensive stability / snap-roll / dive-brake correction;
- six evaluation/preproduction aircraft in 1939;
- Navy production order / Type 99 carrier-bomber adoption in December 1939.

V2 already closed a **6–10 week reduction in post-prototype analysis/rework burden**, while preserving physical dive tests.

Therefore close the worldline administrative target:

- **technical production-release target:** 1939-10 to early 1939-11;
- **formal Navy order/adoption target:** **1939-11-15 to 1939-12-01** if the service-evaluation batch shows no disqualifying defect.

Do not backdate combat service before the service-test aircraft physically exist. Exact order day remains a later event datum, but the allowed window is now closed.

## 0.3 B5N2 exhaust baseline — CLOSED; no generic E1 bonus

Recovered B5N2/Sakae installations and model/airframe documentation show **straight/individual exhaust stacks** on the historical B5N2 installation.

Therefore:

- historical B5N2 already has the core E1 architecture;
- worldline B5N2 does **not** receive the old generic `+4.3..6.2 km/h` exhaust credit;
- Asai may optimize stack length, direction, back-pressure, shielding and thermal durability, but a speed delta is zero by default until type-specific test evidence exists.

This closes the old exhaust-baseline ambiguity.

The B5N2 re-engine / adoption clock may tighten by a few weeks through engine-map and production integration, but there is no separate propulsion-exhaust performance jump to add.

## 0.4 Ki-48 historical first-flight month — CLOSED

The May-vs-July conflict is resolved in favor of **July 1939**.

Reasons:

- Francillon-derived technical histories state the first prototype was not completed until July 1939;
- multiple specialist aircraft references independently give July 1939;
- the known program delay from Takeo Doi's simultaneous Ki-45 workload is consistent with July;
- the isolated May table is treated as an outlier.

Worldline consequence:

- historical H = **July 1939**;
- worldline first-flight target = **late June to first half July 1939**, with no more than ~2–3 weeks fabrication/integration credit;
- severe tail flutter must still be discovered in flight;
- once discovered, the already-closed C effect targets resolution in **September–October 1939**, rather than leaving the full historical September–November trial burden untouched;
- production authorization target becomes **October–November 1939**, conditional on flutter clearance.

## 0.5 Ki-55 historical chronology — CLOSED

Close:

Because Ki-55 is a Ki-36 derivative, Asai calendar credit is deliberately small:

- worldline first-flight target: **late August–early September 1939**;

## 0.6 Ki-46 turbo comparison necessity — CLOSED-YES

The exact conventional prototype performance remains a future flight-test event, but the **need for a parallel exhaust-turbo comparison branch no longer remains open**.

Historical conventional prototype performance was far below the 600 km/h requirement. Even giving the worldline conventional aircraft:

- the higher side of Ha-26-family near-term output,
- E1 thrust exhaust benefit,
- cleaner engine/propeller/cooling integration,

incremental improvements alone do not plausibly close the full historical speed deficit.

Therefore:

- #1 / earliest prototype remains conventional mechanically-supercharged baseline;
- turbo packaging provisions remain designed in;
- **a parallel turbo comparison airframe/installation is mandatory**, not merely optional after disappointment;
- first conventional flight still precedes turbo flight;
- turbo does not receive a prewritten success result: heat, weight, ducting, control, altitude map and durability remain physical gates.

## 0.7 Navy 20 mm domesticization chronology — CLOSED-BAND

Close the safe industrial chronology as:

- 1935: Navy investigation;
- 1936–37: license/industrial decision and import/knock-down preparation;
- 1937: knock-down / early assembly activity can exist;
- 1938: Swiss technical assistance and line domesticization;
- **by July 1938: full local licensed-production capability is active**;
- 1939: formal `Type 99` service/nomenclature era.

Exact monthly gun quantities for 1937–39 remain unknown and are **not to be fabricated**.

Worldline effect remains the already-closed `+5–8% accepted output` planning band once orders/materials absorb it. A6M prototype armament is not blocked by lack of any 20 mm gun.

## 0.8 Radio numerical policy — CLOSED-PROHIBITED

Remove `exact radio MTBF` and `universal fighter-radio fit rate` from the research OPEN list.

This is not an unresolved question requiring a guessed number.

## 0.9 Generic surface-finish bonus — CLOSED-REJECTED

Remove generic `CD0 -3%` from the OPEN list.

Close:

- surface/QC effects first appear as lower aircraft-to-aircraft variation, fewer rigging defects and better realization of design performance;
- a type-specific speed/drag credit requires measured or same-type calibrated evidence.

Old generic surface-finish numbers remain sensitivity-only and cannot generate current worldline A-values.

## 0.10 Ki-44 propeller geometry — CLOSED

Worldline close:

- exact achieved performance remains 1940 flight-test data.

## 0.11 J1N1 propulsion installation — CLOSED-DESIGN

Historical J1N1 used two Sakae 21/22 engines with handed/opposite-rotating three-blade constant-speed propellers on the original escort-fighter prototype series.

Close for the worldline design:

- **three-blade constant-speed handed/opposite-rotating propeller pair** retained;
- E1 individual/thrust-exhaust installation is a mandatory design comparison and preferred branch if thermal/maintenance tests pass, because the original long-range requirement makes small cruise-propulsive gains valuable;
- no standard exhaust turbo;
- C may flag the escort-fighter Pareto conflict early, but flight evidence is still required before mission conversion.

## 0.12 E13A competition trigger — CLOSED

Historical anchors are messy in exact prototype dates, but the competitive structure is clear:

- E13A and E13K are the relevant three-seat competitors;
- both E13K prototypes are lost during the 1939 test period;
- historical formal E13A adoption is December 1940.

Current-branch implication:

- once the E13K line is physically removed by the 1939 losses, there is no reason to keep a full two-design competitive-analysis clock running;
- Aichi/Navy U3-ish data handling and production-drawing discipline can start production preparation before formal Type-0 nomenclature.

Close the trigger rule:

- **after confirmed loss/removal of the second E13K prototype, E13A becomes the sole production-development candidate unless its own tests fail**;
- production engineering may begin in H2 1939;
- worldline technical-selection target: **Q4 1939–Q1 1940**;
- formal adoption target: **Q2–Q3 1940**, rather than automatically waiting until historical December 1940.

Exact future event date remains contingent on the physical fleet tests.
