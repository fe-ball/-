# ASAI 1931–1938 TWIN MILITARY-REQUIREMENT LINEAGE CORRECTION V1

Status: CURRENT-BRANCH CORRECTION
Cutoff: 1939-09-01 retrospective audit

## 0. What was mixed up

The current Gate 64 preserved the early E4 twin test aircraft, but then reclassified the 1936–37 E5 twin as an Asai-funded mission demonstrator with no military requirement.

That is too strong and conflicts with the earlier branch logic in which:

**early-1930s Asai twin testbed
→ military judges the concept worth continued work
→ military technical requirement modifies the next aircraft
→ 1937 E5 twin military prototype**

The error came from conflating two different meanings of "military requirement":

1. **military technical/prototype requirement**
   - an actual military customer asks for specified changes/tests and supports the prototype program;
2. **service/adoption requirement**
   - a formally defined operational type, competition slot, service designation, procurement requirement and series path.

The 1937 aircraft has (1).
It does not yet have (2).

Therefore:
- "no service adoption / no series order in 1937" is retained;
- "no actual military requirement behind the 1937 aircraft" is superseded.

---

# 1. Correct institutional stages

## Stage R — company research
Asai decides what to investigate and owns the research objective.

## Stage M — military technical requirement
A military technical bureau/customer specifies test objectives or required modifications.
The aircraft is a **military-requirement technical prototype** even if:
- Asai pays part of the basic airframe cost;
- it has no service designation;
- no production order exists;
- its final tactical category remains open.

## Stage S — service/adoption requirement
The service defines the operational category, required performance, armament, equipment and procurement path.
A service prototype/competitive prototype exists.

Future files must not use "research aircraft" and "no formal service requirement" as if they prove Stage M was absent.

# 2. Corrected chronology

## 1929–31 — instrumentation preparation
As already retained:
- turbine flight pod work;
- fuel/control plumbing;
- measurement panels;
- safe airborne engine testing.

Status: **Stage R**

## 1931–33 — E4 twin universal systems test aircraft

As already retained:
- approximately 3.6–4.0 t;
- two E4 jet installations;
- roughly 380–405 km/h class;
- interchangeable nacelle/engine experiments;
- one-engine-out behavior;
- dual fuel/control system;
- radio/photo/mission-bay work;
- maintenance-access study.

Purpose:
not a service combat aircraft, but a reusable military-systems flying laboratory.

Status:
**Stage R with military access/observation.**

This is the aircraft that first makes the military conclude that the Asai twin-turbine concept is worth following.

## 1933–35 — military requested test expansion

This stage was lost in Gate 64 and is restored.

Army/Navy technical personnel do more than simply watch demonstrations.
They request structured trials in areas such as:
- one-engine return and restart;
- radio installation;
- photographic equipment;
- long-distance fuel arrangement;
- payload/mission-bay arrangement;
- representative light armament or bomb load;
- turnaround/maintenance access;
- high-speed command/reconnaissance use.

No service type is selected yet.

Institutionally:
- Asai still owns the generic testbed;
- military technical organizations pay for or provide selected instruments, test ranges, fuel, observers and requested modifications.

Status:
**R → M transition.**

## 1935 H2–1936 H1 — military technical prototype requirement

CLOSED correction:

An actual military technical requirement is issued for the next high-speed twin.

The requirement does **not** say:
"build the future standard jet interceptor."

It says, in effect:
"take the twin turbine system that has demonstrated value and produce a faster, more military-useful prototype so that reconnaissance, interception/heavy-fighter and light-strike envelopes can be tested under realistic equipment loads."

Primary requirements:
- two-seat configuration for test/observer/mission work;
- substantially higher speed than the E4 testbed;
- E5 propulsion;
- practical radio/photo installation;
- one-engine return capability;
- usable mission-bay / armament test provision;
- greater fuel capacity;
- maintainable nacelles and quick access;
- military-standard instrumentation and flight-test reporting.

The final operational role remains deliberately open.

### Sponsorship rule
The technical-contract lead is most naturally **Navy-side**, consistent with Asai's turbine relationship and the old military twin line; Army technical observers receive data/access and submit their own reconnaissance/heavy-fighter test items.

This is a technical prototype contract, not yet a Navy service-category competition or a numbered production program.

If a later authority assigns another lead service, sponsorship can be relabeled without changing the engineering chronology.

## 1936–37 — E5 military-requirement twin prototype

The 1937 E5 twin is therefore **not merely an Asai company demonstrator**.

Correct status:
**military-requirement high-speed twin technical prototype / joint evaluation aircraft.**

It is derived from the E4 twin experience but pays substantial redesign:
- cleaner E5 nacelles;
- higher-speed structure/aeroelastic margin;
- revised fuel/control system;
- military radio/photo fit;
- mission-bay provisions;
- stronger landing gear/brakes;
- practical access/turnaround changes requested by military users.

It may still contain substantial Asai-paid structure because the firm wants the data and wants to retain configuration freedom.
Funding source does not erase the customer requirement.

### Flight state
By summer 1937 the aircraft is already beyond "will it fly?"
It is working through mission envelope.

Therefore first flight must precede that stage.

Working first-flight band:
**1937 Q1–Q2**.
Exact day remains open.

## 1937-11-12 — what is and is not true

TRUE:
- military technical prototype requirement already exists;
- military-requested modifications are embodied in the E5 twin;
- military technical evaluation is real;
- the aircraft is a military prototype in the broad engineering sense.

ALSO TRUE:
- no service designation;
- no formal adoption;
- no series-production order;
- no fixed final role;
- no claim that it is already a squadron-ready interceptor.

Correct label:
**MILITARY-REQUIREMENT TECHNICAL PROTOTYPE / ADVANCED JOINT EVALUATION**

Do not label it merely "company mission demonstrator."

# 3. No.2 and aft-fan — same program, not a new research branch

## End 1937

The second E5 twin is authorized.

The existing finance wording can mostly survive:
- Asai may pay the baseline structure;
- military pays requested instrumentation, test work and requirement-specific modifications.

But status changes from:
"company research airframe with military-paid instrumentation"

to:

**second development/prototype airframe inside the existing military technical-prototype program.**

Purpose:
- keep No.1 flying;
- parallel engine/nacelle work;
- aft-fan flight carrier;
- alternate mission-equipment configuration;
- preserve attribution of propulsion changes.

## 1938 H1

Existing physical closure remains:
- No.1 basic envelope ~95%;
- mission/one-engine work ~75–85%;
- endurance ~60–70%;
- No.2 ~60–80% complete, center ~70%;
- E5 aft-fan installed audit PASS;
- first aft-fan flight target Sep–Oct 1938.

No new military requirement is required to justify this work.
It is already inside the military-backed E5 twin prototype program.

# 4. What happens in late 1938

SUPERSEDE the recent-session statement:
**"1938 Q4 is when the military high-speed-twin practical-prototype requirement is first issued."**

Correct interpretation:

1938 Q4 is a **requirement refinement / role-narrowing gate**, not requirement inception.

After:
- E5 pure-jet mission data;
- E5 aft-fan first-flight data;
- China-theater reconnaissance/communications lessons;
- military maintenance experience;

the customer can begin splitting the broad 1937 technical prototype requirement into more specific next-block objectives.

Examples:
- Navy: land-based high-speed interception / local-defense fighter emphasis;
- Army: high-speed reconnaissance / heavy-fighter comparison;
- common: range, radio, reliability, rapid turnaround, one-engine return.

Whether this later becomes one service program or diverges into separate Army/Navy programs is a subsequent gate.

# 5. E6 relationship

E6 does not create the first military twin program.

It is the **next propulsion block inside an already-existing military twin development lineage**.

Correct lineage:

**E4 company twin systems testbed**
→ **military requested test expansion**
→ **1935/36 military technical prototype requirement**
→ **1937 E5 military-requirement twin prototype No.1**
→ **1938 No.2 / E5 aft-fan comparison**
→ **late-1938 requirement refinement**
→ **E6-powered next-block military prototypes**

This is a continuous program, not:
"research demonstrator until 1938, then suddenly a military program."
