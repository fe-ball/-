# ASAI 1939-09-01 AVIATION SYSTEM AUDIT V1

Status date: **1939-09-01 00:00**
Purpose: full post-Nomonhan audit of Japanese aviation equipment and development.
Scope: aircraft, engines, propellers, armament, protection, radio/electrical installation, reconnaissance/photo systems, supercharging/turbo, production readiness.
Rule: historical comparisons belong to the adjudication layer; procurement decisions are made from worldline test results and contemporary military requirements.

---

## 0. Main conclusion

Japan's aviation problem in September 1939 is not a lack of promising aircraft.

The actual situation is a **generation transition**:
- existing first-line types (Ki-27, A5M, Ki-21, Ki-30, B5N1, etc.) remain operationally important;
- several 1937–39 design-origin aircraft are in test, service-trial or first-prototype stages;
- engine/propeller/radio/photo/armament integration is becoming as important as the bare airframe;
- Asai computation/QC primarily shortens redesign loops and raises accepted/serviceable output. It does not permit elimination of flutter, dive, cooling, carrier or water tests.

The post-Nomonhan Army priority is:
1. reconnaissance and photo-to-command speed;
2. fighter firepower and command radio reliability;
3. battle-damage protection studies;
4. reliable high-power propulsion and propeller matching;
5. tactical air-ground observation/attack;
6. high-altitude reconnaissance/interception research.

The Navy is influenced less directly by Nomonhan; China and carrier requirements continue to dominate.

# 1. Fighters

## A6M1 — DEVELOPMENT SUCCESS / NAVY EVALUATION READY

Worldline configuration already CLOSED:
- first flight 1939-03-24..26;
- Z-900N 900-hp-class initial engine;
- three-blade constant-speed propeller from first flight;
- design-origin thrust exhaust;
- no standard exhaust turbo.

Q2 branch flight-test closure remains:
- measured maximum speed approximately **520–524 km/h**, center about 522 km/h;
- cooling is acceptable in normal envelope, with oil-temperature margin work continuing for hot/low-speed climb;
- basic 12-Shi performance requirement is met without treating the initial Mitsubishi engine as a disposable underpowered installation.

By September 1939:
- the A6M1 airframe/installation is considered technically successful enough for Navy evaluation;
- there is no emergency requirement to abandon Mitsubishi's engine merely because the aircraft cannot reach 500 km/h;
- comparative Z-940N / Sakae 12 engine work continues.

Engine-selection rule:
**Sakae 12 remains the central 1940 mass-production candidate**, but for fuel consumption, altitude map, maturity and standardization—not because the Z-900N A6M1 is a failed prototype.

Armament:
- 2 × Navy Type 99 20 mm-class cannon + 2 × 7.7 mm remain the intended fighter armament;
- gun supply is no longer treated as a prototype blocker;
- exact 1939 monthly Type 99 gun quantities are not fabricated.

Protection:
- no automatic armor/self-sealing retrofit is ordered for A6M1;
- Navy range/weight requirements still govern;
- combat-damage data may trigger later trade studies, not a September 1939 redesign.

## Ki-27 — CURRENT ARMY FIGHTER / NO FICTITIOUS UPGRADE

Still the Army's operational fighter backbone.
Worldline benefits:
- accepted-output and replacement flow;
- engine/propeller QC;
- serviceability;
- radio-installation quality where sets are fitted.

No "Ki-27 super-variant" is created.

## Ki-43 — DEVELOPMENT, NOT PRODUCTION-READY

The January–March 1939 first three prototypes and Army pilot criticism remain.

By September:
- structured Nakajima redesign work has narrowed the weight, fuselage/tail, canopy, cooling and maneuverability variables;
- the ten-aircraft development/service-trial series can begin earlier and with fewer blind serial modifications than historical practice;
- **production configuration is not frozen**;
- combat-flap work is a serious development candidate but is not yet treated as a fleet solution;
- constant-speed/three-blade propeller work is tested in the development series but is not retroactively declared standard on all Ki-43s;
- no operational Ho-103 armament is available yet.

Post-Nomonhan addition:
a formal protection trade study is opened for:
- pilot back/seat armor;
- partial fuel-tank self-sealing/protection;
- weight/CG/range penalty.

This is a design/test study, not immediate adoption.

## Ki-44 — HIGH-SPEED FIGHTER PROGRAM / NO CALENDAR MIRACLE

Still a 1940 prototype program.
Nomonhan strengthens the Army's appreciation of:
- speed;
- climb;
- heavier armament;
- interception of fast reconnaissance/bomber aircraft.

It does not justify pulling the first flight into 1939.
Three-blade propeller geometry remains the current design basis.

## Ki-45 — RESTARTED HA-25 BRANCH

The January 1939 failed prototype experience remains.

By September:
- Ha-20-based failure causes are already separated into engine/installation/nacelle/gear issues;
- Ha-25 redesign branch has been authorized;
- nacelle, cooling, propeller and CG work is in detailed redesign/tooling;
- first redesigned flight remains a Q4-1939 to Q1-1940 event.

No claim is made that computation "fixes" the original Ha-20 installation.

# 2. Reconnaissance / observation

## Ki-15-II — EARLY LOW-RATE SERIES / HIGH PRIORITY

Worldline:
- technical release Q1 1939;
- pilot/low-rate production Q2;
- 8–10 accepted by 1939-06-30;
- a small Nomonhan/Hailar allocation has already demonstrated the operational value of speed, availability and rapid photo processing.

By September:
- accepted population may reasonably be in the **mid-teens to around twenty** without treating it as full national mass production;
- Manchuria and high-value command reconnaissance remain priority users;
- the main gain is not a generic speed bonus but shorter exposure time and more repeatable reconnaissance cycles.

## Ki-36 — DIRECT-COOPERATION RECONNAISSANCE / VALIDATED ROLE

Nomonhan confirms the value of:
- artillery observation;
- route/column observation;
- message transfer;
- close photography;
- post-strike observation.

The aircraft remains vulnerable to fighters.
Procurement lesson is therefore not "more Ki-36 everywhere" but:
- enough aircraft;
- fighter cover;
- radio/message procedures;
- photo/plotting cells;
- direct link to artillery headquarters.

## Ki-46 — TOP-PRIORITY STRATEGIC RECON PROGRAM

The 600 km/h-class requirement remains extremely demanding.

Current branch:
- conventional first prototype target remains late October–early November 1939;
- first aircraft uses conventional mechanical supercharging;
- thrust exhaust is incorporated;
- **parallel exhaust-turbo comparison branch is mandatory**;
- turbo installation is kept separate from the conventional prototype's performance.

By September:
- prototype assembly/integration is advanced;
- turbo branch work is at engine/nacelle/duct/firewall/control preparation level;
- no flight performance is prewritten.

Post-Nomonhan result:
Ki-46's priority rises further because the Army has just demonstrated that **early recognition of enemy armored axes can be worth more than several additional bombing sorties**.

## E5 Asai twin mission demonstrators — TECHNICAL ASSET, NOT SERVICE AIRCRAFT

Current physical situation:
- E5 Twin No.1 pure-jet exists;
- E5 Twin No.2 aft-fan exists;
- Twin No.2 reached A3+ durability-demonstration status by 1939-06-30.

They are **not** included in Nomonhan combat OOB and receive no retroactive combat credit.

September interpretation:
- Army interest shifts further toward high-speed photo reconnaissance / requirement formation;
- E5 remains a demonstrator and systems testbed;
- E6-class aircraft remains the proper point for a real service requirement.

Do not convert successful GT industrial/support use into an automatic order for a jet combat aircraft.

## E13A — NAVY LONG-RANGE FLOAT RECON / COMPETITION CONTINUES

Aichi's long-range three-seat reconnaissance floatplane is a valuable future naval reconnaissance type.

September status:
- development/testing continues against E13K;
- after the competitor line is physically removed by its losses, E13A becomes sole production-development candidate unless its own tests fail;
- production engineering may then accelerate.

Do not declare formal Type 0 adoption in September 1939.

## A6M engine competition

September rule remains:
- Z-900N is a successful development engine;
- Z-940N is a follow-on test branch;
- Sakae 12 remains central 1940 mass-production candidate due to fuel consumption, altitude map, maturity and standardization.

## Propeller industry

1939 is a rapid ramp year for Hamilton-family variable/constant-speed propellers.

Worldline:
- priority prototypes and new high-performance types can obtain appropriate three-blade constant-speed hardware more reliably;
- accepted propeller output is improved through drawings, gauges, tooling and QC;
- this is a supply-chain cause of aircraft readiness/output, **not an extra performance multiplier**.

Do not automatically retrofit every existing fighter with a three-blade constant-speed unit.

# 6. Exhaust thrust / exhaust turbo

## Thrust exhaust E1

Rule remains:
- new high-performance aircraft evaluate E1 from design origin;
- no speed credit where the historical aircraft already used individual stacks;
- type-specific flight evidence governs the actual gain.

## Exhaust turbo

1939 Army/Navy conclusion:
- useful and increasingly credible for high-altitude reconnaissance/interception/bombing;
- not attractive as a universal installation for low/medium-altitude fighters and attack aircraft;
- ducting, turbine, heat, fire, weight and CG penalties remain.

Therefore:
- A6M, Ki-43, Ki-51, Ki-48, B5N, D3A: **NO standard turbo**.
- Ki-46: **mandatory parallel turbo comparison**.
- future specialized high-altitude branches: open where mission requirement justifies them.

# 8. Protection / survivability

This is one of the post-Nomonhan additions that should be made explicit.

The Army already has aircraft programs demonstrating that protection is technically understood:
- Ki-49 protection requirements;
- Ki-51 armor;
- self-sealing/protected-tank technology is not an unknown future invention.

What changes in September 1939 is the **procurement question**, especially for fighters and reconnaissance aircraft.

Open formal trade studies for:
- pilot seat/back armor;
- protected oil tank;
- partial self-sealing fuel tanks;
- fire-resistant routing/shielding;
- weight, CG, climb and range penalties.

Application:
- Ki-43 / Ki-44 / Ki-45: compare during development;
- Ki-46: do not compromise the strategic-reconnaissance speed requirement without explicit Army approval;
- Navy carrier aircraft: no automatic protection retrofit; range/carrier weight priorities remain.

This is not a blanket "Japanese aircraft become armored in 1939" rule.

# 9. Radio, electrical and reconnaissance systems

## Radio

Do not assign a universal fighter radio fit percentage or exact MTBF.

Selected high-value aircraft should receive priority installation-QC:
- Ki-15-II / Ki-46;
- Ki-21 / Ki-49;
- G4M and major Navy reconnaissance/strike aircraft;
- command/observation aircraft.

Improvements:
- grounding;
- shielding;
- power regulation;
- connectors;
- resin/impregnation where appropriate;
- burn-in;
- fault isolation;
- installation-specific EMI testing.

No radar, IFF or modern fighter-control network is created in 1939.

## Photo/recon processing

Nomonhan validates the complete chain:
**aircraft → calibrated photography → development → comparison → plotting → target/command handoff**.

September Army action:
- make photo-processing/intelligence cells a standard attachment at major air headquarters;
- improve metadata, photo indexing and previous-photo comparison;
- integrate Ki-15/Ki-36 now and Ki-46 later;
- keep GT utility power as rear-area support where valuable.

This may be strategically more valuable than modest increases in bomber speed.

# 13. Net assessment

At 1939-09-01 the strongest Japanese aviation advantage created by the worldline is not a single super-aircraft.

It is the growing ability to run a **coherent development-production loop**:
- engine maps;
- propeller matching;
- exhaust/cooling integration;
- structured prototype comparison;
- photo/intelligence processing;
- radio installation quality;
- tooling/QC;
- component accepted-output.

The most important near-term combat capability to accelerate is **reconnaissance**:
Ki-15-II now, Ki-46 next, supported by photo processing and reliable communications.

The most important fighter question is not simply "more horsepower":
- Navy: A6M engine/production selection while preserving its already-successful airframe;
- Army: fix Ki-43's handling/mission package without freezing a bad design, while developing heavier guns and protection trade data.

The most important attack/bomber question is not simply "more bomb load":
- keep aircraft available;
- get accurate reconnaissance into the strike plan;
- protect critical crews/fuel where the mission can afford the weight;
- prevent flutter/structural/installation defects from being accepted into series production.
