# ASAI 1939-09-01 — SALES / RESEARCH AIRCRAFT REBASELINE V1

Status date: **1939-09-01 00:00**

Purpose:
Reclassify Asai-owned aviation into:
1. complete aircraft that Asai may actually sell/lease in low volume;
2. customer technical-evaluation aircraft;
3. retained research/test aircraft;
4. concepts that should be transferred/licensed to an aviation specialist if they become volume military programs.

This patch supersedes an overly restrictive reading that Asai must stop at engines/integration and never sell complete aircraft.

## 0. Corporate rule

Asai **may design, build and sell complete aircraft**.

However, its efficient niche is:
- low-rate high-value aircraft;
- utility/survey/STOL aircraft;
- flying laboratories and technical demonstrators;
- initial pilot lots;
- customer-specific engineering aircraft.

Asai should **not** become a Mitsubishi/Nakajima-scale combat-aircraft mass producer.

Rule:
- Asai in-house low-rate production is rational while the aircraft is also generating propulsion/materials/system data.
- once a military type requires dozens per month, transfer/license/second-source airframe production to a specialist manufacturer while Asai retains engine, controls, instrumentation, interface specifications and technical support.

# 1. First real sales aircraft family — small E5 turboprop utility/STOL

## Status
**CLOSED-DERIVED: LOW-RATE SALEABLE AIRCRAFT FAMILY**

Current-branch anchor:
- practical small-turboprop/short-field fleet existed by 1938 at roughly 3–4 aircraft;
- two-aircraft military technical field evaluation existed;
- this is therefore beyond a one-off engine testbed.

The archived STOL-support concept is useful as a design reference but is not automatically restored as historical adoption.
What is restored now is only the physically justified 1939 product category:
**a small E5-powered utility/STOL aircraft family.**

## Sales missions

Civil/government:
- aerial survey / photography;
- mail / high-value courier;
- company executive/engineering transport;
- mine/remote-site supply;
- medical evacuation;
- route/airfield inspection;
- pilot/engine familiarization.

Army/Navy technical/government:
- liaison;
- artillery/route observation;
- rough-field transport;
- photo/survey;
- technical evaluation of kerosene turbine operation;
- training/ground-support familiarization.

No claim is made that a 1939 sales aircraft is a front-line fighter or attack aircraft.

## Propulsion policy

Sales/workhorse version:
- E5-S-T class;
- **derated long-life rating** preferred;
- approximately 800–900 hp continuous planning class with up to ~1,000 hp takeoff/emergency where qualified;
- lower-TIT material allocation preferred where performance permits;
- life, fuel economy, easy starting and maintainability prioritized over headline speed.

Research/high-performance version:
- may use full qualified E5 rating and higher-grade hot-section lots.

This creates a real long-hour fleet without consuming the best hot-section material in every civilian aircraft.

## Airframe policy

Keep the sale version deliberately conventional:
- fixed or simple robust landing gear;
- conventional metal/wood primary structure as qualified;
- simple high-lift devices;
- no dependence on advanced composite primary structure;
- no aggressive powered-lift mechanism that becomes unsafe after engine failure.

Material No.1 remains the material-test derivative, not the standard sales construction.

## In-house production scale

**WORKING planning band, not closed deliveries:**
- Asai in-house pilot/low-rate capacity: about **6–12 complete aircraft/year** in 1939–40 without creating a major airframe factory;
- special surge with subcontracted structures/components: perhaps **12–18/year**;
- if an Army/Navy order demands >~24/year sustained, volume airframe manufacture should migrate to a specialist partner.

This is a company-organization limit, not a technical airframe limit.

## 1939 fleet state

Do not fabricate exact sales/customer serials yet.

Reasonable current interpretation:
- 3–4 practical small-TP aircraft from the earlier branch remain the core fleet;
- some may be company-owned, leased or in military technical evaluation;
- 1939 H2 can close additional customer deliveries individually when contracts/events are simulated.

# 2. Flying-laboratory business — new explicit product/service

## Status
**CLOSED-DERIVED**

Asai's computation/service-bureau model extends naturally into aviation.

Create a **Flying Laboratory / Flight-Test Service**.

Asai retains aircraft and sells:
- instrumented flight hours;
- engine installation trials;
- propeller/reduction matching;
- cooling/inlet/exhaust trials;
- radio/EMI installation tests;
- photo-system calibration;
- materials exposure;
- vibration/strain measurement;
- data reduction and engineering report.

Customers may be:
- Army/Navy arsenals;
- Mitsubishi/Nakajima/Kawasaki/Aichi/etc.;
- optical/electrical manufacturers;
- universities/research institutes;
- civil operators.

This is often economically better than selling an exotic research aircraft outright.

Commercial form:
- flight-hour lease;
- joint test contract;
- customer-funded modification;
- purchase option for conventional utility aircraft only.

E5 pure-jet/aft-fan research aircraft are **not normal commercial sale items**.

# 4. What Asai should sell versus license

## Sell complete aircraft directly
Best candidates:
1. small turboprop utility/STOL;
2. survey/photo variant;
3. courier/light transport variant;
4. flying-lab version for institutes/arsenals;
5. very small pilot lots of customer-specific engineering aircraft.

Reason:
low volume, high technical content, close connection to Asai engines/service.

## Lease rather than sell
Best candidates:
- heavily instrumented test aircraft;
- novel propulsion demonstrators;
- expensive one-off research configurations.

Reason:
Asai keeps the data and controls modifications while customers avoid owning difficult experimental equipment.

## License / partner-build
Mandatory when:
- military demand becomes high volume;
- airframe production requires a large dedicated jig/fixture labor force;
- a specialist already has the right production line.

Asai retains:
- engine;
- controls;
- installation drawings;
- test/acceptance methods;
- key licensed components;
- field-data access.

# 5. New product logic: sales aircraft as endurance machines

This is the strongest new reason to keep an in-house sales-aircraft business.

A military demonstrator may fly 100–300 hours and teach high-load/envelope behavior.
A mundane survey/courier aircraft can generate **far more calendar starts, field maintenance events and long-duration cruise hours**.

Therefore a small commercial/utility fleet is not a distraction from E6.
It is a **durability laboratory distributed among customers**.

Required contract terms:
- mandatory serial life cards;
- periodic core/gearbox return;
- fuel/oil lot records;
- failure reporting;
- access to selected inspection data;
- discounted exchange core for operators who return complete data.

This ties directly into the Field Reliability Bureau.

The airframe shop remains small.

# 7. Research-fleet discipline

Do not retain every prototype forever.

Each aircraft must have:
- research question;
- annual flight-hour target;
- instrumentation purpose;
- remaining airframe life;
- next technology it de-risks.

Retirement rule:
If two newer aircraft cover the same question more cheaply/cleanly, retire or ground the older one as a static/instructional article.

This prevents a rapidly expanding turbine program from accumulating an unmaintainable museum fleet.
