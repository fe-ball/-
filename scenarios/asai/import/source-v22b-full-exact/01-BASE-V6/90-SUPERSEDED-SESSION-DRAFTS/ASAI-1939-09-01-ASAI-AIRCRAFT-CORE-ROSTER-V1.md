Status date: 1939-09-01 00:00
Scope: Asai-owned/sold aircraft and Asai GT core generation only.
This is a current-session closure patch; it does not revive archived pre-v48 service-aircraft histories.

## 1. Core generation

### Technology front
**E6 is the current technical-front generation in September 1939.**

E-generation is a technology front, not one engine model.
Current upstream E6 coordinates:
- PR about 7;
- TIT about 870°C class;
- small core 7.8 kg/s;
- medium core 13.2 kg/s central.

Representative E6 products already physically credible upstream:
- E6-S non-Ni shaft: about 1,300 hp standard;
- E6-S Ni shaft: about 1,500 hp standard;
- E6-M shaft: about 2,100 hp qualified flat-rated;
- E6 pure jet: about 850 kgf non-Ni / about 900 kgf Ni;
- E6 aft-fan: about 950–1,000 kgf.

These are product-capability anchors, not automatic aircraft-installed ratings.

### Product-generation distinction
September 1939 is a **mixed E5/E6 period**:
- E5 = mature, saleable, fieldable product generation;
- E6 = current technical front, entering installed-product/flight qualification by application;
- E4 = old product/research generation, retained only where cheap/derated/simple operation is useful.

Do not call every September Asai turbine product "E6."

## 2. Sale / customer aircraft

### Small TP short-field practical aircraft
Status: **LOW-RATE SALE / LEASE PRODUCT**

This remains the only Asai-origin fixed-wing aircraft that should be described as an actual customer aircraft product in September 1939.

Current lineage:
- 1937: 2–4 flying, center 3; external evaluation / small-sale territory.
- 1938 H1: total practical fleet about 3–4; two-aircraft military technical field evaluation.
- 1938 H2–1939: low-rate customer build continues without becoming a national mass-production type.

September 1939 closure:
- cumulative completed airframes: **about 8–10**;
- customer-operated / leased / external-evaluation aircraft: **about 4–6**;
- Asai-owned development/demo/backup aircraft: **about 3–4**;
- exact customer identities and sales/lease split remain OPEN unless needed.

Powerplant policy:
- keep a mature **E4/E5-derived turboprop package** appropriate to the aircraft;
- do not force E6 merely because E6 exists;
- E6 would add cost, heat, installation load and unnecessary power for this cultivation/product-engineering role.

The old archived STOL designation/specification is NOT restored.
The aircraft remains generically "small TP / short-field practical aircraft" until a naming/adoption gate is deliberately reopened.

### Why Asai does not scale the airframe factory
The sales aircraft exists to teach:
- serial build;
- customer delivery;
- spare parts;
- maintenance manuals;
- field failure return;
- rough-field operation;
- ordinary aircraft product engineering.

Asai does not attempt to become a Mitsubishi/Nakajima-scale airframe manufacturer.
If demand rises above pilot/small-series scale, licensed/contract airframe production should move to an aviation specialist while Asai supplies propulsion, controls, computation/test and critical interfaces.

## 7. Key interpretation

**Current core generation = E6.**
But:
- sale aircraft do not automatically move to E6;
- mature E5 remains the workhorse product generation;
- E5 research aircraft remain useful because they are controlled baselines;
- E6 is being introduced through new research airframes rather than indiscriminate re-engining.

Therefore the September 1939 Asai aircraft picture is not "still flying 1937 machines unchanged."

It is a layered fleet:
1. **saleable mature TP aircraft** — conservative E4/E5-derived product;
2. **E5 comparison aircraft** — mature pure-jet and aft-fan baselines;
3. **E6 successor aircraft** — single advanced-build, twin early-build;
4. **materials/rotorcraft testbeds** — separate research objectives.

This preserves clean experimental comparisons and makes the E5→E6 transition physically visible without inventing an operational E6 combat fleet before its qualification gates are passed.
