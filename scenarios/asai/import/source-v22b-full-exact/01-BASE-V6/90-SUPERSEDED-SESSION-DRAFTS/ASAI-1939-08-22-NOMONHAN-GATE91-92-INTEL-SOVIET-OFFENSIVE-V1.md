# ASAI 1939-08-22 NOMONHAN — GATE 91–92
## Soviet buildup, Japanese reconnaissance, and 20–22 August offensive

Status date: 1939-08-22 24:00

### Gate 91 — 1939-07-26 to 1939-08-19

#### Historical anchor
Japanese intelligence historically intercepted indications of a Soviet general offensive from late July but failed to establish scale and timing. Soviet attacks on 1–2 and 7–8 August were in part mistaken for the expected offensive. Actual Soviet first- and second-echelon strength was roughly double the Japanese estimate. Soviet night movement, camouflage, defensive construction, noise deception, and radio measures remained effective.

#### Branch intelligence effect
Asai-worldline advantages:
- Ki-15-II repeated deep reconnaissance;
- additional Ki-36 local reconnaissance;
- improved aircraft readiness and fuel/engine reliability;
- faster photo development, indexing, stereoscopic comparison, and map plotting;
- more repeat sorties during short weather windows.

These do not see through cloud, darkness, camouflage, or covered bivouacs.

By 1939-08-15 to 18:
- Japanese command correctly concludes that Soviet buildup continued after 7–8 August and that those attacks were not the main offensive;
- estimated Soviet infantry/artillery strength rises to roughly 65–75% of actual;
- armored strength estimate rises to roughly 60–70% of actual;
- both north and south armored concentrations are recognized, but exact brigade count and H-hour remain unknown;
- a major attack between approximately 18 and 25 August is assessed as probable.

#### Command consequence
The planned Japanese 24 August offensive is not formally cancelled before the Soviet attack.
However:
- tank force is retained as mobile reserve rather than assault spearhead;
- anti-tank guns and dense-core 37 mm ammunition are redistributed to likely flank axes;
- artillery is dispersed and alternate positions surveyed;
- forward ammunition/fuel points are split;
- nonessential workshop and administrative assets begin moving east;
- dawn alert state is raised for airfields and headquarters from 18 August.

No doctrinal revolution and no general withdrawal order occurs.

#### Airfield lessons
After Soviet attacks on forward airfields, aircraft dispersal, strip readiness, local GT emergency power, and repair discipline improve.
This reduces vulnerability on 20 August but does not prevent the Soviet air offensive.

#### Armor/support state on 19 August
- combat-ready Japanese tanks in theater: about 50–55;
- additional repair/limited-use pool: about 4–6;
- replacement flow includes approximately 6–8 additional Type 97 Chi-Ha from Manchurian training/evaluation stocks, mostly replacing prior permanent losses;
- Type 97 proportion therefore rises further without expanding the entire regimental establishment;
- removable fiber spall liners: approximately 10–12 Type 97 vehicles;
- dense-core 37 mm AT ammunition forward stock: approximately 900–1,200 rounds;
- mobile GT utility sets: approximately 7–9;
- heavy prime-mover / cargo mechanical-ready advantage: +5–8 points remains;
- routine workshop turnaround advantage: 15–25% shorter where transport access exists.

### Gate 92 — 1939-08-20 to 22

#### Soviet offensive
Historical scale remains essentially unchanged:
- about 57,000 Soviet/Mongolian personnel;
- roughly 438–498 tanks;
- roughly 385 armored cars;
- more than 500 artillery pieces;
- over 600 aircraft in theater.
The attack begins 20 August with mass air attack and artillery preparation followed by a three-group offensive.

#### Surprise
Strategic/operational surprise: PARTIAL SOVIET SUCCESS.
Exact date, H-hour, and full force are not known.

Tactical surprise: REDUCED.
Japanese positions are already at dawn alert; aircraft and artillery are more dispersed and some fighters are at short readiness.

Consequences:
- Japanese fighters begin getting airborne earlier;
- ground aircraft destruction/damage is lower by several aircraft than matched history;
- Japanese artillery reply begins earlier, but is still heavily suppressed by Soviet massed fires;
- distributed GT power prevents some command, workshop, and airfield functions from going completely dark when local power/distribution is hit.

#### 20 August ground battle
Center:
- Soviet fixing attacks remain successful at pinning the Japanese;
- no Japanese operational breakthrough.

North:
- Fui/Palets Heights remain a strong Japanese defensive complex;
- Soviet progress is no faster than matched history.

South:
- this remains the dangerous axis.
- Japanese reconnaissance identifies the large armored movement during the day.
- prepared AT positions, better artillery registration, retained tank reserve, and special AP substantially increase resistance.
- the Soviet southern advance is reduced from roughly a 12 km historical maximum to approximately 7–9 km in the branch.
- the Soviet wing still penetrates the Japanese flank; it is delayed, not stopped.

By the afternoon/evening of 20 August Japanese headquarters recognizes a genuine double-envelopment attempt earlier than in matched history.
Orders:
- move approximately 18–22 tanks toward the southern threatened sector;
- retain approximately 10–12 tanks for northern/central contingencies;
- keep the balance as central mobile reserve;
- evacuate nonessential rear services and repairable equipment eastward;
- move some short-range artillery to alternate positions before routes are cut.

Main 23rd Division infantry strongpoints are NOT ordered to withdraw. Offensive doctrine and the expectation of counterattack remain.

#### Air reconnaissance / strike effect
Branch Japanese aviation has:
- several additional operational aircraft;
- faster and more reliable reconnaissance;
- better photo-to-target turnaround;
- small planned-bombing accuracy advantage against known crossings, parks, artillery, and vehicle concentrations.

Soviet air superiority in numbers remains decisive.
Do not apply a blanket air-combat accuracy bonus.

20–22 August net branch effect:
- several Japanese aircraft saved from ground destruction/damage;
- small additional Soviet vehicle/aircraft losses from better planned strikes;
- better recognition of armored axes and rear-road danger is strategically more important than bombing damage.

#### Armor/AT effects, 20–22 August
Dense-core ammunition is priority-fired against leading BT/armored-car formations.
Japanese tanks are used as local counterattack/reserve forces rather than as independent breakthrough units.

Relative to matched history:
- additional Soviet/Mongolian AFV mission-kills: approximately +12 to +18 by 22 August;
- Japanese tank temporary damage: approximately 8–12;
- additional permanent Japanese tank loss: approximately 2–4;
- most recoverable branch tank casualties remain east of or close to Japanese lines and can enter workshop recovery.

#### Operational result, 22 August 24:00
Soviet offensive: SUCCESSFUL and continuing.
Japanese 23rd Division: not yet operationally destroyed.
Southern rear: threatened and partially penetrated, but the branch retains a narrower functioning corridor longer than matched history.
Fui Heights: still resisting.
Japanese command: now understands that the Soviet operation is a full-scale envelopment, not a local attack.

The branch does NOT cancel Soviet numerical/material superiority.
The likely consequence is delay of encirclement and increased Japanese escape/recovery opportunity, not a Japanese victory.
