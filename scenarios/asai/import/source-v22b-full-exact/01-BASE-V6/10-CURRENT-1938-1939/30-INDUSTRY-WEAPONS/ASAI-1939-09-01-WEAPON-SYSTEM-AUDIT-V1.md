# ASAI 1939-09-01 WEAPON SYSTEM AUDIT V1
Status date: 1939-09-01 00:00
Purpose: post-Nomonhan full-spectrum weapon check.
Perspective: technical/procurement audit. This does not retroactively alter already adjudicated combat.

## 0. Supersession / ledger correction

The old current-branch weapon ledger entry W-001 ("Type 97 Chi-Ha 180PS normal / 200PS max") is stale relative to the later v27 tank parent.

Current worldline Chi-Ha baseline:
- 15.3–15.6 t class;
- turbo-diesel **200 hp continuous / 220 hp short-duration**;
- low-velocity 57 mm infantry-support gun;
- 25 mm-class frontal armor;
- 4 crew / two-man turret;
- radio space/power reserved, but not all vehicles fitted.

Do not use 180/200 hp for new 1939 calculations.

## 1. Infantry small arms

### Type 38 rifle — KEEP / BULK STANDARD
Still the numerical infantry-rifle base.
No forced 1939 fleet conversion.

### Type 99 rifle — INITIAL SERIES / UNIT-BLOCK TRANSITION
Historical 1939 adoption retained.
Worldline tooling, gauges, heat-treatment/QC and drawing release make the ramp smoother, but do not instantly replace Type 38 stocks.

Procurement rule:
- transition by battalion/regiment/equipment block where possible;
- avoid mixing 6.5 mm and 7.7 mm rifle/LMG ammunition inside the smallest tactical supply units;
- prioritize new formations, replacement pools and selected Manchurian units before stripping functioning Type 38 units.

Important ammunition point:
Type 99 rifle/LMG use 7.7 mm rimless ammunition.
Type 92 HMG was designed for semi-rimmed 7.7 mm, but can use Type 99 rimless ammunition when properly strip-loaded.
Therefore the Type 92 HMG does not have to be replaced merely because a formation transitions to Type 99 rifle/LMG.

### Type 96 LMG — KEEP WITH 6.5 mm FORMATIONS
Still useful and numerous.
Asai effect remains production interchangeability, heat treatment, gauge control and accepted-output/availability.
Do not prematurely scrap it.

### Type 99 LMG — INITIAL SERIES
Adoption/ramp in 1939.
Pair with Type 99-rifle unit blocks.
No army-wide 1939 rearm.

### Type 92 HMG — KEEP
No urgent replacement requirement.
The more important improvements are ammunition-lot control, barrels/spares, sights, tripods and transport.

### Type 89 50 mm grenade discharger — KEEP / HIGH VALUE
No replacement program required.
It already gives infantry squads/platoons very portable high-angle fire.
Asai mainly improves ammunition/fuze lot consistency and sights/range tables.

## 2. Battalion/regimental indirect fire

### Type 92 70 mm battalion gun — KEEP
Highly mobile close-support weapon.
No reason to replace it with a heavier system merely because Nomonhan exposed artillery deficiencies.
Its limitation is range, not usefulness.

### Type 97 81 mm mortar — EXPAND SMOOTHLY
Historically in production from 1938.
Simple enough that Asai gauges/tooling/QC can improve accepted output and interchangeability.
Retain earlier rule: no retroactive OOB inflation; supported lots gain several points of availability.

Post-Nomonhan procurement:
- modest priority increase;
- more ammunition, baseplates, sights and trained crews matter as much as tubes.

It does not solve counter-battery warfare because its range is only about 3 km.

### Type 94 / Type 97 90 mm mortar — LIMITED SUPPORT
Useful heavy infantry/artillery mortar family.
The lighter Type 97 concept is preferable for new production, but 90 mm mortar is not the answer to the Soviet long-range-artillery problem.
Keep limited production and ammunition standardization.

### 150 mm mortar family — HOLD LIMITED
Do not turn short-range heavy mortars into a mass response to Nomonhan.
Transport and ammunition burden are high.

## 3. Anti-tank system

### Type 94 37 mm AT gun — KEEP AS CURRENT STANDARD
Nomonhan validates that the gun is not useless against contemporary BT/T-26-class vehicles.
Worldline dense-core special AP increases short-range margin.

But:
- special AP is scarce;
- future Soviet armor growth is obvious;
- the 37 mm does not provide adequate growth margin.

Conclusion:
**retain and expand ammunition/crew quality, while immediately accelerating the 47 mm successor.**

### Type 97 20 mm automatic AT weapon — NICHE / NO MASS PRIORITY
The weapon can be dangerous to light armor, especially with better AP, but remains heavy/complex for its effect.
Keep trial/special-use branch.
Do not divert the main AT program from 47 mm.

### Experimental Type 97 47 mm AT gun — PRIORITY DEVELOPMENT BASE
Historical anchor retained:
- prototype complete/tested 1938–39;
- about 730 m/s;
- mechanically-towed-carriage work in 1939;
- practical mechanized test historically around October 1939.

Worldline closure:
- Nomonhan converts this from "candidate improvement" to **urgent development base**;
- complete the 1939 mechanized practical test at the earliest physically available date;
- build a small Q4 engineering/service-test lot if the carriage passes;
- do not call this interim 730 m/s prototype the final standard weapon.

Successor objective:
- 47 mm, truck-towed;
- higher-velocity 800+ m/s class;
- semi-automatic/fast breech handling where practicable;
- ordinary AP must be effective without relying on dense-core ammunition.

### 47 mm tank gun / new-turret Chi-Ha — HIGH PRIORITY
Existing current rule retained:
- research 1938;
- 1939 test/priority validation;
- technical maturity around 1940 class.

1939 H2 target:
- gun ballistics/recoil rigs;
- full turret mock-up;
- sight/loader/commander task trials;
- common ammunition/component study with infantry 47 mm where beneficial.

No 1939 front-line new-turret Chi-Ha is created.

## 4. Field artillery

### Type 95 75 mm field gun — KEEP AS GENERAL LIGHT FIELD GUN
Light and practical.
No reason to replace every divisional gun with the heavier Type 90.

### Type 90 75 mm field gun — PRIORITY FOR MOTORIZED / MANCHURIAN FORMATIONS
About 15 km range and high muzzle velocity make it especially useful after Nomonhan.
Asai turbo prime movers make the motorized version materially more usable.

Procurement direction:
- increase priority for mechanized/border formations;
- do not universalize it to every division because cost, weight and tractor demand remain.

### Type 91 10 cm howitzer — KEEP
General-purpose medium support.
Useful but not a long-range counter-battery solution.

### Type 92 10 cm cannon — STRONG PRIORITY INCREASE
About 18 km class range is directly relevant to the Nomonhan lesson.
This is one of the clearest "already-existing weapon, insufficient quantity/support" cases.

Priority:
- guns;
- prime movers;
- ammunition;
- survey/met sections;
- sound/flash location;
- alternate firing-position preparation.

### Type 96 15 cm howitzer — KEEP / DESTRUCTION FIRE
Excellent heavy HE effect, but ~12 km range does not solve the long-range duel.
Expand only in balance with prime movers and ammunition.

### Type 89 15 cm cannon — HIGH-VALUE HEAVY GUN, LIMITED BY MOBILITY/COST
~20 km class range is valuable.
But it is very heavy and requires tractor/two-load movement.
Turbo prime movers improve usability; they do not make it a cheap divisional gun.

Post-Nomonhan:
- higher readiness/tractor/ammunition priority for existing heavy artillery;
- limited production increase is rational;
- no mass replacement of lighter artillery.

### Type 96 24 cm howitzer — HOLD
Retain previous branch: few test/limited weapons, earlier diagnosis of dispersion/technical trouble.
Do not solve the artillery problem by buying many questionable 24 cm weapons.

## 5. Self-propelled/direct support

### 75 mm SPG — DEVELOPMENT START CONFIRMED
Existing canonical route retained:
- 1938–39 tractor/chassis self-propelled-gun experiments;
- 1939 Nomonhan validates requirement;
- Chi-Ha-family chassis + Type 90 75 mm-class gun + fixed open fighting compartment;
- prototype 1940;
- limited advance production / troop trial 1941.

Role:
direct infantry/tank support and bunker destruction, with secondary AT capability.
Not a substitute for ordinary artillery.

## 6. Anti-aircraft

### Type 98 20 mm AA cannon — FIELD-VALIDATED / EXPAND
Historical 1938 service entry and Nomonhan combat are retained.
Light, maneuverable, high-velocity system.

Worldline procurement:
- clear priority increase after Nomonhan;
- manufacturing/QC benefits from Asai tooling and heat treatment;
- maintain its secondary emergency anti-armor role, but do not treat it as the main AT gun.

### Type 88 75 mm AA gun — KEEP / FIRE-CONTROL IMPROVEMENT
Still the medium-AA backbone.
Increasing aircraft performance will erode margin over the next few years.

Asai effect:
- optical calibration;
- plotting/firing data;
- power reliability;
- fuze/lot QC;
- not radar and not a new gun.

### Navy Type 96 25 mm AA — KEEP WITH RELIABILITY PACKAGE
Earlier closure retained:
improved firing-pin/heat-treatment package spreads through new production and high-use sites.
Magazine, tracking and director limitations remain.
Do not turn it into a Bofors-equivalent system.

## 7. Tanks and armored vehicles

### Type 95 Ha-Go — KEEP, ROLE LIMIT
Useful light tank/recon/infantry support.
Do not universal-turbo it.
Nomonhan reinforces that it should not be the primary answer to enemy tank formations.

### Type 97 Chi-Ha — MAIN MEDIUM, 200/220 hp CURRENT
Corrected baseline:
200 hp continuous / 220 hp short-duration turbo-diesel.

Nomonhan lesson:
- better support/recovery and higher Chi-Ha share improve survivability;
- 57 mm low-velocity gun is the decisive armament limit;
- 25 mm armor cannot defeat Soviet 45 mm fire.

Therefore:
increase production/Manchurian allocation where resources allow, but pair this with the 47 mm program.
Do not waste major resources merely adding another small engine-power increment.

### Type 97 Te-Ke — RECON / SECURITY
Keep historical role.
Do not treat tankettes as tank-fighting assets.

### 1938 GT tank demonstrator — TECH SUCCESS, NO GENERAL PROCUREMENT
Retain as experimental/cold-weather/special branch.
Nomonhan strengthens the case for turbo-diesel propulsion and GT auxiliary power, not mass direct-GT tank propulsion.

## 8. Support vehicles as weapon-system components

### Turbo heavy prime movers / heavy 6x4 cargo — PROCUREMENT-GRADE POSITIVE
Post-Nomonhan status is now strong:
preferred for heavy-load categories.

### Recovery/workshop vehicle package — STANDARDIZE
Move from ad-hoc useful equipment to a defined package:
- heavy turbo-diesel carrier/tractor;
- winch;
- welding/portable tools;
- standard spares;
- mobile power connection;
- recovery rigging.

### Mobile GT utility set — LIMITED STANDARD SUPPORT EQUIPMENT
Standardize at army/corps, major artillery, airfield and workshop level.
Not one per company.

### Command/radio vehicle — PRIORITY INCREASE
Nomonhan shows that mobile reserves are much less useful without reliable command links.
Use existing radios; improve vehicle power, installation, antenna, spares and maintenance.
Do not invent later-war radio doctrine instantly.

### Engineer/bridging vehicles — PRIORITY STUDY
A vehicle cannot use horsepower where the route/bridge does not exist.
The next mechanization program must include route repair, bridging, recovery and water supply as part of the formation, not as afterthoughts.

## 9. Aviation armament

### Army 7.7 mm fighter guns — CURRENT, INADEQUACY RECOGNIZED
Remain installed on current fighters.
No retroactive rearm.

### 12.7 mm Ho-101/102/103/104 comparison — PROGRAM OPENED 1939
Nomonhan/foreign-aircraft trends justify the historical 1939 requirement.
Asai manufacturing/testing can improve spring, feed, synchronization, ammunition-lot and endurance work.

Do not put operational Ho-103 guns into 1939 combat aircraft yet.
1939 H2 is comparative prototype/test work.

### Navy Type 99 20 mm aircraft cannon — INITIAL PRODUCTION / HIGH VALUE
Historical Oerlikon-derived adoption path retained.
1939 domestic production is real.
Asai contribution is accepted-output, heat treatment, springs, gauges and ammunition lot consistency.

No ballistic "upgrade" is credited beyond the historical gun family.

## 10. Naval heavy weapons

No broad post-Nomonhan rebaseline is required for:
- Type 93 torpedo;
- battleship/cruiser main batteries;
- ordinary naval gun calibers.

Asai effects remain in:
- fire-control computation/information transfer;
- electrical reliability;
- manufacturing/QC;
- Type 96 25 mm reliability;
- Type 99 20 mm aircraft cannon.

Do not use an Army border-war lesson to redesign the entire fleet automatically.

## 11. Immediate 1939 H2 priority order

Tier A — urgent:
1. 47 mm infantry AT successor + new-turret Chi-Ha ballistic/turret work.
2. Type 92 10 cm cannon / Type 90 75 mm motorized support, ammunition and prime movers.
3. Type 98 20 mm AA production/readiness.
4. recovery/workshop, command-radio, bridging and heavy turbo-vehicle packages.
5. artillery survey/met/flash-sound/photo-recon integration.

Tier B — expand without redesign:
6. Type 97 81 mm mortar and ammunition.
7. Type 94 37 mm AT gun + ordinary AP quality + limited dense-core reserve.
8. Type 99 rifle/LMG low-rate ramp by formation block.
9. Type 89 grenade discharger / Type 92 battalion gun supply and ammunition quality.

Tier C — development, not 1939 field issue:
10. 75 mm SPG.
11. Ho-103-class 12.7 mm aircraft gun.
12. new-turret Chi-Ha production.
13. next medium tank / 50 mm frontal / 5-man 3-man-turret branch.

Tier D — do not overreact:
- no army-wide Type 99 small-arms conversion in 1939;
- no universal turbo light vehicles;
- no mass GT tank;
- no dense-core "fix" for the low-velocity Chi-Ha 57 mm;
- no mass Type 96 24 cm howitzer;
- no assumption that better tanks substitute for long-range artillery, ammunition, radios or transport quantity.

## 12. Net assessment

At 1939-09-01 the Japanese Army does not lack individual useful weapons.
The more serious weakness is **system balance**:
- enough long-range guns and shells;
- enough transport;
- command radio density;
- AT growth margin;
- recovery/engineering;
- reconnaissance-to-fire-control integration.

Nomonhan therefore pushes procurement away from "one wonder weapon" and toward a mechanized combined-arms package.
