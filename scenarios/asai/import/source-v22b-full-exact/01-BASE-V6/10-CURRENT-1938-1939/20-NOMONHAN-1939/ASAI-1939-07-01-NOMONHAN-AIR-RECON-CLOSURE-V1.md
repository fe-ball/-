# ASAI 1939-07-01 NOMONHAN AIR / RECON CLOSURE V1

Status: CLOSED patch at 1939-06-30 24:00, for use from Gate 87 onward.

## 1. Asai twin-engine aircraft
- E5 Twin No.1 (pure jet) and E5 Twin No.2 (aft-fan) physically exist and fly.
- Twin No.2 has A3+ durability-demonstration status by 1939-06-30.
- Neither aircraft is a service aircraft, has a unit establishment, or counts toward Nomonhan combat OOB on 1939-07-01.
- Army may request a Hailar field-evaluation detachment for one E5 twin in July 1939, but no combat-reconnaissance credit is allowed until absolute combat radius, field support and restart reliability are validated.

## 2. Ki-15-II early reconnaissance branch
Historical anchors:
- Ki-15-II prototype first flew June 1938.
- Ha-26-I installation raised speed to about 510 km/h and improved forward visibility.
- Historical series production began September 1939.

Worldline closure:
- Existing Mitsubishi U4 engineering, mature Ha-26-I / Z-900 industrial branch, propeller/tooling/QC capacity allow pilot-series transition ahead of historical mass-production date.
- Technical release: 1939 Q1.
- Low-rate pilot production: 1939 Q2.
- Accepted population by 1939-06-30: 8-10 aircraft (not mass production).
- Nomonhan/Hailar priority allocation by late June: 4 Ki-15-II operational, 2 additional reserve/evaluation aircraft in rear-area pool.
- The 1939-06-24 Tamsagbulag reconnaissance mission is treated as a Ki-15-II mission in this worldline. The historical strategic observation (large Soviet aircraft concentration) remains; improved speed/visibility/photo handling reduces exposure and improves target-layout confirmation, but does not create clairvoyance through cloud or weather.
- Existing June 27 bombing-effect delta already includes the improved reconnaissance/target-processing chain; do not add another bombing bonus.

## 3. Ki-36 direct-cooperation reconnaissance
Historical anchors:
- First flight April 1938.
- Production began November 1938.
- Ki-36 operated at Nomonhan historically by August 1939, including reconnaissance and attack missions.

Worldline closure:
- Production/QC improvement creates enough accepted aircraft for an earlier emergency detachment without changing national production order of magnitude.
- A 6-aircraft direct-cooperation detachment is ordered toward the Hailar/front area at end-June.
- Arrival/operational target: 1939-07-06 to 07-10.
- It does NOT participate in the opening July 2 tank offensive.
- Primary roles: artillery observation, close battlefield reconnaissance, message/report transfer, route/column observation, post-strike observation.
- It remains highly vulnerable to Soviet fighters; use is concentrated under fighter cover, low-level terrain masking, early/late-day windows, and short-duration missions.

## 4. Existing bomber/fighter aircraft
- Ki-21: historical twin-engine bomber is already operational at Nomonhan. No new twin reconnaissance role is created. Asai effects primarily raise mission-ready fraction, engine/propeller reliability, bombing preparation and photo/weather-support quality. OOB is not inflated.
- Ki-30: no calendar rewrite. Local serviceability and bombing-preparation quality are slightly better; no automatic attack-effect multiplier.
- Ki-27: no early successor is inserted. Benefit is accepted-output/replacement flow, engine/propeller QC and mission-ready fraction, not a fictional Ki-27 super-variant.

## 5. Aircraft explicitly NOT available for July 1939 combat
- Ki-15-III / Ha-102: NO. Ha-102 is a later major redesign and is not pulled into 1939 Nomonhan.
- Ki-46: NO. Prototype flight remains late 1939 even in worldline.
- Ki-48: first prototype has just flown 1939-06-29 in worldline; NO combat use.
- Ki-51: first prototype is flying, but NO July 2 combat use. If the second prototype and protection package pass in late July, a 2-4 aircraft operational-test detachment to Manchuria may be opened for August; this remains event-gated.
- Ki-43 / Ki-45: development aircraft, NO combat issue.

## 6. Reconnaissance information-processing chain
At Hailar / 2nd Air Division level, establish a reconnaissance-processing cell using existing Asai-derived infrastructure:
- calibrated camera/lens records;
- photo numbering with altitude/time/heading metadata;
- distortion correction and stereo/photo-map comparison where suitable;
- standardized target grid and previous-photo indexing;
- plotting/light-table/darkroom work supported by reliable emergency electrical power;
- card/index support for recurring airfields, artillery sites, bridges, routes and vehicle concentrations.

Effect is not 'better camera = double accuracy'. The military dividend is shorter and more reproducible conversion of photographs into usable map/target information.

A small GT utility set may support the Hailar photo/intelligence and communications work, but no forward real-time digital reconnaissance exists.

## 7. July 2 reconnaissance gate
Historical combat study records a July 2 reconnaissance message reporting a Soviet withdrawal, with visibility/accuracy degraded by cloud/rain.
Worldline rule:
- Ki-15-II speed, availability and processing improve the chance of a confirming sortie.
- Weather remains a hard limiter.
- Do not auto-correct the withdrawal report.
- At Gate 87, explicitly test whether a second observation/photo pass arrives before Yasuoka's pursuit decision. This can materially change the tank/combined-arms attack geometry and must not be buried inside a generic air-power bonus.

## Closure summary
- Asai twin jets exist, but are not Nomonhan service aircraft on July 1.
- The important aviation divergence is an early small Ki-15-II reconnaissance pool plus earlier Ki-36 direct-cooperation availability, backed by a stronger photo-to-map processing chain.
- Historical twin Ki-21 bombers remain the actual operational twin-engine combat aircraft.
- Future July/August combat must account separately for strategic reconnaissance, close reconnaissance/artillery observation, bomber attack quality, fighter availability, and weather.
