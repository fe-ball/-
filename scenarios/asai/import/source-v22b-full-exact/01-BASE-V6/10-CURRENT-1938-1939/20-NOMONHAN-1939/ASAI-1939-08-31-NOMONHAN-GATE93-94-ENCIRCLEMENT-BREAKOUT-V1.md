# ASAI 1939-08-31 NOMONHAN — GATE 93–94
## 23–31 August: corridor battle, encirclement, coordinated breakout, and termination of organized resistance

Status date: 1939-08-31 24:00

Perspective rule:
- Historical comparison is for external adjudication only.
- In-world Japanese decisions use only contemporary reconnaissance, unit reports, road status, fuel/ammunition, surviving reserves, communications, and observed Soviet movement.

## Gate 93 — 23–26 August

### 23 August: Japanese decision
The 23rd Division retains an offensive mindset. It does not order a general withdrawal.
However, repeated Ki-15-II reconnaissance, surviving ground reports, and traffic/road information show:
- major armored forces remain active on the southern wing;
- northern pressure is also increasing;
- the eastern road/corridor is still usable but increasingly exposed;
- mobile tank reserve and some artillery remain intact;
- central and northern strongpoints remain organized.

Therefore the historical-style counterattack concept is narrowed:
**objective = restore and hold the eastward communications corridor, not destroy the entire Soviet enveloping force.**

Approximately 18–22 tanks are committed in limited local counterstrokes with artillery/AT support.
No unsupported mass tank pursuit is authorized.

Branch adjudication, 23–24 August:
- additional Soviet/Mongolian AFV mission-kills relative to baseline: +6 to +10;
- Japanese tank temporary damage: 5–8;
- Japanese tank permanent loss: 1–3;
- limited corridor positions are recovered/held for several additional hours;
- no operational defeat of the Soviet southern wing.

### Fui Heights
Fui remains largely isolated after 22 August.
Vehicle and GT improvements cannot drive through Soviet-controlled ground.
Air reconnaissance and message-drop improve situational awareness but cannot supply a battalion-sized position with ammunition, food, or water.

No early doctrinal miracle is applied:
- headquarters does not issue a timely general retreat order;
- Ioki's defenders continue to hold;
- withdrawal still occurs on the night of 24–25 August.

Because eastern pickup/transport is better prepared and the route is somewhat better known:
- roughly 50–90 additional personnel escape compared with the matched-history outcome.
This is an external adjudication value only.

### 24–25 August: evacuation while the corridor still functions
As the road becomes increasingly threatened, Sixth Army/23rd Division orders:
- nonessential rear services east;
- mobile workshops and repairable vehicles east;
- wounded who can be moved east;
- selected short-range artillery and prime movers to alternate positions east of the threatened closure;
- part of the tank force to screen the east side of the corridor.

The main infantry strongpoints remain ordered to fight.

Before complete closure, branch evacuation removes approximately:
- 1,200–1,800 additional rear/service/wounded personnel from the threatened pocket;
- 70–110 additional motor vehicles, tractors, ambulances and workshop vehicles;
- 8–12 additional guns/mortars or equivalent heavy weapons that would otherwise risk abandonment;
- most mobile GT utility sets in the immediate rear.

Tank disposition by late 25 August:
- 14–18 tanks are deliberately positioned east/outside the most threatened pocket as mobile reserve/route security;
- approximately 20–26 combat-ready tanks remain inside or immediately adjacent to the shrinking position;
- others are in repair or have been permanently lost.

### Encirclement closure
External adjudication:
The Soviet northern and southern wings achieve an operationally continuous ring in the **early hours of 26 August**, rather than treating the exact historical link-up time as information available to Japanese commanders.

In-world Japanese report:
- eastward road movement is no longer reliable;
- Soviet armor/fire blocks the principal route;
- division elements are now isolated in multiple groups.

Operational Soviet success remains intact.

### 26 August relief movement
Historical anchor: Japanese relief elements moving from Hailar on 26 August had good going in the morning; heavy rain after about 15:00 turned ground muddy and vehicle breakdowns sharply slowed the march.

Branch vehicle effect:
- turbo-diesel heavy vehicles, better filters, recovery, and maintenance reduce breakdown and keep more columns together;
- mud remains a physical limit.

Adjudicated effect:
- relief columns gain roughly 2–4 hours of useful movement/assembly margin;
- artillery/ammunition arrives in somewhat better order;
- no same-day magical breakthrough occurs.

## Gate 94 — 27–31 August

### 27 August: coordinated relief/breakout
The branch differs most here.

The external 7th Division relief force and the trapped 23rd Division have:
- better radio/power reliability at higher headquarters;
- repeated aerial reconnaissance;
- a clearer map of Soviet armored axes;
- tanks on both sides of the ring;
- a small remaining stock of dense-core 37 mm AP;
- more coherent motor transport and artillery support.

A coordinated inside-out attack is therefore attempted on a selected eastern/northeastern seam.

Forces available:
- outside: relief infantry/artillery plus approximately 12–16 tanks;
- inside: approximately 14–18 tanks available for the breakout thrust;
- remaining tanks held for flank/rear security or unavailable.

Result:
**TACTICAL TEMPORARY CORRIDOR — SUCCESS**
**OPERATIONAL RELIEF OF THE WHOLE 23RD DIVISION — FAILURE**

The corridor is usable only for approximately 2–4 hours before Soviet armor, artillery, and air attack close it again.

Evacuation priority becomes:
1. wounded and walking wounded;
2. headquarters/signals personnel and records;
3. mobile artillery/AT weapons that can move immediately;
4. intact tanks/tractors/workshop vehicles;
5. coherent infantry groups.

Approximately 2,000–2,800 personnel escape through this organized corridor.
A material fraction remain wounded casualties, but are no longer abandoned inside the pocket.

Tank effects during the 27 August corridor battle:
- additional Soviet/Mongolian AFV mission-kills relative to baseline: +7 to +11;
- Japanese temporary tank damage: 6–9;
- Japanese permanent tank loss: 2–4.

### 28–30 August
Soviet forces restore a solid ring and reduce the remaining pockets with artillery, armor, flamethrower vehicles, infantry and air attack.
Japanese artillery ammunition and water become critical.
No GT or turbo technology solves isolation once roads and supply routes are physically cut.

The branch remaining pockets are smaller because rear/support/mobile elements have already escaped.
Night infiltration and small-unit breakout continue.

Additional branch escape, 28–30 August:
- approximately 600–1,000 personnel beyond the organized 27 August corridor;
- mostly small infantry, signals, reconnaissance and service groups.

The remaining large strongpoints are destroyed or forced east by 30–31 August.

### 31 August 24:00 result
Soviet operational objective:
**ACHIEVED.**
Japanese organized forces no longer hold the disputed Mongolian-side battlefield.

23rd Division:
**severely defeated and combat-ineffective, but less completely annihilated.**
Enough cadres, signals personnel, wounded, vehicle troops and some infantry escape that post-battle reconstruction is materially easier.

External branch-vs-matched-history adjudication:
- additional Japanese personnel extricated from the August encirclement: approximately +2,800 to +3,700;
- of these, roughly 1,000–1,400 are wounded/otherwise already casualties;
- reduction in KIA/MIA/POW/abandoned wounded: approximately 1,900–2,600;
- this is not equal to the reduction in total casualties.

Armored force at 31 August:
- physically surviving/recoverable tanks east of the battlefield: approximately 30–36;
- likely combat-ready after 5–10 days workshop effort: approximately 25–31;
- July/August branch therefore preserves a meaningful armored cadre and maintenance organization.

Mobile GT utility sets:
- 6–8 sets survive in operational/repairable condition;
- 0–1 set is lost or damaged beyond local repair during the final displacement;
- field evaluation remains strongly positive for workshop, signals, airfield and water-support roles.

Dense-core 37 mm ammunition:
- forward stock largely exhausted;
- approximately 100–200 rounds remain immediately usable in the battle area by the end of August;
- tactical effectiveness is validated, but quantity is insufficient to alter the operational balance.

Aviation:
- Soviet numerical air superiority remains.
- Japanese reconnaissance continues to provide better axis/corridor awareness than baseline.
- weather/visibility still governs whether a given sortie can observe.
- speed, fuel/range margin and readiness mainly improve the probability of exploiting short weather windows and repeating reconnaissance.
- no blanket fighter kill-rate bonus is applied.

### Strategic lesson generated inside the worldline
Japanese commanders do not conclude that they nearly won because of comparison with another history.
Their own records show:
- local armored counterattacks can work when tied to artillery and AT guns;
- turbo-diesel prime movers and workshop transport remain useful under heavy load;
- mobile GT utility power is valuable;
- reconnaissance/photo processing materially improves decision quality;
- yet Soviet long-range artillery, ammunition volume, motor transport, armor mass and operational envelopment remain beyond what these improvements can cancel.

Thus the internal lesson is:
**mechanization works, but partial mechanization cannot defeat a fully integrated larger mechanized system.**
