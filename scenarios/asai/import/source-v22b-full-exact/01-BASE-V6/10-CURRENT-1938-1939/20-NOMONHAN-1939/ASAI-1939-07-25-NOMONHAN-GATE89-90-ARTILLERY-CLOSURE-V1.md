## 0. Historical anchors retained

- 10 July: Soviet artillery pressure on Japanese positions and a Japanese night adjustment/attack around Hinomaru Heights.
- 11-12 July: further hard night fighting; Japanese front is locally reorganized and some forward positions are abandoned.
- 12 July: 23rd Division orders preparation for a renewed offensive after reinforced artillery finishes deployment.
- 14-15 July: historical Japanese local command briefly misreads Soviet movements as withdrawal and attempts another night action toward Kawamata Bridge.
- 16-20 July: heavy artillery reinforcements move from Hailar to the battlefield; one recorded heavy-artillery battalion makes a roughly 240 km road movement after unloading at Hailar.
- 17 and 19 July: division attack plans explicitly wait for concentrated artillery and call for destruction/suppression of Soviet artillery before infantry advance.
- 23-25 July: historical Japanese general attack with roughly 82 guns; Japanese artillery ammunition expenditure recorded in a Kwantung Army report totals approximately 19,947 rounds for 23-25 July.
- Soviet artillery retains major advantages in long-range pieces, ammunition depth, mechanized mobility, and west-bank observation/elevation.
- Historical Japanese attack fails to eliminate the Soviet east-bank bridgehead and is halted after 25 July.

## 1. 10-12 July: night fighting with armor retained as reserve

The worldline does NOT put the surviving ~50 Japanese tanks back into night assault spearheads.

CLOSED employment:
- 3rd and 4th Tank Regiments remain dispersed/camouflaged as a mobile reserve.
- company/platoon groups may counterattack Soviet penetrations in daylight or at dawn after artillery/AT preparation;
- no deep night armor movement toward the Halha is authorized.

Worldline support effects:
- Ki-36 artillery observation is now fully operational at 5-6 aircraft strength when weather/light permits;
- Ki-15-II conducts deeper reconnaissance and repeat checks;
- artillery fire plans are more repeatable because survey, lot control, prepared data and transcription discipline are stronger;
- mobile GT sets sustain command, workshop, water-pumping and photo-processing nodes.

Result:
- Japanese night actions still take ground only temporarily;
- Soviet daylight artillery/motorized counteraction still forces Japanese local withdrawals;
- tanks reduce the risk that a Soviet armored probe turns a local withdrawal into a rupture, but do not reverse the bridgehead situation.

Worldline delta for 10-12 July:
- Japanese battle casualties avoided: roughly 45-70;
- additional Soviet/Mongolian casualties/temporary combat losses: roughly 70-120;
- Japanese tank damage: 1-3 temporary, no more than 0-1 irrecoverable in this subperiod.

## 2. 13-15 July: intelligence branch closes a historical false-retreat action

Historical Japanese local command interpreted Soviet disorder/rotation on 12 July as evidence that the Soviets were withdrawing and attempted another movement toward Kawamata Bridge. This assessment was disproved only after troops encountered organized Soviet positions.

In the worldline, the intelligence chain is materially better:
- repeated Ki-15-II sweeps;
- Ki-36 local observation;
- photo comparison against prior trench/vehicle patterns;
- faster map plotting and card-indexed target/state tracking.

CLOSED assessment by 14 July afternoon:
- Soviet east-bank forces have rotated/reorganized but are NOT in general withdrawal;
- west-bank artillery continues active fire;
- the local report of Soviet retreat is marked low confidence and is not accepted as an operational premise.

CLOSED action:
- the historical full local night attempt toward Kawamata Bridge is CANCELLED as an attack;
- it is reduced to reconnaissance/patrol/limited positional adjustment.

Effect:
- no territorial gain is credited;
- Japanese casualties otherwise spent in the false-retreat attack are avoided;
- Soviet bridgehead remains.

Worldline delta:
- Japanese battle casualties avoided: additional ~70-110;
- Soviet casualties are correspondingly slightly lower than a full failed assault would have produced; no false kill credit is added.

This is an intelligence/process success, not a new sensor technology.

## 3. 16-22 July: reinforced artillery concentration

Historical heavy artillery and ammunition moved over long road distances from Hailar. A documented field-heavy-artillery battalion unloaded on 16 July and began a roughly 240 km motor road movement on 17 July.

Worldline transport effects already CLOSED upstream:
- heavy tractor / truck ready fraction +5-8 percentage points for exposed fleets;
- routine repair turnaround -15-25%;
- better dust filtration/cooling/oil-control in priority heavy vehicles;
- better route and load accounting;
- mobile power at repair and communications nodes.

CLOSED deployment consequence:
- heavy artillery units arrive mechanically less depleted;
- individual echelons are generally 8-16 hours ahead of matched-history readiness where route conditions permit;
- by 20-21 July the worldline can physically deploy nearly all of the artillery historically available for the 23 July attack.

However:
- attack date is NOT advanced to 21/22 July.
- ammunition accumulation, survey, counterbattery target preparation, aviation coordination and command timing remain required.
- 23 July remains the attack date.

## 4. Ammunition-production and supply delta

The shallow industrial spillover is now explicitly applied to non-Asai artillery ammunition.

CLOSED 1939 H1 effects for established artillery ammunition families:
- shell-body/fuze accepted output: approximately +3-5% versus matched history in exposed arsenals;
- main causes: tool life, gauges, heat-treatment/lot control, reject/rework reduction and card-based production control;
- explosives/raw filling capacity prevents a larger percentage.

Combined with transport/logistics effects and Manchurian priority after May:
- ammunition physically ready at the Nomonhan artillery parks before 23 July is approximately +6-8% versus matched history.

This does NOT triple Japanese shell stocks and does not solve strategic ammunition scarcity.

## 5. Artillery effective strength on 23 July

Historical nominal Japanese concentration: roughly 82 guns.
Range geometry remains decisive: many Japanese guns cannot conduct effective counterbattery fire against Soviet west-bank heavy artillery.

Worldline CLOSED:
- nominal gun count: unchanged at roughly historical 82;
- immediately serviceable/laid/communications-connected guns at H-hour: +4 to +6 effective pieces compared with matched-history ready state, without exceeding nominal establishment;
- long-range gun count itself is not magically increased;
- Japanese shorter-range pieces still cannot answer Soviet long-range guns merely because they are reliable.

CLOSED fire-control advantage:
- air-photo / map target handoff is faster;
- counterbattery coordinates have fewer transcription/plotting errors;
- prepared fires and lot corrections are more repeatable;
- Ki-36 and optical observation shorten some adjustment cycles;
- favorable known-target engagements can occasionally save one adjustment bracket, but no global accuracy multiplier is used.

## 6. Air and artillery-observation support, 23-25 July

By late July Soviet reinforcements are reducing Japanese air freedom.
Worldline aircraft readiness and fuel/engine quality improve use of available weather windows but do not guarantee air superiority.

CLOSED:
- Ki-15-II photo/recon sweeps continue at higher reliability;
- Ki-36 artillery observation operates when fighters/weather permit;
- Japanese fighter readiness gives modestly more cover sorties than matched history;
- Soviet fighters still contest the air strongly;
- Japanese observation balloons remain vulnerable and are not treated as protected assets.

No universal bombing-accuracy multiplier is applied.

## 7. 23 July opening bombardment

The opening Japanese bombardment is materially better organized than matched history.

Worldline effects are concentrated in the first hours:
- a higher fraction of nominal guns actually fire on schedule;
- long-range counterbattery batteries acquire/suppress several known Soviet positions more quickly;
- better photo/plotting reduces wasted opening salvos on stale coordinates;
- artillery ammunition arrives more steadily to firing positions.

But Soviet artillery advantages remain:
- longer-ranged heavy guns;
- larger ammunition reserve;
- strong west-bank observation;
- mechanized movement;
- ability to replace/switch firing positions.

CLOSED Japanese ammunition expenditure, 23-25 July:
- matched-history anchor: ~19,947 rounds;
- worldline: **~20,800-21,700 rounds**.

The increase is deliberately modest. Higher logistics/ready rates let more planned fire actually occur, but Japanese command begins rationing once the failure of decisive counterbattery becomes obvious.

## 8. Infantry and armor in the 23-25 July offensive

Retained armor is NOT recreated as a Yasuoka-style deep penetration force.

CLOSED armored employment:
- 12-16 combat-ready tanks are committed in rotating close-support groups behind/with infantry after artillery preparation;
- remainder is held as mobile reserve against Soviet armored counterattack;
- no river-crossing/deep-exploitation mission is authorized.

Effect:
- infantry can suppress/overrun some machine-gun positions more cleanly on the first day;
- local advance is several hundred meters to roughly 1 km deeper in favorable sectors than matched history;
- no decisive approach to or seizure of Kawamata Bridge;
- Soviet 45 mm guns and artillery remain fully capable of defeating Japanese tanks.

Armor losses 23-25 July:
- temporary/repairable: ~4-7;
- additional irrecoverable: ~1-2;
- most temporary losses are recoverable after the offensive because the armored force is not stranded in a deep penetration.

## 9. Net artillery-offensive result

CLOSED strategic/tactical result:
- Japanese 23-25 July offensive FAILS to destroy Soviet artillery or eliminate the east-bank bridgehead.
- Japanese infantry obtains somewhat cleaner and slightly deeper first-day gains than matched history.
- Soviet counterbattery and ammunition mass restore the balance during 24 July.
- by 25 July the Japanese command recognizes that continuing the same artillery duel is uneconomic and operationally unpromising.
- major attack is halted on 25 July, same date.

The worldline does NOT continue the offensive simply because initial fire is better: improved ammunition accounting and fire-effect reporting make the unfavorable exchange visible sooner as well.

## 10. Casualty delta, 10-25 July

Historical anchor:
- Kwantung Army report through 22 July: 23rd Division KIA 1,085; WIA 2,436; sick 748; MIA 24.
- historical cumulative through 25 July in Drea's cited Japanese data: KIA 1,377; WIA 3,044; MIA 36; sick 828.

Worldline effects over 10-22 July:
- better fire support and retained armor reserve reduce exposed infantry losses;
- cancellation of the false-retreat local attack avoids additional unnecessary casualties;
- water/pump/transport support reduces a small number of non-battle illness cases but cannot solve water scarcity or disease.

CLOSED worldline delta for 10-22 July:
- Japanese battle casualties avoided: ~180-260;
- illness cases avoided/delayed: ~45-70.

Worldline delta during 23-25 July offensive:
- Japanese battle casualties avoided: ~120-180 versus matched history, despite deeper local gains;
- Soviet/Mongolian additional battle casualties/temporary combat losses: ~250-400, primarily from better opening artillery and closer infantry-support fires.

The Japanese still suffer heavy losses and the artillery offensive still fails.

Support:

- dense-core 37 mm ammunition is materially depleted and is now restricted to priority AT sectors rather than broadly issued.

## 12. Gate conclusion

By 25 July the worldline has changed the *shape* of the Japanese failure, not the strategic outcome.

Matched-history:
- armor effectively removed from the theater;
- repeated infantry night attacks;
- major artillery concentration fails;
- east-bank Soviet bridgehead survives.

Worldline:
- armor remains as a useful reserve;
- one historically false-premise night attack is cancelled through better reconnaissance/intelligence processing;
- artillery deployment/readiness and opening counterbattery effects are better;
- ammunition delivery is better but still finite;
- 23-25 July offensive still fails because Soviet range, shell depth, mechanized mobility and operational mass remain superior.

This leaves the Japanese 23rd Division less physically exhausted and with a surviving armored reserve, but the Soviet side still controls the strategic escalation curve.

Next gate should begin 26 July and close the post-offensive defensive period through the Soviet August buildup / 20 August offensive preparation.
