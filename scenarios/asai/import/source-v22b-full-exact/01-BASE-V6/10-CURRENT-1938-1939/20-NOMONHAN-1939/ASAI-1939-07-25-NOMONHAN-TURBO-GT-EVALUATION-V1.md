# ASAI 1939-07-25 NOMONHAN — TURBO / GT VEHICLE EVALUATION V1

Status date: 1939-07-25 24:00
Authority intent: session closure patch; apply after Gate 89–90 and before the August Nomohan buildup.

## 1. Diesel exhaust-turbo military vehicles

### Status
**PROCUREMENT-GRADE POSITIVE / FIELD-VALIDATED**

The Nomohan campaign does not demonstrate that turbocharging defeats mud, poor roads, fuel shortage, or Soviet artillery.
It does demonstrate that, where a route remains physically passable, low-pressure turbocharged diesel vehicles retain useful power and readiness better under:
- full-load towing,
- high ambient temperature,
- repeated acceleration/deceleration,
- artillery displacement,
- recovery/winch duty,
- long-duration heavy transport.

Existing current-branch rule is retained:
- turbo benefit is allocated primarily to sustained rating, gradeability, speed recovery, high-temperature/high-altitude flat-rating, and durability;
- it is not converted automatically into higher road top speed.

### Army evaluation by 1939-07-25
Priority applications move from "useful special fit" to **preferred standard fit for high-load categories**:
1. heavy artillery prime movers;
2. heavy 6x4 cargo vehicles;
3. recovery/workshop vehicles;
4. heavy pump/generator carriers;
5. selected armored-vehicle engines where cooling/transmission margins are already designed for boost.

Do **not** universalize turbocharging to every light truck or every Type 95 light tank.

### Procurement implication
For 1939 H2 / 1940 planning:
- turbocharger allocation to heavy prime movers and recovery/workshop vehicles: **priority increase**;
- general light transport: retain mixed naturally aspirated/turbo fleet;
- vehicle-engine design programs should increasingly assume turbo-compatible cooling, lubrication, intake filtration, gearing and clutch margins from design origin.

## 2. Mobile GT utility / power vehicles

### Status
**LIMITED-STANDARD SUPPORT EQUIPMENT / FIELD-VALIDATED**

Nomohan use validates the GT where its established commercial advantages matter:
- immediate starting,
- high power density,
- low standby maintenance burden,
- ability to drive electrical generation, pumps and workshop loads,
- useful hot exhaust for water heating, drying, cleaning and cold-start support.

It does not validate the GT as a fuel-efficient continuous prime mover.

### Useful field roles confirmed
- mobile workshop and recovery power;
- headquarters / signals emergency power;
- airfield maintenance and lighting power;
- water pumping / purification support;
- limited medical/engineering utility;
- waste-heat water/air heating attachment.

Field lesson:
**electric-power value > heat value in summer Nomohan; heat value rises sharply in Manchurian autumn/winter.**

### Procurement implication
The Army may standardize a mobile GT utility-set family at corps/army, major artillery, airfield and workshop level.
Do not distribute one per company/battalion.

1939 H2 procurement direction:
- additional mobile GT utility sets: **increase**;
- focus first on packaging, filters, generators, switchgear, pumps, cables, workshop interfaces and trained crews rather than simply producing more bare turbine cores.

## 3. Direct-propulsion GT vehicles

### Status
**CLOSED-TECH / NOT GENERAL-PROCUREMENT**

Current branch retains the 1938 ~15 t, ~300/340 hp GT vehicle/tank demonstrator as technically successful.
Nomohan does not overturn its rejection as the ordinary fleet powerplant.

Nomohan field conditions expose precisely its weaknesses:
- dust ingestion;
- poor part-load fuel economy;
- long fuel supply line;
- limited benefit when terrain becomes physically impassable;
- need for specialized intake and maintenance discipline.

Advantages remain real:
- excellent cold starting;
- high power/weight;
- high peak power;
- strong winter heat availability;
- simple rotary core.

Therefore:
- ordinary tank/truck replacement: **NO**;
- cold-weather / special experimental branch: **YES**;
- rear-area Manchurian winter trials remain justified;
- frontline mass issue at Nomohan: **NO**.

## 4. Resulting doctrinal distinction

By July 1939 the Army should distinguish three propositions:

1. **Turbo-diesel:** good field propulsion technology for sustained heavy duty; expand.
2. **Mobile GT power set:** good specialist support technology; standardize selectively and expand.
3. **Direct GT traction:** technically impressive but logistically premature; retain as experimental/special-weather branch.

This distinction prevents successful GT generators from being misread as evidence that all vehicles should use gas turbines, while also preventing the poor fuel economics of direct GT traction from discrediting the highly successful exhaust-turbo vehicle program.
