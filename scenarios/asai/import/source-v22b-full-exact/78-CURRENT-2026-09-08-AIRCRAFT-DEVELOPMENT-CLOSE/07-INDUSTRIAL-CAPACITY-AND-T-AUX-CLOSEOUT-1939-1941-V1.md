# INDUSTRIAL CAPACITY AND T-AUX CLOSEOUT 1939-1941 — V11

## Purpose
This file connects aircraft feasibility to core production and backend capacity. It is not a combat or exact shop-hour ledger.

## Core production waves
The current prewar envelope keeps the established non-linear ramp:

| state | maturity | sustainable gross core capacity |
|---|---|---:|
| T-0 + T-1 | 1939 spring | 720-880 / year |
| +T-2 | 1939Q4-1940H1 | 1,170-1,530 / year total |
| +T-3 | 1940H2 | 1,970-2,730 / year total |
| +T-4 | 1941H1 | 3,070-4,330 / year total |

The increase is non-linear in absolute capacity but not an unlimited exponential. Later waves exploit replicated tooling, supplier-fed semi-finished parts, dedicated metrology/test cells and a larger skilled base.

## T-5 / T-6 planning closure
V11 accepts the previously discussed capacity-option logic:
- **T-5:** 1939Q4 prewar capacity commitment; 1940H1 civil/utilities; H2 machinery; first qualification 1941Q2-Q3; initial useful output 1941Q4; maturity 1941Q4-1942H1. Planning capacity increment **+1,500-2,200 cores/year** when mature.
- **T-6:** 1940H1 option study only. No capacity number and no automatic build authorization.

T-5 is not needed to explain ordinary 1941 E6 production. Its purpose is to insure the 1942 multi-branch demand: E7, retrofit, carrier TP, heavy aircraft, ground GT and marine GT.

## 1941 full-demand stress
A deliberately strong all-direction stress case reached roughly 2,590 core-equivalent physical units/year before exact scale/backend normalization. A conservative T-4 ramp yields roughly 2,891-4,070 gross core opportunities during 1941. Annual raw-core arithmetic therefore passes, but a low-capacity/high-demand Q4 can become tight. Initial T-5 output is a natural insurance margin.

## Backend separation
Treat at least four backend families separately:
- aviation JF;
- aviation shaft/TP reducer + prop/governor;
- ground electrical/package;
- marine free-power-turbine/reducer/clutch/shaft.

### Working sustainable backend envelopes for planning audits
These are audit working bands, not exact shop-hour canon:

| period | aviation JF / month | aviation T/shaft / month | ground electrical / month | small/mid marine package / month |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1939H2 | 40-50 | 5-8 | 12-20 | 0-1 |
| 1940H1 | 55-65 | 8-12 | 20-30 | 1-2 |
| 1940H2 | 70-85 | 14-20 | 30-45 | 2-4 |
| 1941H1 | 85-100 | 22-32 | 45-65 | 4-8 |
| 1941H2 | 95-120 | 30-45 | 60-90 | 8-12 |

JF is intentionally less dependent on large precision reduction gearing than TP. Pure-J is lighter still. This makes jet/JF expansion a rational way to add aviation power without consuming all of the gearbox/propeller capacity also needed by piston, TP and marine products.

## 1940H1 pinch point
The tightest prewar transition is 1940H1: E5 bridge JF production and rising E6 Twin JF overlap before all T-2/T-3 capacity is mature. Existing 1939 JF experience makes this feasible, provided T-AUX grows and externalizes housings, sheet/frame/piping and selected pumps/filters/electrical boxes.

## Marine constraint
Large numbers of E6-powered high-speed boats are core-feasible before they are gearbox-feasible. Marine reducer, clutch/freewheel, shaft/propeller and salt-qualification cells must grow separately. A 1941 maximum-demand small-boat program can saturate marine backend even while gross cores remain available.

## Airframe material assumption before the Pacific-war gate
Through the prewar/late-1941 planning boundary, **bulk aluminum tonnage is not the default first-order aircraft bottleneck**. The worldline has advance demand visibility, planned light-alloy capacity growth and plant-FRP substitution in fairings, ducts, doors, tanks, secondary panels and similar parts.

This does not erase constraints on:
- sheet rolling/extrusion/forging and heat treatment;
- primary structure;
- landing gear, brakes and tyres;
- instruments/radio/electrical equipment;
- armament;
- propellers/reducers;
- skilled assembly and QA.

CFRP primary-structure mass substitution is not used as a free 1940-41 capacity multiplier.

## Wartime surge rule
After a Pacific-war opening, if facilities physically survive, the model may allow forced shifts, overtime, outsourcing, older-machine return, simplified specs and deferred maintenance to exceed peacetime sustainable rates. The cost can appear as yield, quality, TBO, maintenance load or worker/equipment exhaustion.

This rule cannot create a new material process, giant gear capability, unqualified propeller, destroyed factory or immature engine by willpower.
