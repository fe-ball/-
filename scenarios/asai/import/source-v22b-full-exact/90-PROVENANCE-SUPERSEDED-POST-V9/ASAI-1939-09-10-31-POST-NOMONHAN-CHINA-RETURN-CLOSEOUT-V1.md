## 0. Core decision

The revised Nomonhan outcome does **not** cause a Soviet strategic reallocation large enough to change Soviet aid or pressure on China.

For China accounting:

- **Soviet-to-China delta caused by the revised Nomonhan = 0.**
- Historical / already-established Soviet aid, advisers, aircraft and other China flows remain on their existing timeline.
- Soviet Far East replenishment uses Soviet reserve / replacement capacity; the additional revised-worldline Nomonhan losses do not justify cancelling China consignments.
- Soviet post-ceasefire drawdown / reorganization follows the old-worldline strategic timing. Tactical respect for Japan can rise without creating a new resource-flow delta.

Japanese return is therefore the only new cross-theater flow that must be carried into the China replay.

## 6. Soviet replenishment / China interaction

### Soviet Far East replenishment

The additional revised-worldline losses are repaired / replaced from Soviet systems without a new China-facing diversion gate.

No new changes are credited to:
- Soviet aid aircraft already intended for China;
- Soviet advisers / volunteer-air personnel policy;
- Chinese receipt of Soviet equipment;
- Soviet Far East permanent force level;
- October drawdown timing.

### Why the delta is zero

The old worldline already caused Soviet command to rate Japan as a high-cost, dangerous opponent.

The revised branch increases tactical respect, but does not cross a new strategic threshold:
- the USSR still achieves the territorial / operational objective;
- the Japanese main force withdraws rather than launching a renewed offensive;
- Tokyo stops the September reattack;
- European priorities rise sharply;
- the incremental extra Soviet loss is meaningful locally but not large enough to force a new China-aid or Far-East-allocation policy.

Therefore:

**SOVIET-CAUSED CHINA DELTA = 0.**
