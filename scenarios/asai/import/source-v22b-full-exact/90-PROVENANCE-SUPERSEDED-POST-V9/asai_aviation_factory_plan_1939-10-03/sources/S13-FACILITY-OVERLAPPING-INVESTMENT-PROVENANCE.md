## 1. Construction doctrine

Asai does not build one complete factory, wait for it to mature, then decide whether to build the next.

From 1935–36 onward the turbine-capacity program uses a rolling-wave system:

1. reserve land and trunk utilities one wave ahead;
2. order long-lead machine tools before the current wave is complete;
3. erect inexpensive factory shells before all production equipment is available;
4. install machining, heat-treatment, metrology, balancing, and test equipment as independent subprojects;
5. begin the following wave while the previous wave is still in installation/qualification;
6. credit no production merely because a building exists.

Typical smaller Asai turbine-module timing:
- site/utilities and detailed layout: 3–6 months;
- civil shell: 6–10 months;
- machine installation: 4–7 months, overlapping late civil work;
- gauge correlation / dry qualification: 2–3 months;
- hot-cell / process qualification: 2–4 months;
- mature yield after qualification: another 3–6 months.

Thus groundbreak-to-qualified production is usually about 12–18 months, while mature yield may take 18–24 months.
The next wave can begin roughly 6–9 months after the previous wave, not 18–24 months later.

## 2. Why this is financially and industrially plausible

The plant waves are not Koromo-scale vertically integrated factories.
Steelmaking, much forging, toolmaking, computation, central R&D, and some general machining already exist elsewhere in the Asai group.

A turbine expansion wave mainly adds combinations of:
- precision machining / compressor and disk work;
- hot-section forming/heat treatment;
- grinding;
- balancing and metrology;
- combustion-sheet work;
- final assembly;
- hot test cells;
- electrical, fuel, air, water, and exhaust utilities.

This is compatible with the audited company scale:
- 1937 direct employees ~10,000;
- 1937 capex ~¥3.6m;
- 1938 H1 capex ~¥2.45m;
- 1938-06-30 assets ~¥27.8m;
- strong internal cash generation plus moderate borrowing/customer advances.

The construction program therefore uses multiple **smaller distributed halls/sites**, rather than repeatedly financing a ¥40m-class all-process integrated plant.

## 3. Facility waves

### T-0 — First/mixed turbine works
Status by 1937: mature mixed production.

Role:
- exhaust-turbo-related precision work;
- industrial/stationary GT;
- pilot aviation-core manufacture;
- R&D support.

1937 independent-GT factory completions remain ~125 cores/year.
Under temporary priority it can support aviation-core pilot manufacture, but it is not a dedicated aviation mass line.

### T-1 — Second Turbine Works group
Decision/site work: 1936.
Main civil and equipment program: 1936 H2–1938 H1.
Commissioning: 1938 Q3.
Flight-grade qualification: 1938 Aug–Sep.
Ramp: 1938 Q4.
Mature operating point: approximately 1939 Mar–Apr.

Previously CLOSED capacity values remain:
- Q4 1938 qualified new-works output: 15–25 cores/month;
- 1939 mature sustainable output: 600–750 cores/year;
- gross equipment ceiling: approximately 800–900 cores/year, not sustainable.

T-1 is a distributed group, not one monolithic hall.

### T-2 — Third turbine-capacity wave
**Starts before T-1 is qualified.**

Land/utility reservation and long-lead machine orders: 1937 Q2–Q3.
Civil work: 1937 Q4–1938 Q3.
Machine installation: 1938 Q2–Q4.
Metrology/balancing/hot-cell commissioning: 1938 Q4–1939 Q1.
Flight-grade process qualification target: 1939 Q2–Q3.
Mature-yield target: 1939 Q4–1940 H1.

Primary purpose:
- duplicate the T-1 bottlenecks rather than merely add final-assembly floor;
- second hot-section/heat-treatment stream;
- compressor/disk machining;
- additional balancing and inspection;
- additional hot-test capacity;
- E5/E6 aviation and mobile/marine core flexibility.

Planning capacity increment after full maturation:
approximately **450–650 sustainable cores/year**.
This is a planning envelope, not retroactive output.

### T-3 — Fourth turbine-capacity wave
Again begins before T-2 reaches mature yield.

Detailed layout / utility trunk expansion: 1938 Q2–Q3.
Civil work: 1938 Q3–1939 Q2.
Machine installation: 1939 Q1–Q4.
Qualification: 1940 Q1–Q2.
Mature-yield target: 1940 H2.

Purpose:
- flow-oriented E6-class production;
- larger inspection/test throughput;
- more replaceable fixtures and duplicated machine groups;
- deliberate physical separation for fire/accident/air-raid resilience.

Planning sustainable increment after maturity:
approximately **800–1,200 cores/year**.
This remains planning, dependent on material and skilled-labor allocation.

### T-4 — Fifth / wartime-scale preparation wave
Planning and site reservation begins during 1939 H1, before T-3 completion.
Civil work can begin in 1939 H2.
Equipment and qualification extend through 1940–41.

This wave is not CLOSED as a production quantity at the 1939-08-31 history point.
Its purpose is to make the old archive statement "1940–41 demand surge is met by pre-built capacity" physically meaningful.

Planning architecture:
- more supplier-fed semi-finished parts;
- duplicated test cells;
- non-Ni and Ni hot-section lines separated where useful;
- dedicated turbine/GT final assembly rather than conversion of all existing mixed machinery;
- broader apprenticeship and dilute-labor fixtures.

Do not credit output until later qualification gates.

## 4. Capacity interpretation by date

These are facility/qualification envelopes, not sales.

### End 1937
- T-0 is the only mature production base.
- T-1 is an active construction/equipment program.
- T-2 long-lead orders/site work have already begun.

### 1938-06-30
- T-1 aggregate physical plant is nearly complete but unqualified for series flight-grade output.
- T-2 is already under civil/equipment work.
- T-3 is entering layout/site/utility preparation.
- This is compatible with the CLOSED H1 rule that **no T-1 H1 output is credited**.

### 1938 Q4
- T-1 produces qualified ramp output.
- T-2 is commissioning selected process cells.
- T-3 remains construction/installation.

### 1939 spring
- T-1 reaches mature sustainable operation.
- T-2 is in flight-grade qualification/ramp preparation.
- T-3 is still being equipped.
- therefore the phrase "the second factory starts in spring 1939" is prohibited; spring 1939 is a **maturity gate**.

### 1939 Q4 planning state
If T-2 qualification succeeds on schedule, total sustainable independent-GT capacity can move into roughly the **1,150–1,500 cores/year** band across T-0 + T-1 + T-2.
This is capacity, not guaranteed utilization.

### 1940 H2 planning state
If T-3 qualifies, total sustainable capacity can reach roughly **1,950–2,700 cores/year** before later wartime duplication/conversion.
This remains below the later 5,000–12,000/year wartime-peak envelope and therefore leaves room for 1940–44 expansion rather than achieving the final peak prematurely.

## 5. Capex logic

Do not model each wave as a fresh greenfield integrated factory.

Working allocation logic:
- 1937 CLOSED capex ~¥3.6m includes heavy T-1 work, T-2 long-lead/site expenditure, and other company investment.
- 1938 H1 CLOSED capex ~¥2.45m includes T-1 completion/installation, T-2 construction/equipment, and other business needs.
- H2 1938 and 1939 continue overlapping T-2/T-3 spending; exact full-year figures remain a later corporate-closeout task.
- oversized utility trunks, reserved land, prebuilt shells, machine-tool orders, and supplier tooling allow physical construction to lead qualified output.

This is the correct place for Asai foresight to act:
**build the physical option early; decide exact product mix later.**

## 6. What this does NOT change retroactively

This facility correction alone does not reopen:
- 1937 ~125/year independent-GT completion audit;
- 1938 H1 92 independent GT cores and 21 flight-grade E5 subset;
- 1938-06-30 T-1 completion percentages;
- 1938 Q4 T-1 ramp band;
- previously adjudicated 1939 Q1/Q2 qualified-core output;
- any China or Nomonhan front-line result.

The facility layer is now ahead of the production layer, as it should be.

Future output can rise only when:
- hot-section material lots pass;
- skilled/semiskilled labor and supervisors are available;
- balancing/metrology are correlated;
- test cells are commissioned;
- customer demand/allocation exists.
