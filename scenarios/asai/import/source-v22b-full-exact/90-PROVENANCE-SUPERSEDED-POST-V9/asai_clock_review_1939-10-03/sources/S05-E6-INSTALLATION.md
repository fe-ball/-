## 1. E6 Single

Current first measured target remains ~2.6-2.9 t all-up and May-Jun 1939 first-flight target.

With E6-M-J complete dry center ~525 kg:

- a 2.75 t representative aircraft at ~850 kgf has static T/W ~0.31;
- selected Ni ~900 kgf gives ~0.33.

Therefore the existing initial H->A band remains physically coherent. No new upward speed claim is made before flight.

A JF derivative is not a simple backend swap: the engine adds ~125 kg center plus local rear structure/duct/CG work and grows the rear envelope to ~0.66-0.72 m. A representative 2.75 t pure-J machine would move toward roughly 2.9 t class after a real JF conversion, but ~984-1,003 kgf gives static T/W roughly 0.34-0.35. Thus the JF branch buys real launch/climb/mission margin despite its mass.

Because E6-M pure-J is slimmer than JF, a high-speed drag penalty is physically plausible on the JF single. The exact top-speed difference remains measured H->A, not a fixed percentage.

## 2. E6 Twin

The current sequence remains coherent:

- airframe first flight Apr-May 1939 with interim propulsion permitted;
- E6-M pure-J package flight Jun-Jul target;
- E6-M-JF package flight Aug-Sep target.

Two JF engines add roughly ~250 kg engine dry mass versus two pure-J engines, before local nacelle/rear-structure changes, but static thrust rises from ~1.70 tf non-Ni pure-J to ~1.97 tf at the 835 C JF center and ~2.01-2.06 tf at the 850-870 C candidate band. The branch therefore has a real acceleration/climb/mission rationale even before measured aircraft performance is known.
