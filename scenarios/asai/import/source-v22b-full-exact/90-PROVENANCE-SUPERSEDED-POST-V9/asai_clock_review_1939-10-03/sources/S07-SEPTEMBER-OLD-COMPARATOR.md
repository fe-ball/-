# 0. What Asai is in September 1939

Asai should no longer be described primarily as a "gas-turbine company."

The company's real integrated structure is:

1. **special steels / HSS / carbide / heat treatment**;
2. **machine tools, jigs, gauges, metrology and quality-control methods**;
3. **diesel exhaust turbochargers and heavy-duty auxiliary machinery**;
4. **independent gas-turbine cores and power modules**;
5. **scientific/technical computation and card-based business systems**;
6. **industrial turbomachinery**;
7. **small but strategically important resin/electrical/materials line**;
8. **R&D/test capability in aviation, marine and vehicle propulsion**;
9. **field service, commissioning and data-return network**.

The most important competitive advantage is the feedback loop:

**materials → machining → measurement → calculation → prototype → instrumented test → failure classification → redesign → production gauges → field return data.**

Gas turbines are the most conspicuous output of that loop, not the only business that sustains it.

# 3. Hot-section and strategic-material allocation

## Two-rating policy — CLOSED-DERIVED

Asai should not put scarce high-grade Ni hot-section material into every turbine product.

By September 1939 the product system should explicitly separate:

### High-end / compact line
Priority:
- flight-grade aviation;
- high-performance experimental marine;
- the highest power-density special applications.

Uses the best available high-temperature material/process lots.

### Quantity / lower-TIT line
Priority:
- stationary/emergency power;
- mobile utility power where mass is less critical;
- some industrial duty;
- selected non-flight applications.

Runs at a lower material-temperature/TIT rating to preserve life with less strategic alloy burden.

This is not a new engine generation; it is a **rating/allocation policy**.

## Strategic rule
The next war-economy problem is not only "can Asai build a turbine?"
It is "which applications deserve the best blades/disks/process lots?"

# 23. Main new conclusion

By September 1939, the company has entered a different growth phase.

### 1920s–mid-1930s
The scarce item was **knowing how to make the machine at all**.

### 1936–38
The scarce item became **qualified plant capacity**.

### 1939 onward
The scarce item increasingly becomes **system completion and organizational replication**:
- controls;
- accessories;
- interfaces;
- customer technicians;
- field data;
- overhaul;
- test capacity;
- strategic-material allocation.

This means Asai's next decisive expansion is partly organizational rather than another factory.

The company's mature form is not:
**"a firm that builds remarkable engines."**

It is:
**"a firm that can repeatedly turn difficult rotating machinery into a fieldable, measurable, maintainable industrial system while selling the tools, calculations, materials and service methods that make the customer ecosystem work."**
