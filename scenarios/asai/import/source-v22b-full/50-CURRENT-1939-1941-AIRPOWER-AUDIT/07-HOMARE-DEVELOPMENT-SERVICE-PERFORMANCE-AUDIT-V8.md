# HOMARE DEVELOPMENT / SERVICE PERFORMANCE AUDIT — V8 AIRPOWER3

> **AIRPOWER3 current Homare development interpretation.** Exact later-war production/service statistics remain branch-dependent. This file closes the development mechanism and early maturity path through 1943Q1.

## 0. Central correction

Do not characterize Homare as a fundamentally bad design that simply needs a future engineer to “fix it.” Historical prototype/development performance was strong; much of the later reputation reflects the difficulty of reproducing and operating a very compact high-specific-output engine under degraded fuel/oil/material/manpower/production conditions.

The worldline divergence is therefore primarily:

- earlier identification of production-sensitive geometry/processes;
- stronger bearing/lubrication/temperature instrumentation;
- multiple fuel/oil maps designed from the start;
- better acceptance/lot traceability;
- earlier feedback from production engines to design.

Headline design horsepower need not jump far above history to create a much better service engine.

## 1. Historical/technical anchor

Retain the historical Homare architecture as the main anchor:

- compact 18-cylinder two-row air-cooled radial;
- 130 mm bore x 150 mm stroke class;
- ~35.8 L displacement;
- very high power-to-frontal-area for Japanese production practice;
- historical development approximately 1940–41 with strong prototype tests;
- later production/service problems centered heavily on materials, finish, bearings, lubrication, fuel/oil quality and manufacturing consistency.

The worldline does not turn it into a giant low-stress engine.

## 2. Pre-Homare preparation

Asai-origin transfer before the formal program can legitimately provide:

- supercharger test/map methods;
- cylinder/head temperature mapping;
- knock/worst-cylinder instrumentation;
- bearing materials/process comparisons;
- crankshaft torsion measurement;
- oil aeration/scavenge testing;
- balance and precision gauges;
- statistical acceptance/lot records;
- multi-fuel calibration strategy.

Nakajima engineers remain the engine designers.

## 3. Formal program chronology

Working worldline path:

- **late 1939:** concept/architecture work deepens as prior engine teams free and high-power fighter/bomber demand becomes clearer;
- **1940Q1:** formal design with production/fuel/oil test architecture included;
- **1940H2:** component/full-engine rigs build rapidly, but durability still consumes real time;
- **1941Q1:** first running engine class;
- **1941Q2:** ~1,800 hp-class development point plus long endurance closure, including a 300-hour-class development test target;
- **1941H2:** low-octane/domestic-oil/altitude/bearing validation and production-process hardening;
- **1942H1:** pilot/pre-series plus development-aircraft EA-D availability;
- **1942Q3:** main formal production window;
- **1942Q4:** early-series stabilization, 2,000 hp-class qualification path;
- **1943Q1:** technically mature/repeatable good-engine specification exists.

This is not a promise that every factory/field engine is perfect in 1943.

## 4. Fuel strategy

Homare is not designed around one ideal fuel and then helplessly derated later.

From 1940 design work, establish multiple maps:

- premium fuel/high rating;
- standard service fuel + ADI;
- lower-grade restricted rating.

Worst-cylinder knock/temperature controls allowable boost. Water-methanol/ADI is treated as an integrated control/fuel-system problem, not a late emergency add-on.

## 5. Oil strategy

Oil quality/class must be explicit.

Development includes:

- pump/scavenge capacity;
- aeration;
- temperature;
- bearing flow;
- altitude behavior;
- oil-class-specific time limits at high output.

A good engine on bad oil may receive an operating restriction without implying the engine's design rating is false.

## 6. Bearing/crank process

Homare-specific bearing distress is treated through:

- crankpin geometry/finish;
- edge loading and deflection;
- bearing overlay/material;
- clearance;
- oil delivery;
- acceptance inspection.

The worldline does not know the exact defect before measuring it, but it knows this is a critical test area and instruments it early.

## 7. Production repeatability

Every production engine needs acceptance records for:

- corrected output;
- oil pressure/temp/scavenge;
- worst-cylinder temperature spread;
- vibration/balance;
- ignition/mixture;
- leaks/consumption;
- serial linkage to critical bearing/crank/casting/gear lots.

Engines that miss full rating but are mechanically healthy may be reworked or explicitly derated rather than silently shipped as nominal full-spec.

## 8. 1942–43 performance semantics

### ~1942Q2 development aircraft

A priority project may receive credible ~1,800 hp-class Homare EA-D engines.

### late 1942

10/12-series engines become repeatable enough for serious aircraft development. Do not assume constant engine failure.

### 1942Q4–1943Q1

Selected 20/21-series development/qualification engines reach ~1,950–2,000 hp takeoff/emergency class, with high-altitude good-engine center around historical-plus-modest-realization rather than fantasy output.

Near ~6 km, use roughly **1,600–1,680 hp good-engine band, center ~1,640–1,650 hp** for a suitably qualified 20/21-class configuration, subject to fuel/oil/installation.

## 9. Service reliability guard

Do not assign one fleet TBO.

Life depends on:

- fuel/oil;
- boost use;
- cooling installation;
- dust;
- maintenance;
- production lot;
- mission cycle.

The strong current claim is only:

> Under intact supply and controlled production, Homare need not be intrinsically unable to hold its qualified rating. Under degraded supply/production it can still deteriorate sharply.

## 10. Aircraft implications

Worldline aircraft using Homare may differ from history because:

- development engines meet intended rating more consistently;
- exhaust/cooling/propeller integration starts from more mature methods;
- production aircraft have smaller lot variance;
- service units may maintain rating better if supply remains healthy.

But each aircraft still needs its own installation gate.

## 11. Turbo branch

Keep turbo Homare separate. Asai turbine/control experience can accelerate it, but turbo aircraft still require exhaust turbine, wastegate/control, ducting, fire protection, altitude calibration and aircraft qualification.

Do not count turbo availability as ordinary mechanically supercharged Homare service status.
