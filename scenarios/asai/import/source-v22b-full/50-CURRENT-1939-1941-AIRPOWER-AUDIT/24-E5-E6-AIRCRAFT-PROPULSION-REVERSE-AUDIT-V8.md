## 4. E5 Twin No.2 JF — PASS with one major drag-interpretation correction

Status remains:

- first aft-fan flight **1937 Q4**;
- second military development/comparison prototype;
- **2 x E5-JF**;
- 1938 emphasis on durability, controls, mission fuel, module exchange and maintenance.

Compared with a pair of E5-J engines, the JF propulsion package adds about **140-190 kg total, center ~170 kg** before aircraft-local structure/duct differences.

Against a representative 3.8 t pure-J twin, the propulsion mass addition alone is roughly 4-5%, while static thrust rises by about 20% and same-condition static TSFC falls about 15-18%. Therefore:

- takeoff acceleration: robustly better;
- initial climb: robustly better;
- go-around: robustly better;
- useful mission/endurance: strongly favored after local fuel/drag accounting;
- maximum speed: **OPEN H->A**, not automatically a fixed -5% or -10%.

### 4.1 Geometry correction

E5-J maximum engine OD is ~0.69-0.76 m, dominated by the centrifugal compressor/diffuser. E5-JF fan local casing is smaller than that maximum envelope. The JF aircraft therefore pays primarily:

- additional rear length;
- additional wetted area;
- propulsion mass;
- CG/mount/rear-structure changes;
- added spool/control/inspection burden.

Any older statement that explains the JF top-speed penalty mainly through a much larger maximum frontal area is superseded.
