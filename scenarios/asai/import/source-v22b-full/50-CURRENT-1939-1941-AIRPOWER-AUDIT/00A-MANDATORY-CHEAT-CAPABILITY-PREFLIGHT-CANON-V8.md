# MANDATORY CHEAT / CAPABILITY PREFLIGHT CANON — V8 GTCORE1

> **AIRPOWER5 / GTCORE1 highest capability-audit guard.** Read this before any propulsion, material, aviation-performance, high-speed or future-technology capability audit. This file does not grant a numerical bonus by itself; it prevents both historical-ceiling under-rating and double-counting of the reincarnator/computation advantage.

## 0. Why this guard exists

The v38-v46 rebaseline already rebuilt Asai capability using reincarnator foreknowledge, pre-paid industrial clocks, computation, test/QC infrastructure and organization-level convergence. In v46 this is exactly why the E6-M installed audit moved the old conservative jet anchors from roughly 500 kgf pure-J / 650-700 kgf JF to about 850-900 kgf pure-J / 950-1,000 kgf JF.

Therefore a later audit must **not** take those rebaselined values and then pull them back toward historical early-jet values merely because they look too advanced for the calendar. That is a second, invalid historical-conservatism correction. Conversely, do not apply an extra generic "cheat multiplier" to already-rebaselined values; any further increase must identify a specific previously-unpaid capability or accounting error.

## 1. Reincarnator rule — the idea gate is intentionally very strong

The governing upstream rule is not "history plus a modest head start". It is:

- the protagonist knows the answer and the route sufficiently to open the **idea gate** almost for free;
- a later historical invention may be proposed much earlier when its physical/industrial prerequisites can be assembled;
- methods from another field may be transferred early;
- dead-end search can be greatly reduced because the correct branch and the relevant failure modes are known in advance;
- long clocks can be started years or decades before demand appears;
- once test standards, computation, QC and failure feedback have accumulated, the **organization itself** may converge toward answers more accurately than the protagonist can specify in detail.

The exact coefficient, map, alloy heat, vibration mode, field durability or manufacturing yield can still require physical work. Knowing the class of solution does not create unmeasured coefficients, but it changes what is tested, how early the test clock starts, and how little time is spent on wrong branches.

## 2. Historical dates are anchors, never ceilings

Do not reject or down-rate a result because an analogous historical product appeared later. Historical examples are used to extract invariant physics, loading, losses, failure mechanisms and scale. Their calendar dates are not the Asai worldline's capability ceiling.

Phrases such as "too early", "too advanced for 1939", "miraculous", "magic", or "historically nobody had it yet" are **not engineering gates**. If a claim is to be rejected or reduced, name the actual unpaid gate: material/process, geometry/precision, measurement, test duration, flow map, rotordynamics, cooling, control, endurance, yield, mass/volume, supply, or installation.

## 3. Two gates and the conversion rule

Every capability question must separate:

1. **idea/design gate** — mostly opened by foreknowledge and later by organization-level analysis;
2. **physical/industrial gate** — what can actually be melted, machined, joined, measured, tested, supplied, maintained and reproduced at that date.

A constraint is not a single "ceiling". Decompose it and check which sub-gates have already been prepaid by earlier facilities/programs.

## 4. No second cheat multiplier

If a current value already came from v38-v46 cheat/computation/test/QC rebaseline, do **not**:

- apply a fresh generic performance multiplier;
- apply a historical-date penalty;
- lower it toward a famous historical reference because the current result looks uncomfortable;
- assume early difficulty must recur if the known failure was explicitly anticipated and tested years earlier.

Further change is allowed only when a **specific** accounting item or capability was omitted or mis-modeled.

## 5. Pre-paid long clocks

Long clocks that have an accepted earlier start event count as having run. Examples include:

- hot-section creep / oxidation / coating exposure;
- bearings and rotordynamics;
- fuel controls and governor rigs;
- free-turbine / fan rigs;
- compressor maps and bleed / VIGV development;
- manufacturing repeatability and statistical QC;
- test-cell infrastructure;
- maintenance/failure ledgers;
- resins/fibers/material coupons and structural subcomponents;
- customer training and production engineering.

Do not reset these clocks to a historical invention date. Do verify the causal start event and elapsed test/production duration.

## 6. Engineering-team release is real but not magic

Efficient design/test may free teams earlier, allowing pre-design of the next project. This is not a universal schedule scalar.

For each project distinguish:

- formal military requirement date;
- internal company concept/pre-design;
- design start/freeze;
- wind-tunnel/mockup;
- prototype fabrication;
- first flight;
- qualification;
- production.

Only the stages that have a causal reason to move should move.

## 7. Product-family continuity

A later E-generation does not automatically kill lower E products. Mature lower-generation hardware may remain rational because of:

- lower cost;
- higher life/yield;
- smaller logistical footprint;
- adequate mission performance;
- existing tooling/training;
- different core scale/backend.

E-generation is a technology coordinate, not a model number or mandatory fleet replacement order.

## 8. Capability / production / service / combat remain separate

Always separate:

- **technical possibility**;
- **working bench/rig article**;
- **flight article**;
- **qualified product**;
- **repeatable production**;
- **accepted inventory**;
- **trained unit deployment**;
- **serviceability/ready rate**;
- **combat use/effect**.

A capability closure cannot create a combat unit or retroactively alter a campaign.

## 9. External diffusion is not zero-lag

Other countries/companies/services do not automatically obtain the answer because an Asai product is visible. External diffusion needs:

1. observation/intelligence/capture/publication;
2. recognition of what matters;
3. requirement and engineering adaptation;
4. physical test/production;
5. service adoption.

Use actual espionage, licensing, captured hardware, open publication, attachments or joint work where relevant.

## 10. Historical calibration is a physics tool, not an authority override

Historical aircraft/engines may be used to calibrate:

- exhaust thrust;
- cooling drag;
- propeller limits;
- wing loading;
- compressibility;
- specific fuel consumption;
- endurance and failure modes;
- industrial complexity.

But never overwrite an accepted worldline value solely because it differs from a historical product reached later under different organizational/testing conditions.

## 11. Current 1939-41 implication

For the active airpower audit:

- E5/E6 current accepted products remain the baseline unless a local accounting error is identified;
- E7 1939-42 integrated-core schedule remains a **product/qualification** clock, not the start date of all E7-enabling concepts;

## 12. Audit template

For every future capability question, answer in this order:

1. **Accepted upstream worldline state:** what technology/facility/test/QC already exists?
2. **Foreknowledge effect:** what branch/dead end/failure mode is known early?
3. **Historical physics anchors:** what invariant physical data are useful?
4. **Unpaid physical gates:** what still must actually be built/tested/qualified?
5. **Double-count check:** was this benefit already paid into the current value?
6. **Product/service gate:** does the result exist only as capability, or as a repeatable usable product?
7. **Chronology guard:** does it change only future technical state, or has combat/deployment also been closed?

If step 4 cannot name a real gate, do not manufacture one from historical calendar discomfort.
