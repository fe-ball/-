## 1. Historical anchor
High-authority Smithsonian records place Mitsubishi Ha-43 development beginning in 1941 and describe a 41.7 L, 18-cylinder, two-row radial rated about 2,200 hp at 2,900 rpm, approximately 1.23 m diameter and ~980 kg in the referenced Ha-211 Ru artifact.

A detailed secondary synthesis based on Mitsubishi/Japanese engine histories gives:
- work underway mid-1941;
- design completed October 1941;
- first engine completed February 1942;
- operational testing not passed until June 1943 in reference history;
- development slowed after the Navy selected Homare for multiple high-power fighter programs and because some high-altitude variants added remote-turbo development problems.

The engine was therefore not physically impossible. It was an ambitious, under-prioritized and incompletely matured program.

## 2. Severity comparison with Homare — CLOSED
Approximate base-engine comparison:

| item | Homare 21 class | Ha-43-11 class |
|---|---:|---:|
| cylinders | 18 | 18 |
| bore × stroke | 130 × 150 mm | 140 × 150 mm |
| displacement | ~35.9 L | ~41.6–41.7 L |
| max-power rpm | ~3,000 | ~2,900 |
| takeoff power | ~2,000 hp | ~2,200 hp |
| mean piston speed | ~15.0 m/s | ~14.5 m/s |
| approximate BMEP at headline takeoff power | ~16.6 bar | ~16.3 bar |
| diameter | ~1.18 m | ~1.23 m |
| reference mass | ~830 kg | ~980 kg |

Interpretation:
- Ha-43 obtains much of its extra power from larger displacement rather than a radically higher BMEP;
- in pure cylinder-pressure/rpm severity it is not automatically more extreme than Homare;
- it pays instead in size, mass, cooling demand, large-bore head/valve thermal problems and later availability.

This is why a technically healthy Ha-43 can be a strong growth engine without making the earlier/lighter Homare irrational.

## 3. V8 program start — CLOSED
Existing V8 engine-team canon remains:
- `1940Q3`: larger Mitsubishi 18-cylinder pre-study may begin;
- `1941Q1`: formal project start;
- `late 1941–early 1942`: first hardware window.

This is modestly ahead/deeper than reference history but not a one-year miracle.

Why pre-study may begin in 1940Q3:
- Mitsubishi already owns the Kinsei lineage and 140 × 150 mm cylinder experience;
- Homare makes the compact 18-cylinder fighter-engine requirement visible as an industrial trend;
- Asai high-output combustion/supercharging/bearing/test methods are already circulating through controlled military/industrial channels;
- four-blade/high-power propeller rig work exists as an enabling system program.

Pre-study is not formal mass development and does not produce an aircraft engine in 1940.

## 5. Ordinary Ha-43 and turbo Ha-43 must be separated — CLOSED
Later discussion must not burden every Ha-43 with the schedule of a remote turbosupercharger.

### Base fighter-engine branch
Use a conventional mechanically driven supercharger path for the ordinary Ha-43-11/MK9A-class fighter engine.
This is the relevant comparison to Homare Reppu-type applications.

### `Ru` / remote-turbo branch
High-altitude variants add separate:
- exhaust collection;
- turbo hardware;
- ducting/intercooling;
- fire protection;
- oil/bearing system;
- wastegate/control;
- airframe packaging and mass.

That branch may be later even when the base engine is healthy.

## 7. Performance interpretation

Do not assign a universal Ha-43 rating before the local model/date is named.

For broad screening only:
- takeoff development class reaches ~2,000–2,200 hp over the 1942–43 qualification window;
- high-altitude power depends strongly on supercharger branch;
- base mechanical-supercharger version is not the same as Ha-211 Ru remote turbo.

Use actual local engine data before aircraft calculation.

## 8. Why Homare remains rational

Ha-43 is larger/heavier and later. Homare already exists as the compact engine for multiple 1942 programs.

Even if Ha-43 eventually has better growth margin, replacing all Homare programs would cost:
- redesign;
- CG/cowling/gear/propeller changes;
- production-tooling disruption;
- delayed service entry.

Therefore the rational worldline uses both families rather than retroactively declaring one “the correct engine.”

## 9. No-backflow

Later historical or branch-worldline success of Ha-43 aircraft does not prove 1941 service availability. The only allowed 1941 effect is design/component/hardware maturation according to the physical schedule above.
