# WATER AIRCRAFT / FLYING-BOAT LINEAGE CANON — V8 AIRPOWER2

> **AIRPOWER2 current water-aircraft chronology guard.** This file exists because later package layers contain extensive seaplane/flying-boat development. Do not backflow later H8K/Seiran-type results into 1939.

## 0. Core rule

Water-aircraft projects retain their own requirement/design/test clocks. Improvements in resin, composites, engines, propellers, corrosion protection, computation or hull hydrodynamics may accelerate specific subproblems, but do not automatically create a later named aircraft early.

## 1. 1939 state interpretation

By 1939, Japanese services may possess improved:

- waterproofing/sealing;
- corrosion protection;
- wood/resin/composite secondary structures;
- float/hull model testing;
- propeller/engine installation practice;
- radio/navigation equipment;
- structural calculation/test workflow.

This means historical seaplane/flying-boat projects can differ materially from the historical hardware while retaining similar mission categories.

## 2. Requirement clocks

Do not move a formal Navy requirement simply because Asai technology exists unless a service event creates the requirement.

An earlier internal company study is allowed. Distinguish:

- company concept;
- Navy requirement;
- prototype;
- first flight;
- service adoption;
- production.

## 3. Existing aircraft

Older operational types may receive selective upgrades where overhaul economics allow:

- radio/navigation;
- sealing/corrosion;
- propeller/engine serviceability;
- limited exhaust improvements;
- maintenance/QC.

Do not rebuild whole fleets into new designs.

## 4. New aircraft

For a new water-aircraft project, check:

- hull/float resistance and spray;
- step geometry;
- takeoff power;
- propeller clearance;
- salt-water corrosion;
- structural fatigue/impact;
- stability on water;
- beaching/catapult/folding constraints;
- ship/submarine stowage if relevant.

A land-aircraft performance improvement does not translate directly because water-installation drag and structural requirements are large.

## 5. Later lineage guard

H8K, E14Y/Seiran lineage, later special reconnaissance, and submarine aviation remain future projects unless their local ledgers say otherwise. Their later success may justify why earlier research areas matter, but not that the finished hardware exists in 1939.

## 6. Current use

When reconstructing 1940–41, treat E13A/F1M/H6K and other historical types as design/procurement questions under the same identity guard as land aircraft. Do not assume identical historical performance if worldline sealing, exhaust, engine, propeller or radio states differ.
