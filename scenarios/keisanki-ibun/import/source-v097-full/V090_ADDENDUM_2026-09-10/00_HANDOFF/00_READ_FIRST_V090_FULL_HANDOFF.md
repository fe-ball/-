## v090 hard rulings

- The old `ALEUTIANS_ATTU_KISKA_REAUDIT_1943-03_07_SETTLEMENT_v001.md` from the legacy branch is **provenance/reference only**, not current authority.
- Aleutians must be treated as a **theater logistics system**, not as isolated Attu/Kiska garrisons.
- The decisive Branch difference is not magical ship speed or weather prediction. It is improved mission completion through:
  - better 12–24 h synoptic weather use;
  - better machinery reliability and convoy cohesion;
  - improved navigation and abort/continue judgment;
  - better unloading organization and local craft availability.
  Hull-specific seakeeping and flight-deck limits remain physical constraints.
- By end-1942, Kiska and Attu have converted a larger fraction of the historical Japanese North-Pacific construction/supply plan into actual landed material. This is a real base-development change, not merely more rations.
- U.S. intelligence detects visible base/airfield growth but underestimates the **landed stock depth and repair/recovery capacity**.
- LANDCRAB remains launched on **1943-05-11**. U.S. planning is less naive than history, but does not fully redesign around the true stock depth.
- The first Japanese carrier-air intervention is not discounted merely because Ryujo/Zuiho are “second-line” carriers. Branch pilot rotation/cellularization and the absence of the historical 1942H2–43 aircrew bleed keep selected strike elements near first-line skill. The major limits are carrier hull/flight-deck capability and weather, not a blanket pilot-quality penalty.
- Current local center through **1943-05-20 evening**:
  - Japanese first carrier echelon has intervened;
  - a first Japanese counterlanding has succeeded at Chichagof Harbor on the night of 19/20 May;
  - the 20 May Japanese ground counterattack blocks or partially reverses the historical Clevesy/Jarmin breakthrough;
