# Branch B — Attu Counterlanding / Naval-Air-Sea Gate
## 1943-05-19 night through 1943-05-20 evening
Version: v001 / 2026-09-10
Status: **current center through 20 May evening; later escalation OPEN**

## 1. Precondition

By 19 May the Branch situation differs from history in four coupled ways:

1. Attu resistance and stock depth have delayed U.S. north/south convergence.
2. Japanese first carrier echelon has already demonstrated that carrier/land-based air can reach the U.S. support/shipping system.
3. Second carrier echelon is arriving, creating local Japanese carrier-air superiority without requiring the entire Combined Fleet.
4. The Japanese surface/DD wallet is materially healthier because there is no Komandorski damage and no historical Solomons destroyer attrition chain.

Therefore a **conditional counterlanding** becomes a serious option, not a desperation fantasy.

## 2. Japanese objective

Do not define the mission as “drive 13,000 U.S. troops into the sea in one night.”

Operational objective:
- hold open the Japanese Chichagof/Sarana base sector;
- prevent stable U.S. north/south linkage;
- suppress Massacre Bay artillery/logistics;
- inject fresh troops into friendly-held harbor terrain;
- free terrain-experienced Attu veterans for a concentrated Clevesy/Jarmin counterattack;
- create the option for a second night of escalation if the first day succeeds.

## 3. U.S. sea disposition — working center on 19 May

The U.S. force is not absent. It is dispersed by mission and Japanese air threat.

### Attu close-in
- local destroyers / small gunfire-support ships;
- unloading/landing craft and limited local support.

### Attu north/northeast
- Nassau and old-battleship support elements held farther from the Japanese carrier-air threat;
- DD escort.

### Outer cover
- radar-equipped CA/CL/DD force capable of night interception;
- SG/PPI/CIC advantage remains decisive for weapon-quality tracking in fog/night.

### East / Adak-Amukta LOC
- reinforcement, logistics and additional old-battleship/cover assets;
- the U.S. Aleutian LOC is not abandoned.

Current rule: Japan can obtain **local one-night sea control**, not theater-wide sea control.

## 4. Japanese sea organization — center

Ship names below are a working formation; DD names remain intentionally open where MI loss-name allocation is unresolved.

### Carrier cover
- Ryujo / Zuiho / Junyo / Hiyo;
- Kongo-class 1–2 as carrier-cover/contingency;
- Tone/Chikuma-type reconnaissance/cruiser support;
- DD 8–10.

Mission:
- CAP and sea denial;
- hold U.S. BB/CVE/large support units away from the island;
- cover Japanese withdrawal after dawn;
- do not waste the carriers on low-value continuous CAS.

### South bombardment / diversion
- CA Nachi / Maya / Myoko / Haguro center;
- DD 4–6.

Mission:
- 20-cm HE suppression of Massacre Bay;
- draw U.S. SG cover toward the southern side;
- break contact after a short torpedo/turn-away fight if radar opposition develops.

### North counterlanding group
- CL Abukuma / Kiso plus one CL/large-DD equivalent center;
- DD 6–8;
- local Attu landing craft used on arrival.

Mission:
- enter friendly-known Chichagof Harbor at night;
- unload personnel/high-value light combat cargo;
- depart before daylight even if some planned cargo remains aboard.

### Outer reconnaissance
- submarines roughly 5–8 in the broader system;
- floatplanes / carrier search aircraft;
- task is warning/contact, not guaranteed target coordinates.

## 5. Why Chichagof works

This is not a blind amphibious assault on an unknown hostile beach.

Chichagof is:
- a Japanese-held/known harbor sector;
- supported by months of local hydrographic/handling knowledge;
- connected to existing Japanese positions;
- less directly exposed to the main Massacre Bay U.S. logistics/fire complex.

Therefore the first counterlanding can be much lighter than a new opposed invasion package.

## 6. Weather rule

The operation requires a narrow band:
- low cloud/fog limiting U.S. air observation;
- sea state still usable by CL/DD and local landing craft;
- not a full gale.

Branch improvement is better GO/NO-GO judgment, not immunity to bad sea state.

## 7. Night 19/20 — bombardment

Working center:
- 3–4 heavy cruisers fire **~200–300 rounds total** of 20-cm Common HE over roughly 30–45 min;
- priority targets:
  1. Massacre Bay 105-mm firing positions and FDC/communications;
  2. ammunition/vehicle/landing-craft/logistics areas;
  3. access/temporary roads and confirmed HQ/communication nodes.

Do not try to kill dispersed riflemen in ridge foxholes.

Center effect:
- **1–2 U.S. 105-mm pieces mission-killed**;
- additional batteries temporarily degraded by FDC/telephone/ammunition movement disruption;
- vehicles/craft/ammunition suffer small-to-moderate losses;
- Massacre Bay logistics disrupted **~6–12 h**.

This is suppression/neutralization, not destruction of U.S. artillery as a system.

## 8. Night surface reaction

U.S. radar cover reacts.

Core asymmetry retained:
- U.S. has superior SG/PPI weapon-quality track;
- Japan has improved navigation/formation/torpedo reliability but not SG equivalence.

Japanese bombardment group therefore does **not** accept a long radar gun duel.

Center encounter pattern:
- short U.S. radar fire first;
- Japanese short return fire + torpedo launch;
- Japanese turn-away west;
- no cruiser sinking center.

Working center loss envelope:
- Japan: one DD medium damage; one CA may take light/moderate damage;
- U.S.: one DD heavy damage / possible mission kill;
- exact hull names remain OPEN.

## 9. First counterlanding result

Planned first echelon:
- **~1,700–1,900 men**.

Actually landed center before dawn:
- **~1,500–1,750 men**.

High-value cargo landed:
- **~120–180 t**.

Cargo priority:
- small arms / MG / grenade dischargers;
- limited 70-mm infantry gun / 75-mm mountain-gun ammunition and possibly a few pieces;
- radios/batteries;
- medical;
- engineer explosives/tools;
- 2–3 days immediate rations/ammunition.

Do not land tanks, heavy AA/coast guns, or truck-heavy equipment in the first night.

All Japanese ships withdraw before daylight; incomplete unloading is accepted.

## 10. 20 May air

Historical weather is important: U.S. Attu ground-support missions are strongly weather-limited/cancelled in the center.

Japanese carrier-air priority:
- carrier/withdrawal CAP;
- prevent Nassau/old-BB/CA support from freely re-entering the close-in zone;
- reconnaissance/sea denial;
- only **0–8 aircraft** opportunity ground support if cloud breaks permit.

Thus air superiority helps mainly by **removing U.S. support**, not by mass Japanese CAS.

## 11. 20 May ground result — current center

After the landing:
- Japanese total combat effective rises to roughly **~3,800–4,300**.
- new troops primarily backfill Chichagof/Sarana/Fish Hook defensive sectors.
- terrain-experienced veterans are released for the key counterattack.

Focused counterattack force:
- **~1,300–1,700 men** in Clevesy/Jarmin axis;
- supported by Type-92 70-mm guns, Type-41 75-mm mountain guns, grenade dischargers/MG and surviving registered fires.

Current center:
- historical U.S. Clevesy/Jarmin breakthrough is halted or partly reversed;
- one or more local heights are retained/retaken by Japan;
- U.S. southern force gives ground locally or abandons the planned advance;
- stable U.S. north/south linkage is delayed **at least another 1–2 days**.

This is **not yet** a full U.S. encirclement.

## 12. Current decision gate — evening 20 May

If the 20 May counterattack holds and Japanese local sea/air denial remains credible, Japan now has a rational escalation option:
- second night counterlanding (~1,500–2,000 more troops);
- heavier artillery/ammunition delivery;
- possible Kongo-class 35.6-cm HE bombardment of Massacre Bay logistics/artillery;
- attempt to convert temporary separation into an operational U.S. pocket.

If the U.S. restores strong naval/air support, holds Jarmin/Clevesy, or inflicts significant carrier/surface loss, Japan should not feed the island indefinitely and must reopen relief/evacuation logic.

**Night 20/21 May is OPEN.**
