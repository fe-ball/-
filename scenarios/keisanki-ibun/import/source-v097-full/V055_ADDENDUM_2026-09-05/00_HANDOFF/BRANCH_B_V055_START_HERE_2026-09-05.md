# BRANCH B v055 — NC surface-warfare / floatplane spillover re-audit

Status: working-canon overlay
Scenario scope: 1942-11-08 through 1942-11-14
Supersedes: only surface-warfare and naval-gunfire details affected by the v053-v054 water-air correction.

## 1. Historical/doctrinal anchor retained
IJN 1942 night-attack doctrine can include pre-launched cruiser floatplanes for:
- tactical reconnaissance,
- reporting enemy disposition,
- route/position markers,
- parachute-flare illumination.

This does NOT turn floatplane illumination into radar fire control.
At long battle ranges, flares materially improve target bearing/silhouette/identification and help optical acquisition, but do not supply SG-like continuous precision range/course/speed data.

## 2. 1942-11-08 first night: differential audit
Japanese forward surface forces should have 2~4 cruiser/battleship floatplanes airborne or prepared before close night action, rather than carrying the whole floatplane complement fueled on deck into the engagement.

Effects:
- somewhat better awareness of the separated US heavy groups;
- gunfire-flash / flare-assisted optical acquisition after contact;
- reduced topside aviation-fire hazard on Japanese cruisers that had already launched aircraft;
- easier post-mission recovery because selected aircraft can recover to the Japanese water-air support system rather than force a combatant mother ship to slow and recover them immediately.

Center result:
- US SG still obtains the superior first-quality surface track.
- Hiei, Kirishima, South Dakota, Atlanta, Gwin etc. previously adjudicated statuses remain.
- Japanese optical gunfire convergence improves by roughly 1~2 minutes in portions of the action.
- allow ~1~2 additional medium-caliber Japanese shell hits / damaging near misses distributed among already-engaged US cruiser/DD targets.
- no new US battleship main-caliber hit is added.
- first-night operational result is unchanged.

No qualitative threshold crossed.

## 3. 1942-11-09 second night: salvo-level closure
### Airborne support
The forward group launches/maintains approximately 2~3 tactical floatplanes before the decisive contact.
Primary functions:
- track gun flashes and enemy course changes;
- relay coarse position/course reports to Yamato/Mutsu main body;
- lay flare illumination after surface contact;
- maintain contact during the US torpedo-evasion turn.

The forward lagoon/water-air system matters by making recovery/dispersal less dependent on immediate recovery by the launching combatant.
A6M2-N itself does not fight in the night surface engagement.

### Fire-control consequence
Previous line:
- US main-body firing: ~22:10
- Yamato firing: ~22:17
- Mutsu slightly later

Revised center:
- US main-body firing: **~22:10 unchanged**
- Yamato first salvo: **~22:15 to 22:15:30**
- Mutsu first salvo: **~22:16:30 to 22:17:30**

Reason:
- Type 21 + forward surface report + floatplane track/illumination lets Japanese directors acquire the correct sector and silhouette earlier.
- At ~23,000~24,000 yd, flare illumination does NOT replace precision optical ranging or provide an American SG-quality plot.
- Lee is already alerted and can maneuver/smoke when illuminated, limiting the Japanese gain.

### Revised hit sequence center
- ~22:15: Mutsu still receives the first Washington 16-in hit.
- ~22:17: South Dakota's first 16-in hit on Yamato remains.
- ~22:18~19: Yamato reaches first useful straddle/effective bracket.
- **~22:21:30~22:23: Washington receives the single 46-cm hit**, about 1~2 minutes earlier than the previous center.
- Washington retains SG and main battery; damage shock and maneuver interrupt optimum firing for roughly 1~2 minutes but do not silence it.
- Washington's second 16-in hit on Mutsu remains a centerline result, but shifts later / has a lower margin; treat it as a less-robust event than before.
- Mutsu's 41-cm hit on South Dakota remains.
- second Type 93 wave still produces the Walke hit; no battleship torpedo hit is added.

### Damage-state result
UNCHANGED at campaign level:
- Yamato: light-medium, main capability retained.
- Mutsu: heavy / campaign battle mission kill, roughly 20~22 kt.
- Kongo: medium-heavy, withdrawal.
- Washington: heavy / campaign mission kill, 18~20 kt restricted by shaft-system damage.
- South Dakota: medium, still high-speed combat-capable.

No qualitative threshold crossed.
The second-night result was close enough to require a replay, but floatplane illumination does not overturn SG first-fire dominance.

## 4. US naval-gunfire spotting suppression, 1942-11-10~12
Previous water-air correction counted:
- Japanese F1M/E13A observation being protected by A6M2-N.

It had not fully counted the reciprocal effect:
- A6M2-N can intercept/drive off slow US SOC/OS2U-type cruiser/battleship spotting aircraft unless escorted.

US naval gunfire therefore remains substantial through:
- ground observers,
- pre-registered/map fire,
- radar/naval navigation,
- short exposed spotting sorties with fighter escort.

But aerial spotting against:
- moving Japanese artillery,
- reverse-slope positions,
- road columns,
- rapidly shifting forward targets
is degraded.

Working delta:
- effective responsive/observed US naval gunfire quality against mobile/obscured land targets: **~10~20% lower during periods when floatplane spotting is suppressed**.
- total shell volume is NOT reduced by 10~20%; this is an observation/target-correction quality effect.
- US compensates with approximately **6~12 additional fighter sorties per day** during the most important 11/10~12 periods for spotter escort / water-fighter suppression, where available.

Ground/campaign delta beyond v054:
- Japanese ground K/W avoided: **~70~130**
- Japanese guns/vehicles saved or restored earlier: **~1~3 equipment-equivalents**
- Tontouta sustained-shelling/ground-pressure clock gains another roughly **15~25 minutes** over the already corrected v054 line.
- Tontouta fall date remains **1942-11-12**.
- revised occupation center: **~14:55~15:25** (previous v054 differential replay ~15:20~15:45; original line ~16:10).
- US organized withdrawal still succeeds; no encirclement is created by this delta.

## 5. Additional surface-system effects to carry forward
- pre-launching Japanese floatplanes before night action reduces fueled-aircraft topside fire risk.
- forward lagoon recovery permits tactical floatplanes to avoid forcing their launching cruiser/battleship to slow for immediate recovery after a night action.
- protected E13A/F1M search layers improve daytime tracking of US cruisers and damaged battleships, but do not automatically generate firing-quality tracks.
- local float fighters can protect ASW/search floatplanes around transports/damaged ships; apply only when an actual submarine/contact event is adjudicated.
- US floatplane spotting remains possible when fighter escort or local air superiority is available.
