# Branch B — NC counterattack clock / fire-observation coupling close, 1942-07-30 v001

Status: **PLANNING CLOCK CLOSED / EXACT 754TH DEPLOYMENT & BATTLE OUTCOME OPEN**  
Clock: **1942-07-30 00:00**

## 0. Core ruling

The decisive first-day question is not whether the United States owns M3 light tanks. It is:

**how soon a road-mobile U.S. tank/infantry packet can reach the selected beach exits, and whether Japan has an organic AT/artillery system ashore before that time.**

Naval and air fire support should delay, split and disorganize the counterattack. They are not the last-800-meter antitank defense.

## 1. U.S. counterattack clock — center planning bands

Exact 754th Tank Battalion location on the eventual NC D-day remains OPEN. Planning must survive all plausible southern/central placements.

### Against the Boulouparis / Baie de Saint-Vincent main axis

If a useful 754th packet is already staged in the Tontouta/southern-road system:
- beach identity/landing confirmation to movement order: ~30–75 min;
- assembly/road column formation: ~30–60 min;
- road movement to the Boulouparis sector: roughly another ~60–120 min depending exact start/landing point, traffic, demolitions and air/naval interdiction;
- **earliest credible limited armored contact: H+2.5 to H+4.5; center ~H+3.5.**

If the principal tank reserve is nearer Noumea:
- additional road distance and congestion apply;
- **credible main packet contact: ~H+4.5 to H+7 center band.**

If U.S. warning is unusually early and the landing sector is identified before H-hour, these clocks can shift earlier. If Japanese interdiction drops bridges/roadblocks or breaks the column, they can shift later. Neither effect is automatic.

### Against Ouano / La Foa secondary axis

Local Americal infantry/AT can react quickly:
- **local ground contact can be immediate to H+2** after beach identification.

A Tontouta/Noumea armored packet takes longer:
- **center H+4 to H+7+**, depending road status and whether the main Boulouparis lodgment already demands the reserve.

This is why the secondary axis can remain light: its mission is pinning/road disruption, not defeating the whole tank battalion alone.

## 2. Japanese first-hour organic-defense clock

### H to H+2/3
The main lodgment must already possess:
- infantry anti-tank teams;
- mines/demolition/road-obstacle parties;
- MG/mortars;
- functioning signals and beach traffic control;
- forward observers/naval liaison;
- at least a first towed/direct-fire AT layer.

### H+2.5 to H+4
Before the earliest Tontouta-origin armored response can become decisive:
- additional AT/direct-fire pieces must be in action;
- first 75-mm-class guns or equivalent direct-fire support must be registered;
- engineers must have at least one usable road exit and one blocked/defended approach;
- ammunition and prime-mover flow cannot remain beach-bound.

### H+4 to H+8
If Japanese armor is committed:
- first tank packet enters only after road exit/assembly ground exists;
- center role is mobile reserve / counter-counterattack;
- medium tanks do not replace the AT/mines/gun layer already ashore.

## 3. Fire-support priority transition

### Pre-H to H
Priority:
1. known coast/field artillery and AA threatening the landing box;
2. Tontouta / southern road-airfield fixed nodes;
3. known bridges/junctions and likely reserve assembly zones;
4. beach strongpoints.

Primary tools:
- bounded BB heavy fire;
- CA parallel heavy/sustained fire;
- airfield/AA suppression by carrier/land air;
- 2–3 main-axis airborne spot/mark channels.

### H to H+2
Priority shifts to:
1. surviving beach/road-exit fire points;
2. artillery muzzle flashes;
3. roadblocks/bridge obstacles;
4. first detected mobile reserve movement.

Primary tools:
- CA/CL/DD responsive fire;
- ground liaison starts taking over visible tactical targets;
- main-axis observation channels remain 3–4.

### H+2 to H+6 — armored-counterattack danger window
Priority becomes:
1. confirmed road-moving M3/infantry columns;
2. bridges, culverts, road constrictions in front of those columns;
3. artillery supporting the counterattack;
4. beachhead-breaking infantry concentrations.

Naval fire aims to **delay and fragment** the road column. It is not credited with precision destruction of individual tanks under cover.

### H+6 onward
As Japanese ground artillery becomes functional:
- danger-close/local infantry missions transition ashore;
- CA/CL/DD shift deeper to road interdiction/artillery suppression;
- BB/CA anti-surface readiness rises as the U.S. night-response window approaches.

## 4. Observation-channel allocation in the critical counterattack period

Authorization center under v086 remains **5–8 simultaneous useful channels**.

During H+2 to H+6 use:
- Boulouparis beach/road exits: **1–2**;
- Tontouta-to-Boulouparis movement corridor: **1–2**;
- secondary La Foa axis: **1**;
- artillery/assembly or relief: **1**;
- external fleet-contact/search: preserved separately, normally **1–2 search/confirmation functions** not swallowed by shore support.

A single M3 sighting does not retask every observer. Track freshness and corridor constraint decide whether naval area fire is worthwhile.

## 5. Road interdiction effects — planning use

For a clearly observed road column:
- 20.3-cm / 14-cm / 12.7-cm fire can crater road, damage soft vehicles, force deployment and block bridges/culverts;
- D3A/B5N/land-air attacks can further disperse/slow the column;
- expected operational benefit is often **30–90 min of delay / smaller simultaneous arrival mass**, not a guaranteed tank-kill count.

If a key bridge is actually destroyed and the U.S. lacks an immediate bypass, delay can exceed this. That result must be generated by the event, not prebooked.

## 6. Allied counter-observation and counterfire

Patch's defenders start with local ground observation and communications advantages.
Likely effects:
- Japanese landing sites become increasingly well localized after first touch;
- U.S. artillery/armor can receive road/beach reports faster than a completely improvised force;
- Japanese CL/DD/CA that fire repeatedly from the same box become easier to target by shore air/night surface forces.

Therefore Japanese close-support ships use **short mission windows + displacement**, not static coastal anchoring.

## 7. Surface-threat switch rule

If a fresh U.S. fast-BB/cruiser contact appears during the H+2–H+6 ground danger window:
- transport direct-cover stays with transports;
- heavy night/interception group takes anti-surface priority;
- the shore-fire BB pair may shorten/terminate its mission;
- CA/CL/DD close-support capacity remains, but total shore-fire weight falls;
- organic ground AT/artillery must still be able to survive the U.S. mobile counterattack.

This is the test that prevents the amphibious plan from depending on battleship shore fire every minute.

## 8. Authorization gates created by the counterattack clock

Before NC D-day can be approved:

1. Main beach must allow organic AT/mines/signals ashore by **H+2/3**.
2. Main beach must support first useful 75-mm/direct-fire layer by roughly **H+4** center.
3. The main axis must have at least one defended road exit before a Tontouta-origin M3 packet can arrive.
4. Fire-support plan must be able to keep **one live observation channel on the southern mobile-reserve corridor** during the H+2–H+6 window without blinding fleet search.
5. If medium tanks are part of the plan, a first useful tank packet must be capable of combat readiness by **H+6–8** without blocking gun/ammunition landing.

Failure of gates 1–3 is an operation-delay trigger regardless of carrier or battleship strength.
