# Branch B — Madagascar / other-theater tradeoff snapshot, 8 Jul 1942

## Why this snapshot exists
The Madagascar and submarine ledgers are now large enough that the next operation cannot be adjudicated in isolation. This note fixes the first affordability checkpoint.

## What Britain has already paid or committed
Known Madagascar+WIO minimum through 8 Jul:
- 339 DD-days
- 157 cruiser-days
- 46 carrier-days
- 106 corvette-days
- 100 minesweeper-days
- 26 AMC-days
- Ramillies: 32 deployment-days before damage, then 40 unavailable-days and 30 dockyard-days through cutoff
- British Loyalty: 40 tanker-unavailable-days / 279,720 GRT-days
- 28 specialized landing-hull-days minimum
- 55 assault-transport-days minimum
- 162 brigade-days + 65 No.5 Commando unit-days

These totals deliberately exclude unresolved base-engineering, aircraft-day, salvage and merchant-delay bills.

## The Mediterranean did not wait
The Eastern Fleet still sent 2 cruisers and 10 destroyers toward VIGOROUS/JULIUS in late May. This implements the existing Branch rule: Britain initially protects Malta/European priorities by spending Indian Ocean safety margin, not by instantly cancelling European operations.

By 8 Jul, Birmingham plus six destroyers are just back at Kilindini. They are not a deep free reserve; they are the same hulls the WIO response immediately wants to use. Nestor has been lost.

## The carrier asymmetry is worse than the raw hull count suggests
History lost Hermes/Vampire/Hollyhock on 9 Apr; Branch saves them. Branch instead loses Indomitable and Formidable on 5 Apr. Therefore counting 'three saved hulls vs two lost hulls' is strategically misleading.

At the working 8 Jul point:
- Branch has Illustrious as its one modern fleet carrier in the theater system, plus old/small Hermes.
- Historical Britain still had three modern fleet-carrier hulls in the wider system before Indomitable's 9 Jul withdrawal.
- Illustrious is about to be pulled west for PEDESTAL in Branch.

Hence the main deficit is modern flight-deck redundancy, not simple ship count.

## Affordability conclusion
Britain can still pay the already-booked bill through 8 Jul without rewriting VIGOROUS or IRONCLAD. But the margin is now compressed enough that new August-September demands must be paid from identifiable sources.

From WIO-4A onward, every new British countermeasure must answer one of these questions:
1. Which named hull/air unit/yard slot pays it?
2. If that asset is already booked, which existing task loses it?
3. If Britain accepts the gap instead, which Japanese contact/raid/convoy risk is no longer suppressed?
4. If an historical operation disappears in Branch (e.g. STAB), the credit exists only after its actual surviving hulls/fuel/aircraft are shown free and reassigned.

This is the point where the project moves from 'same historical loss, weaker RN' into genuine resource-tradeoff divergence.
