# Branch B v063 — Madagascar/Ironclad cumulative wallet through 1942-07-08

Status:
- Consolidation anchor: pre-/during IRONCLAD through WIO-2 cutoff, 1942-07-08T24.
- WIO-1C and WIO-2 tactical outcomes are unchanged: cumulative regular-submarine sinkings remain 22 ships / 103,496 GRT, Branch center delta vs historical traffic/outcomes = 0.
- This addendum does NOT advance WIO-4A. It consolidates the British gross resource wallet before the first major H2 divergence gate.

## Controlling rule
Keep three layers separate and never silently net them:
1. **Branch structural delta** vs history (permanent losses/saves caused by Operation C divergence).
2. **Madagascar/WIO gross burden** actually committed in Branch, including mission-specific hull-days, formation-days, repair/unavailability and response deployments.
3. **Other-theater hard claims/credits** (Mediterranean, convoy routes, avoided historical commitments), used for affordability checks. Same hull-day must not be charged twice when one transit serves both return/redeployment and WIO response.

Full cost does not imply full tactical effect. Planned/funded patrol slots that do not fly do not suppress Japanese contacts.

## 1. Structural RN delta created before IRONCLAD
Operation C on 5 Apr 1942 remains authoritative:
- CORNWALL and DORSETSHIRE lost as in history.
- **INDOMITABLE and FORMIDABLE sunk in Branch**; this is the principal Branch-specific RN loss.
- WARSPITE damaged and survives; exact Branch repair/unavailability clock remains OPEN and must not be silently treated as historical.
- Historical 9 Apr Trincomalee/HERMES loss chain does not occur; **HERMES, VAMPIRE, HOLLYHOCK, ATHELSTANE and BRITISH SERGEANT survive** that chain.

Interpretation at 8 Jul:
- Raw hull count is not a simple pure reduction because HERMES/VAMPIRE/HOLLYHOCK and about 11k GRT of ATHELSTANE+BRITISH SERGEANT shipping are saved.
- Combat-quality delta is nevertheless strongly negative: two modern fleet-carrier hulls are gone, replaced only in part by an old small carrier and saved escorts.

## 4. Diego Suarez base/occupation hardening debt
The capture did not end the bill. Eastern Fleet reporting explicitly treated possession of only northern Madagascar as a dangerous/wearing commitment and showed that underwater defences were incomplete after occupation.

Already-booked/nonzero burden includes:
- indicator-net / anti-submarine-boom requirement;
- controlled minefield work by MANCHESTER CITY / JAY;
- GUARDIAN / KIRRIEMOOR net-laying chain;
- local A/S craft and harbor defence;
- SAAF/RAF reconnaissance and support allocations;
- later Mayotte forward-ASW base development.

Numeric engineering, tug, marine-craft, net-laying and shore-labour days remain OPEN debt tickets and must only increase the gross bill.

## 7. Cross-theater affordability: Mediterranean claim already bites
The historical/Branch audit retains the decision to preserve the Malta operation rather than cancel it immediately for Indian Ocean relief.

A hard Eastern Fleet transfer occurred in late May for VIGOROUS/JULIUS:
- Group 1, 25 May: BIRMINGHAM + PAKENHAM, FORTUNE, NIZAM, NORMAN.
- Group 2, 27 May: NEWCASTLE + INCONSTANT, PALADIN, HOTSPUR, GRIFFIN, NAPIER, NESTOR.
- This is **2 cruisers + 10 destroyers removed from the Eastern Fleet claim set** during the critical June window.
- NESTOR's historical loss remains Branch canon.
- By 8 Jul, BIRMINGHAM and six destroyers (NIZAM, NORMAN, INCONSTANT, HOTSPUR, GRIFFIN, FORTUNE) have returned to Kilindini; their 1-8 Jul return transit is already counted in the WIO response ledger where appropriate and must not be double-charged as a second simultaneous commitment.

This shows the intended tradeoff mechanism: Britain initially preserves Mediterranean priorities and consumes Indian Ocean elasticity. The WIO response in early July is partly fed by ships just coming back from that Mediterranean loan.

## 8. Affordability ruling at 1942-07-08
**PAYABLE_THROUGH_CUTOFF_BUT_MARGIN_SEVERELY_COMPRESSED.**

Britain is not declared insolvent by 8 Jul. No retroactive cancellation of VIGOROUS or IRONCLAD is imposed. However:
- two modern carrier hulls are permanently gone in Branch;
- a large Madagascar/WIO gross bill is already active;
- 2 cruisers + 10 destroyers were simultaneously borrowed by the Mediterranean in late May/June;
- one of those destroyers, NESTOR, is permanently lost;
- returning Mediterranean ships are immediately useful to the Indian Ocean response rather than constituting a free reserve;
- exact WARSPITE Branch repair debt, SAAF/RAF aircraft-days, base engineering, salvage and merchant-delay costs are still unbooked numerically.

Therefore the correct 8 Jul state is not yet a forced European schedule slip, but **very low reserve elasticity**, especially in modern carrier cover and destroyers. From WIO-4A onward, new commitments must be tested against named hull clocks rather than assumed affordable.

## 9. Shipping comparison vs history through cutoff
Regular-submarine shipping losses through 8 Jul remain historical-center (22 / 103,496 GRT), so they create no Branch delta yet.
The cancelled 9 Apr HERMES strike chain saves ATHELSTANE + BRITISH SERGEANT, roughly 11k GRT combined, so direct merchant-tonnage balance through this cutoff is actually slightly better than history before later H2 divergences. This credit must not be confused with RN combat-power balance, which is much worse because of the two fleet-carrier losses.
