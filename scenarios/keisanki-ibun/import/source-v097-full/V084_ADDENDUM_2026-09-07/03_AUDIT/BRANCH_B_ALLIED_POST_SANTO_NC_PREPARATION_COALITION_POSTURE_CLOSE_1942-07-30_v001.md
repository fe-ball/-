## 0. Main ruling

Santo's loss does not allow the Allies to know that New Caledonia is definitely next. It does, however, make NC the logical place to place the **mobile theater reserve** because NC is both the most valuable remaining South-Pacific LOC fortress and one of the most plausible Japanese next targets.

The correct Allied response is therefore **concentrated mobility + distributed fixed defense**, not “strip Fiji/Efate/Samoa and put everything at Noumea.”

## 1. New Caledonia resident ground skeleton

### Americal / Task Force 6814 lineage — HARD institutional presence
By late July the island has a complete division-scale U.S. Army defense, not the light force assumed in the original Japanese FS scheme.

Resident combat structure includes:
- Americal Division: 132d, 164th, 182d Infantry;
- divisional field-artillery, engineers, signals, medical, ordnance and quartermaster;
- **754th Tank Battalion (Light)** — resident since March;
- antiaircraft and coast-artillery/base-defense elements from the original reinforced task force;
- Army Air Forces ground/base personnel and aviation engineers;
- Navy port/base/construction personnel growing at Noumea.

### 30-Jul working planning band
Exact personnel accounting is still to be named, but a useful current strategic band is:

These are not final battle OOB numbers.

## 7. Base / construction system

NC defense is multi-node:
- Noumea: large protected harbor, command/logistics and naval-base growth;
- Tontouta: principal airfield/air-defense node;
- La Foa / Plaines des Gaiacs and road corridor: secondary air/ground maneuver system;
- northern road/airfield positions provide depth rather than one Noumea perimeter.

Part of CUB 1 / 3rd Naval Construction Battalion arrived Noumea on **26 Jun 1942**. Base development has begun, but July unloading, cranes, storage and stevedoring remain real bottlenecks. Santo loss therefore accelerates:
- revetments and dispersal;
- AA/radar/communications;
- fuel/ordnance dispersion;
- road/bridge obstacles and demolition points;
- port denial preparations;
- additional construction/base-unit requests.

No later-1942 giant Noumea base is teleported into July.

### Reinforcement logic
Because carriers are missing, fast battleships and radar-equipped surface forces become unusually important for NC.

- **South Dakota:** just finishing shakedown/yard preparation in late Jul; historically departed for the Pacific in mid-Aug and reached Tonga 4 Sep. Branch Santo loss makes at-least-that-priority South-Pacific dispatch highly rational. Exact Branch departure/arrival is to be closed, but **early-Sep NC-area usefulness is plausible**.
- **Washington:** in New York overhaul from late Jul to late Aug historically; no credible early-Sep NC combat credit. Mid-Sep arrival is a later threat to Japan.
- SWPA Australian/U.S. cruisers: conditional donor pool only; Port Moresby loss makes MacArthur reluctant to release them permanently.
- RNZN cruisers: conditional regional contribution, not automatic U.S. hulls.
