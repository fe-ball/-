## 0. Main ruling

Japan is not yet executing NC. It is now doing the work that must precede an executable order.

The original historical FS concept, which contemplated only a very small Army force for New Caledonia, is obsolete in this Branch. Historical Japanese planning in spring 1942 estimated roughly 16,000 U.S. Army land personnel and about 40 pursuit aircraft at NC, while the NC ground allotment was centered on only two infantry battalions. By 30 Jul Branch Japan has seen:
- strong Allied reaction to Santo;
- a full Americal/divisional base system at NC;
- growing shore air and base construction;
- the probability of Marine/NZ/Australian reinforcement after Santo falls.

Therefore NC is planned as a **two-division-scale campaign**, not a detachment raid.

## 1. Army–Navy bargain

### Army position
Army accepts NC if:
1. the 2nd and 38th Divisions / Southern-Army lineage carry the main bill;
2. China/Manchuria Five-Go formations are protected;
3. the operation has an explicit stop/reassess gate after NC;
4. shipping/engineers are returned toward continental buildup after the campaign unless a new national decision is made.

Army does **not** oppose NC because of raw manpower scarcity. Its concerns are:
- lift and merchant-hull concentration;
- replacement occupation troops;
- artillery/vehicle/engineer state;
- time lost from Five-Go stockpiling;
- Soviet-contingency/continental depth.

### Navy position
Navy wants to exploit:
- Santo as a forward ISR/base hinge;
- Wasp's repair absence;
- Japanese temporary carrier/surface initiative;
- the strategic value of cutting the U.S.–Australia LOC.

Navy will not wait for Soryu merely to restore a larger deck count. Hiryu is optional if the calendar naturally reaches its first-line gate.

## 6. Shipping / landing problem — likely first real Japanese bottleneck

The original FS planners intended to reuse shipping returning from Java. Branch B has better scheduling/turnaround, but hulls still occupy sea-days.

A 55–60k NC package needs:
- multiple echelons, not one magically simultaneous landing;
- bulk troop transports;

- landing craft/tenders;
