# Branch B — NC first-day combined-arms landing authorization close, 1942-07-30 v001

Status: **FIRST-DAY DESIGN CLOSED / EXACT UNIT & WEAPON MANIFEST OPEN**  
Clock: **1942-07-30 00:00**

## 0. Core ruling

The first day is designed around converting the Boulouparis main landing into a road-capable combined-arms lodgment before Americal/754th can mass a counterattack.

Headcount alone is not a success criterion.

## 1. First-touch target

Retain the v085 total first-touch goal: **~3,000–4,000 physical plus critical heavy-support loads**.

Closed center split:
- **Boulouparis / Baie de Saint-Vincent main:** ~2,400–2,900 physical first-touch/first-cycle-equivalent;
- **Ouano / La Foa secondary:** ~700–1,000;
- small floating reserve/late first-cycle share remains uncommitted.

These are throughput targets, not guaranteed combat-ready numbers.

## 2. Main-axis first-wave content

The first main-axis cycle must sacrifice pure infantry capacity to land:
- beach-control engineers;
- road/bridge engineers;
- signals;
- naval-fire liaison / artillery FOs;
- MG and mortars;
- towed/direct-fire AT pieces and ammunition;
- mines/demolition stores;
- first priority medical/recovery capability;
- selected gun/prime-mover loads where craft/gradient permit.

### Minimum planning firepower — requirement, not current inventory proof
By H+2/3 main axis should have:
- roughly **8–12 AT/direct-fire gun-equivalent pieces** or an equivalent named mix capable of engaging M3-class light armor;
- mines/obstacle teams on the main approach roads;
- heavy mortars/MG sufficient to defend a compact lodgment.

By H+4–6:
- roughly **16–24 AT/direct-fire gun-equivalent pieces** across the main lodgment;
- roughly **8–12 75-mm-class field/mountain/direct-fire guns** usable or being registered;
- sufficient prime movers/ammunition/engineer tools to keep them off the beach.

Exact Type 1 47-mm / older 37-mm / 75-mm mix remains a named Army OOB audit. These numbers are authorization minima, not an assertion of automatic availability.

## 3. Secondary-axis content

Ouano/La Foa first touch emphasizes:
- infantry;
- engineers/signals;
- mortars/MG;
- light AT;
- road demolition/ambush capability.

Medium tanks: **0 early first-wave center**.

The secondary force is required to hold a real lodgment and cut/pin the road system, but it is not expected to defeat the entire U.S. mobile reserve unsupported.

## 4. H+6 combined-arms objective

If sea state/boat losses remain bounded, planning center by H+6:
- **~8,500–11,500 physical ashore / in controlled immediate landing movement** across both axes;
- main axis contains the majority of field/AT guns, engineers and ammunition;
- at least one main road exit supports controlled vehicle/gun movement;
- ground artillery/FOs begin taking danger-close missions from naval fire;
- the lodgment can survive a limited M3/infantry counterattack without waiting for carrier aircraft.

This supersedes any interpretation that the first six hours are only a troop-unloading race.

## 5. Japanese armor branch

### If no regiment-scale medium-tank commitment
- first-day heavy-equivalent craft are concentrated on guns, prime movers, engineer plant and selected light armor/tankettes;
- organic AT/artillery must bear the mobile-defense burden;
- Ha-Go/light vehicles can use 14-m craft where physically suitable.

### If 8th Tank Regiment / meaningful Chi-Ha packet is committed
The early tank plan is:
- **0 medium tanks in the first-touch center**;
- first useful medium-tank packet: **~8–12 vehicles target at H+6–8**, only after road exits/assembly ground are available;
- further regimental vehicles follow during later D-day/D+1 cycles;
- 17-m/heavy-equivalent landing-craft gate remains mandatory.

The first tank packet is a mobile reserve/counter-counterattack force. It is not allowed to consume the gun/ammunition slots needed to survive before H+6.

## 6. End-D-day authorization goal

Retain v085 end-day target:
- **~15,000–20,000 physical ashore / controlled landing movement** if losses/weather remain bounded.

But close the required quality condition:
- majority of first-priority AT/field-artillery ammunition landed;
- engineer/road-control system functioning;
- at least one main and one secondary logistics lane usable;
- main lodgment no longer depends on continuous naval danger-close fire;
- medium tanks, if committed, have at least a small operational packet off the beach;
- U.S. surface intervention has not forced the carrier/heavy cover system off station through oiler/escort collapse.

## 7. Fire-support / observation integration

### H-2 to H
- BB/CA fixed-target fire;
- 2–3 main-axis spot/mark channels;
- airfield/AA/road-node suppression.

### H to H+2
- CA/CL/DD take visible beach/road exits;
- ground FO teams begin transmitting;
- main-axis live observer load ~3–4 channels.

### H+2 to H+6
- one or two channels prioritize the Tontouta mobile-reserve corridor;
- ship/air fire seeks delay/disorganization;
- organic AT/guns kill or repel whatever reaches the lodgment.

### H+6 onward
- ground guns/mortars assume more local support;
- naval force frees observation/fire capacity for deeper road interdiction and U.S. surface response.

## 8. Landing failure/abort conditions sharpened

The NC operation is delayed/reshaped before final execution if any of these cannot be met:
- Boulouparis main sector hydrography cannot pass heavy craft reliably;
- first AT/signals/engineer layer cannot be ashore by H+2/3;
- 75-mm/direct-fire layer cannot be usable by the H+4–6 counterattack window;
- more than ~20–25% of the critical heavy-craft system is expected to be unavailable before road exits open;
- observation plan cannot keep a live southern-road channel while preserving fleet search;
- medium-tank plan requires heavy craft that do not physically exist in the named wallet.
