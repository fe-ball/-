# Branch B — NC naval fire-support mission allocation close, 1942-07-30 v001

Status: **ROLE / SWITCHING RULES CLOSED; NAMED HULLS, AMMUNITION AND BEACHES OPEN**
Clock: **1942-07-30 00:00**

## 0. Correction

NC shore fire is not centered on battleships alone.
The support system is divided by caliber, response time and surface-war switching cost.

## 1. Heavy bombardment pair — BB + attached CA
Mission:
- pre-H fixed target destruction/suppression;
- airfields, known batteries, road nodes, command/base infrastructure.

Observation:
- pre-registered target pack + 2–3 airborne spot/mark channels center.

Switch rule:
- credible U.S. fast-BB surface contact can terminate/shorten the shore-fire cycle;
- retain meaningful AP/anti-surface readiness.

## 2. CA sustained support layer
Mission:
- 20.3-cm suppression after BB pair begins to clear;
- observed artillery/AA positions;
- road/tank assembly areas;
- deeper base targets that exceed DD/CL close-support utility.

Observation:
- 2–3 air/ground observation channels center across assigned CA sectors.

CA fire is an important part of the planned weight and must receive ammunition/observer allocation. Do not write NC bombardment as “450 BB rounds + miscellaneous cruiser fire.”

## 3. CL/DD close-support layer
Mission:
- beach exits;
- visible strongpoints;
- local infantry concentrations;
- roadblocks;
- immediate fire requests from ground observers.

Observation:
- ship optics for visible coastal targets;
- ground liaison / nearby F1M/E13A for targets behind local cover.

Switch rule:
- direct-cover DDs do not abandon transports for distant targets;
- dedicated fire-support DD/CL may shift to surface defense under raid warning.

## 4. Fire-support vs night-interception deconfliction

The same hull may change roles over the operation, but not simultaneously.
Working planning model:
- BB pair A: heavy shore-fire window;
- BB pair B: heavy/night interception reserve;
- CA group 1: sustained shore support;
- CA/DD group 2: night/cutoff surface role;
- CL/DD close-support group: transport/beach box.

Rotation occurs after ammunition/fuel/observer state is updated.

## 5. Observation is the allocation key

A ship without a current target/observer does not receive a generic accuracy bonus.
When observation channels are saturated:
priority is:
1. hostile coast/field artillery threatening landing craft/transport;
2. tank/mobile counterattack corridors;
3. beachhead-breaking infantry concentrations;
4. AA/radar suppressing essential air support;
5. fixed base destruction.

Fixed targets that can wait are deferred rather than consuming the last live observer channel.

## 6. Ground-fire transition

By H+3–6, as Japanese field guns/AT/mortars become operational, shipfire increasingly shifts away from danger-close infantry targets toward:
- deeper reserves;
- road interdiction;
- artillery suppression;
- base/airfield denial;
- surface-war readiness.

This makes first-wave artillery/FO throughput a direct way to release naval combat power back to the fleet mission.
