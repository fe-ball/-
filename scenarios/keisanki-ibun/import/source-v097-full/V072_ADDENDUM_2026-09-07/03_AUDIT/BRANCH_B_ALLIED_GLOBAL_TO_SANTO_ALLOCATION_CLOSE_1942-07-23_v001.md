# Branch B — Allied global → Pacific → South Pacific → Santo allocation close, 1942-07-23 v001

Status: **CLOSED FOR D-4 STRATEGIC ALLOCATION; CONTACT/LOSS RESULTS OPEN**

## 0. Purpose
This closes the missing layer above the v070 local Allied mirror. The question is not how many Allied troops, aircraft and ships exist in the world, but how many can be released through four gates by 27 July 1942:

**global commitments → Pacific commitments → South Pacific fixed garrisons → Santo-reactive mobile force.**

Authority labels:
- **HARD** — directly retained Branch state or historical fact not broken by Branch chronology.
- **DERIVED CLOSED** — strong consequence of retained Branch state; fixed unless a direct contradiction is found.
- **WORKING CLOSED** — reconstructed number needed to run the event engine; not claimed as archival exact strength.
- **CONDITIONAL** — available only after the listed warning / transport / readiness gate.

Anti-teleport rule: a formation may exist in the theater and still contribute **zero** to Santo D-day if moving it strips a base, requires combat loading, lacks lift/escort, or cannot arrive by H-hour.

## 7. Why this is different from historical Watchtower-era concentration
The historical Allies were beginning to convert from static LOC defense to an offensive concentration. Branch B has three additional brakes:
1. the U.S. carrier force has suffered heavier losses and Wasp is the only healthy immediate fleet carrier;
2. Japanese-held Midway keeps Hawaii/Central-Pacific air and defenses less releasable;
3. Port Moresby loss and continuing Japanese South-Pacific initiative make New Caledonia, Fiji, Samoa, Tonga and Efate rational targets, so their garrisons cannot all be treated as reserve.

Britain simultaneously has less modern carrier redundancy and is still paying the Madagascar/WIO/European strategic bill.

Therefore Branch B does **not** remove Allied strength from Oceania; it makes the distinction between **present in Oceania** and **spendable on Santo by 27 Jul** more severe.
