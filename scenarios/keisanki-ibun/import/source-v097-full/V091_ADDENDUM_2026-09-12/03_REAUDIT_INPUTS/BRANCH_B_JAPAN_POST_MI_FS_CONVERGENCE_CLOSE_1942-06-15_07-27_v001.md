## 1. Scope

This audit closes only the Japanese-side strategic/convergence clock after the current MI result. It does not preserve the prior 1942-09-25 Santo date, does not preserve the old NC start, and does not yet resolve the Allied July-20 mirror state or individual combat contacts.

The question is: given the Branch post-MI fleet, existing FS preparation, Madagascar/Indian-Ocean commitments, carrier-air rebuilding, shipping/oiler requirements and forward bases, when can Japan rationally launch a limited Santo operation without waiting for a full fleet-wide rebuild?

## 2. Strategic priority after MI

Current center priority:
1. **Limited-FS / South Pacific exploitation**
2. retain Midway / Port Moresby / Solomons positions
3. continue Indian-Ocean submarine pressure
4. indirect Madagascar/Vichy support
5. Bay of Bengal major surface raiding only if genuinely surplus

The historical FS preparation is not treated as erased. The 17th Army/FS machinery existed before historical cancellation; in Branch B, Port Moresby is already taken and MI is a Japanese strategic victory, so the historical reasons to dismantle the FS sequence are absent.

However, MI also costs Japan Akagi/Kaga and leaves Soryu/Hiryu badly damaged. Therefore the old full FS sequence is reduced to:

**Santo → New Caledonia → stop/reassess.**

Fiji/Samoa are not automatic continuations.

## 3. Madagascar / Western Indian Ocean

Madagascar support is **finite parallel pressure**, not the Japanese strategic main effort.

Japan does not need to abandon its own Pacific exploitation in order to “save” Madagascar. The west-Indian-Ocean operation already has high leverage: submarines, special-mission boats, auxiliary raiders and reconnaissance can force Britain to pay ASW, escort, patrol-aircraft and base-defense costs disproportionate to the Japanese investment.

Therefore:
- submarines already committed west continue their patrol/return cycles;
- Penang-based submarine rotations remain valid;
- auxiliary-raider/reconnaissance activity may continue;
- no new fleet-carrier, fast-battleship, large Pacific DD mass or heavy-cruiser main effort is diverted merely to keep Madagascar under pressure.

The best strategic help to the Axis/Vichy position is to keep the Indian Ocean uncertain **while creating another major Allied crisis through FS**.

**Heavy-cruiser-centered Operation B: CENTER NOT ACTIVATED.**

The prior WIO-4A/4B line assumed a late Santo date and thus found an August window for Suzuya/Kumano, DD and oiler resources in the Bay of Bengal. With Santo centered in late July, that logic no longer holds.

Current center:

- Indian-Ocean pressure continues through cheaper submarine/auxiliary/reconnaissance means.

Any downstream WIO result whose causal input is the 8–18 Aug Operation-B surface raid is reopened for later global propagation.

Santo center package:

- **Junyo or Ryujo** as primary support/CAP/landing-support deck;

Do not wait for:
- Hiryu full recovery;
- Soryu full recovery;
- every air group to return to homogeneous prewar-quality establishment.

Branch aviation doctrine continues to concentrate surviving experienced personnel on the first-line fleet carriers and uses medium/light carriers for mixed/support roles rather than degrading the whole training system.
