# Branch B — Santo D-2 command personality and decision doctrine close, 1942-07-25 v001

Status: **CLOSED FOR D-2 / D-1 DECISION ADJUDICATION**  
Clock: **1942-07-25 00:00**. No D-2 tactical event has been executed by this file.  
Scope: commander roles, risk posture, information thresholds, command friction and target-priority rules.  
Method: personalities act only through choices among physically/doctrinally plausible options. They are **not combat multipliers**.

## 0. Governing rule

A commander may change:
- which physically available target is prioritized;
- how fresh/credible a contact must be before committing a strike;
- how much reserve/CAP is retained;
- whether to pursue, disengage, dogleg, delay or accept risk;
- how quickly delegated authority is used;
- how much weight is placed on carrier preservation versus the landing mission.

A commander may **not**:
- create range, fuel, aircraft, escorts, landing craft or information that do not exist;
- convert COMINT/DF into a tactical coordinate without the existing acquisition/fusion/handoff chain;
- erase weather, deck-cycle, maintenance, recovery or communications delay;
- make separate commands behave as a modern fused headquarters;
- receive postwar knowledge of the true enemy order of battle.

Branch computation/reporting improvements reduce avoidable arithmetic, plotting and formatted-report errors. They do not create a real-time common operating picture.

Classification used below:
- **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE** — historical office/command on or immediately around this date survives unless Branch events displaced it.
- **BRANCH WORKING CLOSED** — alternate-MI continuity plus existing Branch lineage makes the assignment the center solution; do not change without reopening personnel authority.
- **ROLE-BASED CLOSED** — behavior is constrained mainly by mission and organization because evidence for individual psychology is insufficient.

# 1. Japanese command stack

## 1.1 Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto — Combined Fleet
Classification: **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE; BRANCH-ADJUSTED STRATEGIC POSTURE**.

Function here:
- strategic authorizer, not minute-to-minute Santo tactical commander;
- establishes that Limited-FS exploitation remains worth paying for after modified MI;
- decides the relative strategic value of destroying surviving U.S. carrier strength versus merely completing the landing.

Closed posture:
1. **Enemy fleet-carrier destruction remains the highest-value naval opportunity.** Historically Yamamoto made drawing out and destroying U.S. carriers a primary objective. Branch MI has, unusually, validated much of that strategic premise: Japan occupies Midway and the U.S. has suffered catastrophic carrier losses.
2. The success does **not** erase the cost of Akagi/Kaga and other MI damage. Yamamoto therefore supports rapid exploitation, but not a blind carrier chase on stale coordinates.
3. If a fresh U.S. fleet-carrier contact and the invasion-support mission compete in the same launch window, Yamamoto's intent favors the carrier **provided the assault body is not thereby left under an obvious immediate mortal threat**.
4. He accepts distributed/deceptive force geometry and does not demand that carrier, heavy cover and transports collapse into one visible formation merely for reassurance.

Branch effect on personality:
- confidence in the strategic utility of carrier annihilation: **higher than historical post-Midway**;
- willingness to exploit South-Pacific initiative: **high**;
- tolerance for sacrificing irreplaceable decks on low-confidence information: **not high**.

## 1.2 Vice Admiral Chuichi Nagumo — Santo carrier striking force
Classification: **BRANCH WORKING CLOSED**.

Assignment:
- on-scene tactical commander of the committed Zuikaku + Shokaku + Ryujo carrier package;
- existing Branch lineage had a “Nagumo center” for the same three-deck role; historical mid-July reorganization also retained Nagumo over the Japanese carrier force.

Do **not** model Nagumo as generically timid or frozen. Historical reassessment shows that many apparently slow choices at Midway were constrained by doctrine, recovery cycles and incomplete information. By late July Japanese carrier doctrine was explicitly incorporating Midway lessons, including carrier primacy and first-strike urgency.

Closed Santo posture:
1. **Fresh carrier contact:** offensive. If a tactically usable enemy fleet-carrier position exists, he launches promptly within deck-cycle constraints.
2. **Stale/ambiguous carrier contact:** disciplined confirmation before emptying decks. He preserves CAP, search and a reserve rather than sending the whole attack group into an expanding uncertainty ellipse.
3. **Only transports located, enemy carrier unlocated:** willing to hit the transports, but not with an automatic all-in deck strike. Land-based air is preferred for the first cheap attempt when geometry allows; carrier aviation may add a bounded strike while keeping anti-carrier reserve/CAP.
4. **Carrier plus transport contacts simultaneously usable:** carrier first, unless a transport strike is already airborne or the assault body faces an immediate attack that only carrier aviation can stop.
5. Recovery synchronization matters. He will not knowingly produce a Midway-style deck conflict merely to satisfy “aggressiveness.”

Contact release thresholds — WORKING CLOSED:
- **FRESH / STRIKE-USABLE:** report age <= 90 min at launch decision, course/speed usable, target ellipse roughly <= 45 nm center, with high-quality visual or equivalent physical identification.
- **AGING:** 90–150 min. Full carrier strike requires a shadower, second fix, or fast confirmation plan likely to refresh the track en route.
- **STALE:** >150 min without reacquisition. No full first anti-carrier strike solely on extrapolation.

These are decision thresholds, not detection probabilities.

## 1.3 Vice Admiral Tamon Yamaguchi — surviving MI carrier leader
Classification: **HARD BRANCH SURVIVAL; NOT ON-SCENE COMMANDER**.

Current authority already closes only that:
- Yamaguchi survives with Hiryu;

This package does **not** invent an emergency transfer placing Yamaguchi aboard the Santo carrier force. Therefore:

If later personnel evidence explicitly places him in the Santo force, reopen this item only.

## 1.4 Vice Admiral Nobutake Kondo — heavy/advance cover
Classification: **BRANCH WORKING CLOSED**.

Assignment:
- operational commander for the heavy surface/advance-cover element centered on Atago/Takao/Maya and fast-battleship support, without authority to micro-manage Nagumo's flight operations or Tanaka's convoy station-keeping.

Closed posture:
- mission-oriented and willing to place heavy ships between a known hostile surface threat and the invasion body;
- strong preference for night/favorable surface geometry over daylight movement inside unresolved carrier-air threat;
- does not turn a transport-defense mission into a long daylight pursuit of a retreating cruiser group;
- if Allied heavy surface forces are physically located on an interceptable night track and Japanese carrier/land-air cover makes daylight exit plausible, he accepts a high-intensity surface action.

This is primarily a **role-based operational rule**; no arbitrary personal combat bonus is attached.

## 1.5 Rear Admiral Raizo Tanaka — Jintsu / transport direct cover
Classification: **BRANCH WORKING CLOSED, STRONGLY HISTORICALLY ANCHORED**.

Assignment:
- tactical escort commander for Jintsu + the six-destroyer direct-cover group around the assault transports.

Closed posture:
1. **Transport mission first.** He does not chase a tempting target away from the invasion body unless that target is the direct threat.
2. Uses speed changes, zig-zag, smoke, screening destroyers and night maneuver aggressively to preserve transports.
3. Will accept substantial destroyer exposure to break up a surface or air attack against transports.
4. At night, if an Allied surface force penetrates the screen, he is highly willing to counterattack with torpedoes rather than merely shepherd the convoy away.
5. If daylight air attack becomes plainly unsustainable and cover fails, he may recommend a dogleg, temporary retirement or timing change. He cannot cancel Limited-FS on his own.

Historical Tanaka later emphasized that successful amphibious operations require detailed target study and close escort/landing-force liaison; this fits the Branch emphasis on improved planning rather than reckless improvisation.

## 1.6 Vice Admiral Gunichi Mikawa — Eighth Fleet / local Outer South Seas transition

Historical anchor:

- Mikawa hoisted flag in Chokai 16 Jul;
- was assigned Chokai/CruDiv 6 and Outer South Seas responsibility 23 Jul;

Closed behavior:
- high willingness to exploit a favorable **night** surface opportunity;
- aggressive but not careless;
- after achieving a local objective, strong bias to clear the area before daylight carrier exposure if enemy carrier status is unresolved.

## 1.7 Vice Admiral Nishizo Tsukahara — Eleventh Air Fleet / land-based naval air
Classification: **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE; COMMAND-SEPARATION CLOSED**.

Function:
- land/tender-based naval aviation is a separate command node from Nagumo's carrier force;
- G3M/G4M, flying-boat and forward-node activity may support the same operational purpose, but is not one perfectly synchronized air wing.

Closed posture:
- aggressive long-range search/recontact and attack when target position and range permit;
- preserves enough search effort to prevent a one-shot strike from blinding the theater;
- repeated long-range attacks require actual turnaround, fuel, ordnance and crew readiness.

Command friction:
- formatted Branch reports improve clarity and reduce plot error;
- normal local reporting/handoff to carrier command still costs time. Center additional handoff latency for a useful land-air contact reaching Nagumo's tactical plot: **10–30 min**, unless direct radio circumstances justify less.

# 2. Allied command stack

## 2.1 Admiral Chester W. Nimitz — CINCPAC/CINCPOA
Classification: **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE; BRANCH-ADJUSTED STRATEGIC POSTURE**.

Closed posture:
- follows calculated risk rather than either carrier-hoarding at all costs or desperate gambling;
- gives broad intent and delegates local tactical choices;
- Branch MI losses make Wasp extraordinarily valuable, but Midway occupation + Santo threat make complete passivity strategically unacceptable.

Therefore:
1. Wasp may be exposed when the expected operational payoff is high and the information advantage is real.
2. Wasp is not ordered into a symmetric close search duel against an inferred two-fleet-carrier-plus-support Japanese force merely to demonstrate aggressiveness.
3. Ground reinforcement, search and land-based air are the preferred cheaper ways to contest Santo while carrier information remains poor.
4. A firm first-strike opportunity against Japanese fleet carriers is an exception: refusing it merely because Wasp is precious can be more dangerous than attacking.

Nimitz is not a real-time strike-release authority at Santo D-2.

## 2.2 Vice Admiral Robert L. Ghormley — COMSOPAC
Classification: **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE; CAUTIOUS BRANCH POSTURE CLOSED**.

Historical/organizational anchor:
- Ghormley assumed South Pacific command in June;
- his authority over incoming Pacific Fleet task forces was strategically important but tactically limited/delegated;
- later historical assessments and Nimitz's eventual relief of him support a cautious/pessimistic, logistics-conscious profile rather than an aggressive Halsey-style one.

Branch adjustment:
- his caution is **more objectively justified** than historically because the U.S. has lost additional carriers and Midway itself.

Closed Santo policy:
1. **Ground reinforcements:** commit quickly once W1/W2 make Santo the likely objective. This already occurred with 2d Marines and Raiders.
2. **Wasp:** maintain remote cover rather than stationing it beside the transports or Santo.
3. Do not order Wasp to hunt blindly north/west for Japanese carriers on LOW-quality information.
4. Permit Noyes to exploit a high-confidence, tactically favorable carrier or invasion-force opportunity under standing mission authority; do not require a long headquarters approval loop for every strike.
5. If Wasp is physically detected or Japanese carriers are fixed inside counterstrike geometry, carrier survival becomes a very high priority unless a simultaneous first-strike opportunity is compelling.

Ghormley therefore sets a **conservative strategic ceiling**, not a veto on every offensive action.

## 2.3 Rear Admiral Leigh Noyes — Wasp task group tactical commander
Classification: **HARD/WORKING-CLOSED TACTICAL ROLE**.

Historical anchor:
- Noyes commanded the Wasp-centered carrier support force;
- during the July 1942 movement he was sufficiently willing to consider offensive use of his embarked Marines that he asked Colonel John M. Arthur for a plan to seize Guadalcanal-Tulagi with 2d Marines plus one Raider battalion.

Closed posture:
- more willing than Ghormley to exploit a firm local opportunity;
- constrained by the remote-cover mission and by Wasp being the only healthy immediately available U.S. fleet carrier in this Branch theater;
- will not conduct a blind search thrust toward an inferred multi-deck Japanese force.

Wasp strike-release thresholds — WORKING CLOSED:
- **Japanese carrier contact:** normally strike-usable if report age <= 75 min, carrier identification/size sufficiently firm, course/speed usable and recovery/egress can be planned. 75–120 min requires continuing shadow/recontact or a tight constrained track. >120 min without refresh is not enough for a major deck strike.
- **Japanese assault transports only:** Wasp strike is discretionary, not automatic. It requires a flank/offset geometry that does not deliberately move Wasp into the center of the likely Japanese carrier search/strike cone and must leave a credible recovery/displacement route.
- **Wasp detected:** immediate CAP/maneuver priority; transport strike can be held or cancelled. If the enemy carrier is simultaneously fixed, first strike remains an option rather than automatic flight.

## 2.4 Captain Forrest P. Sherman — commanding officer, Wasp
Classification: **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE; ROLE-BASED CLOSED**.

Sherman controls ship execution, not theater strategy:
- flight-deck cycle quality;

- ship readiness and damage control;
- implementation of Noyes's tactical orders.

No independent “Sherman personality” may overrule Noyes/Ghormley strike policy. His professional carrier/war-plans background supports competent execution, not a free decision bonus.

## 2.5 Rear Admiral John S. McCain — ComAirSoPac / shore-based aircraft
Classification: **HARD HISTORICAL ROLE; COMMAND-FRICTION CLOSED**.

All land/tender-based SOPAC aircraft are a separate air command from Wasp's carrier aviation.

Implications:
- PBY/B-17/VMF reports do not instantly become a fused Wasp track;
- McCain must balance search, base defense and strike, rather than committing every MR aircraft to the newest contact;
- shore-air support and carrier action are coordinated by cooperation/requests, not one modern tactical air commander.

Center handoff latency for a shore-air contact becoming a tactically usable Wasp update:
- **15–45 min** after the original report, depending on direct relay, report quality and whether Noyes can hear the source transmission;
- this is in addition to the age of the observation itself.

If a PBY/B-17 keeps shadowing and repeatedly transmits updates, the track may stay useful. One isolated sighting does not.

# 3. Information-belief matrix at 1942-07-25 00:00

Truth and commander belief are deliberately separate.

## 3.1 Japanese belief — CLOSED CENTER

What Japan knows:
- its assault convoy has been repeatedly found and attacked; Allied warning quality is HIGH;
- the Allied assault-body track quality is good enough to be dangerous;
- no physical major reinforcement to Santo has yet been confirmed;
- Wasp has not been physically detected.

What Japan does **not** know:
- that Wasp is the sole healthy immediately available U.S. fleet carrier in SOPAC;
- exact 2d Marines or Raider positions;
- exact Allied land-air MR count.

Japanese carrier-strength estimate of the enemy:
- U.S. Pacific carrier strength is believed severely depleted after Branch MI;
- center operational belief: **one U.S. fleet carrier could plausibly be in or approaching SOPAC, with a second operational carrier not ruled out** because battle-damage assessment and theater transfer information are imperfect;
- therefore a fresh carrier contact is extraordinarily valuable, but is not treated with omniscient certainty as “the last American carrier.”

## 3.2 Allied belief — CLOSED CENTER

What the Allies know:
- Santo is the immediate Japanese objective with HIGH confidence;
- the Japanese assault-body route is MEDIUM-HIGH but the last exact track is aging after the evening dogleg;
- a strong Japanese carrier cover force is inferred, exact position LOW.

Carrier estimate:
- Shokaku and Zuikaku are treated as likely operational fleet-carrier threats;
- at least one support/light deck is plausible;
- damaged Soryu/Hiryu are **not** assumed operational merely because they survived Branch MI;
- center planning therefore treats Wasp as facing **at least two fleet decks and probably a support deck** if it pushes into close carrier battle.

This is why Wasp's baseline posture remains remote cover even though Noyes has authority to exploit a first-strike opportunity.

# 4. Runtime decision gates for D-2 / D-1

These rules are now authoritative unless an event creates new information.

## Japanese gates

**J-CARRIER-1 — firm U.S. carrier contact**  
If a physically identified U.S. fleet carrier meets Nagumo's fresh/usable threshold and is in strike range:
- anti-carrier strike becomes first naval target priority;
- launch begins as soon as deck/recovery/CAP conditions permit;
- meaningful CAP/search reserve remains unless a deliberate all-in decision is separately justified by the event state.

**J-TRANS-1 — 2d Marines transport convoy confirmed, U.S. carrier unknown**  
- Japanese ~1,000-man contingency reserve gate FIRES;
- land-based air gets first economical anti-shipping opportunity where range/contact permits;
- carrier aviation may attack, but does not automatically strip carrier defense/search while U.S. carrier location remains unknown.

**J-RAIDER-1 — Raider/APD reinforcement confirmed near Santo**  
- contingency reserve gate FIRES because reinforcement is physically confirmed;
- target priority is below a fleet carrier and below the large 2d Marines convoy;
- use forward/land-based air or local surface assets if the contact is exploitable; do not divert the principal carrier strike solely for four APDs unless no higher target exists.

**J-SURFACE-1 — Allied surface penetration at night**  
- Tanaka defends transports; Kondo/Mikawa/local forces exploit favorable night intercept geometry if physically available;
- pursuit that produces severe daylight carrier-air exposure is not automatic.

**J-NOCONTACT-1 — no useful Allied contact**  
- continue layered search, CAP, convoy cover and Santo pre-shaping;
- do not invent a strike from inferred positions.

## Allied gates

**A-CARRIER-1 — firm Japanese carrier contact**  
If Noyes receives a carrier track meeting his usable threshold and Wasp can launch/recover while retaining a credible displacement route:
- a carrier strike is authorized under standing mission intent;
- Ghormley's caution does not require waiting for headquarters permission until the contact goes stale.

**A-TRANS-1 — only Japanese assault transports firm, carriers unlocated**  
- McCain shore-based air continues reconnaissance/attack effort;
- Wasp remains remote by default;
- Noyes may strike transports only if offset geometry and recovery route keep risk from becoming a deliberate close duel with unlocated Japanese carriers.

**A-WASP-DETECTED-1**  
On credible evidence that Wasp has been sighted/reported by Japanese search:
- increase CAP/ready fighters and maneuver immediately;
- hold/cancel lower-value transport strike if necessary;
- if Japanese carriers are simultaneously fixed, Noyes may still choose first strike because displacement alone may not restore surprise.

**A-GROUND-1 — Raider arrival**  
- Edson begins immediate light-force perimeter/recon integration;
- Rose protects base functionality;
- no full-battalion or vehicle credit is created.

**A-GROUND-2 — 2d Marines arrival**  
- Arthur unloads combat elements first and builds a coherent defensive system before deep maneuver;
- physical headcount and combat-effective strength remain separately tracked.
