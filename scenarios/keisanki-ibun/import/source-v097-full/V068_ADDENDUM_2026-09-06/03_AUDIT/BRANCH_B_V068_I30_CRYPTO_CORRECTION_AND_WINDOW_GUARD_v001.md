# v068 I-30 correction and cryptographic-window audit

## Finding

v067 reintroduced a historical event explicitly removed by active Branch authority. The I-30 Singapore mine loss is therefore a bookkeeping/authority regression, not a fresh historical adjudication.

The more important systemic finding is that the same legacy authority already contains a strong communications-intelligence divergence:

- Japanese high-grade COMSEC is a domestic line and is not dependent on I-30.
- U.S. historical high-grade short-strategic Japanese plaintext reconstruction is not copied automatically.
- U.S. residual SIGINT/ISR channels remain active.
- Japanese offensive COMINT is meaningful but probabilistic and lower-grade; it narrows search and warning rather than supplying omniscient fixes.
- I-30 strengthens secure Axis liaison later; it does not instantly produce a new Japanese code or a SIGABA break.

## Operational interpretation

The most valuable Japanese crypto success is often **denial**, not decryption:
- a U.S. submarine is not positioned from an Ultra-quality cue;
- a carrier sortie is not predicted to the historical hour;
- a Japanese movement is detected only as traffic/DF activity rather than read in detail;
- Japanese HQ gets enough residual cues to disperse, delay, reroute, or focus reconnaissance.

This can create temporary windows where Japan has the initiative despite not reading the enemy's highest-grade traffic.

## Anti-magic rule

A crypto advantage may alter:
- warning time,
- route choice,
- sortie timing,
- search sector,
- readiness posture,
- probability of an uncued encounter,
- probability that a historical Ultra-cued ambush happens at all.

It does not directly alter:
- torpedo reliability,
- bomb accuracy,
- aircraft range,
- radar range,
- visual detection physics,
- sea state,
- crew endurance.

Those remain separate gates.
