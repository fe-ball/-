## 0. Core ruling

The early-September NC plan does **not** center on a direct Noumea harbor assault.
The closed operational geometry is:

- **MAIN AXIS — Boulouparis / Baie de Saint-Vincent sector on the west coast.**
- **SECONDARY AXIS — Ouano / La Foa sector.**
- Tontouta, Noumea and the east-coast Thio axis are target/interdiction/deception problems, not center first-wave landing sectors.

The exact sand/coral landing lanes inside the two selected sectors remain conditional on hydrographic reconnaissance, tide, reef-passage survey and local defenses. A sector is closed here; a fictitious precision beach coordinate is not.

## 1. Geographic baseline

Historical U.S. engineering analysis recognized that:
- Noumea was the only major port worth mentioning;
- Tontouta was selected as the principal defended airfield;
- the island was mountainous;
- the principal west-coast highway was narrow and twisting;
- road/communications quality was a major constraint on mobile defense.

By September 1942 U.S. units historically occupied a dispersed west-coast defense system rather than a single Noumea wall. Branch B keeps Americal intact and adds a stronger mobile-reserve possibility, so this historical dispersion is an anchor, not a copied September OOB.

The west coast is protected by a barrier reef/lagoon system. Reef passages and reef flats make landing feasibility a hydrographic problem as well as a beach-gradient problem.

## 3. Main axis — Boulouparis / Baie de Saint-Vincent

### Purpose
1. create a heavy-craft-capable main lodgment outside the densest Noumea defensive system;
2. seize/cut the west-coast highway and Boulouparis junctions;
3. force U.S. mobile armor/infantry to approach along a small number of road/bridge corridors that can be observed and interdicted;
4. turn south toward Tontouta only after field guns, AT, engineers and traffic control are ashore;
5. keep a landward route open for eventual connection with the La Foa secondary axis.

### Why not center on Tomo
Modern geographic anchors place Boulouparis roughly 70–80 km by road from Noumea and Tontouta about 45–50 km from Noumea; Tomo lies well south of Boulouparis. Therefore a far-south Boulouparis/Tomo landing shortens the American Tontouta counterattack clock too much for little strategic gain.

The main sector is consequently centered farther north/west within the Baie de Saint-Vincent/Boulouparis operational area, subject to reef/pass and beach-lane survey.

### First-day allocation
Planning share of first-day amphibious effort:
- **~65–72%** of troop/heavy-support throughput;
- most 17-m/heavy-equivalent craft if medium tanks are authorized;
- primary engineers, signals, AT, 75-mm/direct-fire, road-control and recovery assets;
- main naval-fire observation concentration.

## 4. Secondary axis — Ouano / La Foa

### Purpose
1. force Patch/Americal to defend a second west-coast road node;
2. pin or dislocate central/northern Americal elements;
3. threaten La Foa road crossings and prevent a clean north-to-south reinforcement flow;
4. create a northern pincer that can join the Boulouparis axis along the west-coast road if the main landing succeeds.

### Limitation
Ouano has a substantial reef-flat/shallow-water problem. Therefore:
- secondary first touch is **light infantry / engineers / mortars / signals / light AT** first;
- no center early medium-tank landing;
- heavy guns/prime movers follow only after marked channels/tides/local lighter work prove usable;
- failure of heavy-lift access does not cancel the axis if its pinning/road-cut mission remains useful.

### First-day allocation
- **~18–25%** of first-day throughput;
- limited heavy-equipment share;
- enough strength to be a real lodgment, not a token feint.

## 5. Floating reserve / exploitation share

Keep **~8–12%** of first-day lift/assault strength uncommitted to a beach until first reports establish:
- which sector has a usable road exit;
- whether U.S. armor has concentrated south or north;
- whether one beach is losing heavy-craft capacity;
- whether a U.S. surface/air attack is disrupting one transport box.

The reserve may reinforce the main axis, convert a secondary lodgment into the main effort, or remain afloat/second-echelon. It is not a third beach by default.

## 6. Hydrographic authorization gates

The sector decision above does **not** waive reef/tide physics.
Before final D-day authorization, the Japanese plan must close:

1. at least two usable outer-reef/lagoon entry solutions for the main sector or one primary + one emergency alternative;
2. marked approaches for large ramped craft at the main landing sector;
3. tide/reef-flat timetable for Ouano/La Foa;
4. obstacle/mangrove/creek survey for beach exits;
5. hydrographic pilot/boat-leader packets distributed before H-hour;
6. wreck/disabled-craft bypass lanes so one loss does not block an entire landing stream.

If heavy-equivalent craft cannot reliably reach/discharge in the main sector, the medium-tank option must be reduced or removed even if the tanks themselves are available.

## 7. Expected Allied defensive logic — role-based, not omniscient

Before Japan reveals the exact beach, Patch's rational center remains:
- defend Noumea and Tontouta as the strategic core;
- distribute Americal along the west-coast road/airfield system rather than every beach;
- hold **754th Tank Battalion (Light)** as a mobile road reserve rather than static beach guns;
- use 1st Marine Division echelons, if arrived, as a second mobile/strongpoint reserve around the southern core rather than strip every fixed base northward;
- prepare bridge/culvert/road demolition and denial.

This makes Boulouparis dangerous but exploitable: U.S. armor can reach it, yet must expose itself on predictable road corridors.
