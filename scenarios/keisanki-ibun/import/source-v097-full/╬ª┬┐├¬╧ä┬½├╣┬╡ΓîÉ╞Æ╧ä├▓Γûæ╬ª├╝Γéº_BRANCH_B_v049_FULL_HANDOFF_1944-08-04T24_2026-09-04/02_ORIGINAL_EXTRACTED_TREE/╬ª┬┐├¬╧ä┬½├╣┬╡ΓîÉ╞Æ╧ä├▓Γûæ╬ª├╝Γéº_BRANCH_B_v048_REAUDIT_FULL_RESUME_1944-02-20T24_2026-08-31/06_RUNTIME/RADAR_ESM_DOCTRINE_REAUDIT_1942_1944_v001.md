## 1. Stop using one scalar 'radar advantage'

Every contact should distinguish:
1. EM WARNING — passive detection that somebody is radiating;
2. SENSOR CONTACT — own sensor/visual echo/sighting;
3. TRACK — stable position/motion estimate;
4. BATTLE PREPARATION — crew/formation/torpedo/CAP/AA readiness bought by earlier warning;
5. WEAPON-QUALITY — a solution usable for firing/interception/strike commitment.

Japanese selected-unit metric ESM may precede US target-quality track. US SG/CIC/fighter-direction can still dominate CONTACT->TRACK->WEAPON-QUALITY. These statements are compatible and must not be collapsed into 'who sees first'.

## 2. I-30/Würzburg effect

Würzburg data/hardware fills precision-tracking/servo/calibration cards in an already-developed Japanese sensor/plot/fire-control stack. Strongest early effect is on fixed sites and test/AA tracking. It does not provide US SG/PPI/mature CIC capability.

Current TECH radar production gains are narrowed to metrology/availability/variance. No generic +5-15% maximum range should be applied when RF power, receiver noise or antenna gain binds.

## 3. Metric ESM / passive cueing

By 1943 Q2 selected Japanese ships/submarines/bases can field compatible metric-band radar-warning receivers. Their output is normally bearing/warning, not target identity or range. By 1943 Q2-Q3 major sectors may use:
passive cue -> sector narrowing -> D4Y/seaplane/flying-boat/submarine visual/active confirm -> recontact/update -> Type-2P correlation.

This reduces wasted search and surprise tails; it does not infer a carrier from one emission.

Centimetric US surface-search/airborne radar remains a major blind band before a later centimetric-RWR gate. Never treat the 1943 metric RWR as SG/10cm immunity.

## 4. Tool-driven organizational derivatives

At A-grade bases during 1943 H2, the tools plausibly drive:
- a separate air-defense plot/control cell linking radar, visual posts, DF/weather, fighter readiness and AA warning;
- staged fighter readiness instead of paying continuous CAP for all warning duty;
- electronics field-service detachments, common spares/test gear, calibration logs and module exchange;
- distributed/backup radar power, spare sets and separation of antenna/transmitter/plot functions where practical;
- passive-heavy EMCON approaches for selected mobile forces and controlled active-search windows;
- emitter libraries and ESM-cued reconnaissance tasking;
- recovery/navigation assistance for returning long-range aircraft as part of existing non-combat-loss reduction (do not double-count a new global bonus).

These are not US-style mature GCI/CIC and not fleetwide automatic capabilities.

## 5. RCM / Giman-shi

Chaff is not a Branch invention. Branch advantage, where applicable, is better matching of use/frequency/task to known enemy radar behavior. Against compatible long-wave air-warning radar it can create clutter, false tracks or short fighter-direction friction. Do not apply it to centimetric SG as a general blind mechanism.

For carrier/air raids this primarily changes penetration/timing variance and may save or pass a few aircraft through terminal defense; do not automatically translate to extra ship hits.

## 6. Night surface warfare

ESM is not merely a surprise-avoidance device. Early bearing warning can buy battle-preparation time: torpedo crews at stations, tubes oriented, formation opened for timed attack, optics concentrated on a sector, and short active radar scans used after passive cueing. This interacts at a different causal stage with existing improved torpedo solution/reload/formation doctrine and may therefore compound without double counting.

US radar can still create weapon-quality track first. The Branch change is that Japanese units need not begin battle preparation only when US shells arrive.

Night-fighting confidence may therefore rise in trained surviving units by late 1943. Model confidence as a decision/willingness and procedural-discipline variable, not an accuracy bonus. It may increase willingness to make a limited probe/torpedo attack while still avoiding a suicidal prolonged radar gunfight.

## 7. Manifestation clock

late 1942: I-30 hardware analysis.
1943 Q1: prototypes/bench; selected experimental fixed use.
1943 Q2: selected metric RWR/acoustic-deception/field-service and A-grade base plot systems begin operational use.
1943 Q3: passive cueing and fixed-base radar/C2 become more useful; selected surface-force doctrine can integrate warning into torpedo preparation.
1943 Q4 / GALVANIC: mature enough to be mandatory event inputs, but band/unit distribution must be explicitly resolved.
1944 Q1: another three months of training and I-8 post-return technical feedback may change procedure/calibration before FLINTLOCK; do not simply copy GALVANIC maturity forward unchanged.
