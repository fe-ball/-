The centered Branch-B line now gives Japan a **firm but incomplete carrier contact on the morning of 23 November, centered about 0630 local**, after a weaker passive/DF warning chain during the preceding night. This is not a firing solution and does not identify the whole US carrier force.

## 2. Why contact is more likely than a no-contact morning

Three independent effects point the same way.

First, by 21-23 November the mobilization ledger already has Japanese target inference narrowed to the Gilbert/Marshall axis. Second, the historical Operation-RO stripping of Marshall aircraft is not inherited in Branch B, so the local reconnaissance/attack system is healthier. Third, Branch 1943H2 passive-warning/DF integration can cue scarce visual search aircraft into a narrower sector instead of asking them to search the entire ocean.

US routing is different because Midway remains Japanese-held and Espiritu Santo/New Caledonia are unavailable. That prevents copying a historical coordinate. It does **not** remove the D-1 geometry: carrier cover and assault forces still have to enter the Gilbert/southern-Marshall operating region to suppress Mili/Jaluit/Tarawa/Makin and cover the landing.

Historical GALVANIC is a useful sanity check rather than an input. A Japanese account summarized by Stan Jersey has two US carrier striking groups discovered about 0630 on historical D-1. The Branch therefore does not need a miracle to obtain an early-morning carrier sighting; if anything its cue-to-confirm process is somewhat better, although US CAP/radar is also better organized.

## 3. Environmental state

Historical GALVANIC aerology gives partly cloudy skies, roughly 12-20 mile visibility, easterly trade winds and generally satisfactory reconnaissance/landing weather through the succeeding days. Branch D-day is four days later, so the exact observation is not copied; centered event weather is kept in the same favorable trade-weather band rather than inventing a storm.

The lunar state is more consequential. 23-24 November 1943 is a waning crescent near new moon: about 15-20% illumination on the 23rd and about 10% on the 24th. The moon is chiefly a pre-dawn object. Daybreak visual search is therefore feasible, while the following evening/first half of night is very dark.

## 4. Contact ladder

### Late 22 Nov — EM warning only

Selected southern-Marshalls priority nodes receive intermittent compatible metric emissions and correlate them with RI/DF/traffic/pattern information. This says **a major emitting naval force is in a bearing sector**. It gives neither range nor carrier identity.

The centered model uses at least two effective reporting nodes across the Mili/Jaluit/southern-Marshalls approach sector. This is a Branch allocation assumption for a priority theater, not a claim that every island has a full ESM suite.

### 0130-0430, 23 Nov — bearing history and preparation

Repeated/shifted bearings narrow the dawn-search sectors. Search crews are tasked, selected attack units go to higher readiness, submarine sectors receive a broad vector, and a possible night light-force mission can start preparation.

Nothing at this stage is weapon-quality.

### 0530 onward — cue-assisted dawn search

The broad-search backbone remains legacy long-range aircraft and mature E13A/F1M/flying-boat practice. D4Y and the tiny new Zuiun layer are not used as magic ocean-search multipliers. Their value is to revisit a suspicious sector quickly.

Centered event-level useful search effort is about 7-10 dawn sorties across the relevant southern search ring after maintenance/fatigue/other duties. One or two faster confirmation sorties are plausible where the local D4Y/Zuiun detachment is actually ready.

### 0610-0710 — first firm sensor contact

A long-range Japanese scout visually acquires **one US carrier-cover formation** in the Gilbert/southern-Marshall operating area and gets a short report out before CAP can completely erase the contact.

The report is good enough for something like:

- carrier task group;
- multiple flight decks probable;
- heavy escorts present;
- approximate position/course.

It is **not** good enough for:

- exact named carriers;
- the entire ten-fast-deck disposition;
- exact assault-convoy location;
- a strike solution valid several hours later.

### 0645-0815 — partial recontact

A confirmation/re-search attempt refreshes the carrier contact. If the local Zuiun/D4Y pair is serviceable, this is the first place the new aircraft matter materially: not by finding the US fleet from nothing, but by reducing contact age after the broad-search aircraft has already found something.

US CAP/radar pressure prevents clean continuous shadowing. Centered operational-command contact age after confirmation remains about 20-50 minutes with roughly 20-40 nmi-class position uncertainty after relay and target motion.

That is a **partial operational track**, not weapon-quality.

## 5. What each side knows by roughly 0800

Japan now knows a major carrier-covered US operation is physically in the battle space, and at least one carrier formation has been seen. The pre-existing amphibious inference becomes very strong.

Japan still does not know whether the sighted group is the main target, a covering/interceptor group, where the other carrier groups are, or exactly where the assault convoy is.

The US probably knows Japanese reconnaissance is active because radar/CAP will detect or chase at least one scout or confirming aircraft. It does not know how much of the sighting was transmitted or whether the Japanese combined it with passive bearing history.

The Japanese four-carrier main body therefore **still does not receive an automatic GO** from this contact alone.
