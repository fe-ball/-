## General attribution rule

Do not infer intrinsic weapon/troop quality directly from historical outcomes where COMINT, logistics, radar/CIC, rescue, repair, basing, escort availability, route rigidity or replacement systems were major causal stages. Correct the causal box first; do not simply buff Japan.

## Aviation

Do not use historical production + extra survivors = automatic larger inventory. Separate production capacity from actual orders. Lower losses can temporarily thicken inventory, then reduce/reallocate orders; Branch also has more surviving carrier decks and South-Pacific base establishments that create genuine demand. Reclose airframes -> engines -> spares -> maintainers -> pilots -> fuel/training hours -> authorized frontline slots before using a total-aircraft figure.

Historical Japan was still producing large numbers of aircraft in early 1944; the sharp material/engine quality collapse is more a mid/late-1944 phenomenon. Branch may delay the poor-lot tail where material/shipping conditions justify it, but cannot ignore aluminum, copper, engine or training bottlenecks.

## South-Pacific bases

Port Moresby, Espiritu Santo, New Caledonia/Tontouta and Lunga/Tulagi already pay explicit maintenance/shipping costs. Do not add a second vague logistics tax. Their benefits include denial of Allied platforms, repair/ferry/recon hub functions, and temporary aircraft transfer options. New Caledonia is particularly a quasi-rear air/repair hub by 1944 rather than only a combat strip.

## Materials

New Caledonia nickel/chromite is not a flat weapon-performance bonus. Use a Priority War-Spec Floor: high-priority military production can more often hold intended 1943 Japanese wartime specifications, with lower reject/failure tails and better life, while economy-wide scarcity remains.

## Merchant shipping / tankers / oil

Historical Japanese late-war standard-ship productivity must not receive a second blanket Branch productivity multiplier. Branch gains are earlier standardization, fewer bad compromises, better conventional-ship survival and higher effective ship-days.

Working 1944-02 bands remain provisional: merchant tanker afloat about 0.88-0.93m GRT, serviceable about 0.82-0.87m; non-tanker steel >500GRT afloat about 4.65-5.00m GRT. These are audit bands, not locked canon.

NEI remains overwhelmingly the main oil source. Synthetic/shale/coal-liquefaction/bio are shock absorbers. More tankers do not automatically mean more Home Islands inventory because wide forward geography consumes tanker-days. Model tonnage x distance x cycle time and direct forward deliveries.

## Naval construction

High-grade DD build-time improvements are modest; benefits lean toward reliability/rework/yard-days. Fleet-DD advantage in early 1944 comes mainly from avoided historical Solomon losses plus escort-role separation, not mass newbuilding.

Kaibokan quantity difference is modest; availability/doctrine matter. Conventional submarine strength advantage likewise comes primarily from survival/operability and patrol-days, with the six first-generation high-speed-submerged boats as a separate capability. Do not mass-increase normal I-boats without a hull ledger.

## Damage control / torpedo adjudication

Type-95 torpedo Branch improvement is straight-running/depth/gyro/lot variance/reduced dud, not a default larger warhead. A hit triggers a real loss/mission-kill draw. US damage control improves survival odds but consumes escorts, tugs, repair ships, forward repair slots, transit time and yard capacity.

For every major hit resolve: immediate survival -> 6-48h battlefield escape -> finite forward repair -> permanent repair/return.
