# Dodecanese planned withdrawal — Branch B Nov 1943 v001

status: AUTHORITATIVE_RETROACTIVE_ALLIED_SETTLEMENT

## Decision

Kos falls 1943-10-03/04. Britain does not instantly abandon Leros: the early historical gamble is still taken and the October losses already incurred remain. By 1943-11-01..03, however, the lost fighter-base geometry, falling value of holding Leros without Rhodes/Kos, accumulated cruiser/destroyer damage and reduced Royal Navy reserve elasticity produce a planned evacuation decision.

The Bengal offensive is only a weak background warning at the decision date, **not** the primary cause. London cannot yet know that Chittagong will fall. Do not back-port later Bengal knowledge into this decision.

## Execution center

- Reinforcements scheduled for roughly 10/31-11/07 are mostly cancelled; Leros British strength at evacuation start ~3,000-3,300.
- 11/02-03: wounded, signals/radar/naval-base/air personnel and nonessential headquarters begin leaving by small craft.
- Samos British/Greek combat elements are pulled toward the Turkish coast early.
- 11/03-07: distributed night evacuation. Destroyers are used primarily as fast lifts/cover while small sailing craft, landing craft and local vessels do much of the movement.
- 11/07-09: demolition of port fuel, torpedo facilities, ammunition, communications, repair assets and fixed guns while rearguards remain.
- ~11/09-11: German advance/occupation proceeds against only residual resistance rather than the historical four-day main battle.

## Working outcome

British Leros evacuation: ~2,700-3,000 saved; total death/missing/capture ~200-350.
Italian Leros organized evacuation: ~3,200-3,800 plus later independent small-craft escape; ~1,800-2,500 remain/captured center.
Small craft: ~6-10 lost, ~3-6 heavily damaged.
No centered new British destroyer loss during the evacuation.

The later historical Rockwood damage and Dulverton sinking are avoided **in this theater/event chain**. They are not guaranteed to survive subsequent war service.

German benefit: most historical Leros battle casualties are avoided, landing craft and air effort are released earlier, and islands are secured earlier. Ground troops may remain committed to Aegean garrison/occupation; only the aviation/transport component is readily movable on short notice.

## Accounting interpretation

Do not call the saved British ships/personnel automatically 'free'. They re-enter the global Royal Navy/Army ledger and may be committed elsewhere. The correct effect is prevention of an additional negative balance, not creation ex nihilo of reserve strength.
