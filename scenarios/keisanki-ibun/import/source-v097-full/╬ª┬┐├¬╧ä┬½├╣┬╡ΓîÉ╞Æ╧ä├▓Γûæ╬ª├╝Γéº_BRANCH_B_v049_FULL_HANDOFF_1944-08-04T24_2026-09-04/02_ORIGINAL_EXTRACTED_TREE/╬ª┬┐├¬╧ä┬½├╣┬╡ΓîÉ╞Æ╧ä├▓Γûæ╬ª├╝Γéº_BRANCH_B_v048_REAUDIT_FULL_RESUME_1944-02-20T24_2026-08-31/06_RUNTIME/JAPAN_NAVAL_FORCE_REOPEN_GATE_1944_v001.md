## Carrier roster correction
After Taiho completion, physical Japanese carriers = **11**: established core Shokaku, Zuikaku, Hiryu, Soryu; Taiho; second-line Junyo, Hiyo, Ryuho, Ryujo, Zuiho, Shoho. Hull existence, hull availability, air-group existence, carrier qualification, integrated strike readiness and sortie capacity must remain separate.

The branch surface fleet is also materially healthier than historical because major Solomon attrition did not occur on the historical scale. Prior working bands retained for reopening: 11 battleships existing, ~18 heavy cruisers center, fleet-type DD ~55–62 with ~45–52 normally ready in spring-1944 order bands. Exact operation allocation must be rebuilt before FORAGER.

## I-400 system
I-400: Jan-1944 commissioning, 3-Seiran integrated combat capability Mar-Apr. I-401: Apr commissioning, joint six-aircraft readiness May-Jun. No first combat sortie is yet canonical. Rational early gate remains late-May/early-Jun only if a high-value fixed target (AO/AE/AR/fuel/ammunition/transport concentration) exists and 5/6+ aircraft are ready.

## 1944 carrier recovery horizon
Do not treat a Marianas carrier loss as automatically terminal, and do not treat replacement hulls as automatic combat power. Branch center has Unryu and Amagi completing/work-up in summer and Katsuragi later in 1944. By year-end, physical carrier hull count can recover substantially if summer losses are moderate. The critical separate ledgers are: hull loss, air group loss, carrier-qualified pilot/observer loss, deck crew/work-up, spares and escorts.

This changes rational risk: Japan can accept some hull attrition if it preserves trained carrier aviation and inflicts meaningful US operational losses; catastrophic aircrew destruction remains far harder to regenerate than hull count.

## Indian Ocean interaction
Eastern Fleet is weaker than historical in this branch: Saratoga CV-3 was lost at branch Midway; Formidable and Indomitable were lost in Operation C. Historical 1944 Sabang/Surabaya carrier pattern therefore cannot be copied. Any Japanese Indian Ocean option competes with Central Pacific carrier/surface/submarine posture and tanker/escort capacity. Normandy/Home Fleet reinforcement timing is likewise a finite-allocation question.
