## 1. Method
Historical fact, branch capability, working estimate, realized central outcome are kept separate. No historical Komandorski/LANDCRAB result is copied automatically.

## 2. Komandorski / March resupply
Historical convoy already had two fast transports, Asaka Maru and Sakito Maru. The historical operational defect was the ~10kt Sanko Maru mixed with ~19–20kt cargo liners, plus weather-driven cohesion/rendezvous consequences.

Branch central composition:
- cover: Nachi, Maya, Tama; Abukuma; Wakaba, Hatsushimo, Ikazuchi, Inazuma, Usugumo in main body;
- transports: Asaka Maru, Sakito Maru, plus one surviving large fast cargo vessel of Higashiyama Maru performance class;
- Sanko Maru is not in the main convoy and is probably not sent.

Planned convoy speed 15.5–16kt; bad weather 12–14kt while remaining cohesive. Branch navigation/machinery improvement affects probability of cohesion and mission completion, not magic top speed.

US/Japanese surface contact remains possible, but exact contact probability is only working 30–45%. Central realized outcome: **no historical Komandorski surface battle**. Reduced ULTRA cueing plus different convoy timing/geometry prevents the historical interception. Convoy reaches Attu 27–28 March.

## 3. Attu cargo and April continuation
March cargo includes roughly 550 reinforcements including Yamasaki command elements and high-priority command/signals, infantry ammunition, AA ready ammunition, food/medicine, batteries/spares, cold-weather gear, engineer tools/explosives and selected construction stores. Approximately two Daihatsu/larger craft plus possibly 1–2 small craft, nets/tackle/winches remain locally.

April central sequence:
- 2 Apr Usugumo-type fast mission aborts in severe weather;
- 7–9 Apr second fast mission succeeds;
- ~16–20 Apr I-35 supply succeeds;
- 21 Apr–4 May I-7 sequence succeeds;
- after late April surface fast transport becomes more cautious.

Additional high-value cargo to early May: 90–130t central, 150–200t upside if one further DD run occurs. Troop increase is small.

## 4. Attu May state
10/11 May present strength central 3,150–3,220; combat-effective 2,950–3,050. Yamasaki has had roughly six additional weeks to organize fallback positions, distributed dumps, fire plans, drainage, dry shelters and communications.

Weapons are improved in serviceability, survey, registration, propellant/temperature correction and ammunition discipline, not in magic ballistic performance. Central AA basis remains roughly 16 Type 88 75mm + ~10 20mm, 5–6 serviceable mountain guns, Type 92 70mm/Type 94 37mm elements; no tanks central.

Early warning: one operational station during April; second limited/test around early May. No fire-control radar.

## 5. Waterplanes
1 May central inventory:
- Attu: A6M2-N 4, F1M2 1, E13A1 1; 4–5 flyable;
- Kiska: A6M2-N 5, F1M2 3, E13A1 2; 5–6 flyable;
- total 16, about 10–11 flyable.

No Kyofu at Attu/Kiska central. Attu local waterplane base collapses on 11 May; surviving aircraft retreat/ferry toward Kiska. Kimikawa reinforcement is rerouted toward Kiska, yielding ~17–19 aircraft / 12–14 ready around 14 May, declining through the month.

## 6. LANDCRAB and ground trajectory
US D-Day remains 11 May central. US pre-invasion estimate roughly 2,600–2,900; actual 3,150–3,220. Japanese defense emphasizes ridges/passes rather than beach death positions. Historical reserve 4th Infantry is committed about 1–2 days earlier; Brown relief around 17 May. North/South link delayed to 20–21 May.

Without rescue, final resistance would extend into early June.

## 7. Yamamoto survival and limited rescue
Operation Vengeance does not kill Yamamoto on 18 Apr. Precise high-level itinerary extraction from JN-25 and the historical Guadalcanal/P-38 geometry are both broken. Combined Fleet remains under Yamamoto in May–June.

Strategic rescue window: ~20–29 May, best information 23–27 May. Northern relief uses secondary carriers and Fifth Fleet assets rather than Shokaku/Zuikaku/Hiryu/Soryu:
- Junyo, Hiyo, Ryujo, Zuiho;
- Kongo, Haruna;
- Tone, Chikuma;
- Fifth Fleet cruisers/DD.

Mission is to displace US support long enough for one fast extraction, not annihilate the fleet. Central 26 May afternoon US receives a short contact/warning of a major Japanese carrier force west of the Aleutians and shifts old-BB/CA support away rather than accept carrier air risk.

26–27 May night: Abukuma + ~6 DD make one large run.
- inbound high-value supplies: 80–110t;
- evacuated: ~800–1,000, mostly 450–550 wounded/sick plus specialists/some troops;
- Yamasaki remains.

No second large run: US adaptation makes repetition unattractive.

## 8. Outcome
Attu final organized resistance ends 3–5 June, central 4 June.
Working losses:
- US: 650–800 KIA, 1,300–1,600 wounded, plus 2,200–2,700 cold/disease cases;
- Japan: 2,050–2,300 killed, ~800–1,000 evacuated.

Kiska evacuation decision formalized ~29–30 May. Submarines remove selected personnel; later Fifth Fleet CL/DD evacuation is executed around 27–28 July and succeeds at roughly 5,000-person scale. Secondary carriers are released after the Attu intervention and are not permanently tied north.

## 9. Strategic handback
Aleutians consume real fuel, maintenance and secondary-carrier/DD availability, but not the first-line carrier core. The result removes the need to downsize the subsequent Efate force for a long northern commitment. The correct lesson is objective discipline: one displacement operation + one extraction, not repeated rescue runs.
