## Strategic forecast
A US Central Pacific amphibious offensive in late 1943 is plausible-to-likely as Essex/Independence-class carrier groups and F6F capability mature. The exact first objective is not fixed. Gilberts/Marshalls are candidates because they create an approach toward the Marshalls and onward Central Pacific bases, but Midway/Wake and the branch South Pacific geography alter staging and deception.

## Mandatory planning factors
- Historical Efate/Espiritu Santo staging cannot be copied because Espiritu Santo is Japanese-held and New Caledonia is under Japanese military control.
- Midway and Wake are observation/toll nodes, not impassable walls. US can route around them at infrastructure, escort, AO, patrol and concealment cost.
- US branch carrier losses remain paid; new-build carriers add strength without resurrecting old hulls.
- Japanese submarines must be adjudicated by actual late-1943 hull/readiness/on-station state and improved operability, not historical output defaults.
- Japanese land defense must include branch tank-maintenance/reappearance, Daihatsu/logistics, engineers, communications and pre-registered fortress gunnery where applicable.
- Water aviation and layered search/contact systems matter to detection/update but do not create omniscience.
- Japanese first-line carriers are not automatically committed merely because a US carrier raid is detected. A real amphibious/logistics signature should be a separate escalation gate.

## Open questions
- Does the US first suppress Midway/Wake, raid Marshalls, or create a southern deception before invasion?
- Which Gilbert/Marshall objectives are selected, and when?
- What Japanese aircraft/submarine/surface force is actually allocated after paying other theaters?
- Does Japan choose a fundamentally different late-summer/early-autumn strategy that changes the eventual US target or timetable?
