## 1. Core loss in Operation C

1942-04-05 Branch battle:
- Cornwall/Dorsetshire lost as historical.
- Indomitable and Formidable sunk.
- Warspite damaged, survives.
- Japanese carrier raid ends early after the fleet battle; the 4/09 Trincomalee/Hermes strike does not occur.
- Hermes, Vampire, Hollyhock, Athelstane and British Sergeant therefore survive that event chain.

Working personnel settlement for the two modern carriers:
- shipboard dead combined: ~280-480, center ~380;
- Fleet Air Arm dead/missing while airborne/operating separately: ~20-40;
- roughly 82-89% of the nominal combined ship companies survive, leaving more than two thousand experienced carrier personnel available for redistribution after recovery/medical filtering.

Personnel consequence: the 1942-43 British carrier crisis is principally a **large-flight-deck hull shortage**, not total loss of the carrier-skilled manpower base. Survivors become cadres for Illustrious, Victorious, Unicorn, escort carriers and training establishments.

## 2. Sequential operation audit

### Ironclad / Madagascar
Britain still GOes. Illustrious + surviving Hermes provide direct support. Serious distant carrier-vs-carrier cover is abandoned; withdrawal is planned if the Japanese main carrier force appears. Diego Suarez falls; no new capital loss. Historical 5/30 Ramillies damage/British Loyalty loss remains.

### Vigorous/Julius
Mediterranean priority still draws cruisers/destroyers from Eastern Fleet. Historical Nestor loss remains. Branch effect is thinner Eastern Fleet, not a new capital loss.

### Pedestal
Malta urgency makes GO unavoidable. Illustrious replaces sunk Indomitable; Eagle/Victorious/Illustrious are the main carrier set, Furious remains fighter ferry/local role. Eagle historical sinking remains.

Illustrious takes a Branch-centered heavy attack: two direct hits plus serious near misses; armored deck limits one hit, an aft lift/less-armored hit causes penetration, fire, electrical/fuel damage and local flooding. Damage control succeeds; carrier mission-killed but survives. Working deaths ~40-55, wounded ~55-80. Force turns away on the historical operational logic and Pedestal still succeeds.

Illustrious receives essential repair rather than full modernization: repair late 1942/early 1943, trials/work-up Jan, limited combat-ready around Feb 1943. Major modernization debt is deferred.

### TORCH
Still GO. Victorious is the sole fast fleet-cover carrier. Furious + small carriers provide local Oran/Algiers support. Italian capital ships do not exploit the gap because fuel/operational constraints remain severe. No Branch-specific additional capital loss. Avenger's historical 11/15 U-155 loss remains rationally possible and retained.

### Victorious US loan
Loan is delayed until Illustrious is back to limited combat readiness. Working Branch delay ~1.5-2 months (broadly compatible with the older 2-3 month planning band). The US demand is stronger than history because Branch US old-carrier losses are worse; Britain nevertheless cannot give up its only fast carrier earlier.

### Sicily
HUSKY remains GO. Branch does not copy the missing two-carrier historical screen with two substitutes. Illustrious is the principal fleet carrier, held more conservatively, with battleships and land-based air carrying much of the deterrence/cover burden. Italian battlefleet does not sortie. The historical Indomitable torpedo event is not copied onto Illustrious because posture/geometry differs.

### Salerno
Small carriers + Unicorn can provide much of the beachhead fighter role. Italian surrender removes the principal battlefleet threat. Historical Warspite guided-bomb heavy damage and Uganda damage remain; no new Branch fleet-carrier loss.

### Dodecanese
Early/mid-October historical losses are still paid. Planned early-Nov evacuation then avoids the later Leros endgame loss chain as described in `DODECANESE_WITHDRAWAL_BRANCH_B_1943-11_SETTLEMENT_v001.md`.

## 3. Carrier replacement response

- Unicorn historical 'complete first as operational carrier, full repair-ship role later' decision is reinforced. Branch may gain days/weeks, not a magical half-year.
- Illustrious is returned rapidly with modernization debt.
- Furious remains operationally useful longer.
- escort carriers and merchant-aircraft-carriers become more important because they free fleet carriers from convoy CAP/ASW/ferry/local support.
- US carrier starvation creates pressure to retain/delay some later Lend-Lease escort carriers, so historical British 1943 escort-carrier inflow is not automatically guaranteed.
- Nairana-type domestic conversions and MAC ships gain priority, but effects are mostly late 1943 onward.
- Implacable/Indefatigable can be prioritized, realistically gaining weeks/few months rather than becoming 1943 ships.
- no emergency cruiser/battleship fleet-carrier conversion is accepted.

## 4. Indian Ocean / escort burden

Do not encode '20-40 ships/month diverted' as if they were lost ships. Use ship-days and escort-days.

Working Branch-specific extra Indian Ocean escort/ASW requirement:
- 1942H2: ~5-8 escort/ASW hull-equivalents continuously;
- 1943H1: roughly ~3-6 as pressure/adaptation evolve;
- 1943H2 to Oct: roughly ~2-4 average before Bengal crisis retasking;
- cumulative order: ~56-100 escort-hull-months, center ~78, before later 1943 Bengal emergency adjustments.

Working consequence of extra operating tempo rather than scripted sinkings:
- additional permanent destroyer/escort loss expectation by late 1943: 0-2 hulls, center ~1, left unnamed unless a specific event is adjudicated;
- additional repair/maintenance debt at an autumn snapshot: roughly 1-2 hulls normally unavailable, occasionally ~3;
- raw destroyer/escort hull count can still be near historical or slightly higher because Hermes-group and late-Dodecanese historical losses are avoided, but freely assignable escort reserve is roughly 2-4 hulls worse than history.

This is the correct meaning of 'Royal Navy is worn down': not a collapsed fleet list, but reduced reserve elasticity and more ships already tasked or in maintenance.

## 5. Merchant shipping working audit

The old Branch-specific direct extra Indian Ocean loss estimate of only 20-40k GRT is probably too low if the retained heavy 1942H2 submarine patrol effort is kept; the old 80-130k GRT is likely too high after adaptation. Reopened working center for Japanese-caused Branch-specific 1942H2 extra direct loss is ~50-90k GRT.

Offset the 4/09 ships saved by Operation C ending early (Athelstane + British Sergeant roughly 11k GRT combined) when asking for **net Allied shipping difference**. A provisional net extra loss band around ~40-80k GRT is more coherent than either extreme, pending patrol-day-by-patrol-day comparison.

The larger operational tax remains waiting, diversion, convoying, escort and repair days rather than sunk tonnage.

## 6. Year-end 1943 interpretation

Britain has largely ended the Italian battlefleet problem, so heavy ships can again be sent east. But the fleet is asymmetrical:
- battleship/surface gun strength available for Eastern Fleet can be substantial;
- modern fleet-carrier strength is thin;
- escort destroyers are the tightest routine constraint.

Hence Britain can send an intimidating Eastern Fleet without wanting a symmetric attrition battle. An old British battleship exchanged for a Japanese old battleship may be tolerable under compelling conditions; losing Illustrious for Fuso/Yamashiro is an unacceptable trade; repeated DD-for-DD exchanges are also unattractive because British destroyers have global convoy value.
