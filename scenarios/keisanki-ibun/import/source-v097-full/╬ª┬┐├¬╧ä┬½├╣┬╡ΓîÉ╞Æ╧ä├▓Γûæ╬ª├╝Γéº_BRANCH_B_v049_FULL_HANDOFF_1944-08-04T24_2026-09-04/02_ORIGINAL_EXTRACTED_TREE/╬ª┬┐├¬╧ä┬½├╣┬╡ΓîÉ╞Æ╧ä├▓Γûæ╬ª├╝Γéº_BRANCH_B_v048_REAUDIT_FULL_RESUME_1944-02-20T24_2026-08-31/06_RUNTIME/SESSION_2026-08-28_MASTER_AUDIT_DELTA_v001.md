- Technology is a card/dependency deck, not a one-dimensional national score. Foreign data can have nonlinear value when it fills a missing card in a strong domestic environment; conversely impressive drawings can remain inert when prerequisites or production/material cards are absent.

## C. High-level COMINT / Type 3A

## D. Aviation pool — demand feedback
- Do not compute Branch air strength as historical production + extra survivors. Production orders respond to loss forecast, backlog, unit establishments, target reserve, new carrier/base requirements and existing surplus.
- Lower losses can create a temporary inventory bulge then reduce/reallocate production; surviving four first-line carriers plus Midway/Port Moresby/Santo/New Caledonia and other bases also create genuinely larger establishment demand.
- Full chain for 1944 adjudication: airframes -> engines -> spares -> maintainers -> pilots -> fuel/training hours -> front-line slots.
- Any previous precise Feb-1944 total-aircraft band not supported by this endogenous model is demoted until rebuilt.

## F. Materials — priority war-spec floor, not magic alloy bonus
- New Caledonia Ni/Cr backhaul is meaningful and rides already-paid return shipping where possible. Earlier `20-40 t Ni` values were special research allocations, not total economy.
- Historical Japan was already alloy-constrained in 1943. Branch does not restore rich prewar/Western alloy conditions; it more reliably meets the intended Japanese 1943 wartime specification for high-priority lots, reducing reject/failure/life-tail deterioration.
- Working Feb-1944 material states: Ni priority GREEN/economy YELLOW; Cr GREEN to GREEN-YELLOW; W YELLOW; Mo YELLOW-RED; Co RED; Al/bauxite GREEN-YELLOW; Cu YELLOW-RED and a Branch-specific concern because computing/radar/radio/wiring demand is higher; rubber GREEN; salt GREEN; food YELLOW; oil logistics-limited YELLOW.

## G. Merchant shipping / shipbuilding
- Historical 1943-44 Japanese shipbuilding already improved; do not stack Branch process improvement as a flat multiplier on top of historical late-war standard-ship productivity.
- High-grade DD build times only modestly improve; payoff is reliability, rework and yard-day reduction. Kaibokan difference is earlier doctrine/availability more than huge count inflation.
- Lower 1942-43 losses preserve many conventional merchant ships; this is more important than conjuring large extra standard-ship counts.
- Branch second-generation standard cargo ships should use standardization/block build without automatically copying the most extreme historical 2A/2D performance degradation. Wooden coastal/feeder shipping can free ocean steel hulls but is not added to >500-GRT ocean fleet.
- Tanker fleet early 1944 is somewhat healthier than history, not a giant surplus. Extra empire commitments consume tanker-days. Evaluate fuel logistics by tonnage x distance x cycle time.
- NEI remains overwhelmingly the main oil source; synthetic/shale/coal-liquefaction/bio are shock absorbers. Branch difference is mainly transport/delivery stability, not miraculous field output.

## H. Damage model correction
- Branch Type 95 torpedo Feb-1944 default warhead remains roughly historical Mod-1 scale (~405 kg); improved straight-running/depth/gyro/lot/fuze reliability, not automatically larger explosive power. Larger Mod-2 is not standard before its dated gate.
- A single torpedo hit is a major damage event. Do not auto-convert US hits to `campaign-out but survives` because of good damage control.
- Resolve four stages: (1) immediate 0.5-6h survival, (2) 6-48h battlefield escape, (3) finite forward emergency repair/tug/escort slots, (4) permanent repair and return over weeks/months.
- Saved ships consume tugs, DD escorts, repair ships, ASW, materials, dock slots and transit time.
- Working one-hit loss bands are sensitivity guides only: DD/DE 40-70% loss; CL 20-40%; CA 15-30%; CVL 20-40%; large CV 10-25%; BB 3-10%; AO/large tanker 25-50%, with much higher mission-kill probabilities. Hit location and prior damage dominate individual outcomes.
