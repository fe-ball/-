# Branch B GALVANIC settlement — 1943-11-23..12-06 v001

status: AUTHORITATIVE_CURRENT_BRANCH_SETTLEMENT

## Date and strategic frame

Branch D-Day is 1943-11-24, delayed from historical timing because old US carrier losses and Branch logistics/geography slow assembly. Japan holds Midway, Wake, Espiritu Santo and New Caledonia; Efate is US-held. Japanese first-line carriers remain Shokaku/Zuikaku/Hiryu/Soryu.

## Defense and Japanese commitment

Tarawa defenders ~5,300 total, combat ~4,500-4,700. Makin ~1,250. Japanese direct-war-zone submarine concentration is ~10-12 boats, broad ~14-17 peak. A light night surface group can nuisance-raid Makin. Hiei/Kirishima + cruiser/DD heavy group remains conditional and never receives GO. Yamato/Musashi/Nagato remain rearward.

## Realized sequence

- 11/23-24: light surface raid causes severe US destroyer damage possible; Japanese DD damaged.
- 11/24: landings.
- 11/24-25 night: Marshall air strike launches ~28-32, ~22-26 arrive, ~15-18 attack. Independence takes a torpedo and becomes campaign-out. Japanese air loss ~9-12.
- Liscome Bay is sunk by submarine attack; an APA/AKA is damaged; Japanese submarine one sunk, two damaged working.
- 11/25: reconnaissance identifies part of US carrier disposition; no decisive second night strike.
- 11/26: first-line four-carrier strike ~115 aircraft. Cowpens torpedoed campaign-out; Lexington bombed temporary/light-moderate damage; Yorktown near-missed. Japanese aircraft loss ~28-34. Japanese carriers withdraw immediately; US gets no carrier counterstrike on them.
- 11/27: US heavy raids against Mili/Maloelap; Makin fighting ends evening, US ground casualties ~550-750.
- 11/28: Tarawa fighting ends; US casualties ~3,300-3,800; Japanese garrison near annihilation.
- early Dec: direct Japanese submarine density falls toward ~6-7, broad ~12-14 as boats redeploy to supply-lane work. A working 12/02 event has a Japanese submarine torpedo a fleet oiler campaign-out but not sink it; attacker damaged.

## Settlement

US wins the Gilberts. It loses Liscome Bay; Independence/Cowpens are campaign-out and transports/escorts are damaged. Japanese first-line four-carrier core survives; Ryujo/Zuiho and the heavy surface group survive. No Kongo-class Tarawa bombardment is realized.

The US likely pauses/rebuilds but continues the Central Pacific campaign. 1944 Kwajalein timing is reopened around a Feb 7-12 center, not fixed fate.
