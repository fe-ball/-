## Operational concept and force

The main advance remains overland north along the west Burma/Arakan coast. Naval forces move heavy artillery, protect the sea LOC and conduct short front-line transplants tens of kilometers ahead of the existing advance. True assault landings are exceptional.

The main Army formation is 55th Division with engineers, mountain guns, communications and bounded theater transport reinforcement under Burma Area Army/direct Arakan command. The 54th Division transfer remains date-gated; only increasing advance/rear/logistics elements are available in mid/late December.

No independent tank regiment or centered combat-tank force is added. The corrected vehicle layer is primarily trucks, prime movers, light tractors, engineer plant, recovery/workshops and their crews. Physical land-motor pool is 430-560; start-serviceable/crewed/fueled is 355-465. The operation does not receive extra hulls, armor or gun energy.

## Naval and air allocation

Old-battleship rotation remains Fuso/Yamashiro and Ise/Hyuga. First-line carriers, Kongo-class fast battleships and the centered secondary-carrier pool remain Pacific/reserve assets; Bengal centered carrier commitment is zero.

Japanese 5th Air Division is leaned west but not wholly committed. Spitfire Mk V presence increasingly constrains reconnaissance and daylight movement. The historical 5-Dec Calcutta raid is still not copied. British 224 Group and incoming units displace north rather than being destroyed at Chittagong.

## Allied force accounting

The British field army is not annihilated. 5th and 26th Indian Divisions withdraw north. 7th Indian Division begins escape before Chittagong falls; about 7,700-9,100 men survive from a roughly 10,000-11,000 forward-area strength, with much heavy equipment abandoned and reequipment required.

The 81st West African Division main body remains comparatively fresh north of Chittagong for Hill Tracts/Feni defense. IV Corps 17th/20th/23rd Indian Divisions remain constrained by the intact Japanese 15th Army on the Chindwin threat axis.

## Vehicle and repair result

The corrected 24-72h reappearance band is 1.20-1.40x historical-equivalent only for reachable, repairable faults on the Cox's-Chakaria-Chiringa road axis. River crossings, mud, bridge demolition, Allied air interdiction and displaced workshops block the 1.6x mobile-formation ceiling.

Japanese old vehicle working loss 80-150 is replaced by:

- permanent destroyed/drowned/abandoned/writeoff: 55-95;
- temporary campaign mission-kill: 45-80;
- of those, returned inside 24-72h: 25-50;
- year-end long repair/cannibalization: 15-30.

The coastal supply spine remains 150-220t/day in the contested/night-heavy central case. Better vehicles reduce last-mile interruption but do not create new sea tonnage.

British/Commonwealth combat casualties: about 7,700-11,100, center about 9,300; KIA 1,700-2,350; wounded 4,500-6,100; POW/MIA 1,400-2,200. Disease/non-battle episodes remain 3,500-5,500.

Old battleships suffer no major combat damage.

British equipment losses: artillery/AT/AA 38-60; AFVs 5-10; vehicles 390-660; pack animals 900-1,550; river/coastal craft 20-40; aircraft 40-55 plus major infrastructure denial/loss.

## Year-end posture and limits

55th Division remains exhausted and pauses/reconstitutes, but relative to v001 has about 300-600 more effective personnel and 20-45 more dispatchable vehicles. Late-December Chittagong throughput is about 320-520t/day if repair and air conditions cooperate; Patenga regular fighter/recon presence remains 15-25.

Feni/Comilla/Agartala remains the British defensive and air-support arc. The earlier city entry and stronger Japanese regeneration harden the January front but do not authorize an immediate Feni drive.
