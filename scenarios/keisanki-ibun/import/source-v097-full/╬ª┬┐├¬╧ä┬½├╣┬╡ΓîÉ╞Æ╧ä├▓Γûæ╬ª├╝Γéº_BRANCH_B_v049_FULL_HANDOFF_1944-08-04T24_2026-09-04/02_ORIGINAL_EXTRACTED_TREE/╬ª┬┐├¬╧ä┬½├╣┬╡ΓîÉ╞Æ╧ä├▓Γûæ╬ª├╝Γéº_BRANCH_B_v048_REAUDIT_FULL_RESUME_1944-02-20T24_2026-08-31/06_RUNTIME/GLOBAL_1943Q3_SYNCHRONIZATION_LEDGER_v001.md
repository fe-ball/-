## 5. India/Burma / future Imphal difference
Historical U-Go/Imphal catastrophe is not current central line. Japan has not learned this from a Guadalcanal analogue; the deep-mountain supply failure is rejected from logistics analysis and observed Allied air-supply capability.

If historical Imphal battle never occurs, later British/Indian forces avoid much of the historical ~17,000 casualty bill and large emergency air-supply expenditure. However this does **not** make those divisions globally free: Japanese 15th Army also remains healthy, Bay of Bengal threat persists, and India/Burma becomes a stronger stare-down front.

Expected future distinction:
- Britain/India Army becomes healthier than historical;
- globally transferable divisions do not increase proportionally;
- transport-aircraft sorties, fuel, replacement drafts, medical/road/vehicle emergency expenditure are more likely to be released than whole infantry divisions;
- a historical-style Allied Burma counteroffensive cannot be assumed free because it no longer attacks a wrecked Japanese 15th Army.

## 6. Strategic state at Sep end
Japan has a temporary but narrowing window:
- Britain is still stretched from Mediterranean commitments and lost carrier depth;
- Hump/Assam is expanding but not yet a mature massive system;
- India coastal operation appears technically feasible at one-division-forward scale;
- US Pacific new-carrier pressure is rising rapidly.

Therefore waiting improves dry-season ground conditions but worsens naval freedom. The first plausible India execution window is late Oct–early Nov, subject to October Pacific and Bengal-air gates. No GO has yet been issued.
