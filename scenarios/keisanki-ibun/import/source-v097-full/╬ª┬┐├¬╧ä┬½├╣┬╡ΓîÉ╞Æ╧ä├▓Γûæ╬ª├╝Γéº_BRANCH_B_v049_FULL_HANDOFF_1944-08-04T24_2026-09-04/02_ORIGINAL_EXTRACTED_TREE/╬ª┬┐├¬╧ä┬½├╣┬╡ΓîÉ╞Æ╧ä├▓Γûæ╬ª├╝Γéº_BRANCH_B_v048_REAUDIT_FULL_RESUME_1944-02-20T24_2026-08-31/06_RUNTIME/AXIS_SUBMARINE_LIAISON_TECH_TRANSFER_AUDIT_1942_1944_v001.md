## 1. Core adjudication rule

Treat technology as a deck of interdependent cards, not a scalar national score. A German datum can have a nonlinear effect in Japan when Japanese prerequisite cards already exist; conversely a complete foreign design may sit idle if materials, manufacturing, control, training or doctrine cards are absent. Do not double-count a capability already present in current TECH: first reattribute causal support, then add only genuinely new capability.

For each transfer resolve: arrival -> unloading/translation -> measurement/comparison -> bench/field trial -> standardization -> production/retrofit -> operational use. A successful transport leg changes the next request list; later cargo becomes more specialized rather than simply duplicating the prior shipment.

## 2. I-30 branch correction

Branch centerline: I-30 completes the 1942 Europe mission and returns from Singapore to Japan instead of drawing the historical mine-loss outcome near Singapore. This is not a generic survival rule; it is a low-frequency local accident re-draw under better Branch navigation/key/port-interface conditions.

The important complete-return cards are:
- Würzburg-class precision tracking / servo / calibration / director-integration reference hardware;
- Metox-class metric radar-warning receiver and operating knowledge;
- Bold/Pillenwerfer-class acoustic deception samples;
- German torpedo/TDC/electric-torpedo comparison material;
- T-Enigma-class Japan-Germany communications hardware;
- optical/AA/fire-control and precision-tool samples.

Manifestation clock:
- late 1942: disassembly, measurement, comparison;
- 1943 Q1: bench prototypes / selected trials;
- 1943 Q2 onward: selected metric-RWR and acoustic-deception issue; fixed-base precision-radar work begins to become operationally meaningful;
- 1943 H2: doctrine/field-service/search-tasking effects matter in selected units; no centimetric RWR and no US-style CIC parity.

## 3. U-180 <-> I-29 first 1943 exchange

Historical physical exchange remains broadly successful. Branch difference: I-30 cargo is not being replaced. The German-to-Japanese package therefore becomes a second-pass answer set: IXC/40 drawings remain useful, but servo tolerances, calibration/test information, Type-IX mounting/noise data, electric-torpedo production details and related compact technical documentation have higher marginal priority.

Priority documents/small parts can move onward from Singapore before I-29 itself reaches Kure; use late May-June 1943 as the earliest research input window. Do not turn this into another complete radar set or a fleetwide immediate equipment change.

Japanese-to-German exchange does not become a free transfer of Type-2/Type-3 cryptanalytic computing. General process/measurement reliability knowledge may leak through normal technical contacts; high-grade cryptanalytic architecture, traffic-correlation system design and central machine layouts remain restricted.

## 4. U-511 -> RO-500

U-511/RO-500 arrival is historical and therefore not a new Branch transport success. The Branch difference is absorption efficiency because Japan already has first-generation high-speed-submerged boats, self-noise work and computational production management.

High-value cards:
- welded pressure-hull practice and distortion control;
- prefabricated/section construction;
- machinery mounting and vibration isolation;
- layout/maintenance/accessibility;
- German production tolerances and inspection practice;
- reference acoustic/noise measurements.

Low-value / non-transfer cards:
- the concept of high underwater speed (Japan already has it);
- a wholesale Type-IX copy as the next Japanese submarine.

Ko-1..6 receive zero retroactive structural benefit. Ko-7/8 may receive late fitting, mounting, piping, vibration-isolation and test-procedure changes only if construction state permits. Full structural/design integration belongs mainly to 1944 design work and 1945 combat availability.

RO-500 is also a controlled foreign acoustic target for Japanese ASW training. This can improve both own-sub quieting diagnosis and escort sonar training after data accumulation; it is not an instant sonar-range multiplier.

## 5. I-8

I-8 historical round trip remains successful. Branch effect is cargo/personnel specialization after RO-500 initial evaluation and I-30/Würzburg/Metox work. The return mission should emphasize technicians and measurement/maintenance knowledge in acoustics, radar calibration/electronics and associated test equipment rather than duplicate complete equipment already understood.

I-8 returns to Japan 1943-12-21. Therefore at the current 1944-01-01 year-end descendant only unloading, translation, first briefing and experimental assignment are immediate. Mass hardware effects do not back-port into 1943. By Feb 1944, some calibration, operating procedure, submarine/ASW testing and fixed-site experimentation may have moved; widespread new hardware has not.

## 6. I-34 / RO-501 / I-29 / I-52 future gates

I-34's historical Penang-area Ultra-directed ambush is not inherited because the Branch high-grade Japanese communications environment differs. This does NOT adjudicate successful arrival in Europe or successful return. I-34 remains a future/ongoing gate at the appropriate date.

If I-34 reaches Europe, successful preceding exchanges allow second/third-pass cargo specialization (e.g. then-current ESM, submarine manufacturing/acoustic documentation, measuring gear). No Naxos, Type-XXI, schnorchel or later hardware is back-ported before actual physical/document arrival dates.

RO-501 transfer project may still occur; outcome and arrival are unresolved. Its special value, if successful, is production documentation + trained Japanese crew, not merely a second Type-IX hull. Structural effects belong mainly to later designs.

I-29: Europe-bound mission and its later Singapore arrival/physical-hardware return are separate gates. At Singapore, personnel/documents may be detached and sent onward independently. Jumo/HWK or other large hardware only enters Japanese physical research if actually delivered. Do not decide the post-Singapore fate before the 1944 operational line reaches it.

I-52 remains fully unresolved. Its resource cargo could matter more to German priority allocations than earlier small cargoes, but no effect exists until actual arrival is adjudicated.

## 7. German-side effects

Do not count I-30 return as a German-side new success: I-30's outward leg already historically reached Europe. Same logic for other historically successful outward legs.

Resource cargo is small relative to the German macro economy but can have high marginal value in tungsten/molybdenum/tooling/special-alloy or special rubber uses. Resolve resource tons -> allocation -> industrial or ammunition output -> actual battle exposure before converting to casualties. Do not apply a generic German production bonus.

Japanese Type-91 aerial-torpedo information historically reaches Germany; Branch difference may be better test/tolerance/failure-statistics packaging. Potential effect is limited 1943+ improvement to German aerial-torpedo testing/operating envelope or selected existing torpedo practice, gated by German aircraft, organizational, training and production constraints. This is a local Allied loss-risk modifier, not a guaranteed strategic change.

Type-95 information later has less transformative value because Germany already has a mature and differently optimized submarine-torpedo family. Treat as partial comparison data, not a German oxygen-torpedo revolution.

## 8. Death-difference ledger rule to Normandy

Until 1944-06-06, Axis-transfer casualty effects are event-ledger items, not a fixed accumulated number. Keep:
- JP aerial-torpedo data -> GER: SMALL POSITIVE Allied loss-risk modifier from 1943 H1 where a real German aerial-torpedo engagement occurs;

- Japanese computing/cryptanalytic architecture: no transfer in base line.
