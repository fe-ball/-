# Bengal / Arakan / Chittagong campaign settlement 1943-10-28..12-31 v001

status: AUTHORITATIVE_CURRENT_BRANCH_SETTLEMENT

## Operational concept

Main advance is overland north along the west Burma/Arakan coast. Naval forces move heavy artillery, protect the sea LOC and conduct short front-line transplants tens of kilometers ahead of the existing advance. True assault landings are exceptional, not the default method.

Main Army formation: 55th Division with engineers, mountain guns and communications reinforcement. Burma Area Army/direct Arakan command, not 15th Army/Mutaguchi. 54th Division transfer from Java is not instant; only increasing advance/rear/logistics elements are available by mid/late December.

## Naval allocation

Old battleship rotation: Fuso/Yamashiro and Ise/Hyuga. Ise/Hyuga were not converted to hybrids in Branch B. Role: shore bombardment, heavy convoy cover/deterrence and moving artillery effect. First-line carriers Shokaku/Zuikaku/Hiryu/Soryu and Kongo-class fast battleships stay Pacific.

Local cruiser/transport assets include Ashigara backbone, Kuma and Oi/Kitakami according to their actual Singapore/Andaman/Nicobar clocks. Kinu is not stripped during substantial refit. Myoko/Haguro remain Pacific. Nisshin survives the Branch and is a possible fast heavy transplant carrier when available.

## Milestones

- 10/28 operation begins; Naf crossing succeeds; Gate A ~10/30.
- ~11/05 Teknaf captured.
- 11/14..15 Ukhia captured.
- 11/21 Cox's Bazar captured; Gate B GREEN. British 5th/26th withdraw rather than collapse.
- 11/21..30 logistics conversion and northward advance via Chakaria/Chiringa.
- 12/03 attack Dohazari begins.
- 12/03..04 night reinforced-battalion short coastal insertion north of Sangu/Banshkhali; ~200-300 Japanese casualties and craft loss/damage, operationally succeeds.
- evening 12/05..early 12/06 Dohazari captured; Arakan LOC root severed.
- 12/06..08 Patiya/Anwara advance; first serious anti-port fixed-target battleship bombardment; Chittagong normal port use heavily constrained.
- 12/09..10 British final evacuation/demolition preparation; 243 Wing/IAF 8 Sqn reception is displaced north rather than destroyed at Chittagong.
- 12/11..12 Kalurghat bridge demolished after rear guards cross.
- 12/12 Japanese reach south bank and reject frontal bridge seizure; upstream boat crossing begins.
- 12/12..13 crossing costs ~350-500 Japanese casualties and 6-10 craft lost/damaged; bridgehead survives.
- 12/14 small Patenga-side landing under one battalion accelerates withdrawal.
- afternoon/evening 12/15 Japanese enter port/city: CHITTAGONG CAPTURED, Gate C GREEN.

## Allied force accounting

Do not spawn four fresh divisions. XV Corps transition is messy:
- 26th Indian Division still held in theater longer than historical by Branch attack.
- 5th and 7th Indian Divisions are active in the transition/fighting.
- 81st West African Division main body was only beginning its historical Dec deployment; Japanese speed prevents the historical Kaladan commitment from being copied. It remains comparatively fresh north of Chittagong for Hill Tracts/Feni defense and receives escapees.
- IV Corps 17th/20th/23rd Indian Divisions are not free westward because Japanese 15th Army remains intact on the Chindwin threat axis.

7th Indian Division begins escape before Chittagong falls. Working center: ~8,000-9,500 men saved from ~10-11k forward-area strength, but much heavy equipment abandoned and reequipment required.

## Air

Japanese 5th Air Division is leaned west, not wholly committed. Spitfire Mk V appearance from Nov increasingly constrains Japanese reconnaissance/air work. Historical 12/05 Calcutta raid is **not** copied: concentrated Japanese effort is redirected toward the active Chittagong/Karnaphuli front.

British 224 Group and incoming wings/squadrons are displaced to Feni/Comilla rather than destroyed. British air capability remains alive; its local density around Chittagong is temporarily reduced during the decisive days.

## Casualty/equipment working bands through 12/31

Japanese combat casualties: ~5,800-8,300, center ~6,900; KIA/MIA ~1,700-2,400. Disease episodes ~4,500-7,000 with ~1,200-1,800 still hospital/rest at year-end.

British/Commonwealth combat casualties: ~7,200-10,500, center ~8,700; KIA ~1,500-2,100; wounded ~4,200-5,500; POW/MIA ~1,200-2,000. Disease/non-battle episodes ~3,500-5,500.

Japanese equipment working losses: artillery 8-15; vehicles 80-150; landing craft 20-30 lost +15-25 heavily damaged; small coastal ships 3-6; destroyers no sink but 2-3 temporarily damaged; aircraft ~45-60 writeoff. Old battleships suffer no major combat damage.

British equipment working losses: artillery/AT/AA total ~35-55; AFVs 5-10; vehicles ~350-600; pack animals 800-1,500; river/coastal craft 20-40; aircraft ~40-55 plus major infrastructure denial/loss.

## Year-end posture

Japanese 55th Division pauses/reconstitutes. Chittagong repair and sea LOC are the priority. Feni/Comilla/Agartala becomes the British air/ground defensive arc. No immediate realized 1944 drive beyond Chittagong is inherited.
