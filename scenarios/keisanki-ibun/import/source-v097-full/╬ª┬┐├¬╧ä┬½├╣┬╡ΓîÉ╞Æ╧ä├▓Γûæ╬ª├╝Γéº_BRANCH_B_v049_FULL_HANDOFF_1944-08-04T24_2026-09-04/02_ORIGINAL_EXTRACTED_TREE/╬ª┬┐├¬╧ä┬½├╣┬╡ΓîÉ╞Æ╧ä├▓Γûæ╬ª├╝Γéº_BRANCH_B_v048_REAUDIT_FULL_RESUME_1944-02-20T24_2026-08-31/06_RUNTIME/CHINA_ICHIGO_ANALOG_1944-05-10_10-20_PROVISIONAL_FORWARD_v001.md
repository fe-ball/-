This file preserves the post-v019 chat adjudication. It is not erased by the Pacific rollback. Exact air/logistics density and some dates may receive targeted retro-adjustment after Hump/Indian Ocean/Pacific closure.

## Hunan launch and Changsha
- launch: 1944-05-10 05:00
- first echelon six division-equivalents; staged ten-division D-Day package remains
- Miluo by 5/15; Changsha main city/Yuelu positions fall by 5/26; 5/27 current after Changsha
- Chinese 4th Army withdraws earlier and preserves most manpower but loses disproportionate heavy equipment
- logistics modeled as node network, not monotonic distance decay: Changsha/Xiangtan/Zhuzhou/Hengshan capture can raise effective throughput after disruption

## Hengyang
- airfield physically occupied 6/8-9 but unusable until city artillery threat removed
- methodical siege begins 6/13 after stock/engineer/artillery preparation
- attack cycles 6/13-17, 6/21-24, 6/28-30, 7/5-8, 7/13, 7/17-20
- 60th Division remains theater reserve and is never committed to Hengyang
- 64th Division plus short counterstrokes and Tank Regiment No.3 rapid packets restore outer isolation lines at July crises
- organized resistance ends ~1944-07-21 08:00; current 7/21 12:00
- fall is calendar-earlier mainly because the campaign reached/started earlier; siege duration itself is only modestly shorter than historical analog

## Node conversion and Phase C
- Hengyang becomes river/rail/air logistics node progressively after 7/21
- by late Jul node net inflow working band ~780–920 t/day; forward south stable ~520–650; short burst ~700–820 for 3–4 days
- 7/30 Phase C north-axis resumes
- Lingling falls 8/9 after Allied evacuation/demolition; not a Hengyang-style last stand
- Quanzhou 8/18-19, Xing'an 8/22; terrain creates real narrow-corridor delay

## Guilin
- large airfield complex evacuated/demolished before ground fall
- defense compromise: 31st Army core in city, 46th Army kept largely outside/south for mobility and Liuzhou
- Japanese attack cycles 9/5-8, 9/13-18, 9/23-29; terrain is strong but defense area is broad relative to density
- organized resistance ends ~1944-09-29 08:00; 31st Army partly escapes but is operationally broken

## Liuzhou
- 46th Army avoids full city entrapment; defense is outer-field delaying action plus planned withdrawal after demolition
- Japanese 11th Army from NE and 23rd Army from Wuzhou/Guiping axis create converging pressure
- Liuzhou airfield normal operation ends around 10/17 after evacuation/demolition
- main organized resistance ends ~1944-10-20 10:00; 46th Army saves roughly 65–75% manpower but loses a disproportionate share of artillery, vehicles, communications and heavy equipment
- Liuzhou creates potential junction between the long Wuhan-Hunan-Guilin line and the Guangdong-West River logistical system; junction functionality still requires repair and cannot be assumed instantaneous

## China-wide interpretation
Five-Go's Hanzhong-Guangyuan pressure is not a past tuition fee. It remains a chronic active tax on Sichuan divisions, fighters, POL, trucks, repair, communications and decision freedom. Chinese operational learning improves withdrawal/encirclement avoidance but is paid for by continued strategic constraint.
