## 1. Historical/logistical anchor

- HumpでKunmingへ物資を入れることは中国内輸送の終了ではない。米陸軍史では、Kunming以東のELOCがrail/road/river/coolie/animalを組み合わせた最終ボトルネックとされ、道路は粗悪、車両は旧式かつ不足していた。
- 1944年にはTIGAR 26-Aとして700 trucks + 2,000 tons spare parts + American drivers/maintenance personnelを中国へ空輸する計画が組まれた。これは車両・整備能力が高優先輸送資源であったことを示す。
- 同時代の四川道路記録では、主要道でも大河川をferryで渡る区間があり、路面・culvert・spring破損・工具/整備不足による故障が常態化していた。
- Fourteenth Air Force自身も1944年、中国戦域でbridges, trucks, river shipping, rail/road traffic, depotsを継続して攻撃しており、交通結節を航空阻止目標とすること自体は戦域の実態に合う。

## 2. What Hanzhong/Guangyuan can actually target besides airfields

### Tier A — highest operational payoff
1. **truck parks / repair garages / spare-parts concentrations**
   - scarce vehicles are more valuable than ordinary cargo tonnage.
   - destroying or long-repairing a truck removes repeated future lift, not one load only.
2. **POL transfer/storage outside major airfields**
   - road-side and city-edge transfer points, drums, pump/handling concentrations.
   - purpose is to force dispersion and repeated short-haul handling.
3. **ferries / bridge approaches / river-crossing equipment**
   - especially where a damaged crossing forces transshipment, queueing or a long detour.
4. **warehouse + transshipment nodes**
   - ammunition, aviation stores, tires, filters, engines, radio/repair items.
   - not indiscriminate city bombing.

### Tier B — persistent nuisance / corridor pressure
5. **Jiange–Zitong–Mianyang north-Sichuan route nodes**
   - immediate military supply line for forces covering the Guangyuan/Jianmen approaches.
6. **Mianyang / Chengdu peripheral road junctions and dispatch nodes**
   - road-control, convoy assembly, repair and redistribution rather than urban area bombing.
7. **Jialing / Fu / upper-Yangtze related river craft and loading points where identified**
   - low-volume targets individually, but useful when they are the alternative to a damaged road crossing.
8. **communications / traffic-control sites**
   - radio/telephone/dispatch offices and relay points when intelligence is good enough.

### Tier C — deep/occasional
9. **Chongqing-area river-port / warehouse / truck-transfer targets**
   - within light-bomber/heavy-fighter-class reach from the western system, but weather, distance, warning and escort reduce frequency.
   - use as occasional operational raid, not daily interdiction.
10. **Chengdu–Chongqing road movement**
   - valuable primarily at ferries, depots, truck concentrations and bottlenecks; road surface alone is low-payoff.

### Explicit low-payoff / do-not-overstate
- random bombing of road surface
- indiscriminate urban bombing as logistics accounting
- assuming every bridge hit remains closed for weeks
- claiming a strategic rail blockade of Chengdu
- continuous fighter-bomber road sweeps at European-theater density

## 4. Why the effect is nonlinear

A road network does not lose only the cargo physically destroyed.

Working identity:
`effective throughput ~ serviceable vehicle-days / turnaround time`

Illustrative center case:
- serviceability/availability falls 6%
- turnaround time rises 8% from night movement, detour, ferry queueing, dispersion and repeated handling
- effective throughput becomes roughly `0.94 / 1.08 = 0.87`
- to restore the original delivery rate requires roughly 15% more vehicle-days

Additional burdens appear as:
1. replacement of cargo physically destroyed
2. replacement/repair of trucks, tires, springs, engines, pumps, radio equipment
3. extra fuel and driver-hours for detours and lower average speed
4. transshipment at damaged crossings
5. dispersed smaller depots and repeated short-haul movement
6. night convoy organization, blackout, traffic control and higher accident rate
7. bridge/ferry/road repair labor and material
8. defensive CAP/AA/decoy/dispersal requirements
9. higher safety-stock requirement because arrival time becomes less predictable
10. evacuation/repositioning moves that move the same material twice

Therefore direct physical loss tonnage must not be used as the logistics tax.

## 5. Working transport effects — Apr/May 1944

These are bounded effects for the affected north-Sichuan/Chengdu military-air corridors, not all Free China transport.

### Physical attrition sensitivity per active month
- trucks irrecoverable / captured by fire loss: **12–25**
- trucks long-repair / temporarily unavailable: **25–45**
- ferries / river craft / crossing equipment destroyed or long-repair: **3–8**
- bridge/crossing closures causing >24 h meaningful disruption: **2–5 events**
- direct high-value cargo/POL destruction or abandonment: **50–110 t/month equivalent**

Do not convert these working bands into guaranteed event counts without daily adjudication.

### Throughput / cycle effect
- local strike-day throughput penalty at hit corridor: **10–25%**
- monthly average effective-capacity penalty across the affected north-Sichuan/Chengdu military-air network: **4–8%**
- required vehicle-days / driver-hours / local fuel to hold the same delivered output: **+10–18%**
- occasional severe week after a successful crossing/repair-node strike: **+15–25% vehicle-day requirement locally**

## 6. Hump and high-priority cargo consequence

v017 airfield/defense pressure already imposed 450–700 t/month of additional Hump/high-priority allocation burden.

Transport interdiction adds a separate but overlapping increment:
- truck/engine/tire/spare/tool replacement and emergency maintenance: **70–120 t/month**
- additional aviation/motor fuel attributable to dispersion, detour and replacement movement: **70–110 t/month**
- communications, pumps, repair equipment, specialist stores and emergency crossing material: **25–50 t/month**
- extra high-priority cargo caused by destroyed/abandoned stores and emergency redistribution: **30–70 t/month**
- gross incremental high-priority burden: **195–350 t/month**
- overlap/double-count adjustment with v017 airfield-dispersal tax: **15–50 t/month**
- net new increment: **180–320 t/month**

Revised combined Hanzhong/Guangyuan pressure tax:
- **650–1,000 t/month high-priority Hump-equivalent allocation**
- center working value: **~820 t/month**
- at ~11–12k t/month early-1944 Hump scale this is roughly **6–9% of gross lift**, but a larger share of discretionary aviation/maintenance cargo.

Not every bridge timber or road-stone ton is flown over the Hump. Local material, labor and requisition bear much of the physical repair. The Hump figure is specifically high-priority imported opportunity cost.

## 7. Cross-domain allocation effect

The transport tax is not paid only by Fourteenth Air Force.

It competes with:
- Hunan/eastern Fourteenth Air Force fuel/ammunition buildup
- Y-Force equipment and Salween/Burma air-support demands
- Matterhorn fuel/spares/airfield support
- north-Sichuan Chinese ground-force ammunition and motor movement
- China Theater truck modernization and spare-parts reserve
- emergency evacuation/redeployment if the eastern airfield situation deteriorates

Thus Hanzhong pressure can be strategically valuable even when direct aircraft exchange remains Allied-favorable.

## 8. Matterhorn and Fourteenth Air Force adjustment

Matterhorn is not stopped.

Revised no-pressure comparison:
- Matterhorn combat-stockpile growth: **~12–18% below no-pressure potential**, center ~15%
- one-day Fourteenth Air Force Hunan surge remains close to v017: **~90–95% of historical-potential instantaneous output**
- 3–5 day sustained Hunan output: retain **~80–85%** working band
- >5 day maximum concentration becomes harder because transport/repair stock and western CAP replacement cannot be deferred indefinitely

The main effect is endurance and recovery, not the number of aircraft physically capable of taking off on one chosen day.

## 9. Japanese cost and limits

This is not free interdiction.

Monthly western-air consumption rises within the already-fixed package:
- additional fuel and sortie-hour consumption from transport hunting: bounded inside v017 western aviation budget
- light bombers and reconnaissance aircraft accumulate fatigue and maintenance faster than in a passive-defense posture
- low-altitude truck/ferry attacks increase small-arms/AA exposure
- deep Chongqing raids have poor sortie-efficiency compared with Mianyang/Chengdu-corridor targets

Therefore the Japanese optimal pattern is selective intelligence-led strikes on nodes, not continuous road strafing.
