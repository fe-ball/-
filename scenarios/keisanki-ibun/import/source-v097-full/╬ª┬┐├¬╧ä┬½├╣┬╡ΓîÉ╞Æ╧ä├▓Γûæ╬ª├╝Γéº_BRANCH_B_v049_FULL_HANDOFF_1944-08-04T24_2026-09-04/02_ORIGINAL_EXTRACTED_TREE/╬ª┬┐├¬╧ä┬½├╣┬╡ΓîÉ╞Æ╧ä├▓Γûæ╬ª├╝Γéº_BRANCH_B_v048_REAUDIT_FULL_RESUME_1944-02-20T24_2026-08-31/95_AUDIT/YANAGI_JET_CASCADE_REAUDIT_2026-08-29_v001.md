## 1. Why the previous jet schedule needed reopening

The old chain mixed three different questions: domestic Japanese jet progress, information that can travel without the submarine hull reaching Japan, and bulky German hardware that physically requires a successful return. It also kept the legacy shorthand of a Mar-May 1944 "Ne-4 pure turbojet first flight" too close to a late-1943 short bench-run gate.

The re-audit therefore separates:
1. domestic Army and Navy jet lines;
2. Yanagi route survival and passenger/cargo substitution;
3. secure low-bandwidth technical observation;
4. paper/personnel/small-hardware return;
5. complete German physical-engine return;
6. bench endurance, captive airborne test, flight-rated engine, airframe integration and operational evaluation.

## 2. I-30 survival: material but not every benefit starts from zero

Historical I-30 carried a complete Wuerzburg set/blueprints, Metox-related equipment, G7 torpedoes/TDCs, 240 Bold rounds, AA fire-control equipment, industrial tooling/material and T-Enigma machines. Ten T-Enigmas were historically unloaded at Singapore before the mine loss. Therefore Branch I-30 survival should not be double-counted as the sole origin of Germany-Japan secure liaison.

Its largest additional Branch value is that the remaining complete technical cargo reaches Japan intact rather than as partial salt-water salvage: Wuerzburg hardware/calibration references, radar-detector practice, Bold, torpedo/TDC comparison hardware, tooling/metrology and the rest of the communications package.

Because this first pass survives, the Apr-1943 U-180/I-29 exchange changes function. It becomes a second-pass answer packet: tolerances, calibration questions, component details, emitter observations, Type-IX mounting/noise comparisons and test methods.

## 3. I-34: the important route-state correction

Historical I-34 loaded a heavy strategic cargo in Singapore in Nov 1943. The load upset trim and prevented crash diving, delaying departure; Rear Admiral Kojima and two Mitsubishi engineers were among the passengers who planned to rejoin at Penang. The actual 13 Nov loss to HMS Taurus was an Ultra-directed ambush.

Two Branch divergences intersect that event before the torpedo is fired:
- high-grade Japanese/Axis COMSEC does not expose the same mission traffic;
- after I-30/I-8 experience, Yanagi stowage has cargo partitions, fixed racks, weight-specific loading diagrams and fuel-burn trim tables, so the exact one-week historical delay is not centered.

Working center: I-34 leaves Singapore/Seletar around 7-10 Nov (center 8/9 Nov), carries the original technical passenger party instead of losing them to I-29, and is not in the historical 13 Nov Taurus ambush geometry. This does **not** grant Atlantic immunity. Ordinary patrol aircraft, ASW, weather, machinery and navigation remain.

At the current 28 Jan discussion clock, I-34 is therefore adjudicated **alive and Atlantic-inbound**, not mysteriously frozen at the Nov-1943 gate. Europe arrival remains future, projected around 3-12 Feb if no new interruption.

This matters more than an extra submarine hull. If I-34 reaches Europe, the two Mitsubishi engineers and Kojima's party are physically present around a month before historical I-29's 11 Mar arrival.

## 4. The early-information gate created by I-34

A technical party in Germany does not need to wait for its submarine to return before Japan learns anything. Secure text traffic can transmit relatively low-bandwidth engineering facts:
- BMW003/Jumo004 axial architecture and approximate stage/thrust/mass/diameter class;
- which design branches the Germans abandoned;
- modular/replaceable-engine and short hot-section life philosophy;
- high-level Me262/Me163 integration observations;
- which exact drawings, tests and samples Japanese engineers should request next.

It cannot transmit exact blade profiles/stagger maps, detailed compressor/turbine drawings, tolerance maps, complete fuel-control schematics, tooling geometry or full metallurgy/heat-treatment recipes. Those remain paper/small-hardware or physical-engine cards.

Therefore an I-34 Europe success can move Japan from "generic reports of German axial jets" to a **serious preliminary axial parallel study in Mar-Apr 1944**, months before a bulky engine reaches Japan, without pretending that radio text is a blueprint.

## 7. Domestic Japanese jet line as of 28 Jan 1944

This remains independent of German physical jet hardware.

Navy: the First Air Technical Arsenal TR family begins in 1942. Historical TR-10/TR-12/TR-30 work shows a real domestic centrifugal/hybrid line.

Army/Kawasaki: Ne-0..4 is separate. Historical company records show Ne-0 airborne testing and Ne-3/Ne-4 1943 design work with spring-1944 ground-run plans.

Branch compute, high-speed rotor analysis, measurement discipline, failure classification and selected high-temperature material allocation can plausibly produce repeatable short self-sustaining domestic bench runs by late 1943. That does **not** close turbine life, throttle/restart, seals/bearings, compressor repeatability or airframe integration.

## 8. Correcting the old Mar-May 1944 pure-jet flight

The re-audit rejects a dedicated self-propelled pure-jet first flight in Mar-May 1944 as the working center.

What *is* strongly supported in 1944 H1 is:
- longer selected ground runs;
- narrow throttle-band and fuel-control trials;
- underwing/captive airborne hot-run and thrust/combustion tests in Q2;
- instrumented nacelle/inlet work using a mother aircraft.

This uses the historical precedent of Japanese airborne auxiliary-jet testing without skipping from "short bench run" to "reliable takeoff/landing powerplant".

A purpose-built pure-jet airframe requires an engine with enough repeatable thrust duration, a fuel system, throttle/transient behavior, inlet/nacelle integration and safe takeoff/landing margins. Branch computation reduces failed iterations but cannot remove those physical gates.

## 10. Why earlier information does not automatically create an earlier combat jet

There are two countervailing effects.

Acceleration:
- domestic failure database already exists;
- German information prunes wrong compressor/turbine branches;
- I-30/I-8/RO-500 improve measurement, fault diagnosis and manufacturing comparison culture;
- an I-34 technical party can ask better questions than a cold-start mission.

Deceleration/limits:
- nickel/high-temperature alloy allocation is prototype-scale, not mass-production scale;
- bearings, seals, balance, compressor blades, combustor sheet/weld quality, turbine blade life and fuel controls remain scarce;
- jet work competes with Homare/Ha-43/Kinsei piston engines, radar and ship machinery for skilled tool-room capacity;
- this Branch is less desperate than historical Japan, so it has less reason to rush a one-way short-life jet merely to claim a first flight.

Therefore the realistic Branch gain is **earlier convergence on a practical architecture**, not a 1944 miracle fighter.

## 11. Operational implication

There is no jet CAP for the 1944 Marianas/Marshall fighting. Kinsei-Zero, Raiden, Shiden-Kai, Reppu/Ki-84 and conventional carrier-aircraft clocks remain decisive.

The jet re-audit matters mainly for 1945 and for engineering allocation decisions during 1944. The strongest favorable cascade can advance practical axial bench and experimental flight by several months relative to the corrected historical path, but it does not move operational jets into 1944.
