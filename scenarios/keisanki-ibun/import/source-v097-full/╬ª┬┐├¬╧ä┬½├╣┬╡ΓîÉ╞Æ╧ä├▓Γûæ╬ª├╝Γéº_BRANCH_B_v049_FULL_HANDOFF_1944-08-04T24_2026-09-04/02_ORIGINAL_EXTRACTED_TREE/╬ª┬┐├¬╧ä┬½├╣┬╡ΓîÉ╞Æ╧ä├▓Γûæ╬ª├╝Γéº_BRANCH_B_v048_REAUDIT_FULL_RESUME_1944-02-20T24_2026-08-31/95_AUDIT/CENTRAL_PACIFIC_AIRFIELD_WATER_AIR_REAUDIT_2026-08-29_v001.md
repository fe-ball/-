## Finding
v039 was incomplete because it modeled the first suppression morning mainly as TF58 versus the already-closed 136-serviceable Marshall local network. Historical FLINTLOCK had a separate Gilbert land-air system of about 365 assigned aircraft by end-January, while the Japanese side also had a real floatplane/water-fighter layer and surrounding staging depth. Both must be represented without adding every regional aircraft to local CAP.

## US Gilbert system
Historical official/NHHC anchors give 365 aircraft at Tarawa/Makin/Apamama by end January: 137 bomber, 130 fighter, 98 search/patrol. Tarawa alone is described with 44 Navy fighters, seven Catalinas and 49 bombers. Branch capture dates are only days later and the Branch FLINTLOCK-equivalent is also later, so field development has effectively re-converged by 2 February.

Working Branch: 350–370 assigned/staging, 300–325 mission-ready. First-morning relevant output is only 48–68 sorties, not 365: about 16–24 B-24/B-25 northern/central, 20–28 A-24/P-39/P-40 southern suppression, and 10–16 search/photo/ASW/Dumbo. Many fighters remain base defense and cannot fly an unpriced 500–600 nmi northern-Marshall sweep.

Water support is substantial. Curtiss is the Commander Air Central Pacific tender/flagship at Tarawa; Mackinac moves to Makin with VP-72 for continuous tending. PBY/PBM improve search/rescue/ASW and lower aircrew attrition, but they are not additional dive bombers.

## Japanese local OOB correction
The USSBS Japanese 25-Jan disposition explicitly gives: Roi 20 fighters + 15 land attack, Taroa 50 fighters + 9 land attack, Wotje 9 Tenzan, Kwajalein 952 AG 10 float reconnaissance, Eniwetok 15 land attack. This exposed two v039 composition errors: Ebeye/Kwaj water aviation was too compressed, and Eniwetok had an unsupported fighter/Raiden allocation.

The Branch Jan-28 total remains **136 serviceable**. It is re-decomposed rather than increased: 72 fighter/local cover, 40 attack, 24 recon/water/other. Within those slots are about 9 serviceable Kyofu, 6 Zuiun and 14 legacy float-recon aircraft. Eniwetok is 11 land-attack + 1 recon/liaison centered, with zero resident fighter credit.

## Surrounding Japanese nodes
- Ponape: intact airfield/seaplane base but no 25-Jan combat group; latent staging only, zero free resident force.
- Kusaie: airfield historically never operational; no land-air contribution.
- Nauru: Branch-limited night staging after Nov damage, no mass resident force.
- Wake: repaired and less suppressed than historical because Midway is Japanese-held; local residual Kyofu can defend Wake, not Kwajalein.
- Truk/Saipan: real regional fighter/float/flying-boat/land-attack reserves, but distance prevents same-morning fighter reinforcement. Long-range reconnaissance/staging remains possible.
- Midway: strategic sensor/route-observability already included in warning; no duplicate bonus.

## Water-air implications
Runway suppression is not equivalent to water-air suppression. The US must separately attack ramps, fuel, tenders/support craft, cranes and anchorages. Conversely, the US Gilbert PBY/PBM/PB4Y/PV network and tenders make Japanese submarine/recon operations more dangerous. Information persistence improves on both sides.
