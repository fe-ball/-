## 1. Why reopen Raiden

The Branch had already closed Raiden as a real 1943 system: late-1942/early-1943 combat-capable units, 220–250 completed and 150–175 frontline by Aug 1943, four-20-mm aircraft materially present, H2 production centered 30–40/month, and 18–30-aircraft concentration at priority bases. What remained under-specified was the heavy-bomber interception chain. A single `Raiden ready` inventory number was therefore at risk of being used as if every aircraft had identical 9–10 km capability.

## 2. Airframe performance correction

The legacy 590–600 km/h line is retained. The old 6,000 m 5:20–5:45 line is no longer the national production median. Branch production/testing advantages are better represented by a median 5:35–5:55, with 5:20–5:34 retained only as a good-aircraft/prototype tail. This keeps the causal benefit in cooling, vibration, tolerance, repeatability and readiness rather than granting every production aircraft a requirement-level climb figure.

Historical sanity checks support the physical envelope: published J2M2/J2M3 data place maximum speed around 596 km/h near 5.45 km and climb to 6 km around 5:38–5:40. Historical J2M4 turbo-supercharging was troublesome, while the mechanically supercharged J2M5 demonstrates a plausible high-altitude branch without making turbo maturity free.

## 3. Variant split

- **Standard production Raiden:** strongest around 3–6 km; useful through 6–8 km; increasingly geometry-dependent above 8 km.
- **High-altitude mechanically-supercharged subset:** domestic Kasei high-alt line, limited fielding in 1943H2 and standardization beginning 1944; 14–22 serviceable nationwide working band on 28 Jan, included inside national Raiden totals. Useful interception can extend into roughly 8.5–9.3 km with adequate warning.
- **Exhaust-turbo experimental:** combat-ready count 0 on 28 Jan.

This split prevents the later `260–300 frontline ready` inventory line from becoming `260–300 B-29 interceptors`.

## 4. Armament

By Jan 1944 four 20-mm cannon dominate frontline Raiden, but ballistic unification is incomplete. Working mix: 75–85% frontline four-cannon aircraft; 35–50% of all frontline Raiden in the fully unified four-Type-99-Model-2-equivalent class. Early two-20-mm plus two-7.7-mm aircraft remain a minority. Against heavy bombers the centered doctrine is short front-quarter/beam/high-side attacks rather than prolonged tail pursuit.

## 5. Raiden is a system, not an aircraft scalar

At A-grade fixed bases the useful chain is radar/visual/DF/passive warning -> plot/weather correlation -> staged scramble -> coarse radio vector -> visual acquisition -> section attack -> rejoin/rearm. This can buy the aircraft the initial energy state that GALVANIC showed to be decisive. It still is **not** US-style continuous precision GCI/CIC.

A representative 18–30-aircraft unit under high alert can hold roughly 4–6 at five-minute readiness, another 6–10 at 10–15 minutes, and the remaining serviceable aircraft at 20–30 minutes. Priority-base normal serviceability is modeled around 72–82%; remote bases are lower.

## 6. Warning and target class

- **B-24 class:** published B-24D data are about 303 mph maximum and 28,000 ft ceiling. At 5–7.5 km the standard Raiden has real speed/climb margin if radar/plot gives useful warning. Ten .50-cal defensive guns and escort still prevent an easy-kill assumption.
- **B-29 class:** published B-29 data are about 357 mph maximum and 33,600 ft ceiling. Standard Raiden cannot reliably sustain chase at 8.5–10 km. Long warning may create one front-quarter pass; the limited high-altitude subset is the actual anti-B-29 core.
- **F6F/F4U:** Raiden remains a base-defense energy interceptor, not a sweep-duel replacement. US carrier CIC/fighter direction remains a superior system.
