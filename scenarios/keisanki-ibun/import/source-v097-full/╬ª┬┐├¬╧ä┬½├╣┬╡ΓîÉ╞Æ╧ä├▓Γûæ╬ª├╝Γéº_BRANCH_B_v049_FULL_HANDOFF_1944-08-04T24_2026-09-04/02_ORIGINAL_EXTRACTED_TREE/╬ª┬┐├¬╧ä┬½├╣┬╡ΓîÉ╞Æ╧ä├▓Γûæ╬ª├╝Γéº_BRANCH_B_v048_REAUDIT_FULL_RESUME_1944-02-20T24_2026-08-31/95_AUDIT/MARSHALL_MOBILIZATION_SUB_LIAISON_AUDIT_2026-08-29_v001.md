## Submarine liaison/contact effect carried forward

The important previously-settled effect is information processing, not a flat torpedo bonus.

Internal accepted rules retained:
- short structured report: time / position / enemy type / course / speed / confidence / own state;
- headquarters fuses multiple submarine reports with air reconnaissance, DF and traffic analysis;
- submerged boats have no modern continuous data link; information ages;
- a reporting boat may preserve contact rather than take a low-quality shot at a high-value formation;
- better Branch status discipline reduces false silent-node ambiguity in the patrol network.

For the Marshall emergency, the centered allocation is 16 assigned boats, ~12 effectively on station by 28 Jan: 4 outer reporting/search, 7 middle conventional intercept, 5 inner Sentaka/recontact (with some still moving/held in reserve so layer paper totals are not identical to simultaneous on-station count).

By 27-28 Jan, correlated submarine reports + traffic/DF + US land-air suppression activity make a major Central-Pacific operation HIGH confidence and the Marshalls MEDIUM-HIGH. No weapon-quality carrier track is granted yet. This prevents the common error of either ignoring the submarine liaison effect or turning it into a real-time wolfpack solution.
