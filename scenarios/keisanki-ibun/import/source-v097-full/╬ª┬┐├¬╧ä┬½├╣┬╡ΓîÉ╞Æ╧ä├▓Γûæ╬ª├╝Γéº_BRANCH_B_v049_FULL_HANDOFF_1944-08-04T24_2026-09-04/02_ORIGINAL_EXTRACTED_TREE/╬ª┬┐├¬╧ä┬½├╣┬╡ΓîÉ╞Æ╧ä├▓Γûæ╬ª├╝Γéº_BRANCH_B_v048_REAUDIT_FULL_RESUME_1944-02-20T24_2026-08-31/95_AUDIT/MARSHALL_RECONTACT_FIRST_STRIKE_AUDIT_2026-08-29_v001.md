## Why the first night strike does not score a carrier hit

The Branch improvement creates the **opportunity**, not the hit. US forces have already paid for GALVANIC and have strong radar pickets, disciplined maneuver and task-force-wide night-defense procedures. Japanese aircraft still lack airborne search radar and must find a maneuvering formation in squalls from a contact with finite position error.

## Why US losses rise anyway

Historical FLINTLOCK approached launch points with near-complete tactical surprise and rapidly eliminated the remaining Marshall air strength. Branch Japan has: submarine-cued arcs; more dispersed aircraft; staged fighter alert; Raiden detachments inside the existing fighter total; radar/plot field-service; and dated post-GALVANIC procedural learning. The result is not parity: it is a first cycle in which a larger fraction of Japanese fighters meet the raid at usable altitude and fewer aircraft are destroyed parked.
