# Eastern Fleet medium/light OOB audit v001 — 2026-08-29

status: WORKING / NO CURRENT AUTHORITY PROMOTION

## Adjudication rule
Named historical movements are preserved unless a Branch divergence causally intersects them. The existing Branch penalty (raw DD/escort hulls near historical/slightly higher, but freely assignable reserve about 2–4 hulls worse) is therefore applied to availability, convoy/ASW opportunity cost and maintenance, not by arbitrarily deleting Petard/Paladin/Pathfinder/Rocket or other named arrivals.

Branch-saved 1942 hulls are not automatically free or at Ceylon. Hermes, Vampire and Hollyhock remain excluded from the Jan-1944 concentration until a dated location/readiness trace is built.

## What actually adds to the theatre in January
Medium/lower true reinforcement set:
- HNMS Tromp: Fremantle 4 Jan -> Colombo 13 Jan -> Trincomalee 20 Jan.
- HMS Petard, Paladin, Pathfinder, Rocket: take over the through fleet-DD screen at Suez on 16 Jan and proceed east with Group A.
- HMS Stonehenge and HMS Truculent: both reach Trincomalee 23 Jan.

This is the clean January net-new medium/light set: 1 cruiser + 4 destroyers + 2 submarines = 7 named hulls.

Termagant, Tenacious and Kempenfelt travel from the UK to Port Said but are recalled there on 12 Jan, so they are not counted as Indian-Ocean arrivals from this passage.

Rotherham, Roebuck, Racehorse, Rapid and Norman are existing Eastern Fleet destroyers sent to Aden to reinforce Group A on 19 Jan; Napier and Nizam are local/RAN station destroyers joining near Ceylon on 27 Jan. These strengthen the arriving screen but are not new theatre hulls.

## Cruiser layer: why the station is not cruiser-rich at the end of January
The standing Eastern Fleet cruiser layer is geographically dispersed:
- Newcastle, Kenya and Suffolk leave Colombo for Mauritius on 14 Jan.
- Ceylon leaves Colombo for Addu on 23 Jan and then troopship escort toward Australia.
- Frobisher is in Durban refit, docked 18–28 Jan.
- Sussex departs Trincomalee 10 Jan for Fremantle.
- Hawkins is still moving from Simonstown/Durban to Kilindini and reaches Kilindini 24 Jan.
- Danae passes Trincomalee/Colombo but leaves Cochin for Aden on 29 Jan.
- Emerald remains a Ceylon-area cruiser and exercises with new-arrival Tromp 25–26 Jan.

Therefore the late-Jan immediate Ceylon cruiser core is centered on Emerald + Tromp, not the whole paper cruiser roster. The heavy-ship reinforcement is genuine; a simultaneous large cruiser concentration is not.

## Submarines
Existing named nucleus used for the Jan model: Tally-Ho, Tactician, Templar, Taurus, Trespasser. Stonehenge and Truculent raise the named physical late-Jan total to seven, but patrol/turnaround/defect clocks prevent treating all seven as simultaneously ready/in-port.

Surf and Sea Rover arrive 10 Feb and are excluded from January. Maidstone does not reach Trincomalee until 9 Mar, so a January 'new depot ship at Ceylon' is rejected.

## Low-end escort layer
The theatre contains a wide convoy/ASW network (destroyers plus sloops/frigates/corvettes/minesweepers) spread over Aden, Bombay, Ceylon, Calcutta and Kilindini routes. Representative late-1943 names include Falmouth, Shoreham, Jumna, Sutlej, Narbada, Bann, Derg, Kale, Plym, Lulworth, Freesia and Snowflake. They are not converted into fleet-screen equivalents.

At the end of the Group A passage, eleven named destroyers are associated with the arrival sequence (Rotherham, Roebuck, Racehorse, Rapid, Norman, Napier, Nizam, Petard, Paladin, Pathfinder, Rocket), but they are distributed among Colombo, Trincomalee and Cochin and Norman needs repair. A Branch short-notice free screen around 5–7 is more coherent than treating all eleven as freely concentrated. Seven can be assembled for a deliberate sortie, but the diary itself later records destroyer escort duties curtailing heavy-ship sea training.

## Later arrivals explicitly kept out of January
Gambia: 20 Feb Trincomalee.
Maidstone: 9 Mar Trincomalee.
London/Cumberland: March reinforcement layer.
Phoebe: May layer.
Surf/Sea Rover: 10 Feb Trincomalee.
