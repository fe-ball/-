# US FLINTLOCK force delta gate — historical vs prior Branch B vs current rerun

status: WORKING_FORCE_ACCOUNTING_GATE
as_of: 1944-02-10T24 Marshall local

## Bottom line
The US ground assault OOB remains close to historical in gross personnel and heavy ground equipment. The main material divergence before FLINTLOCK is in fast-carrier availability and operational allocation, not in the 4th Marine / 7th Infantry assault mass. Therefore the target choice and two-division assault plan do not require retroactive redesign. Tactical efficiency, replacement dilution, LVT reserve/traffic, escort allocation, and post-FLINTLOCK regeneration do require explicit accounting.

## Historical anchors
- TF51: about 297 ships and about 54,000 Marine/Army assault troops.
- Southern Landing Force: 7th Infantry Division plus assigned units, 21,768 officers and men.
- Northern Landing Force: 4th Marine Division plus assigned units, 20,778 officers and men.
- Attack Reserve: reinforced 22nd Marines plus 106th Infantry less 2nd Battalion and assigned units, 9,325 officers and men.
- Kwajalein southern LVT/LVTA package: 174 amphibian vehicles total, including 79 armored amphibians; 21-vehicle reserve pool; four LSTs configured for LVT repair support.
- Historical first-night southern ground state: six infantry battalions, 44 medium tanks, 18 light tanks, five SP 75-mm guns, and two platoons of 4.2-inch mortars ashore.
- Historical fast carrier support: 6 CV + 6 CVL, over 700 carrier aircraft.
- Historical close-support baseline: 7 old battleships and 6 CVEs.

## Prior v048 Branch B anchors
- Ground assault divisions remained the same 4th Marine Division / 7th Infantry Division pairing and historical-scale amphibious/fire-support force.
- Fast-carrier wallet was already closed at 5 CV + 6 CVL, about 610 ready aircraft centered, organized into three stronger TGs.
- Old D-Day ledger used 40-44 medium and 16-18 light tanks locally available around the first day, derived from historical anchors plus unloading/serviceability friction.
- Old branch treated the assault force difference as primarily naval/air/replenishment timing, not a major infantry-strength deficit.

## Current rerun corrections
### Personnel
- 4th Marine Division: no direct GALVANIC depletion; gross assault strength remains historical-scale.
- 7th Infantry Division: no direct GALVANIC depletion, but Branch Attu was costlier than history. Branch Attu settlement gives US 650-800 KIA, 1,300-1,600 WIA, plus 2,200-2,700 cold/disease cases versus historical roughly 550 KIA, 1,100-1,140 WIA, and ~1,800 cold/disease casualties.
- Eight months of US replacement/retraining capacity is enough to restore authorized gross strength by FLINTLOCK. The remaining effect is replacement mix/cadre dilution rather than a missing regiment.
- Working effect for 7th Division: gross headcount approximately historical; veteran small-unit cadre density somewhat thinner in Attu-exposed units, offset partly by greater combat experience among survivors and months of retraining. Keep this inside tactical-efficiency/casualty bands, not as a force-count subtraction.
- 22nd Marines / 106th Infantry reserve was not consumed in the current FLINTLOCK main assault and remains a real post-FLINTLOCK/CATCHPOLE resource.

### Ground vehicles and landing craft
- Current Kwajalein D-Day state: 40-44 medium tanks ashore/locally available by evening, 37-42 immediately ready; 16-18 light tanks available, 14-17 immediately ready.
- This is essentially historical heavy-armor mass with serviceability/unloading friction, not a Branch production increase.
- Current D-Day southern LVT/LVTA losses: 8-12 irreversible plus 11-17 temporary/short-repair mission kills.
- Tarawa learning justifies more reserve, wreck-removal, engineer, traffic-control and reattack capacity, but no hard numerical +20/+30 LVT increment should be invented without a hull/transport trace.
- One unnamed LST-class hull is mission-killed on 5 Feb, reducing local reserve/unloading margin. Cargo is not fixed; no tank company is deleted by fiat.

### Naval/air support
- Current pre-battle TF58 remains the prior v048 11-deck wallet: 5 CV + 6 CVL, 595-625 ready aircraft centered 610.
- This is materially below historical FLINTLOCK's 12 decks / >700 aircraft. It is the largest pre-assault US material delta and already affects suppression, CAP/search reserve, replenishment and timing.
- Seven old-BB close-support slots and six CVE close-support groups remain approximately historical in count, but their time/allocation wallets are more constrained by Japanese submarine/carrier/surface threats.
- GALVANIC's DD loss can be backfilled from the large US escort pool, but backfill is not free: it removes a hull from another screen/convoy/training assignment. This is a margin effect, not an assault-cancellation effect.

## Does this require a retroactive FLINTLOCK plan change?
No for target choice and the two-division assault concept.

Reasons:
1. 4th Marines and 7th Infantry remain division-scale, fully supportable assault formations.
2. The 7th Division's Attu delta is predominantly a replacement-quality/cohesion issue by Feb 1944, not a gross manpower shortage.
3. Medium/light tank mass is near historical; current variations are readiness and unloading, not missing battalions.
4. LVT reserve is a throughput/congestion problem rather than a reef-crossing capability failure.
5. The large difference — fewer fast-carrier aircraft/decks and stronger Japanese mobile threat — has already changed the operation's suppression calendar and naval geometry in the current rerun.

## What must change going forward
- Carry a 7th Division replacement/cadre-quality flag into any exact casualty/tempo reconstruction; do not subtract thousands of men from its nominal OOB.
- Keep physical / serviceable / immediately committed vehicle counts separate.
- Do not turn stronger Tarawa lessons into invented new weapon types; most US change is more reserve, more ammunition, more reconnaissance, better JASCO/traffic/wreck-clearance use, and repeated target-system attack.
- Post-FLINTLOCK, the 1,570-2,215 main-ground casualty band hits 4th Marines/7th Infantry, while the 22nd Marines/106th reserve remains largely intact. Therefore CATCHPOLE troop availability is much less affected than HAILSTONE carrier-air availability.
- HAILSTONE planning must be re-audited from the current post-2-Feb carrier state: Cabot campaign-out, current carrier-air losses/cadre dilution, and replenishment/deck congestion matter more than FLINTLOCK infantry losses.
