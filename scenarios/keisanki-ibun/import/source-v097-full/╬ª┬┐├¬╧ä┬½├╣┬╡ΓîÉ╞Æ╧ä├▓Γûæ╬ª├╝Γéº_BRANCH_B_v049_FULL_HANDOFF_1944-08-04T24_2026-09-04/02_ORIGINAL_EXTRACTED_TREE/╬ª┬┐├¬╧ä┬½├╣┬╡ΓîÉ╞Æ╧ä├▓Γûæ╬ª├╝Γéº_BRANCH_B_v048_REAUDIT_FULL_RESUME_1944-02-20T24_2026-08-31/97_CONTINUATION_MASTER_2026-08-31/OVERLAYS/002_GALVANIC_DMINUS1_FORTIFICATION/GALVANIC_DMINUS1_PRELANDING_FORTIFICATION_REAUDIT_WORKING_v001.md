## 1. Nauru 1943-11-23

Wasp + Princeton strike is retained. Nauru remains a useful lower-priority base; Japan does not strip Mili/Jaluit to save it.

New upstream input: Nauru has better revetment, dispersed fuel/ordnance, repair shelters, communications protection and engineer plant uptime than the old implicit historical-material-envelope model.

Centered effect:
- exposed aircraft / runway / obvious facilities still take severe damage;
- one carrier cycle no longer equals destruction of the whole base system;
- US must spend a larger fraction of its Nauru strike wallet on revetments, repair/support sites and repeat attack rather than assume runway cratering closes the node;
- Nauru ends 11/23 **heavily suppressed but not physically sterilized**;
- immediate mass strike generation remains unavailable; emergency reconnaissance / night staging after field repair becomes a conditional 11/24+ possibility if US follow-up pressure relaxes.

No extra Nauru aircraft count is created in this v001; local OOB must be traced before adding sorties.

## 2. Marshall / Gilbert air and water-air survival before D-Day

Current pre-suppression physical-class bands remain:
- land fighters capable of Gilbert reaction: 80–95 across Marshall network;
- attack aircraft: 35–45;
- legacy recon / flying boat / float: 15–20;
- Kyofu: 12–20 physical / 8–16 normally ready across priority lagoon nodes;
- Zuiun: 5–9 physical / 4–7 ready.

No aircraft is added. Hardening affects **ground loss and sortie persistence**:
- revetments / small dispersed stores / protected comms reduce the probability that a suppression pass destroys aircraft, fuel and maintenance capability together;
- runway attack and water-air attack are separate target sets;
- Mili / Maloelap / Wotje / Jaluit can therefore require more US search/strike/escort work to achieve the same operational suppression.

Working D-Day-ready delta versus v004 implicit state: modest positive persistence only; do not yet book a new exact ready-aircraft total until base-by-base allocation is closed.

## 3. Tarawa / Betio prelanding physical state

Tarawa weapon and personnel OOB remain unchanged in this gate. In particular:
- total personnel 4,900–5,300;
- first-line combat 2,600–2,900;
- limited armed support 700–1,000;
- Type 95 physical 14;
- no automatic new coastal-gun battery.

What changes is the conversion of existing time/labor/material into completed protection.

Working physical interpretation:
- earth/timber/revetment/protected-position completion materially higher than historical-material-envelope assumption;
- concrete-heavy works only modestly higher because cement/rebar/curing/time remain limiting;
- ammunition and communications are more dispersed;
- observation/registered-fire network has more redundant physical paths;
- construction/engineer plant has somewhat higher uptime;
- obstacle and local route preparation is more complete, but still follows pre-GALVANIC orientation priorities.

### Bombardment-survival gate

Do not increase all artillery by a flat survival multiplier. Resolve each surviving battery/fire sector by:
1. physical emplacement and camouflage;
2. observation/communications survival;
3. ammunition survival;
4. suppression state at H-hour;
5. preregistered lane relevance.

### Tactical-doctrine guard

No GALVANIC lessons before GALVANIC:

## 4. Makin prelanding state

Makin receives better base hardening, obstacles, protected stores and seaplane-support survivability, but trained combat manpower remains a severe cap. Construction/labor personnel are not converted into SNLF infantry.

The island must be rerun, but a Tarawa-scale qualitative change is not expected without a new dated combat-unit allocation.

## 5. Expected GALVANIC sensitivity before full D-Day rerun

These are directional expectations, not booked outcomes:
- Nauru suppression costs more US work and leaves more recoverable support function.
- Marshall air/water-air network stays useful slightly longer, increasing US suppression/CAP/search burden.
- Tarawa first three LVT waves likely face a somewhat denser surviving fire system than v004.
- Japanese tank physical count does not rise; bombardment survival/reappearance may shift upward within the existing 14-vehicle envelope.
- US high-water / verified-lane advantage on 11/24 remains and still lands tanks, engineers, ammunition, water and dry radios earlier than historical 20-Nov Tarawa.
- Atoll geometry remains the decisive ceiling; stronger fortification can raise cost and delay without making Betio strategically sustainable against the full US combined-arms system.
