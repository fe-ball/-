## 2. Tarawa warning / radar / C2
- A Japanese radar installation physically existed on Betio historically; Branch-B reliability, protected power, spares, plot discipline, wire redundancy and repair practice can improve availability.

- Current H-hour tactical-comms and coordinated-fire bands remain the centered representation; no reopening of D-Day solely for radar.

## 5. Intelligence / lessons exploitation
- US forces can inspect surviving Japanese fortifications, revetments, communications, magazines and Betio radar hardware after capture. They can learn that pre-assault damage assessment was optimistic and that protected C2/ammunition/aircraft support survived better than expected.
- They do not automatically infer Japan-wide construction productivity, hidden logistics reserves, or future island layouts.
- Japanese Tarawa AAR quality is limited by garrison destruction. Lessons propagate from pre-battle plans, radio/air/naval observations, surviving regional staff, intercepted reports and physical outcomes — not from a perfect survivor debrief.

## 6. Final no-reopen rule
Makin/Tarawa ground combat is considered CLOSED unless a newly discovered upstream fact changes one of: combat manpower, D-day tide/lane logic, physical Type-95 count, major coastal-gun OOB, or US assault-force composition. Radar/C2/hardening refinements that are already represented by the present warning, survival and coordination bands do not by themselves reopen the battles.
