## Centerline maritime state at 2-Feb T24

### Japan
- Shokaku, Zuikaku and Soryu remain operational; Hiryu is mobile but cannot conduct normal flight operations.
- The first-line force retains Hiei/Kirishima, Tone/Chikuma, Noshiro and its destroyer screen. No battleship/cruiser night dash is detached toward Kwajalein: doing so would uncover a damaged carrier force inside the radius of roughly ten still-effective US fast decks.
- Junyo/Hiyo/Ryujo/Zuiho remain rear-deck depth. They absorb diverted/orphan aircraft, rotate CAP and reduce recovery loss, but do not regenerate the lost strike leaders/navigators.
- The carrier system has many more flyable airframes than coherent offensive crews. Immediate 3-Feb value is CAP/recovery/withdrawal, not another fleet strike.

### United States
- Cabot is stable but campaign-out; it withdraws under a 2-3 destroyer-class escort allocation. This escort cost is removed from the local carrier/assault screen while the damaged CVL is exposed.
- Intrepid regains limited deck function late on 2-Feb and reaches near-normal deck-cycle capability during the pre-dawn/early-morning 3-Feb inspection window, but its air group and deck crews remain fatigued.
- Cabot airborne survivors are cross-decked. They add aircraft, not flight decks, and temporarily worsen spotting/parking/maintenance congestion on receiving carriers.
- TF58 has burned two heavy suppression days plus a fleet action. 3-Feb is therefore a recovery/refuel/reorganization day with defensive search/CAP/ASW first and only a bounded island-suppression wallet.

## 3-Feb centerline
- No renewed carrier battle. US searches obtain enough negative evidence to judge the Japanese carrier force is withdrawing, but not enough to know every Japanese surface unit's exact position.
- Fast battleships remain with TF58 through most of 3-Feb; they are not immediately converted into extra bombardment ships. The surviving Japanese Hiei/Kirishima/Tone-Chikuma screen is still unlocated at tactical precision.
- TF58 conducts roughly 105-145 offensive suppression sorties during 3-Feb, rather than another 200+ sortie day. Gilbert land air contributes about 48-62 relevant sorties. Large additional sortie counts are CAP/search/ASW/recovery and should not be misreported as island-strike output.
- Marshall combat-serviceable local aviation closes 3-Feb around 39-49 centered 44: fighters 18-23, attack 7-10, water/recon 8-11, with fragmented local C2.

## Submarine retasking
- The submarine network does not teleport from carrier-search boxes into assault lanes. Conventional boats require one or two night movements; Sentaka can make useful short geometry corrections but not strategic high-speed transit.
- One or two boats are briefly assigned a cripple-intercept problem against Cabot's probable eastward withdrawal corridor because Japanese strike reports identify a damaged CVL. Centerline: no firing-quality solution; the search consumes boat-hours and slightly delays assault-lane concentration.
- By 3-Feb evening, about 7-9 boats are in useful assault/support approach geometry, 3-4 are still relocating, and 1-2 remain outer reporting/cripple-hunt assets. No US ship hit is centered on 3-Feb.

## Why this matters
The sea problem now contains explicit finite wallets:
- Japanese carrier screen versus surface-raid temptation;
- US fast-battleship screen versus bombardment temptation;
- Cabot survivor aircraft versus receiving-deck congestion;
- TF58 suppression sorties versus CAP/search/ASW/refuel;
- Japanese submarine cripple-hunt versus assault-ship interdiction.
