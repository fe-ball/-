## Captured system, not just captured guns
US technical exploitation recovers enough damaged radar/plot/power/telephone/spares material and physical fortification evidence to recognize that Branch-B island defense is a distributed system. Immediate field lessons are redundancy, separate power/plot functions, local phone routing, ammunition/blast separation and the need to verify supposedly suppressed positions. Metric passive-warning/ESM equipment may be present in the technical pool, but its operational meaning requires weeks of analysis and does not imply Japanese centimetric/SG awareness.

## US base transition
- Roi: limited clearance begins before full atoll closeout; emergency/liaison and individual fighter use around 11-13 Feb, limited CAP/ferry/recon detachment 13-15 Feb, regular fighter-squadron-level operations working gate 16-18 Feb.
- Kwajalein Island: emergency transport/liaison 11-13 Feb, limited staging 15-19 Feb. A usable strip is not the same thing as a mature high-throughput bomber base.
- Ebeye/lagoon water-air facilities: limited tender/PBY/ASW/recon use can return around 11-14 Feb despite permanent-facility damage.
- Majuro remains the immediate fast-fleet replenishment/assembly hub.

Historical sanity anchor: US sources describe air-base construction as an immediate post-assault priority at Kwajalein/Roi, while the fully developed Roi field and aviation-supply complex took much longer. The working Branch clocks therefore separate emergency/limited runway use from mature base throughput.

## Support release
Ground victory releases demand but does not teleport ships. DD/DE close-fire assignments shift gradually to ASW/escort/replenishment; CVE sortie wallets shift from CAS/spotting to CAP/ASW/ferry over 9-12 Feb; old BB/CA fire-support elements require ammunition/fuel/formation turnaround before a new assault concentration.

## Japanese lesson propagation
Kwajalein is the decisive second step after Tarawa: stronger lagoon/beach defenses, local C2 and ammunition dispersion worked tactically but still could not survive repeated US carrier/battleship/direct-fire reduction on a shallow atoll. The resulting 11-15 Feb directive preserves dispersion/redundancy while moving the main battle concept deeper where terrain permits. Eniwetok can only make short-warning field changes; Palau and Marianas have enough time for materially deeper redesign and must be reopened downstream.

## Maritime and next-operation gate
