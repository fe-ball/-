# FLINTLOCK 1944-02-06 D-Day integrated rerun — WORKING

## Result in one line
The 6-Feb landings succeed operationally, but the newly integrated lagoon-side defense, distributed ammunition/comms, remanning and local-command layer raises first-day friction above the superseded v003 line. Roi still collapses rapidly; Namur and Kwajalein remain organized battles at T24.

## Why the result is not a simple 'stronger Japan = more US casualties' multiplier
US preparation is also stronger after GALVANIC: longer repeated bombardment, better obstacle/channel work, more LVT/LVT(A) redundancy, deliberate close destroyer fire, CVE spotting and same-day tank/engineer reduction. Added US physical mass creates lagoon/beach-exit queues, but commanders meter waves rather than blindly creating a permanent traffic jam.

## Roi versus Namur
Roi is an exposed airfield island. Protected personnel survive the preparation better than in a fully surprised historical-equivalent case, but once they reman exposed positions they are rapidly found and reduced. Organized resistance breaks by late afternoon/early evening.

Namur is a different fight: vegetation, structures, debris and stronger local sectors preserve more short-range resistance. The one traced Type 94 tankette can at most act as a machine-gun firepoint and is gone by T24 centered.

A major anti-trace correction is the ammunition-magazine event. Historically a Marine demolition charge ignited a torpedo-warhead/aviation-ordnance store and caused a catastrophic secondary explosion. Branch B has smaller distributed magazines and blast separation. The rerun therefore does not inherit that historical mass-casualty event. Local secondary detonations remain plausible, but the centerline personnel effect is only 8-25 and a short local disruption.

## Kwajalein
This is where the post-Tarawa Japanese lesson is most visible. Lagoon-facing fire plans, registered mortars, smaller protected magazines, local reserve/succession plans and surviving phone/radio fragments make the first waves materially harder than a surprised defense.

They still cannot deny the landing. Direct fire and mortar positions are repeatedly re-suppressed by LVT(A), destroyers, artillery and CVE observation. Added US tractors and support craft create local 20-40 minute queues at some exits; the response is to meter later waves, not feed the blockage.

By T24 the western beachhead is secure and sustainable. Medium/light tanks, engineers and artillery are ashore in useful mass. Organized Japanese defense remains farther east and the 6/7-Feb night fight is still open.

Regional Japanese combat-serviceable air falls from 27 at dawn to 16-21 centered 18. Small fighter/recon/water-air activity survives, but no coherent mass strike remains. No additional major US ship hit is centered on 6-Feb. Target-island radar/AA remains locally dangerous in fragments, not as a coherent system.
