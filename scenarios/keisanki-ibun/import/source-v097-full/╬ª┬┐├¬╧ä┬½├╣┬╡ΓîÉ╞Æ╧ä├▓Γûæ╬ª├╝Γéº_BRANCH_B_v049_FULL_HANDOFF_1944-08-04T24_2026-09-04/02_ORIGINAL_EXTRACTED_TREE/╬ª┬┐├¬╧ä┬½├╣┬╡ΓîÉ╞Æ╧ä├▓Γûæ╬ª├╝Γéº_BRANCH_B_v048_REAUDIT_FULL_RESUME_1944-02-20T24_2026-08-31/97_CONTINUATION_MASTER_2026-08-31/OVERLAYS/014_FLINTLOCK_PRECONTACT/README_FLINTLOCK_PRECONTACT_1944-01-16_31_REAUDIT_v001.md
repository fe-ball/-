# FLINTLOCK pre-contact / mobilization rerun — 1944-01-16 through 1944-01-31

Status: WORKING / not canonical promotion.

This package starts from the revised 1944-01-15 Marshall closeout after the GALVANIC fortification/airbase/sensor/C2 rerun. It intentionally does not import the old FLINTLOCK combat outcome.

Key changes carried here:

- Marshall late construction continues after 15 Jan; roughly 9.5-13.5 kt of additional useful defense cargo is delivered/redistributed by 31 Jan, mostly finishing existing projects.
- Radar/DF/plot/power/telephone/water-air are treated as a defense system, not a single radar hit point.
- Marshall regional combat-serviceable air remains around the low/mid-140s by 28 Jan despite continued Gilbert pressure because hardening/repair/replacement offset part of attrition. Kinsei Zero remains absent from the Marshall land OOB.
- Soryu is operational. The Japanese first-line holding force is therefore Shokaku/Zuikaku/Hiryu/Soryu rather than the superseded three-deck line. Secondary carriers Junyo/Hiyo/Ryujo/Zuiho remain a rear-deck reserve rather than free first-wave aircraft.
- I-175 is not locked into the superseded Pierce-hit/heavy-repair line, modestly increasing late-Jan effective submarine coverage.
- US planners still center a direct Kwajalein/Roi-Namur + Majuro operation. Tarawa learning increases fire/recon/engineer reserves but finite deck, DD, oiler, landing-craft and beach-throughput constraints remain.
- Provisional main D-Day remains centered 6 Feb (5-7 Feb sensitivity); it can still move after the carrier/suppression fight.
- Improved Japanese search/contact converts the old near-miss into a firm one-TG visual on 30 Jan. A submarine night recontact enables a real 8-10 aircraft night torpedo attempt; centerline no hit, 3-5 Japanese aircraft lost.
- By 31 Jan tactical surprise is degraded, but Japan still lacks continuous all-TF58 geometry. Yamamoto prepares but withholds the four-carrier strike pending a fresh target/mission-split/recovery-state gate.
