# HAILSTONE / CATCHPOLE integrated advance — 1944-02-11 to 1944-02-20T24

Status: **WORKING rerun from the post-FLINTLOCK closeout.** Historical event outcomes are reference-only; current results are generated from the Branch-B force/warning state.

## What changed in this pass

- Truk warning is treated as a **base-defense system**, not one radar set. Radar, visual posts, RI/DF, weather, plot, communications, backup power, AA fire direction and independent water aviation all contribute, with finite throughput and failure modes.
- Water aviation is kept separate from runway aircraft. It improves search/recontact and keeps a residual night/recon layer alive after runway suppression; it is not counted twice when Kyofu acts as a fighter.
- Japanese airfield/fuel/communications hardening lowers correlated ground losses and fuel-system collapse, but does not prevent repeated U.S. carrier cycles from gaining daylight air superiority.
- U.S. exploitation of Kwajalein-captured defensive architecture improves target-system thinking by 20 Feb, but technical understanding of Japanese passive-warning equipment is still incomplete.
- High-value mobile shipping evacuates Truk before the raid. Therefore HAILSTONE becomes more of an air-defense/base-system suppression battle and less of a historical shipping massacre.
- Eniwetok gets only short-warning expedients. Its nine Type 95 tanks are kept as three hidden local reserves per main island; no extra tank or radar unit is spawned.

- U.S.: 5 CV + 3 CVL, about 465 ready aircraft.

- No strategic surprise and no alert stand-down; exact TF58 launch geometry is still unknown.

## HAILSTONE Day 1 centered result

The first U.S. sweep remains a tactical win, not a massacre. By dusk:

- Japanese aircraft irrecoverable: **40–50, center 45**.
- U.S. aircraft irrecoverable: **24–31, center 27**.
- Japanese serviceable fighters: **16–22**.
- Japanese night-capable attack/recon/water aircraft: **13–19**.
- All major strips are cycle-limited, but at least one can still launch small emergency packages after repair work.
- Warning/plot throughput remains around **50–65%** of dawn effectiveness; one precision AA channel remains reliable centered.
- Fuel lost/isolated/contaminated: about **2,000–4,500 tons**, less correlated than a non-hardened base because storage and support functions are dispersed.
- Lagoon shipping losses: **3–5 merchant/auxiliary hulls, about 14–26k GRT**, plus small craft; no major historical leaker surface force is inserted.

The U.S. therefore needs a second day if it wants Truk unusable as an intervention base. A Japanese night counterstrike remains physically possible, but it starts from a stale sector cue against a fully alerted TF58.
