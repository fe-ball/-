## Why the Japanese night defense does not become a mass counterattack
The surviving northeast defense is physically compressed, so local groups are closer and can unintentionally converge. But island-wide command, telephone continuity and ammunition distribution are too degraded to build a controlled fortress-wide attack. The result is sharper local fighting, not a restored operational counterattack capability.

## Why D+2 accelerates
By D+2, US beach exits and heavy-equipment flow are no longer the dominant rate limit. The pace is set by identifying surviving shelters and physically reducing them. Tarawa-derived verification behavior prevents suppressed positions from being assumed dead, while tanks, direct fire, engineers and observed artillery systematically split the remaining clusters.

## Air / sensor / submarine
- Japanese regional serviceable aircraft decline from 10-14 to 6-9 (center 7), mainly recon/water/local-defense assets. No coherent strike package and no major US ship hit.
- Target-island tactical radar/director C2 is effectively gone as a unified system, but outer Marshall nodes continue operational warning.
- Roughly 5-7 submarines begin the day with useful/near-useful geometry. One marginal torpedo firing produces no confirmed hit; one boat is pushed into 24-48h local unavailability. About 4-5 remain useful/near-useful at T24.

## Support release
Roi-Namur secure status and late-day Kwajalein collapse begin to release some CVE observation/direct-fire and DD task capacity, but ammunition, transit, replenishment and ASW prevent instant reassignment. The submarine threat remains sufficient to keep outer escort/air-ASW elevated.
