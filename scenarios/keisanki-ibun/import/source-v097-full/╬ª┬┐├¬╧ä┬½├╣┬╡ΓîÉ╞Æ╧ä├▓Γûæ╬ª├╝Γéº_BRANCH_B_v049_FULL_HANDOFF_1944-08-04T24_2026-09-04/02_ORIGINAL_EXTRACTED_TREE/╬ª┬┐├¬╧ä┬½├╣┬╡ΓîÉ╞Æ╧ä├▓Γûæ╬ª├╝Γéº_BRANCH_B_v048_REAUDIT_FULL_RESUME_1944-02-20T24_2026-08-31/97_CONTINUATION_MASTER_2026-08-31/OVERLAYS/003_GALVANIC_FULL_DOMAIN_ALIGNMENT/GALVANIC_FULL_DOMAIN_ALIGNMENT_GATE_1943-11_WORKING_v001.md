## 6. Cross-domain coupling: why stronger land defense can reopen naval outcomes

Do not treat ground closeout as isolated after D-Day.

If organized Japanese resistance remains coherent longer or initial beachhead expansion is slower:
1. US CVE close-support / spotting / ASW / local CAP wallet remains tied to Gilberts longer.
2. US fast carriers must preserve a larger fleet-defense reserve while spending additional suppression sorties against Tarawa/Nauru/Marshall air-base systems.
3. replenishment, recovery, fatigue and deck-cycle states on 11/25-27 can shift even with unchanged nominal carrier OOB.
4. Japanese first-line carriers receive more time to obtain a favorable contact while US decks are mission-split; this does not force a strike but changes positive-EV geometry.
5. Japanese secondary carriers receive more time to move east, provide CAP/search/ferry/orphan-aircraft capacity and free first-line decks; they still are not equal strike decks.
6. Marshall land/water air gets more re-search/recontact opportunities if US suppression must be repeated against hardened base systems.
7. submarine patrol-days and attack windows increase; US ASW vigilance also rises, so opportunity count and hit probability must be resolved separately.
8. Japanese light/heavy surface units may arrive while the assault/support force is still tactically committed. Their objective may shift from nuisance raid to supply/support disruption, but US SG/CIC and carrier air remain powerful constraints.
9. Conversely, longer resistance allows the US to learn/adapt, add naval gunfire/direct-fire/engineer cycles and increase suppression. Delay is not one-sided compounding.
