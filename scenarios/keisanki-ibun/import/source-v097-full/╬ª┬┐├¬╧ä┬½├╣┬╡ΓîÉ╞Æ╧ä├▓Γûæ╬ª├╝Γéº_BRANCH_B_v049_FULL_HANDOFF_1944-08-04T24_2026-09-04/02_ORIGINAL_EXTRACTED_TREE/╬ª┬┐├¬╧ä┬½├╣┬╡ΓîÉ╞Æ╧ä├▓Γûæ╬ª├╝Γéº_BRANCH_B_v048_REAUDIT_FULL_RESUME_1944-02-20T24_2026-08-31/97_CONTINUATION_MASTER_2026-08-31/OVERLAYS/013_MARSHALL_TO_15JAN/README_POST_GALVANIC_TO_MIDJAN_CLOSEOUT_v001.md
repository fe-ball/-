# Post-GALVANIC -> Marshall 1944-01-15 closeout (WORKING)

This package closes the 29-Nov-1943 through 15-Jan-1944 reconstruction after the revised GALVANIC rerun. It is deliberately **not** a FLINTLOCK settlement.

## Centered outcomes

- Japan can deliver roughly **43-55 kt** of military cargo into the Marshall main network during the 47-day window, centered ~49 kt, while still paying submarine/air/turnaround losses. The main Branch delta is useful cargo completion and field-service depth, not a giant new infantry army.
- Another **12-17 kt** is delivered into Kusaie/Ponape as separate eastern-Caroline staging/defense depth.
- Post-Tarawa construction improves revetment completion, distributed ammunition/fuel, wire/C2 redundancy, radar-power/plot survivability and lagoon-side fire plans. Flat atolls remain flat; no later cave-defense doctrine is backdated.
- Kusaie receives enough plant/support to become a **limited staging/emergency strip** in early January, not a free resident fighter base.
- Marshall regional air reaches roughly **138-158 serviceable, center 148** on 15 Jan because the historical 4-Dec mass raid is not automatic, while US Gilbert-based pressure is still rising. No Kinsei-Zero detachment is credited.
- Soryu's revised one-bomb/near-miss damage no longer requires a home-yard absence. Centered first-line return is about **12 Dec** (8-15 Dec band). By 15 Jan Japan again has four operational first-line carrier decks, but experienced strike-cadre debt remains.
- US Tarawa/Abemama/Makin airfields expand quickly enough that by mid-Jan roughly **205-245 mission-ready** land/search aircraft can support the Gilbert-forward system. This is a network, not a single morning strike count.
- US post-Tarawa learning is real and historical in direction: more recon, bombardment ammunition, close-support ships, LVT/engineer robustness and repeated reattack. But DD roles, carrier CAP/search, reef lanes, beach exits, fuel, assault transports and command bandwidth create hard throughput limits.
- Kwajalein-direct remains the centered US option, but **no final FLINTLOCK D-Day/OOB is fixed here**.

## Important correction / clarification

Tarawa's post-capture airfield has two clocks: the repaired existing surface can accept emergency/garrison fighters quickly (roughly 1-2 Dec in this Branch), while the permanent fighter/bomber base complex takes weeks. Historical Seabee records show the permanent fighter strip became usable around 18 Dec and aircraft arrived around 20 Dec; the Branch closeout therefore does **not** treat 1 Dec as a mature high-output air base.
