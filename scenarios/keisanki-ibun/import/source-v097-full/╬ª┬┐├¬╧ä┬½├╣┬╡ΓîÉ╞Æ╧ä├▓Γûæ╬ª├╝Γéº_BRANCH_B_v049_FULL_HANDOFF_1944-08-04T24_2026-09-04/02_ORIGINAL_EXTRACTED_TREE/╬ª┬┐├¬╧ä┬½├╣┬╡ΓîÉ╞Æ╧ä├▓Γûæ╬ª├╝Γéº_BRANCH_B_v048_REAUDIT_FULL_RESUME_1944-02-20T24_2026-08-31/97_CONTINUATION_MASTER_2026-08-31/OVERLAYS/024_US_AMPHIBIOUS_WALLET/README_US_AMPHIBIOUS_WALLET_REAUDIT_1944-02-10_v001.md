# US amphibious wallet re-audit — 1944-02-10

Status: **WORKING / replaces the implicit assumption that CATCHPOLE must wait on FLINTLOCK assault-LVT repair.**

## Main finding

The key correction is force separation. Historical CATCHPOLE's reserve formation sailed with its own **106 amtracs and 17 amphibian tanks**, plus a nine-transport core, **9 LSTs, 6 LCIs, Ashland (LSD)** and associated support. Therefore the Branch's FLINTLOCK LVT losses in the 4th Marine and 7th Infantry assault packages do **not** automatically reduce CATCHPOLE assault vehicle count.

The unnamed 5-Feb Branch LST mission-kill is also not automatically one of the dedicated CATCHPOLE nine. Its event geometry placed it in the southern FLINTLOCK tractor/landing echelon. Until a named hull/OOB trace says otherwise, it is a theater repair/escort burden rather than a direct -1 to the CATCHPOLE nine-LST package.

## Tarawa vehicle closeout

Historical Tarawa committed 125 LVTs and ended with only 35 operational. The Branch differs materially because the 24-Nov tide/observed-depth plan permits verified LCVP/LCM use later on D-Day, reducing multi-day exposed reef shuttle demand. The working closeout is therefore **71-85 immediately useful LVTs (center 77)** on 29 Nov, with **18-24 irrecoverable (center 21)**, rather than importing the historical 90-out-of-action result.

This is a major equipment/cadre preservation effect, but it stays with the owning/rebuilding formations unless later transferred. It is not free CATCHPOLE lift.

## Experience versus wear

Removing historical Guadalcanal/Solomons does not simply make amphibious units better. Some hull wear is avoided, but some boat crews also lose historical combat experience. Branch Tarawa supplies unusually strong institutional learning. These must remain separate effects rather than a single readiness multiplier.
