# 16. Global mandatory personnel rule

Effective immediately for all future and all re-opened historical intervals:

> **No named historical death, wounding, relief, promotion, or appointment is inherited merely because the historical calendar date arrives.**

Every personnel transition must pass:

1. Did the causal Branch event occur?
2. Was the person physically present?
3. Did the relevant ship/unit survive long enough for evacuation?
4. Did the person's Branch combat record change reputation/fitness?
5. Does rank/seniority permit the proposed assignment?
6. Is a more suitable surviving officer now available?
7. Does the proposed headquarters itself still exist in Branch organization?

Historical assignment remains a prior, not a result.

- its flag officer,

- unwounded,
- psychologically/politically unaffected,

For every major hull loss:
**ship damage clock → abandon/flag-transfer clock → rescue clock → personnel casualty state → career consequence.**

# 19. Net strategic interpretation

The personnel divergence is asymmetric, but not because the Allies lose all their admirals.

## Japan
Main gain:
- Yamamoto survives;
- Yamaguchi survives;
- carrier and surface command pools remain deeper;
- many Solomons-derived deaths/disgraces disappear;
- more veteran carrier organizations survive;
- expanding 1944 carrier hull schedule can be matched to more command/training layers.

## United States
- 1942 carrier hull losses are catastrophic;
- most top flag officers survive centered;
- some historical Guadalcanal flag deaths are also avoided;
- individual career paths change sharply;
- industrial and training depth rebuilds the middle layer.

## Britain
- major modern carrier capacity and FAA/deck cadre loss;
- senior flag survival centered.

Therefore the Japanese advantage should be modeled as:
**better continuity of mature operational organizations and more simultaneous command bandwidth**, not as a generic leadership-stat bonus.
