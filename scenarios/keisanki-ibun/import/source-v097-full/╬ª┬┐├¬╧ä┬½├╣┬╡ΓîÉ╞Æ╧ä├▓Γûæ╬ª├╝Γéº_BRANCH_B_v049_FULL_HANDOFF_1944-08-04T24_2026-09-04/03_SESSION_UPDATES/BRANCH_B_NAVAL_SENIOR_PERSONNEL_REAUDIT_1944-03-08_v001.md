# 8. Main comparative conclusion

The Branch does **not** center on wholesale Allied admiral decapitation.

Instead:

### Japan
- saves Yamamoto from a unique COMINT/ambush kill;
- saves Yamaguchi from voluntarily remaining on a doomed carrier that is not doomed in Branch;
- saves much of the carrier-division / deck / aviation staff system because Hiryu/Soryu/Shokaku/Zuikaku survive;
- avoids much Solomon-era destroyer-command attrition/disfavor;
- has enough senior officers to split strategic, fleet, tactical-carrier, heavy-surface and night-surface command layers.

### United States
- loses five prewar fleet carriers but most senior flag officers can physically evacuate;
- likely retains Spruance, Mitscher and Fitch;
- Fletcher and especially Kinkaid remain casualty-sensitive;
- suffers a much larger loss of ship-level carrier specialists, department heads, staff officers and experienced deck/aviation personnel than the flag-officer table alone shows;
- industrial/training depth subsequently repairs much of this middle-layer deficit.

### Britain
- loses flight decks and FAA/deck cadre far more severely than flag command.

The Japanese advantage is therefore best described as **command bandwidth + carrier institutional memory**, not merely "more famous admirals survive."
