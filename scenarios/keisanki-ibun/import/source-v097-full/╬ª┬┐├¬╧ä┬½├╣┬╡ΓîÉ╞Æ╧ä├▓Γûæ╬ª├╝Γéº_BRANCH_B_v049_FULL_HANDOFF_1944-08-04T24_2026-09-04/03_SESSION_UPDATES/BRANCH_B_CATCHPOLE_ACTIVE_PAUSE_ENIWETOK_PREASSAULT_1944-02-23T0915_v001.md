## 1. U.S. decision on 22 Feb

At 09:30 on 22 Feb Engebi is operationally secure, but:
- 1/22 and 2/22 are materially fatigued and depleted;
- elements of 3/22, Marine tanks and engineers were tied to the Engebi night/dawn sweep;
- the captured-intelligence clock is still running and deliberate Japanese burning plus U.S. reduction demolitions lower document yield;
- the 106th Infantry assault battalions remain comparatively fresh;
- the amphibious-vehicle wallet is largely intact but has repair/crew-fatigue backlog;
- medium tanks require rearm/refuel/recovery and a lagoon transfer from the Engebi sector.

Decision: **NO 22-Feb afternoon assault.**
This is an **active pause**, not a theater withdrawal.

Operational reason:
a same-day assault would begin late enough to buy a second night fight on a longer island while armor, engineer and intelligence support are below their next-morning state. The value of one extra day of fire preparation and support recovery is assessed greater than the value of denying Japan one additional day of field-expedient work.

## 9. Fortification-growth model for later islands

Branch fortification is modeled by layer and time, not a national scalar.

### Fast layer — days to 2 weeks
- camouflage/decoys;
- alternate foxholes and firing points;
- preregistration;
- small distributed ammo/water/medical stores;
- redundant wire/runner routes;
- burn/demolition protocols;
- local night routes and packet-sized reserves.

### Medium layer — 2 to 8 weeks
- more complete overhead cover;
- protected CPs and alternate CPs;
- better revetments and blast separation;
- improved gun/mortar alternate positions;
- anti-tank/LVT lanes, obstacles and demolition points;
- terrain-linked fallback compartments;
- more reliable repair/engineer plant use.

### Slow layer — 1 to 4+ months
- concrete works where cement/rebar/labor are actually delivered;
- cave/tunnel enlargement where geology permits;
- multiple exits, protected galleries and deeper ammunition storage;
- integrated reverse-slope/terrain-masked fire networks.

Guard:
More depth is **not automatically safer**. Engebi shows that a sealed or bypassed shelter can become a demolition target. Later Japanese design therefore trends toward:
- multiple exits;
- fire-and-displace positions;
- sacrificial visible positions covering deeper cells;
- anti-engineer ambushes;
- delayed/reappearing fire;
- decentralized night movement;
rather than merely "dig deeper."

### Marianas projection as of this checkpoint
By mid-June, if the westward shipping and engineer clocks remain healthy:

- Tinian's flatter geometry limits the depth gain more than Saipan/Guam;
