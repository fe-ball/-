# Branch B — Carrier-Air Personnel Cellularization Overlay v001

## Scope
Applies from modified MI (Jun 1942) through the current Mar 1944 frontier and forward into Taiho / Unryu-class work-up.

Status: **WORKING INSTITUTIONAL OVERLAY**
Clock advance: **none**

This overlay connects:
- air-ground separation,
- natural personnel circulation,
- carrier-air portability,
- preserved carrier hulls/cadres,
- senior command depth,
- new-carrier construction.

---

## 1. Core interpretation

By early 1944, Branch IJN carrier aviation is no longer best represented as:

> one carrier hull = one permanently attached air group = one permanently attached support organization.

It is better represented as four partially separable layers:

1. **Hull-organic layer**
   - flight-deck crews;
   - arresting/handling/fueling organization;
   - shipboard aviation fuel/ordnance spaces;
   - ship communications;
   - ship engineering/damage control.

2. **Mobile flying layer**
   - pilots / observers / radio-gunners;
   - flight leaders and attack-group staff;
   - directly attached aircraft maintenance;
   - type-specific ordnance/radio specialists;
   - minimum portable spares/tools.

3. **Carrier-division / fleet pool**
   - replacement crews;
   - recently recovered wounded/fatigued personnel;
   - cross-deck qualified personnel;
   - navigation/search specialists;
   - temporary instructors;
   - work-up cadres;
   - personnel awaiting a repaired/new carrier.

4. **Training / regeneration layer**
   - schools and conversion units;
   - ferry/aircraft-acceptance organizations;
   - experimental/test units;
   - future Taiho/Unryu/Amagi cadre.

The important Branch change is not that layer 2 becomes infinitely interchangeable with layer 1.
It is that layers 2–4 are increasingly managed independently from an individual hull.

---

## 2. Why “cell division” occurs

### Jun–Sep 1942: forced first split
Modified MI creates:
- wounded and convalescent experienced personnel;
- surviving Hiryu/Soryu cadres;
- carrier repair periods;
- a major fleet reorganization window.

Historical Guadalcanal emergency consumption does not interrupt the reorganization at the same intensity.

Experienced personnel naturally accumulate ashore and in repair/work-up organizations.

### Late 1942–early 1943: nuclei appear
Reorganization reveals that a new/rebuilt unit reaches useful performance faster if it contains:
- experienced flight leaders;
- navigation/search personnel;
- experienced direct-maintenance leaders;
- deck/air-department petty-officer seeds.

The Navy does not need a revolutionary “instructor rotation” policy to discover this.

### 1943: separation becomes repeatable
Air-ground separation trials and carrier-air portability make it possible to:
- keep the veteran core of a frontline unit;
- peel off a small experienced nucleus;
- combine that nucleus with new personnel;
- work the new cell up separately;
- later recombine or cross-deck as required.

### Late 1943–early 1944: demand institutionalizes the process
The system now has continuing demand from:
- repaired Hiryu/Soryu;
- surviving Shokaku/Zuikaku;
- second-line carriers;
- Taiho;
- future Unryu/Amagi;
- land-based deployments requiring carrier-qualified personnel.

What began as ad hoc redistribution becomes a personnel pipeline.

---

## 3. “More personnel” — what this actually means

Branch does **not** create pilots from nothing.

The apparent personnel abundance comes from six effects:

1. fewer carrier organizations destroyed with their ships;
2. less Guadalcanal/Rabaul-style sequential consumption;
3. wounded/fatigued personnel survive and return;
4. repaired carriers temporarily put experienced personnel ashore;
5. dedicated ferry/acceptance functions reduce waste of combat aircrew;
6. air-ground separation prevents every transfer from dragging an entire base organization with it.

Therefore the Navy sees a larger **usable qualified pool** than a hull-by-hull OOB suggests.

It can simultaneously have:
- frontline personnel;
- people in short requalification;
- new-carrier cadres;
- temporary instructors;
- replacement personnel;
- carrier-qualified personnel currently operating ashore.

This can look “oddly manpower-rich” without increasing total naval manpower by an implausible amount.

## 4. March 1944 working personnel bands

These are planning bands, not a final name-by-name roster.

### Four established first-line carriers
Aircraft immediately combat-usable: **225–255**

Corresponding flying personnel needed for the operational aircraft mix:
- approximately **420–500 individual aircrew**
  (single-seat fighters plus 2–3-man attack/recon aircraft)

### Carrier-compatible flying personnel outside the immediate four-core assignment
Working pool:
- **180–300**

Includes:
- rear/second-line decks;
- temporary shore-based carrier-qualified crews;
- convalescent/requalification personnel;
- replacement crews;
- Taiho work-up personnel;
- cadre/instructor personnel.

### Total carrier-compatible flying-personnel system
Working March band:
**~600–800 personnel**

This is not “800 elite pilots.”
It contains different qualification levels:
- first-line day/night carrier;
- day-carrier only;
- search/navigation specialist;
- land-combat qualified but carrier requalification required;
- work-up/training status.

### Key result
The four frontline carriers do not consume the entire carrier-compatible manpower pool.
A meaningful personnel margin exists for Taiho work-up and prospective Unryu/Amagi cadre formation.

## 5. Experienced nucleus rule

Do not seed a new carrier unit by taking an equal percentage of everybody.

A new/rebuilt carrier-air cell preferentially receives experienced personnel in bottleneck positions:

### Flying organization
- air-group / flight leadership;
- section/division leaders;
- long-range navigation/search;
- torpedo/dive-bomb coordination;
- radio/formation control.

### Maintenance
- senior type-specific maintainers;
- ordnance supervisors;
- radio/instrument leaders.

### Ship/air interface
- selected experienced flight-deck / air-department petty officers;
- landing-control / signal personnel;
- fueling/arming supervisors.

Working principle:
- **small experienced nucleus, large new body**;
- veteran frontline unit retains most of its experienced mass.

For planning, an experienced nucleus may occupy roughly **15–30% of leadership/specialist billets** in a new cell while representing a much smaller fraction of the total carrier crew.

This is not a universal 15–30% personnel transfer rule.

---

## 6. Hull-organic personnel are the main brake

Aircrew portability must not be confused with instant carrier readiness.

A new carrier still requires a hull-specific team for:
- deck handling;
- fueling/arming routes;
- elevator timing;
- arresting-gear practice;
- damage control around aviation spaces;
- deck-cycle communications;
- night/emergency recovery.

Therefore:

> aircraft + carrier-qualified pilots ≠ combat-ready carrier.

Taiho/Unryu/Amagi readiness remains gated by hull-team work-up.

This prevents the personnel-pool mechanism from becoming a magical “instant fifth/sixth carrier” bonus.

---

## 7. Command-level cellularization

The senior-personnel reaudit makes the same process occur above the air-group level.

### Strategic layer
Yamamoto — Combined Fleet release authority.

### Operational carrier-fleet layer
Ozawa — First Mobile Fleet.

### Tactical carrier layer
Yamaguchi — principal first-line carrier striking-group command.

### Work-up / future-carrier layer
A separate prospective training/new-carrier staff can form without forcing Ozawa or Yamaguchi to personally supervise every new deck.

Thus the command organization itself can divide in parallel with the air groups.

This is one of Yamaguchi's highest Branch values.

---

## 8. Taiho effect

Taiho is the first major test of the system.

Correct process:
1. hull acceptance/trials;
2. ship-organic aviation department work-up;
3. experienced nucleus arrives;
4. new personnel fill out the body;
5. cross-deck and shore practice;
6. limited CAP/recovery qualification;
7. independent strike package;
8. integration with first-line carrier group.

Taiho therefore consumes some veteran cadres but should **not** hollow out Shokaku/Zuikaku/Soryu/Hiryu.

---

## 9. Unryu / Amagi effect

By March 1944 the Navy knows these hulls are coming.

Therefore the personnel system starts before physical completion:

- prospective captains/XOs and air officers identified;
- air-group HQ nuclei formed;
- deck/air-department seed personnel reserved;
- future flying crews tracked by qualification;
- instructors protected from immediate frontline absorption;
- a second carrier tactical-group headquarters planned.

The key planning problem shifts from:
> “where do we find an entire air group when the ship finishes?”

to:
> “how many trained nuclei can we mature without degrading the first-line four?”

This is a much healthier institutional problem.

---

## 10. Consequences for personnel accounting

Future carrier OOB must track at least:

### Flying personnel
- physical;
- medically fit;
- aircraft/type qualified;
- carrier qualified;
- night qualified;
- immediately mission-ready;
- assigned;
- reserve/work-up/training.

### Maintenance / air-department personnel
- physical;
- type-qualified;
- ship-specific qualified;
- assigned;
- portable direct-maintenance;
- hull-organic deck/air department.

### Command cadres
- carrier-fleet HQ;
- carrier tactical-group HQ;
- carrier-division HQ;
- air-group HQ;
- work-up/training HQ.

No single “pilot count” or “air group count” can represent the system.

---

## 11. Failure modes / guards

The system can still break.

### Fuel
More surviving personnel require more training fuel.
A fuel squeeze can leave a large qualified-on-paper pool with inadequate current proficiency.

### Over-expansion
Taiho + Unryu + Amagi + later Katsuragi can grow faster than:
- hull-specific deck teams;
- navigators;
- strike leaders;
- experienced maintenance supervisors.

### Battle loss
A carrier battle can destroy the exact experienced nuclei that make the system reproduce.

### Emergency commitment
If Japan repeatedly sends work-up cadres into combat, the “cell division” mechanism reverses and begins consuming its seed stock.

### Qualification mismatch
A large land-based aircrew pool is not automatically a carrier-air pool.

---

## 12. March 1944 interpretation

At the current frontier, the IJN carrier-air system is entering a **rapid organizational mitosis phase**.

It is not yet an American replacement-group system.

But it has moved beyond:
> “each carrier owns one indivisible air group.”

The better model is:
> **surviving veteran organizations retain combat cores while repeatedly budding experienced nuclei into repair, training, new-carrier and second-line formations.**

This is why personnel can appear unusually numerous in early 1944:
many are alive, qualified, and institutionally usable even when they are not currently counted in the four frontline carriers' immediately committed strike package.
