**D2.3–D2.7**

4. alternate entrances/exits;

6. decoy storage and dummy work areas;
7. repair teams distributed before the next raid.

The raid therefore slows construction for days while making the later Palau defensive architecture more “late-war” in form.

# 14. Nagumo night/surface system

The Palau raid becomes the first real strategic test of the retained First Fleet/night-surface concept.

Result:
**NO surface sortie.**

- Davao/Lingga-based groups cannot reach a fresh carrier contact before it expires;
- a 600+ nm dash toward Palau risks arriving into daylight carrier-air geometry;
- no transport anchorage or crippled U.S. subgroup is fixed.

Operational lesson:
Nagumo's system is not a standing “night attack whenever carriers appear” machine.
It is a **contact-quality + darkness + egress** gate.

The headquarters nonetheless uses Palau reports to refine:
- submarine/air-to-surface contact handoff;
- approach timing;
- second-salvo doctrine;
- dawn breakoff thresholds.

## Japan
Success:
- main fleet preserved;
- high-value logistics mostly preserved;
- 14th Division not sailed into the raid;
- ISR proves useful for dispersal.

Cost:
- 62–78 aircraft irrecoverable/abandoned;
- fixed Palau/Yap/Woleai base capacity badly hit;
- 2–4 merchant/auxiliary ships plus small craft lost;
- mines impose real routing delay;
- 14th Division arrival slips several days from the aggressive Branch target;
- fortification material/plant losses slow near-term construction.

## Main operational lesson
The Branch Japanese system can preserve **mobile value** from a carrier raid.
It still cannot protect **fixed value** from a 12-deck TF58.

This reinforces the strategic concept:
- islands must delay/fix the amphibious system;
- mobile fleet must not be exchanged merely to save a base from carrier suppression.
