# 1. The correct unit of analysis

By early 1944 Branch IJN carrier aviation is not one indivisible object per hull.

It consists of four linked personnel systems:

### A. hull-organic carrier team
- deck handling;
- landing signals;
- elevator/hangar cycle;
- aviation fuel/ordnance movement;
- ship-air communications;
- aviation-space damage control;
- hull-specific engineering and repair.

### B. mobile flying/direct-maintenance cell
- pilots;
- observers/navigators/radio-gunners;
- flight and attack leaders;
- directly attached maintainers;
- type-specific radio/ordnance/instrument specialists;
- minimum portable spares/tools.

### C. carrier-fleet qualification pool
- replacement crews;
- wounded/recovered personnel;
- recently rotated veterans;
- cross-deck-qualified aircrew;
- personnel temporarily on secondary decks or ashore;
- requalification and conversion personnel.

### D. reproduction/work-up system
- training establishments;
- ferry/aircraft-acceptance organizations;
- test/experimental units;
- Taiho work-up;
- prospective Unryu/Amagi/Katsuragi cadres;
- temporary instructors and seed cadres.

A carrier becomes a combat system only where A+B are both mature and C/D can sustain them.

# 2. Why cellularization accelerates in this Branch

## 1942 MI creates personnel circulation
Modified MI destroys Akagi/Kaga but preserves Soryu/Hiryu.
Shokaku/Zuikaku also survive the 1942 campaign.

This creates:
- repair periods without total organizational destruction;
- wounded/recovered veterans;
- personnel temporarily detached from damaged hulls;
- several intact carrier organizations available for comparison and cross-training.

The historical Guadalcanal/Rabaul emergency consumption is greatly reduced.

Therefore personnel who would historically be fed forward and lost can finish reorganization and training cycles.

## 1943 creates repeatable rules
Air-ground separation and qualification tracking make it possible to:
- keep a veteran fighting core;
- remove a small number of scarce leaders/specialists;
- combine them with new graduates and intermediate personnel;
- mature a new/rebuilt cell ashore or on a secondary deck;
- later assign that cell to a repaired/new carrier.

## 1944 creates continuous demand
The system now has simultaneous consumers:
- four established first-line carriers;
- six second-line carrier hulls;
- Taiho;
- expected Unryu and Amagi;
- later Katsuragi;
- shore-based naval air units needing carrier-qualified specialists;
- training/ferry/test organizations.

Ad hoc circulation therefore becomes a de facto pipeline.

# 5. Why the number can look surprisingly large

The apparent surplus is mostly a visibility effect.

Historical hull-by-hull OOB accounting tends to hide personnel who are:
- ashore while their carrier repairs;
- between units;
- in short requalification;
- on secondary carriers;
- ferrying/training;
- recovering from wounds;
- allocated to future units.

Branch air-ground separation and personnel tracking make these people visible as a common qualification pool.

The pool is larger in absolute terms too, because:
- fewer carrier organizations are annihilated;
- fewer Guadalcanal/Rabaul sequential losses occur;
- more wounded personnel return;
- second-line decks survive and perform training/ferry functions;
- combat aircrew are progressively removed from routine replacement-aircraft ferry duty.

# 6. Shipboard deck/air personnel: the other half of cellularization

Flying personnel can be redistributed faster than hull-organic deck personnel.

The Branch has **11 physical carrier hulls** on 8 Mar:
- 4 established first-line;
- Taiho;
- 6 second-line.

That means the IJN possesses a large physical population of:
- flight-deck handlers;
- aviation-ordnance personnel;
- fueling teams;
- air-department signalmen;
- aircraft elevator/hangar personnel;
- shipboard aviation maintenance supervisors.

These are numbered in the thousands across the carrier establishment, but they are **not one interchangeable pool**.

Useful classifications:
1. fully qualified on present hull;
2. cross-deck experienced on similar fleet carrier;
3. carrier-experienced but requires new-hull familiarization;
4. secondary-carrier experienced;
5. novice/new-build crew.

The important Branch advantage is that categories 2–4 are much larger than historical late-1944 Japan.

# 7. The reproduction mechanism

The Navy does not duplicate a veteran air group by halving it.

It buds off scarce experience.

## Experienced nucleus positions
### flying side
- air-group/flight leadership;
- section/division leaders;
- torpedo/dive-bomb coordination;
- long-range navigation/search;
- radio/formation control.

### maintenance side
- senior type-specific maintainers;
- ordnance supervisors;
- radio/instrument leaders.

### hull-air interface
- selected deck/air-department petty officers;
- landing-control personnel;
- fueling/arming supervisors.

A new cell can therefore contain:
- a **small experienced nucleus**;
- a much larger body of new/intermediate personnel.

Working guard:
experienced personnel may occupy roughly **15–30% of bottleneck leadership/specialist billets** in a new cell while representing a much smaller share of total manpower.

This is not a 15–30% transfer from every frontline unit.

# 8. Reproduction rate

Under protected training conditions and no major carrier battle:

## Four veteran carrier groups
Can collectively support:
- Taiho maturation;
- two prospective future-carrier nuclei;
- continued four-core regeneration,

without hollowing the four-core.

But the process is not exponential in aircraft count.

### Approximate institutional cadence
- one substantial new carrier-air/deck nucleus can be matured every **4–8 weeks** across the total carrier establishment;
- several smaller specialist nuclei can be formed in parallel;
- a finished combat air group still needs aircraft, fuel, full-body personnel and a hull work-up clock.

Thus “cell division” is fast at the **cadre** level and slower at the **complete combat-system** level.

# 9. Secondary carriers as incubators

The six second-line decks are one of the most important hidden Branch assets.

They absorb:
- first carrier qualifications;
- day landing practice;
- replacement crews;
- ferry/rear-CAP duty;
- cross-deck training;
- limited combat tasks.

Therefore Shokaku/Zuikaku/Hiryu/Soryu do not have to spend all their deck cycles training novices.

This improves:
- first-line readiness;
- new-carrier maturation;
- retention of experienced deck teams.

The benefit is organizational, not a combat multiplier.

# 12. Katsuragi

Branch completion target:
**Aug–Sep 1944**

Its main significance in Mar 1944 is personnel planning.

If Unryu and Amagi each consume all available cadres, Katsuragi becomes the point where the system starts cannibalizing itself.

This is the clearest sense in which the system becomes self-reproducing.

# 13. Three different growth speeds

Carrier strength must be modeled as three clocks.

## Fast: command/cadre replication
Weeks.
- leaders;
- staff procedures;
- standardized cards/checklists;
- selected maintenance/deck supervisors.

## Medium: flying-unit maturation
Weeks to months.
- conversion;
- navigation;
- formation;
- weapons;
- carrier landing;
- multi-seat crew coordination.

## Slow: hull-specific carrier team
Months around completion/work-up.
- deck cycle;
- damage control;
- elevator/hangar rhythm;
- emergency recovery;
- night/adverse-weather operation.

The slow clock prevents “personnel abundance” from generating instant carrier divisions.

---

# 14. Fuel becomes more important as manpower improves

Personnel preservation creates a new problem:
more qualified people require more training fuel.

If fuel is rationed, the Branch can have:
- a large qualified roster;
- but declining current proficiency.

Therefore the true reproduction equation is:

**surviving cadre × training fuel × usable decks × aircraft availability × time**

not simply surviving cadre.

The second-line carrier system is valuable precisely because it converts fuel expenditure into deck qualifications without consuming all veteran-core deck time.

---

# 15. Battle-risk inversion

The cellular system raises Japan's option value of waiting.

But it also creates concentrated high-value personnel stocks.

A carrier battle that kills:
- strike leaders;
- navigators/observers;
- deck supervisors;
- newly formed Taiho/Unryu cadre

can destroy several future cells at once.

Therefore future battle accounting must charge:
1. aircraft;
2. flying crew;
3. rare qualification;
4. cadre role;
5. future-unit allocation.

A lost “one experienced navigator” may have more downstream value than one replacement aircraft.

# 16. Historical sanity anchors

The concept is not based on a fictional late-war personnel institution appearing from nowhere.

Historical IJN itself:
- formed division-level carrier air groups in Feb 1944;
- prepared the 601st NAG for CarDiv 1;
- worked Taiho through trials and carrier training before fleet integration;
- completed Unryu/Amagi later in 1944, but their operational value was crippled by the historical shortage of trained carrier aircrew.

Branch changes the preconditions:
- more veteran carriers and organizations survive;
- personnel circulation begins earlier;
- air-ground separation is implemented earlier;
- the second-line carrier pool is larger;
- training/cadre losses are smaller.

# 17. Current March 8 model

The most accurate shorthand is:

> **The IJN does not have “too many elite pilots.” It has an unusually large carrier-aviation ecosystem with enough surviving qualified nuclei to reproduce organizations faster than historical Japan.**

Current hierarchy:

- four veteran combat cells;
- six heterogeneous second-line/training decks;
- one Taiho cell rapidly maturing;
- two Unryu/Amagi shadow cells authorized to form;
- one later Katsuragi demand already visible;
- command layer split between fleet-level and tactical carrier-level leadership.

This is rapid organizational mitosis, but every daughter cell still has to pay aircraft, fuel, qualification and hull-work-up costs.
