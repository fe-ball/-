# 1. The original Branch compute doctrine that must be re-applied

The original canon explicitly says:

## A. Historical difference is NOT an implicit ceiling
Source:
`.../03_検討資料_設定正本/00_常時コンテキスト_研究規約と正本索引.md`

Core rule:
> 史実から離れすぎるという理由だけで、当時の理論、入力、計算量、材料、試験、工場で回収可能な欠陥を残してはならない。

This means the next analysis must **not** force B7A to stay within a few percent of historical performance simply because a larger gain feels “too magical.”

## B. Six-condition recovery rule
The same canon says computation effects are judged by six gates:
1. physics known at the time;
2. necessary inputs measurable from drawings/material/model/full-scale tests;
3. computation quantity feasible;
4. output convertible to usable tables/curves/cams/etc.;
5. discovery arrives in time for design/detail/build/next-lot correction;
6. no concrete institutional/budget/manufacturing reason prevents adoption.

**If all are met, the defect is to be recovered in principle.**

This is a much stronger rule than “use calculation mainly for QC.”

## C. Four compute-effect categories
1. mass repetition of conventional calculations;
2. design-space exploration;
3. uncertainty / tolerance / variation analysis;
4. shortened design–test–production feedback loop.

## D. Benefits can be absorbed or re-invested
The canon explicitly allows recovered margin to move into:
- speed;
- range/fuel;
- weapons;
- protection;
- structure/life;
- landing gear;
- radios/equipment;
- manufacturing tolerance;
- reliability/growth margin.

The phrase “computer does not directly create horsepower” does **not** mean “actual aircraft performance gains must be small.”
Recovered drag/weight/cooling/structural margin can be deliberately re-invested into real mission performance.

## E. Threshold amplification / second- and third-order propagation
The technology-to-military-effect canon distinguishes:
- threshold amplification;
- direct force conversion;
- absorption/reinvestment;
- bottleneck/time-window attenuation;
- enemy adaptation;
- personnel circulation/organizational mixing.

It also warns against the opposite error:
> individual improvements being small does not mean the whole weapon/system is only ~10% different.

Other canon examples explicitly use **two-stage / three-stage propagation** where several-percent design gains become much larger mission-value differences.

For Ryusei this is central:
- lower empty/structural weight may permit more fuel or stronger landing structure;
- lower drag may permit equal speed at lower engine stress or greater radius;
- better dive-brake/control analysis may permit a larger safe dive envelope;
- better torpedo-release geometry may expand tactical approach choices;
- improved wing/gear/fold/CG integration may cut deck-cycle penalties;
- better production uniformity may make the designed performance routine rather than exceptional;
- one common attack-airframe family may reduce spare-airframe and maintenance fragmentation;
- two-person crew vs B6N three-person crew changes personnel reproduction rates.

All of these must be traced separately and then recombined at mission level.
