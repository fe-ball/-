## 1. Why the audit was restarted earlier than MO
User decided cumulative burden must start before the dramatic divergence, when Branch differences are mainly submarine and destroyer performance/availability. The purpose is to prevent later escort/patrol/shipping burdens from appearing ex nihilo.

The agreed conceptual sequence is:

## 2. Net-accounting method for bases, ships, and campaigns
User emphasized the “dead employee” analogy: when an asset is destroyed/captured, its maintenance cost also disappears, but its service disappears too, and a captured asset/base may actively work against the former owner.

Use seven layers:
1. physical loss;
2. lost service;
3. avoided maintenance / avoided historical expenditure;
4. substitute used;
5. substitute opportunity cost;
6. delay/degradation interest;
7. enemy use/hostile service.

Examples:
- Midway loss saves US garrison/base expansion bulk cargo, but loses search/submarine forward-base services and creates containment/suppression demand.
- Watchtower not occurring saves assault shipping, carrier/fleet days and historical surface losses, but also means the US does not obtain Henderson/forward-network returns.
- Santo/NC losses may save future base construction after capture, but destroy already-sunk capital and force a new defense line farther back.

## 3. Delay-interest model
Do not dice the exact cargo of every sunk merchant ship. Use expected cargo vectors by ship type/route/month and then model substitution.

Distinguish:
- consumables;
- combat capital goods;
- logistics capital goods (locomotives, trucks, cranes, bridge/road machinery), which can create compounding delay by postponing throughput growth;
- low-mass special items/spares/radar/engines that can hold entire units idle.

Delay interest is lower where downstream reception is already congested (e.g. Persian Corridor queue), but physical loss remains real and bottleneck-breaking equipment can have high positive feedback value.

## 4. Major-unit “option value”
User clarified Yamato analogy: a major ship matters not only because it might fight, but because **friendly staffs can design operations knowing it exists in reserve**. Its destruction removes whole branches of feasible plans even if those plans would probably not have required actual deployment.

Track four values:
- current combat power;
- friendly option value/planning confidence;
- enemy fleet-in-being burden;
- infrastructure substitution avoided by having mobile force.

Carrier loss has all four strongly. In 1942 Pacific, loss of mobile carrier reserve forces US to duplicate fixed airbase infrastructure across uncertain islands and Australia/NZ lines.

## 5. US South Pacific — main under-audited issue
Post-NC US containment/fortification was under-modeled.

The cycle likely is:
- PM loss: save PM maintenance but push airfield/logistics deeper into Queensland;
- carrier losses: fixed-base substitution increases;
- Santo: US rapidly fortifies, loses part of that capital, evacuates high-value mobile items;
- NC: US again concentrates defensive capital, loses substantial fixed/heavy equipment despite improved demolition/evacuation planning;
- post-NC: build a second defense line in Fiji/Tonga/Samoa/NZ/Australia, maintain security stockpiles, longer Australia route, containment patrol/search/escort and fast-surface/oiler insurance.

Earlier work correctly included Watchtower non-occurrence as an offset, but likely over-relied on that offset and undercounted repeated defensive capital and containment stockpiles.

A useful interpretation: before NC, total South Pacific shipping effort may be similar order to historical offensive effort but with much worse investment return; after NC, route tax + rebuild + containment make the Branch curve clearly worse.

## 8. South Pacific raids as “threat manifestation”
Legacy Efate raid concept should be reconsidered in the period after FS/NC and before US offensive momentum becomes strong.

Legacy raid battle results must be recomputed because old versions depended on Second Midway and old US carrier inventories.

## 11. Nanjing / legitimacy / finance
User reminded that “Nanjing munching” was introduced because political legitimacy divergence should become strongly self-reinforcing around MI–FS and Five-Go.

Effects should not be modeled as simplistic mass ideological conversion. Main channels:
- local elites hedge toward whichever regime is more likely to remain;
- railway/warehouse staff, police, local administration and merchants become more willing to work with Nanjing/Japan;
- local purchasing on credit, labor/cart/animal mobilization, tax collection and repair/restart become easier;
- sabotage intelligence improves;
- Japanese static guard workload drops;
- Chongqing’s relative currency/credit and long-term claim weakens;
- Chongqing units that physically survive may reconstitute more slowly because pay, procurement, horses/trucks, local cooperation and political confidence deteriorate.

Current 1944 audit already credits Nanjing with 3.0–3.8 Japanese division-equivalents of static-duty workload release theater-wide. The missing task is to reconstruct how this benefit ramps up from 1942H2 through Five-Go rather than appearing suddenly in 1944.
