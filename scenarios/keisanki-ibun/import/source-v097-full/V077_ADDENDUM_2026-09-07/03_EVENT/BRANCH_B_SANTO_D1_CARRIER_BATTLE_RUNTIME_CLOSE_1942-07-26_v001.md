## 0. Radar / warning closure

- U.S. Wasp-group radar/air-warning capability remains **historical 1942 capability**, per the inherited Santo air-tech authority. It gives useful warning but not perfect altitude/fighter direction.
- No current Branch authority explicitly advances an operational Japanese carrier radar set to 26 Jul 1942. Therefore **Shokaku/Zuikaku/Ryujo receive no carrier-radar warning credit on D-1 center**. Japanese warning is visual scout/contact reporting, CAP/lookout and formation warning.
- This is not a permanent denial of Japanese radar. A later explicit installation/event may change it.

## 2. Search geometry closure

At 00:00 the Japanese fleet carriers are ~240–270 nm N/NW of Santo and Wasp is remote cover south/southeast of the 2d-Marine transport line. During the morning both groups close the Santo approaches.

Center consequence:
- Japanese dawn search has a prior I-32 fix on the five-ship reinforcement convoy and therefore gets the first efficient search-sector reduction;
- Wasp's first dawn search is too early/wide to find the Japanese fleet-carrier pair;
- Wasp's **second** northern/northwestern search cycle reaches useful overlap after the forces close;
- reciprocal carrier contact is therefore plausible and centered, but Japan obtains the first Wasp fix by roughly an hour.

## 5. Carrier-attack center results

### Japanese attack on Wasp
U.S. radar gives real warning, but 1942 fighter direction is imperfect. North Carolina, San Juan and the screen add significant AA fire.

Center:
- Wasp takes **2 x 250-kg-class bomb hits** and one damaging near miss;
- **0 aerial torpedo hits** center;
- flight operations are suspended for roughly **2–2.5 h** while deck/fire damage is controlled and emergency repairs are made;
- propulsion remains usable; Wasp can make high enough speed to retire with the group;
- casualty working band: roughly **35–55 killed / 55–85 wounded**;
- Wasp is **MISSION-DEGRADED, NOT SUNK**.

U.S. fighter losses in defense center:
- **5 F4F destroyed**, 2 additional F4F damaged/non-MR.

Japanese strike losses center:
- **3 A6M, 5 D3A, 4 B5N destroyed**;
- several additional aircraft return damaged, but are not all write-offs.

### Wasp attack on Japanese fleet carriers
Japanese CAP is strong and veteran-heavy, but there is no center carrier-radar early-warning credit. The U.S. strike attacks the nearer/working lead fleet carrier, **Shokaku**.

Center:
- **2 x 1,000-lb SBD bomb hits on Shokaku**;
- no effective U.S. aerial-torpedo hit; Mk 13 performance remains poor;
- Shokaku remains afloat and maneuverable but **normal flight operations are unavailable for the rest of D-1**;
- casualty working band: roughly **60–100 total killed/wounded**;
- Shokaku is a **flight-deck mission kill for D-day opening**, not a sinking.

U.S. strike losses center:
- **2 F4F, 2 SBD, 2 TBF destroyed**;
- 1 SBD + 2 F4F divert to Efate during Wasp's emergency deck-repair window and are not counted as destroyed.

Japanese defensive/deck losses center:
- **2 A6M destroyed in CAP**;
- **2 additional aircraft destroyed and 3 damaged** on/near Shokaku during the bomb damage;
- because Shokaku cannot recover normally, returning aircraft are redistributed to Zuikaku/Ryujo; **3 damaged/low-fuel aircraft ditch** during the recovery congestion center.

## 6. Post-battle deck state

By mid-afternoon:

- **Shokaku:** afloat, able to maneuver, no normal D-day-opening flight-operation credit.
- **Zuikaku:** operational, absorbs part of Shokaku's returning air group.
- **Ryujo:** operational after its transport strike, but crowded by diverted aircraft and cannot be treated as a fresh untouched deck.

Launchable Japanese operational-deck center for D-day opening after attrition/crowding: **~78–84 MR**, centered **~81**, from Zuikaku + Ryujo. Aircraft physically associated with Shokaku but unable to obtain a usable operational deck are not added for free.

Wasp surviving carrier-air center after combat losses, damage and diversions: roughly **44–48 MR total surviving/repairable**, with fewer actually aboard during immediate retirement. Do not use this as Santo H-hour CAP credit.
