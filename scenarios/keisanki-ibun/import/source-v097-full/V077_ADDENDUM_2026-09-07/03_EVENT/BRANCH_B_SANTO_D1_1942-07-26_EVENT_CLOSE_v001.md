## 3. 07:20–09:20 — Ryujo attacks the reinforcement convoy

Because Wasp is still unlocated at the moment of release, Nagumo follows the closed role split and uses Ryujo for the first economical attempt rather than emptying Shokaku/Zuikaku.

Ryujo launches about **07:20–07:35**:

- 8 B5N with anti-shipping ordnance.

The strike reaches the convoy around **09:00–09:15**. Wasp is too far off the convoy for continuous close CAP; the two destroyers and shipboard AA must carry most of the immediate defense burden.

Center result:
- **Alhena (AK-26) takes one Type-91 aerial-torpedo hit** and loses effective propulsion;
- combined ship/embarked casualties: **~25–40 center band**;
- fires/flooding are controlled well enough that the ship does not sink, but it cannot maintain the 15-kt reinforcement run;
- **Buchanan remains with Alhena** for local protection/assistance;
- Crescent City, President Adams, President Hayes and President Jackson continue toward Santo with Wilson.

Japanese loss:
- 1 B5N destroyed by AA;
- 1 B5N damaged/non-MR after recovery center.

## 4. 08:35–10:10 — Japan finds Wasp; Wasp finds the fleet carriers

### ~08:43 — Japanese Wasp contact
A second D4Y1-C / fast confirmation sector searches south/southeast of the reinforcement track where carrier cover is operationally plausible. It physically identifies:
- one U.S. fleet carrier;
- a battleship-sized escort consistent with North Carolina;

The scout transmits promptly. The contact is fresh, has usable course/speed and meets **J-CARRIER-1** by roughly **~09:00**.

Nagumo immediately changes the principal fleet-carrier target from the transport problem to Wasp. The Ryujo transport strike is already airborne and continues.

### ~09:50–10:00 — Wasp second-cycle carrier contact
Wasp's first dawn scout cycle had been too early/far to find the main pair. A second north/northwestern pattern, informed by the stale Ryujo clue and the known invasion axis, now overlaps the closing Japanese carrier geometry.

An SBD scout physically identifies **two fleet carriers with escorts** around **~09:55 center**. The report is direct enough that Noyes has a tactically usable position within minutes.

Wasp has probably been compromised already and the contact meets **A-CARRIER-1**. Noyes does not wait for Ghormley approval until the position goes stale.

- 0 effective U.S. aerial-torpedo hits;

Zuikaku is not hit center.

## 6. 12:30–14:45 — recovery crisis and both carrier commands disengage from a second immediate duel

Shokaku cannot conduct normal recovery. Returning Japanese aircraft are redistributed to Zuikaku and Ryujo; deck crowding and damaged-aircraft fuel margins cause additional recovery losses. Nagumo preserves the surviving operational decks rather than launching a second blind anti-carrier wave on an aging Wasp track.

Wasp's damage-control parties restore a restricted emergency recovery window around **~13:45–14:05**. Most returning aircraft recover; a small number divert to Efate. Noyes then turns the damaged carrier group south/southeast.

Operational consequence by mid-afternoon:
- **Wasp is no longer Santo's H-hour carrier umbrella**;
- **Shokaku is no longer a D-day operational deck**;
- Zuikaku + Ryujo remain the Japanese immediate carrier-air pair.
