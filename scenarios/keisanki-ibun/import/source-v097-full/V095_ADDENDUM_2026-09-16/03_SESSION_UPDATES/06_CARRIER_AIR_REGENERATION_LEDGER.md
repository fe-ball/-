# Carrier-air regeneration ledger — post 21 Nov strike

## Accounting rule
Do not collapse these categories:
1. airframe irreversible loss
2. repairable airframe damage
3. flying-personnel irreversible/MIA
4. qualified-crew slot disruption
5. experienced leader loss
6. host/deck unavailability
7. direct-maintenance/ship-air-department availability
8. organizational retraining time

Individual deaths are irreversible. Unit capability is not permanently lost by definition; it regenerates through cadre, replacements, repair, cross-deck, rear-deck and land-base training.

## Japan
### First strike fixed values
- irreversible aircraft: 38
- damaged returns: 17–23
- flying personnel irreversible/MIA: 55–70
- four carrier hulls: intact

### Repair classes working band
- light damage 6–9: 24h candidate return
- medium damage 5–7: 2–5 day class
- heavy damage 4–7: rear repair / cannibalization candidate

### Regeneration character
- 0–24h: reorganize surviving crews, repair light damage, restore CAP/search/limited strike.
- 2–4d: individual replacements and secondary-carrier/rear-base transfers begin.
- 5–10d: new 3-plane/9-plane elements built around experienced cadre; numerical strike strength largely recoverable.
- 2–3wk: new team/formation proficiency stabilizes through repeated training.
- 1–2mo: pipeline effects can restore substantial average unit quality, subject to continued combat loss.

### Rear/secondary deck pool
Combat-usable secondary-carrier pool exists (~105–130 class in prior ledger) but is not an instantaneous forward bonus. It supplies ferry, CAP relief, deck-load sharing and regeneration only when location/escort/fuel/crew constraints are satisfied.
