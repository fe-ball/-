# Branch B — Santo D-day event close, 1942-07-27 v001

Status: **CLOSED CENTER EVENT; SANTO CAMPAIGN NOT YET FINISHED**
Cut: **1942-07-27 00:00–24:00, South-Pacific operational local time**
Authority: v078 D-day opening + v079 landing-geography/boat close + v075 commander doctrine.
Method: no random draw. Resolve from actual force geometry, boat throughput, fire support, air readiness, command behavior and event-created losses. Personalities affect decisions, not hit probability.

- carrier aviation: ~100–108 immediate MR, center ~104 across Zuikaku + Ryujo + limited Shokaku;
- Wasp believed hit/retiring, exact damage state unknown;
- Kongo/Kirishima/heavy group intact after the 26-Jul night bombardment.

Allies:
- immediate Santo defense: ~3,900–4,400 effective, center ~4,150;
- principal 3/10 artillery/heavy package absent with damaged Alhena;
- Turtle Bay non-operational and heavily cratered;
- Wasp retiring, H-hour CAP credit 0;
- VMF-212 remains an Efate pulse asset.

## 2. 00:00–05:15 — final approach

No center Allied submarine, mine or surface unit is physically closed in a position capable of breaking the Japanese final approach. The previous bombardment and known convoy track remove strategic surprise, but do not reveal the exact two-beach distribution before first light.

The six transports remain dispersed enough that a single observation does not turn them into one point target. Tanaka keeps the direct cover tight and pushes landing craft preparation under radio discipline.

Center: **no hull loss/damage before boat lowering.**

## 3. 05:15–06:05 — local suppression and first-wave launch

The heavy battleships do not repeat a full 300+ round bombardment. They clear/cover after the night mission. Closer support is provided by Jintsu/direct-cover destroyers and limited cruiser fire against already mapped AA/MG/road/airfield points.

Carrier H-hour allocation center:
- strong fleet/landing-area CAP and ready fighters retained because Wasp damage is not omnisciently known;
- one bounded landing-support package centered on roughly **10 A6M + 12 D3A + 8 B5N** against Turtle Bay/road/known defense points;
- search/recontact remains funded to the south/east;
- Shokaku contributes mainly bounded CAP/search/limited-cycle support rather than being treated as a fresh full-rate strike deck.

No carrier radar credit is introduced.

At ~06:05 the first craft touch:
- Turtle Bay / Vaucluse coast: ~900–1,000 Japanese personnel center;
- Peterson Point–Surundu coast: ~350–400.

## 4. 06:05–08:40 — first beach fight

### Turtle Bay main sector
Edson's forward posts and the local Marine screen identify the main landing quickly. The beach is not undefended. MG, 60/81-mm mortar and small-arms fire catches several craft and forces dispersal.

Japanese close support prevents the defenders from holding an uninterrupted beach-fire line. Engineers/beach-control parties get exits marked and successive craft are redirected around local fires/obstacles rather than repeating the same lane.

### Peterson/Surundu secondary sector
This sector meets a thinner initial screen. The landing is slower in surf/boat-handling terms but receives less concentrated rifle/mortar fire. It establishes a real lodgment rather than a feint.

By ~08:30 center:
- Japanese ashore physical: **~2,300–2,600**;
- immediately combat-effective/organized: **~1,850–2,100**;
- first 75-mm mountain guns: **3–4** ashore/entering action;
- heavy mortars: **~8–10** usable;
- landing craft: **1 destroyed/lost, 2–3 damaged/temporarily unavailable** center.

Japanese ground casualties through this phase: roughly **~140–190**.
Allied casualties/disruption: roughly **~110–160**.

## 5. 08:45–10:15 — Efate fighter pulse

VMF-212 can contest Santo only as a timed pulse. Eight F4F center make the northward run rather than creating an all-day CAP.

The Japanese have deliberately retained landing-area CAP; fighter-heavy Ryujo is particularly useful here.

Center air result:
- Allied: **3 F4F destroyed, 1 damaged/non-MR**;
- Japan: **2 A6M destroyed, 1 damaged/non-MR**;
- surviving F4Fs obtain only brief strafing opportunities before disengaging; one Japanese landing craft is additionally damaged, not destroyed.

The event demonstrates that Santo is not air-unopposed, but the Efate pulse cannot remain overhead long enough to stop the landing cycles.

## 6. 09:50–11:20 — Edson local counterattack

Edson does not wait passively for the main Japanese lodgment to mature. He assembles a bounded local counterattack from Raiders plus a Marine element against the northern/main beachhead's inland edge while Japanese guns and ammunition are still arriving.

The attack gains local surprise and temporarily checks one inland Japanese thrust. It does **not** reach the transport anchorage or destroy the beachhead.

Reason it stops:
- Japanese second/third boat cycles continue;
- mortars and first 75-mm pieces are now ashore;
- carrier aircraft and naval observers identify the counterattack concentration;
- Edson refuses to burn the entire Raider detachment in a daylight frontal fight once the opportunity closes.

Center ground result for the counterattack window:
- Japan: **~90–120 additional casualties**;
- Allies: **~70–100 casualties**, disproportionately Raiders/forward Marines;
- Japanese advance delayed roughly **~45–75 min** on the most threatened inland axis.

## 7. 10:55–12:05 — B-17 attack on the unloading system

Five B-17 center attack the now much less maneuverable transport/unloading area after PBY/shore reports provide a usable position.

The target is easier than a freely maneuvering convoy, but 1942 high-altitude anti-shipping conversion remains poor and Japanese CAP/AA are substantial.

Center:
- **0 direct bomb hits** on transports;
- one damaging near miss / splinter event on **Kazuura Maru** causes light damage and interrupts one unloading station for roughly **30–60 min**, with no propulsion mission kill;
- 1 B-17 damaged/non-MR on return; no center B-17 write-off.

This costs Japanese throughput but does not break the assault body.

## 8. 11:30–13:00 — Japanese lodgments become combined-arms

The Branch amphibious-process layer now matters in its intended form: not extra weapons, but earlier useful organization of the weapons already booked.

By ~12:30 center:
- Japanese physical ashore: **~4,200–4,600**;
- immediate combat-effective: **~3,450–3,750**;
- 75-mm mountain guns: **6–7** usable/being registered;
- infantry/battalion/AT guns: **5–7** usable;
- heavy mortars: **~15–18** usable;
- first useful trucks/tractors and engineer machines have begun leaving the beaches.

The secondary Peterson/Surundu lodgment reaches the coast-road system and becomes operationally important. Arthur must retain/shift a meaningful reserve southward rather than sending every available Marine to Turtle Bay.

## 9. 13:00–16:30 — fight for Turtle Bay strip

Nagumo does not empty every surviving deck against a ground target. A second bounded support cycle is generated from Zuikaku/Ryujo with Shokaku mainly supporting CAP/recovery under restricted deck conditions.

Working center support window: roughly **8–10 A6M + 9–11 D3A + 5–7 B5N** over the main ground axis, paid from the same 104-MR opening pool and after the morning fighter losses/fatigue.

Japanese ground force uses the morning delay to reorganize rather than conducting unsupported deep pursuit. Once guns, mortars, engineers and communications are synchronized, the main lodgment resumes the attack.

Allied problem:
- the field itself is already cratered/non-operational;
- principal 3/10 artillery is absent;
- the secondary landing prevents full concentration;
- the previous night's heavy bombardment has already damaged communications and fresh stores.

Arthur/Edson conduct a fighting withdrawal when holding the incomplete strip begins to risk encirclement without preserving an operating airfield.

### ~15:50–16:30 — Turtle Bay field falls physically
Center ruling:
- Japan **seizes the Turtle Bay construction strip / Fighter-1 site** and immediate beach-road approaches;
- Allied engineers/defenders destroy or disable selected remaining fuel, vehicles and construction stores during withdrawal;
- the captured strip is **not operational for Japanese aircraft** on D-day and requires damage clearance, security and engineering work before any Branch conversion clock can start.

The Allied withdrawal remains coherent enough to avoid a D-day rout. Edson's surviving Raiders fall back in small groups and re-form farther south/west.

## 10. 16:30–18:30 — eastern lodgments expand, but Luganville/Segond holds

The Peterson/Surundu force expands toward the road junctions but lacks the mass to storm the entire southeast base complex independently. The main force pushes south from Turtle Bay and begins reducing the gap between lodgments.

Ground control at dusk:

- Peterson/Surundu secondary lodgment: secure but not fully linked to the main body everywhere;
- east-coast road north of the main Allied southeast-base belt: partially Japanese controlled/interdicted;
- **Pallikulo/Luganville/Segond Channel: ALLIED HELD**;

## 15. Operational result at 27 Jul 24:00

Japan has achieved the **minimum D-day operational success condition**:
- sustainable main and secondary lodgments;
- >90% of booked assault personnel physically ashore or in final controlled landing movement;
- most first-day guns/mortars and a useful vehicle/engineer package ashore;
- Turtle Bay fighter-strip site seized;
- Allied airfield operation on Santo remains denied.

Japan has **not** completed the Santo capture:
- Luganville and Segond Channel remain Allied;
- Pallikulo/southeast base belt remains Allied-held/contested;
- defenders remain coherent and can fight D+1;
- Japanese carriers and aircrews have paid cumulative D-2/D-1/D-day costs;
- the second-echelon ~1,000-man reserve has not been added retroactively to D-day.

D+1 is therefore a ground/port-base reduction problem under Japanese local air/naval superiority, not a completed mop-up and not a failed landing.
