## 2. Historical/geographic anchors

- Turtle Bay / Fighter 1 is on the **eastern coast of Espiritu Santo**, with the airfield site immediately inland and Vaucluse Passage / Matte Vulu Island offshore.
- Peterson Point and the east-coast belt run south from Turtle Bay toward the Luganville / Pallikulo / Segond complex.
- Segond Channel is deep and protected, but a direct first-wave entry would concentrate transports in a narrow, predictable harbor axis. The July force therefore does **not** center on a direct Segond-Channel assault.
- The historical 14-m Daihatsu load anchor is about **70 men or 10–12 t cargo**. It is used only as a throughput anchor.

## 3. Landing concept — CLOSED CENTER

### Main axis: Turtle Bay / Vaucluse-Passage coast
Purpose:
1. seize or permanently deny the nearly complete Turtle Bay fighter strip;
2. obtain an east-coast lodgment with immediate access to the plantation/coastal-road system;
3. roll south toward Peterson Point and the southeast base belt.

Booked assault share center: **~5,300–5,500 physical**, including the strongest infantry grouping, engineers, signals, most mortars, and the first 75-mm guns.

### Secondary axis: Peterson Point–Surundu coast
Purpose:
1. force Arthur/Rose to defend more than the Turtle Bay strip;
2. threaten the coast-road link toward Pallikulo/Luganville;
3. prevent a compact all-in Allied defense immediately south of Turtle Bay.

Booked assault share center: **~1,700–1,900 physical**.

This is a real secondary lodgment, not a token diversion, but it is too small to storm Luganville independently on D-day center.

### Explicitly rejected center axis
**Direct Segond Channel / Luganville harbor assault** is rejected for the first wave. It offers excellent later logistics if captured, but forces vulnerable transports and landing craft into the most obvious defended base/harbor axis.

## 7. Allied defensive geometry at 00:00 — ROLE-BASED CENTER

The ~3,900–4,400 immediate effective defenders cannot know the exact beach before dawn. Arthur/Rose/Edson therefore cover functions rather than producing a perfect anti-landing wall:
- strongest forward observation / light maneuver screen: Turtle Bay / northern-east-coast approach, with a large Raider component;
- substantial 2d-Marine force covering Turtle Bay strip and road exits;
- central screen along Peterson / Surundu approaches;
- fixed-base/Marine force protecting Luganville–Segond technical, harbor, AA and communications functions;
- a regimental mobile reserve retained because the Japanese secondary axis is not known in advance.

Edson does not put all Raiders in static foxholes. A meaningful fraction is held for reconnaissance/local counterattack.
