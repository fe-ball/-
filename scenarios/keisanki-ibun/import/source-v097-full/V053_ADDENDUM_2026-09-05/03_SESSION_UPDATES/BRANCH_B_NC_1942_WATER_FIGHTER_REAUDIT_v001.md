# BRANCH B — New Caledonia 1942-11 water-fighter re-audit v001

## 1. Finding
The running New Caledonia scenario materially undercounted Japanese float-fighter aviation.

The error was not that the project lacked a float-fighter branch. The opposite is true: existing canon had already established an accelerated A6M2-N-equivalent line well before the historical type became numerous. The scenario then counted carrier aviation and Santo land aviation but failed to carry that canonical float-fighter force into the theater OOB as a separate system.

This audit closes that gap.

## 2. Existing canon retained
A6M2-N-equivalent:
- first prototype: 1941-05~07
- enlarged prototype/unit testing: summer-autumn 1941
- pilot production: 1941-09~11
- completed by 1941-12: 30~50
- combat-usable at opening: 18~35
- cumulative completed by 1942-03~04: 90~130
- usable at that point: 60~90 / approximately 6~9 detachments
- max speed: 444~452 km/h
- front-line median: 438~447 km/h

The aircraft remains physically penalized by the center float and outrigger floats. No further performance bonus is added in this audit.

## 3. Historical anchor
Historical A6M2-N:
- approx. 435 km/h maximum speed
- 2 x 20 mm cannon + 2 x 7.7 mm MG
- 327 total production before production ended in 1943
- used in the Solomons and Aleutians as a real interceptor/convoy-cover fighter.

Historical R-Area operations demonstrate that these aircraft were not merely armed scouts:
- KAMIKAWA MARU had 11 A6M2-N + 2 F1M on 1942-09-12.
- its Rufe detachment flew 360 sorties / 211 missions from 1942-09-04 through 1942-11-07, losing 9 pilots.
- on 1942-11-07 six A6M2-N + four F1M covered a destroyer reinforcement convoy against an escorted US strike.
- F1M units were repeatedly used for convoy top cover and light bombing as well as observation.

These examples do not imply parity with F4F. They establish that the water-fighter layer can force escorts to engage, break bomber geometry, attack stragglers and preserve shipping.

## 4. 1942-11 national working inventory
The previous canon ended the hard quantity line at 1942-03~04. For the New Caledonia scenario the following provisional ledger is closed:

| state, 1942-11 | working band |
|---|---:|
| cumulative completed | 185~235 |
| surviving/front-line-capable | 125~160 |
| normally ready | 90~120 |

Rationale:
- starts from the already-canonical 90~130 completed by 1942-03~04;
- assumes continued moderate production, not a 3~4x national floatplane explosion;
- the Branch has no historical Guadalcanal floatplane meat-grinder, improving southern survival;
- Aleutians, Midway/Wake, Santo, training, replacement and dispersed base defense still consume aircraft and pilots.

This ledger must be revised if a later industrial monthly-production audit supplies stronger data.

## 5. New Caledonia theater allocation
A campaign of this size should have deliberately concentrated float fighters.

Working allocation at campaign opening:
- Santo local water-defense detachment: nominal ~12, ready ~9
- mobile/tender-supported operation detachment: nominal ~10~12, ready ~8~9
- invasion / forward-water-base detachment and replacements: nominal ~8~14, ready ~5~10

Theater total:
- assigned: 30~38
- normally ready: 22~28

This pool is separate from:
- carrier air groups;
- Santo wheeled land aviation;
- ordinary E13A/F1M shipborne search/spotting aircraft.

An R-Area-style auxiliary tender/support element is therefore a required rear-support component of the invasion system. Exact tender hull assignment remains a logistics/OOB sub-gate; do not magically place the tender inside the close combat screen.

## 6. Combat meaning
### A6M2-N-equivalent
Treat as a genuine fighter with a specialized target set.

High value:
- PBY/Hudson/OS2U and similar patrol/recon targets
- bomb-laden / separated SBD and TBF
- damaged/straggling attackers
- low/medium-speed attack aircraft
- convoy CAP after A6M land/carrier fighters occupy the enemy escort

Low value:
- sustained F4F/P-39/P-38 fighter-sweep duel
- high-speed chase against clean modern fighters
- energy fight where float drag is repeatedly exposed

### F1M2
F1M2 can:
- perform spotting and reconnaissance;
- provide low-level top cover;
- harass bombers/torpedo aircraft;
- light-bomb ground targets;
- attack patrol aircraft under favorable conditions.

It is not counted one-for-one as a modern fighter.

## 7. Base-system limitation
A float fighter bypasses the runway, not logistics.

Established sheltered-water sustained sortie generation:
- 60~75% of an equivalent land-fighter unit.

New forward lagoon:
- 45~60% until shore handling settles.

Loss channels unique/important to water operations:
- rough-water landing damage
- float leakage/damage
- towing accident
- crane/maintenance bottleneck
- recovery under air attack
- mooring-area strafing/bombing
- corrosion and saltwater service burden
- inability to launch/recover efficiently in poor sea state

New Caledonia's large protected lagoon makes the west coast an unusually favorable theater for this system once shore access is secured, but does not remove those limitations.

### 1942-11-09
Morning landing:

### 1942-11-10~12
Typical local lower CAP:

Effect:
- US strike completion/cohesion modestly worse;
- Japanese landing craft, engineer traffic and unloading modestly safer;

- evening 11-12 / dawn 11-13: 6~8 assigned, 4~6 ready

## 9. Kyofu gate
Do not back-port N1K1 Kyofu to New Caledonia November 1942.

Existing Branch line remains:
- prototypes/evaluation: 1941~42
- practical specification near-fixed: end-1942
- pre-production: 1943-01~02
- first operational unit: 1943-04~05

The November 1942 correction is an A6M2-N-equivalent + F1M operational-system correction, not a Kyofu acceleration.

## 10. Future rule
Whenever a Pacific island/lagoon campaign is adjudicated, explicitly inventory:
1. wheeled land fighters
2. carrier fighters
3. float fighters
4. F1M secondary fighter/spotting pool
5. long-range float reconnaissance
6. flying boats
7. tenders / cranes / fuel / ammunition / moorings
8. sea state and recovery geometry

Do not hide categories 3~7 inside a generic "base aviation" number.
