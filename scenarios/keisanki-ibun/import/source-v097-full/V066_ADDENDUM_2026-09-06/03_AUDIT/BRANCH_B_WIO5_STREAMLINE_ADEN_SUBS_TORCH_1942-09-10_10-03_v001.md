## 1. Governing accounting rules

1. British countermeasures are charged at **gross cost first**.
2. **Full cost does not imply full tactical effect**. A hull, aircraft slot, carrier deck, or patrol can be committed without producing a contact or kill.
3. An unaffordable bill is never erased. It must reappear as delay, exposed route, reduced sortie rate, withdrawal from another theatre, wear, or casualties.
4. Same hull on same day is never booked twice.
5. No named counterfactual casualty is created without a defensible contact/target geometry.
6. A historical loss that occurred under historical countermeasures is not rescued merely because Branch B repeats the same countermeasure.
7. Conversely, an exposed Branch capability gap does **not** automatically produce casualties. The opponent must possess a mechanism to exploit it.

## 2. 10 September: Operation STREAM at Majunga — carrier gap is real but not cashed

### 2.1 Historical demand on carrier aviation

Historical STREAM-LINE-JANE used ILLUSTRIOUS as the modern carrier. The force included a large carrier air group; however the actual September occupation support was unusually light. Contemporary/near-contemporary summaries and ship histories describe the Madagascar occupation as uneventful from the carrier-air perspective, with ILLUSTRIOUS mainly providing cover, reconnaissance and demonstrations of force; one detailed ship history records only 57 sorties in the first three days. The landing at Majunga was opposed locally by machine-gun fire, but the town and airfield were taken rapidly, while Vichy air strength on the island had dwindled to only a few operational aircraft.

Historical source anchors:
- Eastern Fleet Proceedings / STREAM-LINE-JANE narrative: https://www.naval-history.net/xDKWD-EF1942-Introduction.htm
- HMS Illustrious Association, 1942 service: https://www.hmsillustriousassociation.com/general-8-3
- South African Military History Society, SAAF in Madagascar: https://www.samilitaryhistory.org/vol092jc.html
- IWM / archival imagery and September war diary entries.

### 2.2 Branch carrier substitution

Branch state on 10 September:
- FORMIDABLE: sunk 5 April.
- INDOMITABLE: sunk 5 April.
- ILLUSTRIOUS: mission-killed at PEDESTAL 12 August.
- HERMES: survives and is the only available organic carrier hull for the Madagascar force.

HERMES is **not** promoted to an ILLUSTRIOUS-equivalent. Her center working air group remains approximately 12 Swordfish / 814 NAS scale. She can provide reconnaissance, A/S work, spotting and light strike, but cannot reproduce modern fighter CAP, armour, sortie mass or deck-cycle resilience.

### 2.3 Tactical result at Majunga

The missing modern CAP is a genuine vulnerability. It does not, however, convert to center casualties on 10 September:
- historical ILLUSTRIOUS strike demand at Majunga was low;
- Vichy air strength was too small and too weakly employed to exploit the absence of a modern fighter deck;
- land-based Allied air could move forward once Majunga airfield was secured;
- no defensible new Vichy air contact exists from which to manufacture a HERMES hit, transport loss or extra landing casualties.

**CENTER RULING — STREAM, 10 September:**
- Majunga captured on the historical day.
- No Branch-extra ship loss.
- No Branch-extra named landing casualty.
- HERMES remains exposed as a high-value fragile substitute, but the exposure is **UNEXPLOITED AT CENTER**.

This is an important accounting precedent: Britain has a hole, but the opponent does not automatically receive a kill merely because the hole exists.

## 4. Gulf of Aden: historical precautions fail to stop I-29

### 4.1 British defence already active

By 10 September the British are already reacting to the submarine threat:
- three Catalinas from Ceylon have been requested and reached Aden on 7 September;
- numbered shipping routes and coastal lanes are being organized;
- the Persian Gulf convoy system is beginning;
- surface A/S strength remains short because three Cromer-class minesweepers are still required by STREAM-LINE-JANE;
- Mediterranean command has sent HERO and TETCOTT east as emergency A/S reinforcements.

The Eastern Fleet report explicitly links the inability to reinforce the Gulf of Aden with surface A/S vessels to the A/S shortage plus concurrent STREAM-LINE-JANE.

Historical source anchor: Eastern Fleet Proceedings, 18 Aug–30 Sep 1942.

### 4.2 I-29 — HARESFIELD, OCEAN HONOUR, PAUL LUCKENBACH

These attacks are retained because they historically occurred **after** the British precautions above had begun. Reapplying the same precautions cannot rescue the same ships a second time.

- **10 Sep — HARESFIELD, 5,299 GRT:** sunk off Socotra.
- **16 Sep — OCEAN HONOUR, 7,147 GRT:** sunk by torpedo and gunfire west of Socotra. Historical record gives 15 sailors and 5 gunners killed.
- **22 Sep — PAUL LUCKENBACH, 6,579 GRT:** sunk in the Arabian Sea; cargo included 18 tanks and 10 B-25 bombers. Crew escapes in lifeboats.

Source: CombinedFleet I-29 TROM, https://www.combinedfleet.com/I-29.htm

Branch center ruling:
- all three sinkings retained;
- no additional kill is added to already successful attacks;
- the British routing/air/escort expense remains fully charged even though it fails to stop I-29.

## 7. Madagascar vs Gulf of Aden: the hard hull trade

### 7.1 Three Cromer-class minesweepers

CROMER, CROMARTY and ROMNEY are historical dual-role assets required for STREAM-LINE-JANE and desired in the Gulf of Aden.

Center clock:
- **10–26 Sep:** remain committed to Madagascar / release process: 17 days × 3 = **51 minesweeper-days**.

### 7.2 Mediterranean pays with HERO and TETCOTT

Because the Cromers cannot yet be released, Mediterranean command sends destroyers HERO and TETCOTT toward Aden on 5 September. They are both actively used for Red Sea/Gulf of Aden convoy and A/S work during WIO-5.

WIO-5 gross cross-theatre charge:

This is a gross historical burden, not yet a Branch-extra burden; nevertheless it matters because Branch B simultaneously carries the modern-carrier deficit. The same Mediterranean theatre has less slack for future demands.

## 9. The modern-carrier bill moves toward TORCH

Branch B cannot perform that transfer:
- FORMIDABLE and INDOMITABLE are gone;

- replacing FORMIDABLE therefore requires an explicit reallocation that creates another hole, rather than a free substitution.

### Strategic interpretation

The first obvious Madagascar carrier gap does **not** generate extra blood because Vichy forces cannot exploit it decisively. Britain therefore “pays” Madagascar with the surviving HERMES, minesweepers and other resources, while the Gulf of Aden obtains emergency destroyers and Catalinas from elsewhere.

The blood appears instead where the Japanese already have a real attacking mechanism: I-29 and other submarines continue to sink ships despite the defensive bill. Meanwhile the truly Branch-specific unpaid item — the missing modern carrier — is pushed westward toward TORCH rather than being erased.

This block therefore closes with British resources increasingly cross-collateralized, but without manufacturing a Branch-extra casualty before an opponent actually exploits a new gap.
