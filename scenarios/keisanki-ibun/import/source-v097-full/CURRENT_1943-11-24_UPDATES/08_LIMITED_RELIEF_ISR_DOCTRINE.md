# Limited relief and ISR doctrine — Gilbert campaign

## Commander intent
Permanent rescue of Tarawa/Makin is not the primary objective. The purpose of island resistance is to bind US CVE/BB/DD/transport/ASW/logistics and create operational overextension. Relief is a real option only if its expected extension of organized resistance exceeds the risk/cost of the transporting force.

## Relief levels
- R0: no relief
- R1: high-value small cargo/specialists (radio batteries, medical, ammunition, explosives, engineers/signallers), usually single-digit tons to low tens
- R2: ~100–200 personnel + ~20–30t
- R3: several hundred personnel + 40t+ counterlanding

## Timeline
- 15–19 Nov: Makin R3 prepared as invasion probability rises; Tarawa R1 contingency only.
- 20/21 Nov: Makin R3 succeeds, 390–460 personnel + 40–55t.
- 21/22 Nov: Makin R2 force prepares and conditionally advances, but no verified corridor through adapted US SG screen; abort. No Type93 salvo. Tarawa R1 not delivered.
- 22/23 Nov: Makin falls below useful R2 threshold; Tarawa R1 cargo prepared but no delivery window.
- 23/24 Nov: Tarawa R1 air-drop attempted because option is nearing expiry; 2–3t dropped, 0.8–1.4t recovered.
- After 24 Nov organized resistance ends, relief option expires.

## ISR/meta-cognition rule
Japanese HQ distinguishes:
- CONFIRMED
- CORRELATED
- SINGLE-SOURCE
- INFERENCE
and applies freshness decay. ESM bearings cue search sectors but are not coordinates. No attack is credited merely because a target exists historically.

Correlated:

Inference only:

Not supported:

## Operational implication
Japan preserves option value. Surface forces approach only to decision lines. A relief cargo already loaded does not create a requirement to penetrate. A Type93 attack is permitted only when a verified existing window can be widened, not to manufacture a corridor under alert SG coverage.
