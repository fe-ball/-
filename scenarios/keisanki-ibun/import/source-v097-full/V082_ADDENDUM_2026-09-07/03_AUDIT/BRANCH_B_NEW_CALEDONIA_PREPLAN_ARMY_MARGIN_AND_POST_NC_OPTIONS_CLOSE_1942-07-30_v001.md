## 0. Core ruling

The Branch no longer uses the old October NC battle as an executed event. Its useful strategic lesson survives only where independently valid: **New Caledonia is a divisional-scale target, not another 7,000-man Santo.**

The crucial Army-side correction is that Branch B does **not** reproduce the historical Guadalcanal/New-Guinea emergency-transfer cascade. Japan may allocate two Southern-Army divisions to NC while still preserving the China/Manchuria/Korea formations that historically began to be siphoned into the South Pacific in late 1942.

Therefore:
- Army manpower is **not** the first NC bottleneck;
- shipping, landing throughput, engineers, aviation, fuel, artillery/ammunition and time before Allied concentration are the harder bottlenecks;
- “Army has margin” does **not** mean “another two divisions can immediately continue to Fiji after NC.” Strategic reserve and theater-ready mobile reserve are different things.

## 1. Historical anchor: why the Branch Army is less squeezed

Historical FS was initially designed with a surprisingly small Army footprint. The 17th Army was ordered on 18 May 1942 to seize key positions in New Caledonia, Fiji and Samoa; the original New Caledonia mission was centered on the South Seas Force, while other detachments handled Fiji/Samoa/Port Moresby. The Army cooperated partly because FS did not initially demand an Australia-scale ground commitment.

The later historical South-Pacific crisis changed this radically:
- 2nd Division was transferred to 17th Army in Aug 1942 and was devastated at Guadalcanal;
- 38th Division was assigned to 17th Army in Sep 1942 and was devastated there;
- 6th Division was transferred out of China into the 17th-Army/Solomons pipeline in Nov;
- 51st Division was allocated to the southeast theater in Nov;
- 20th and 41st Divisions entered the New-Guinea reinforcement pipeline in Dec.

Branch B has no Guadalcanal campaign consuming these formations in that sequence. NC therefore does not automatically force the Army to cannibalize the same China/Korea reserve structure that historical late-1942 Pacific emergencies did.

## 2. Five-Go interaction — the important distinction

Existing Branch authority for the later Five-Go/Sichuan plan requires roughly:

The Branch political compromise already prohibited stripping China/Manchuria Five-Go divisions merely to make FS larger.

### Current ruling

**NC is built primarily from Southern-Army / already-FS-lineage formations.**

The central NC package does **not** require transfer of the 6th, 41st or 51st Divisions out of China, nor the 20th Division out of Korea/continental reserve. These formations are not “free”; they retain China, garrison and Soviet-contingency duties. But relative to history, their forced emergency transfer to the South Pacific is avoided.

This is the concrete meaning of “the Army is not yet as squeezed as history at the Five-Go decision point.”

## 4. What remains as Army strategic margin after building NC

Relative to historical late-1942 emergency transfers, Branch can preserve approximately **three to four division-equivalents of continental/Korea strategic depth** that otherwise enter the South-Pacific/New-Guinea pipeline: 6th, 41st, 51st, and 20th are the principal examples.

Do not describe all four as “idle reserve.” Correct interpretation:
- 6th/41st/51st can remain in China-related duties and Five-Go preparation instead of being removed;
- 20th remains a Korea/continental strategic reserve rather than being spent in New Guinea;
- replacements, artillery, vehicles, engineers and shipping that would have supported those emergency transfers are also less stressed.

This makes the later Five-Go preparation **materially easier than history**, but its primary bottlenecks still become rail/rolling stock, trucks, engineers, ammunition, aviation fuel, industrial inputs and shipping concentration — not merely division count.

This date is not yet executable authority. It must pass named shipping/oiler, division, engineer, carrier-air and Allied mirror audits.

## 7. Immediate post-NC decision: what “further margin” actually permits

The political agreement remains **Santo → NC → stop/reassess**. NC success does not automatically fire Fiji/Samoa.

### Option A — consolidate and return strategic weight to Army/Five-Go
**CENTER POLICY.**

Actions:
- establish NC/Santo bases;
- return assault shipping/tankers from concentration duty to the general pool;
- accelerate Five-Go continental transport/stockpiling;
- rotate damaged ships/air groups to repair/training;
- keep 2nd/38th as occupation/operational reserve initially, releasing one only after the NC security situation permits.

This is the Army-preferred outcome.

### Option B — Efate / residual New-Hebrides cleanup
**PLAUSIBLE CHEAP FOLLOW-ON.**

Efate remains a strategically awkward Allied base close to Santo. If air suppression alone is costly, the Navy may request its physical neutralization/capture.

This can be structured as a detachment-scale operation using Kawaguchi/Santo-mobile elements plus naval base forces rather than a fresh China division. It is much easier to reconcile with Five-Go than Fiji.

It may occur before or after NC depending air/base geometry; it is not allowed to create a duplicate Kawaguchi force.

### Option C — Fiji / Viti Levu
**CONDITIONAL, NOT CENTER.**

Fiji is the only further conquest that materially deepens the U.S.–Australia communications cut, so the Navy has a coherent case for it.

But by late 1942 it is not the original small-FS target anymore. A credible operation would require roughly division-plus-to-multi-division scale, substantial landing shipping, carrier/land-air cover and a fresh Allied audit.

Policy gates before even authorizing detailed planning:
1. NC key bases secured quickly, without a prolonged northern-island campaign;
2. Japanese NC ground losses low enough that 2nd/38th retain a useful mobile nucleus;
3. no China/Manchuria Five-Go division is required for Fiji;
4. carrier-air veteran losses remain tolerable and at least two reliable fleet/support decks are available;
5. Wasp / new U.S. carrier timing still leaves an information and air window;
6. Five-Go's planned shipping transfer is not consumed by a new Pacific concentration;
7. Fiji defense audit does not show that the required assault has become another 50–60k operation.

Failure of any major gate returns policy to Option A.

### Option D — Samoa / Pago Pago
**CENTER REJECTED.**

Too far, too much shipping/fuel/air-cover exposure, and diminishing marginal strategic return after NC. Reopen only after an unexpectedly cheap Fiji victory and a still-collapsed U.S. carrier situation.

### Option E — renewed Indian-Ocean / Bay-of-Bengal surface pressure
**PLAUSIBLE NAVY ALTERNATIVE TO FIJI.**

If the Army rejects another divisional island campaign, the Navy can argue for a cheaper post-NC use of restored cruisers/DD/oilers and submarines. A revived Operation-B-like raid or stronger Bay-of-Bengal pressure:
- uses little Army manpower;
- exploits continuing British Madagascar/Indian-Ocean obligations;
- preserves the political “no new Pacific conquest” compromise;
- still costs fuel, oiler days, maintenance and escort availability.

Fleet carriers are not automatically assigned; cruiser/submarine/auxiliary pressure remains the center concept.

## 8. Strategic conclusion

Branch B after Santo has **more Army strategic elasticity than historical Japan in late 1942**, because it has not paid the Guadalcanal/New-Guinea division-transfer and attrition bill. That elasticity is sufficient to mount a strong NC campaign from Southern-Army resources **without destroying Five-Go preparation**.

It is not sufficient to make Fiji/Samoa an automatic continuation. After NC, the key constraint changes from “do we possess another division?” to “do we want to spend another shipping/carrier/engineer/logistics concentration instead of cashing the success into continental preparation and base consolidation?”

Therefore the preplan center is:

**Santo captured → NC in early September if named gates pass → stop/reassess.**

After NC:
- **Army preference:** consolidate, release shipping, accelerate Five-Go;
- **Navy cheap option:** Efate cleanup and/or renewed Indian-Ocean surface pressure;
- **Navy maximum option:** Fiji, only under explicit low-loss / carrier / shipping / Five-Go-preservation gates;
- **Samoa:** no center authorization.
