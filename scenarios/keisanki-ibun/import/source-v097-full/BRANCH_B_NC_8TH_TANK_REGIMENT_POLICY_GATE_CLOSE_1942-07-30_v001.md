# Branch B — NC 8th Tank Regiment policy gate close, 1942-07-30 v001

Status: **POLICY CLOSED / ACTUAL TRANSFER AND EXACT TANK MANIFEST OPEN**  
Clock: **1942-07-30 00:00**

## 0. Finding

The recovered 754th Tank Battalion location at St. Vincent makes an armored Japanese reserve more valuable, but does not make a full tank regiment the minimum condition for a viable landing. The first U.S. armored blow must be stopped by AT guns, mines, engineers, 75-mm direct fire and interdiction because Japanese tanks cannot reliably be combat-ready before the first M3 packet.

## 1. Strategic availability

Historical/Branch lineage:
- the 8th Tank Regiment was in the Kwantung/North-China armored system in mid-1942;
- historical later movement toward the 17th-Army/Rabaul system proves physical transfer to the South Pacific was possible, but is not pre-fired in this Branch;
- transferring it to NC removes it from Five-Go / 3rd Tank Division planning and consumes heavy shipping/landing capacity.

## 2. Decision rule

### Minimum NC plan
NC may be authorized **without the full 8th Tank Regiment** only if all are true:
1. main-axis AT/direct-fire H+3/4 gate passes;
2. 754th remains light-tank battalion scale rather than Marine+Army armor massing above the planning ceiling;
3. naval/air interdiction can keep the St. Vincent road corridor under recurring observation;
4. heavy-craft shortage would make a full regiment displace more valuable guns/engineers.

### Transfer gate
Full or major 8th Tank Regiment transfer becomes the center recommendation if, before final combat loading:
- Japanese intelligence confirms **~40+ serviceable U.S. tanks** on NC and/or Marine tank reinforcement arriving;
- 17-m/heavy-equivalent landing capacity reaches the v085 requirement;
- the regiment can reach the NC staging system without breaking Five-Go's protected minimum armor plan;
- first-day artillery/AT/engineer loads are already funded.

## 3. Preferred use if transferred

The regiment is not a first-touch beach asset.

- H to H+3: 0 medium-tank requirement ashore.
- H+4–6: first light/medium packet only if exits are secure.
- H+6–10: **12–20 tank mobile reserve** center becomes useful.
- later D-day/D+1: build toward a larger regimental packet as road and bridge capacity permits.

Primary tasks:
- counter-counterattack against surviving M3 packets;
- exploit road junction seizure after U.S. armor is checked;
- mobile reserve against a local Americal penetration;
- rapid support on the Boulouparis–Tontouta road corridor.

## 4. Equipment-selection implication

Heavy-craft scarcity favors prioritizing vehicles that produce the greatest mobile reserve per heavy slot.

- Ha-Go-class light tanks can use 14-m craft more readily.
- Chi-Ha-class medium tanks require 17-m/heavy-equivalent slots.
- a mixed packet may be superior to loading every available medium tank if it delays AT guns, prime movers or engineers.

Exact 8th-Regiment type/strength remains OPEN pending named equipment audit.

## 5. Five-Go lock

If the 8th Tank Regiment is transferred to NC:
- it is removed from any Five-Go 3rd Tank Division working OOB;
- Five-Go must be rebuilt around the remaining tank regiments and/or receive a later replacement decision;
- no double-counting is permitted even if NC ends cheaply.
