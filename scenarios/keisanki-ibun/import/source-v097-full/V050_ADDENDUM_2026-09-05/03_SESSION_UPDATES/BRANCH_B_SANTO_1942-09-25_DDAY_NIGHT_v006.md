# BRANCH B — Santo 1942-09-25 D-Day Night Action v006
Status: WORKING-PROMOTED CURRENT
Scope: 1942-09-25 evening through early 1942-09-26, naval/night-fire-support adjudication
Supersedes: any earlier provisional D-Day-night result. Does NOT change the already re-adjudicated 1942-09-24 night action except where explicitly noted.

## 1. Starting conditions

### United States
- USS Washington: combat capable; no major damage from 24 Sep action.
- USS Preston: sunk on 24 Sep in Branch action.
- USS Quincy: heavy damage / campaign mission kill from 24 Sep Type 93 hit.
- Core night-defense assets available: Washington, Helena, Atlanta, Walke, Gwin, O'Brien, plus locally available escorts subject to convoy/retreat duty.
- Wasp: campaign mission kill and retiring; North Carolina remains committed to Wasp withdrawal cover and is not center-booked into this night action.
- U.S. command has one night of direct Branch experience with:
  - Japanese long-range torpedo threat;
  - staggered torpedo waves;
  - continued torpedo danger after an initial evasive turn.
- U.S. adaptation:
  - Lee avoids aggressive closure into destroyer torpedo geometry.
  - Washington remains the high-end radar/gunnery cell.
  - escorts are ordered to preserve spacing and avoid masking Washington.
  - large course changes are initiated earlier when Japanese destroyer groups show coordinated attack geometry.

### Japan
- Kongo: medium damage / bombardment mission kill from 24 Sep.
- Haruna: combat capable but not center-booked for a second consecutive close bombardment night.
- Hiei and Kirishima: combat capable and fresh for D-Day night.
- Hagikaze: medium-heavy damage / water-battle mission kill from 24 Sep.
- Tokitsukaze: light-medium damage and not center-booked for the second night.
- Remaining first-night Kagero-class destroyers are not automatically reused; ammunition, reload torpedoes, fatigue, and screening commitments matter.
- Japanese command has direct reports that:
  - Washington can obtain radar contact and accurate fire before optical acquisition;
  - a single obvious turn after torpedo launch can warn Lee;
  - repeated/staggered torpedo threats can disrupt Washington's fire solution and pursuit even without hits.

## 2. Japanese D-Day-night formation

The center-line plan does not repeat the 24 Sep approach.

### Main bombardment group
- Hiei
- Kirishima
- Suzuya
- Chikuma
- selected escort destroyers

Historical feasibility anchor: in October 1942 Abe's vanguard operated Hiei/Kirishima with Suzuya, Tone/Chikuma, Nagara and a large DesRon 10 screen. Branch timing/mission differs, but these hulls are physically available absent Branch losses.

### Advance torpedo/screen group
Center roster:
- Nagara (command / contact support)
- Kazagumo
- Yugumo
- Makigumo
- Akigumo
- Akizuki
- Tanikaze
- Murasame
- Harusame

Notes:
- The first six are chosen from fresh/available DesRon 10 candidates not used as the core of the 24 Sep Branch torpedo groups.
- Murasame/Harusame supplement the screen from destroyer forces historically active in the Solomons in September.
- Akizuki is not treated as a generic Kagero torpedo boat; her principal value is AA, gunfire, screening and contact support. Her torpedo contribution is secondary.
- Older destroyers are not assigned Kagero/Yugumo reload-cycle performance by default.

### Tactical concept
1. Advance group precedes the battleships by enough distance that U.S. radar contacts the screen before the bombardment group.
2. First torpedo attack is intended to force Washington's group to turn rather than guarantee a battleship hit.
3. The bombardment group uses the disruption window to fire a short, pre-planned shore mission.
4. A second destroyer sub-group holds fire until the U.S. new course is observable.
5. Japan disengages rather than chase Washington into a radar-controlled gun action.
6. Reload-capable destroyers reload only after opening distance/smoke; no assumption of instantaneous second salvos.

## 3. U.S. night formation and tactical change

Center force:
- Washington
- Helena
- Atlanta
- Walke
- Gwin
- O'Brien
- additional local DD only if not required for Wasp/convoy cover

U.S. priorities:
1. preserve Washington;
2. prevent a long battleship bombardment;
3. do not chase Japanese destroyers into Type 93 geometry;
4. use Helena/Atlanta and 5-inch batteries to disrupt the forward torpedo screen;
5. engage Hiei/Kirishima at long radar range if contact becomes clean.

The U.S. does not possess a fleet-wide 1944-style integrated radar picture. Washington is the best sensor/fire-control cell; Helena can materially help where fitted/effective, but destroyers do not automatically share the same instantaneous picture.

## 4. Time-line adjudication (center line)

### 21:45–21:55
Japanese advance screen enters the northern/eastern Santo approaches well ahead of Hiei/Kirishima.
U.S. radar contact develops first on the forward screen, not immediately on the battleships.

Lee correctly interprets the geometry as a likely torpedo screen and does not order a rapid close.

### 21:55–22:05
Washington/Helena begin radar-controlled or radar-assisted fire against the forward contacts at long range.
Japanese destroyers maneuver in small groups instead of a single conspicuous mass turn.

Center effect:
- Japanese screen is disrupted but not broken.
- One destroyer receives light damage/splinter damage.
- No immediate catastrophic Japanese loss.

### 22:04–22:12 — First torpedo pressure
A first sub-group fires a broad Type 93 pattern from roughly 11–14 km against the predicted U.S. battle-line track.

The launch is not followed by a simultaneous 180-degree turn by all launchers.
Some ships maintain contact/fire briefly while others open distance.

U.S. reaction:
- Lee initiates an early large turn because of the previous night's experience.
- Washington's main-battery solution is interrupted.
- Escorts have unequal reaction times.

Center result:
- Washington avoids the first pattern.
- No center-line capital-ship torpedo hit.
- One U.S. destroyer suffers a damaging near miss / shell damage in the confused maneuvering, but remains afloat at this stage.

### 22:08–22:25 — Bombardment window
While Washington is turning and re-establishing the radar solution, Hiei/Kirishima conduct a short pre-planned shore bombardment.

Center ammunition expenditure:
- combined 14-inch: about 220–280 rounds, center ~250
- additional 8-inch fire from cruiser support: limited, target-specific

This is larger than the interrupted 24 Sep mission but far below the historical ~1,000-round Kongo/Haruna Henderson benchmark.

Primary targets:
- runway intersections
- fuel/maintenance areas
- communications
- known AA positions
- artillery positions and road nodes

### 22:12–22:25 — U.S. counter-fire
Washington reacquires larger contacts and fires on the bombardment group at approximately 18–23 kyd depending on phase/geometry.

Because:
- range is longer than the decisive historical Kirishima engagement;
- Japan is already maneuvering defensively;
- Lee is simultaneously respecting torpedo warning;
the center line does NOT book an immediate 16-inch hit on Hiei/Kirishima.

Center effects:
- multiple near straddles/near misses;
- splinter/light topside damage is plausible;
- Japanese bombardment accuracy degrades under maneuver;
- the planned bombardment is terminated earlier than desired.

Sensitivity:
- one 16-inch hit on Hiei or Kirishima remains a credible adjacent branch and would create campaign-level mission damage.

### 22:18–22:30 — Second torpedo group
A second Japanese sub-group fires after observing the U.S. post-first-wave course.

Because the U.S. is now fully torpedo-alert and already maneuvering irregularly, hit probability per weapon is lower than on 24 Sep.

Center result:
- Washington again avoids.
- Atlanta receives one Type 93 hit OR equivalent severe underwater damage (center booking: one Type 93 hit).
- Atlanta survives but is a campaign mission kill and must withdraw.
- No second U.S. destroyer is booked sunk in the center line.

Reasoning:
- a cruiser/forward escort remains more likely to absorb the torpedo fan than the radar-aware battleship;
- the repeated-wave tactic achieves operational effect even with only one hit.

### 22:25–22:40 — Gun action against the screen
Helena, Washington secondary battery, and U.S. destroyers concentrate on Japanese forward ships.

Center Japanese damage:
- Murasame: medium damage, water-battle mission kill, survives.
- one additional destroyer: light damage.
- no Japanese destroyer center-booked sunk.

Sensitivity:
- a destroyer sinking is plausible if a 5-inch/6-inch concentration catches reload torpedoes or machinery.

### 22:35–22:50 — Disengagement / reload decision
Japan does not attempt a third close attack merely because some ships can reload.

Reload-capable destroyers begin reload while opening distance, but only a subset could regenerate a useful second firing solution before the battle geometry dissolves.

The continuing possibility of another Type 93 salvo keeps Washington from making a straight high-speed pursuit.

Hiei/Kirishima and cruiser support retire with the bombardment mission partially completed.

## 6. Operational result

The night is not a decisive surface victory for either side.

Japan achieves:
- a materially larger bombardment than on 24 Sep;
- protection of the unloading area from a close Washington sweep;
- another U.S. cruiser mission kill;
- successful disengagement of Hiei/Kirishima.

United States achieves:
- prevention of a long, near-unopposed battleship bombardment;
- preservation of Washington;
- damage to the Japanese destroyer screen;
- retention of a powerful night interdiction threat for later nights.

Operational judgment:
- Japanese partial success in fire-support delivery.
- U.S. partial success in limiting duration and preserving the battleship.
- Surface damage exchange modestly favors Japan; shore-fire denial objective modestly favors the U.S.

## 7. Sea-to-air/ground coupling for 26 Sep

Because approximately 250 14-inch rounds plus limited cruiser fire land during the night:
- Santo airfield system at dawn is reduced to roughly 45–60% immediate sortie-generation, center ~52%.
- Efate remains intact and continues to provide aircraft dispersal/reinforcement.
- several aircraft may be destroyed/damaged on the ground, but no whole-air-group wipeout.
- artillery/communications/road-node disruption is meaningful but localized.
- Japanese D+1 ground forces receive a better fire-support effect than after the first night, but not the historical Henderson-level devastation.

The U.S. night surface force is now thinner:
- Preston sunk;
- Quincy mission-killed;
- Atlanta mission-killed;
- Washington remains the core asset.

This directly increases Japanese freedom of action on the following night, but Washington's survival prevents uncontested repeated bombardment.

## 8. Learning state carried forward

### Japan
- SG-controlled U.S. battleship fire is treated as a known threat, not a surprise.
- Forward torpedo screens become standard for any further bombardment attempt.
- torpedo fire is used to shape U.S. maneuver, not only to score hits.
- no assumption that every reload must be expended; preserving destroyers matters for continued unloading and New Caledonia.

### United States
- a single evasive turn does not end the Type 93 threat.
- Lee becomes more conservative about pursuit into Japanese destroyer screens.
- radar first contact remains a major advantage, but does not itself produce a clean battleship engagement if Japan refuses close action.
- escort attrition is becoming an operational problem.

## 9. Adjacent sensitivity gates
- If Washington takes one Type 93 hit: high probability of campaign mission kill; 26 Sep onward sea-control shifts sharply toward Japan.
- If Washington scores one early 16-inch hit on Hiei/Kirishima: Japanese bombardment falls toward ~100–170 total 14-inch rounds and the damaged BB likely exits the campaign.
- If a Japanese reload torpedo is hit during U.S. gunfire: one DD sinking becomes likely and can interrupt the second-wave plan.
