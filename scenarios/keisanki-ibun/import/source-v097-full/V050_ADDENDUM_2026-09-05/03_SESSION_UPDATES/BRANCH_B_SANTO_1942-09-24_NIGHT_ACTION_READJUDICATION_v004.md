## 2. Geometry and doctrine

- Santo is treated as substantially more open water than Ironbottom Sound.
- Washington/Helena provide the U.S. force-level early radar picture, but this is not a mature distributed CIC network.
- IJN Group A and Group B attack from separated sectors rather than as one eight-destroyer line.
- Type 93 first precision salvo center is 8–12 km; 12–16 km is conditional.
- Group A does not make an immediate obvious fleet-wide 180-degree turn after launch.
- Group B withholds its first salvo until U.S. reaction to Group A can be observed.
- Group A reload is adjudicated ship-by-ship; a second attack requires re-contact and safe geometry.

## 3. Time-slice adjudication

### 2206–2214 — bombardment begins / first radar contact
- Kongo and Haruna begin the planned Santo bombardment.
- Washington obtains the first reliable surface-radar contact after the Japanese bombardment track is already developing.
- U.S. classification takes several minutes; Helena confirms lighter contacts.
- Before effective U.S. fire interrupts the mission, Kongo + Haruna fire approximately 120–150 14-inch shells total (working center ~135), substantially below the planned ~450-shell pre-landing budget.

### 2214–2219 — U.S. first fire
- Washington opens first on the largest heavy contact at roughly 18–20 kyd class range.
- Helena/Atlanta engage lighter forward/flank contacts; Quincy engages cruiser-class contacts.
- IJN bombardment ceases almost immediately and the heavy group begins evasive withdrawal geometry.
- Washington obtains early straddles but no center-booked hit in the first few salvos.
- Torpedo Group A continues closing/holding its solution instead of immediately breaking away.

### 2219–2224 — Group A first torpedo salvo
Controlled Group-A fire, working center 24 Type 93s:
- Arashi: 8
- Hagikaze: 8
- Nowaki: 4
- Maikaze: 4

Targeting:
- broad heavy-center/van geometry rather than single-ship sniper fire;
- launch range roughly 10–12 km class;
- 48-kt run implies roughly 6.7–8.1 min water time at 10–12 km.

Launch concealment:
- no immediate four-ship synchronized reversal;
- Arashi/Hagikaze delay their withdrawal briefly;
- Nowaki/Maikaze maintain contact/pressure long enough to make the exact launch instant ambiguous.

Gunfire effects in this slice:
- Washington's later salvos score one 16-inch hit on Kongo (working center).
- Hit is placed high/forward enough to avoid an automatic fatal machinery event, but causes major local damage, fire-control/communications degradation, casualties and enough operational disruption to cancel further shore bombardment.
- Kongo remains mobile at reduced effective battle condition and withdraws with the group.
- Hagikaze is hit by U.S. 5/6-inch-class fire; torpedo-deck/reload-area damage and fire prevent a safe second torpedo reload in the center line.

### 2224–2229 — U.S. torpedo warning / first Type-93 arrival
- Group A begins a split withdrawal.
- The resulting radar-track changes finally give Lee a strong torpedo warning.
- Washington orders a major course change and higher-speed evasive movement.
- Information propagation to the destroyer van is imperfect and the formation compresses during the maneuver.

First-wave Type-93 effects, working center:
- Preston: one Type 93 hit; catastrophic damage; sinks/abandons in the action period.
- Quincy: one Type 93 hit forward; heavy flooding and structural damage; remains afloat but becomes a campaign mission kill and withdraws at greatly reduced speed.
- Washington, Helena, Atlanta, Walke, Gwin, O'Brien: no first-wave torpedo hit.

### 2225–2233 — Group B fires on the new U.S. course
Group B observes the U.S. turn and takes a new solution.
Working center first salvo: ~20 Type 93s:
- Yukikaze: 8
- Tokitsukaze: 4
- Amatsukaze: 4
- Hatsukaze: 4

Launch range ~9–11 km class.
The U.S. force is now fully torpedo-alert and maintains high-amplitude maneuver.

Center effect:
- no confirmed Group-B torpedo hit;
- several tracks pass close enough to force continued evasive maneuver;
- Washington loses a sustained heavy-gun solution during the most useful withdrawal window.

Gunfire:
- Tokitsukaze receives light-to-medium U.S. gun damage and does not obtain a useful reload/re-attack cycle in the center line.
- Atago/Takao/Jintsu maintain enough gun pressure to complicate U.S. destroyer/cruiser pursuit but do not score a center-booked heavy-ship crippling hit.

### 2230–2239 — reload cycle / pursuit denial
Group A reload state:
- Arashi: reload succeeds, but re-contact still required.
- Hagikaze: reload disabled by battle damage/fire; 0 useful second-salvo torpedoes.
- Nowaki: partial first salvo allows a full ready battery again after reloading the fired mount.
- Maikaze: same as Nowaki.

U.S. Mark 15 attacks:
- U.S. destroyers make limited torpedo attacks where local solutions permit.
- No effective hit is booked; separation is maintained between launch solution and Mark-15 run/depth/exploder reliability.

Washington reacquires heavy contacts intermittently but does not regain the clean pre-torpedo firing geometry before the Japanese heavy group opens distance.

### 2237–2245 — Group A second-attack candidate
- The Japanese mission has shifted from 'complete bombardment' to 'extract Kongo/Haruna and preserve the landing operation.'
- Arashi/Nowaki re-establish enough contact to fire a limited covering second attack.
- Working center: 12–16 Type 93s at ~12–14 km class, against a fully alerted and irregularly maneuvering U.S. force.
- No center-booked hit.
- Operational effect is still important: Lee keeps Washington on torpedo-avoidance geometry and does not conduct a close pursuit of the retiring bombardment force.
- Maikaze retains torpedoes as withdrawal screen rather than mechanically firing every available weapon.

### 2245–2255 — disengagement
- Kongo/Haruna/Atago/Takao and surviving destroyers disengage northward.
- Washington remains battle-capable and holds the Santo defensive sea area rather than pursue through a possible third torpedo geometry.
- U.S. rescue/damage-control effort begins around Preston/Quincy.

## 6. Shore-bombardment consequence

The prior provisional 180–240-shell Kongo/Haruna bombardment is superseded.
Center now: ~120–150 total 14-inch shells before the radar interception breaks the mission.

Dawn 25 Sep Santo effect:
- runway damage real but repairable;
- fuel/communications/parked-aircraft damage below the prior provisional result;
- Santo sortie-generation center at dawn ~65–75% of normal rather than ~50–60%;
- Efate remains intact.

This materially strengthens U.S. D-Day daylight anti-shipping pressure.

## 7. Operational assessment

Tactical mission result:
- U.S.: succeeds in aborting the main planned pre-landing heavy bombardment and preserves Washington.
- Japan: inflicts serious screen/cruiser losses, prevents a close U.S. pursuit, and extracts the bombardment force except for campaign damage.

Operational result:
- U.S. advantage, but not a clean surface victory.
- Japan retains enough sea/air cover to proceed with the 25 Sep landing, but with:
  - Kongo removed from further immediate bombardment;
  - Hagikaze removed from useful near-term torpedo work;
  - reduced D-Day suppression of Santo;
  - a stronger need to unload rapidly under daylight air threat.
