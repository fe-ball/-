## 1. Core intelligence rule

In this Branch, Allied intelligence cannot read:
- exact New Caledonia objective;
- exact D-day;
- exact carrier count;
- complete force organization

from Japanese high-grade operational traffic in time to act.

The Allies can observe or infer:
- radio traffic volume/network changes;
- direction finding;
- low-grade port/air/logistics traffic;
- tanker and transport movement;
- submarine sightings of fleet exits/approaches;
- PBY/B-17 reconnaissance south of the Solomons;
- repeated Japanese reconnaissance emphasis;
- the size and type of assault shipping.

Therefore intelligence is modeled in two layers:

**strategic warning** — "Japan is preparing another major South Pacific operation"

versus

**target identification** — "the operation is specifically against Noumea/Tontouta."

The first can become high confidence weeks earlier than the second.

## 2. Geography creates genuine ambiguity

Approximate great-circle distances from Santo:
- Efate: ~162 nmi
- Noumea / southern New Caledonia: ~415 nmi
- Suva / Fiji: ~681 nmi
- Tonga: ~1,073 nmi
- Samoa: ~1,240 nmi

Santo therefore supports several plausible operational meanings:
1. direct pressure or limited attack against Efate;
2. invasion of southern New Caledonia;
3. reconnaissance/raid/pressure toward Fiji;
4. fleet operations designed to draw U.S. forces without an immediate major landing.

New Caledonia is the most strategically attractive invasion target, but geography alone does not make it certain.

## 3. Japanese deployment philosophy

Combined Fleet does NOT mass every important hull visibly at Santo.

### Santo
Visible / likely to be detected:
- construction and base-restoration traffic;
- land-based fighters/reconnaissance;
- support carrier or convoy-cover activity;
- transports, escorts and tankers in increasing numbers;
- cruiser/destroyer rotations.

### Truk
Retains:
- Yamato/Mutsu heavy reserve;
- fleet command;
- repair/replenishment concentration;
- main-carrier staging and work-up;
- heavy cruiser/destroyer reserve.

### Home waters / yards
Temporarily retain:
- Shokaku during repair;
- Kongo during repair/trials;
- Soryu during final repair/work-up if required;
- damaged destroyers.

### Open ocean
Main fleet carriers should move from Truk toward the battle area without all four entering Santo.
This keeps the main strike arm less visible and prevents Santo reconnaissance/submarine contact from revealing the full carrier OOB.

## 4. Japanese deployment calendar

### 29 Sep–4 Oct — consolidation / apparent pause

Santo:
- mine clearance and channel marking;
- Fighter 1 repair;
- 12–18 fighter sustainable capability developing;
- destroyer/transport/tanker support begins using cleared approaches.

Damaged hull movement:
- Kongo heads north for full repair.
- Hagikaze / Murasame / other damaged DDs enter repair cycle.
- Shokaku remains out of combat circulation for yard repair.
- Zuikaku and Ryujo air groups begin regeneration.

Truk:
- Yamato/Mutsu remain normal heavy-fleet anchors.
- Hiei/Kirishima/Haruna and cruiser forces can rotate through rather than remain permanently at Santo.

Allied interpretation:
- high confidence Japan is consolidating Santo.
- low confidence that another invasion is immediate.
- Truk fleet presence is not target-specific because Truk is the normal Combined Fleet concentration point.

### 5–12 Oct — first redistribution

Santo:
- fighter force rises toward ~20–30 sustainable aircraft.
- reconnaissance toward Efate and New Caledonia increases.
- support shipping and engineering traffic remain heavy.

Fleet:
- Zuikaku/Hiryu can operate from Truk–southern approaches as ready carriers.
- Soryu approaches completion/work-up.
- Junyo/Zuiho/Ryujo can appear in different support roles.
- main carriers are not kept continuously together.

Submarines:
- first wider redeployment toward Efate, New Caledonia approaches and Australia–Noumea traffic begins.

Allied interpretation:
- "major follow-on operation possible" rises.
- still ambiguous between Efate suppression, New Caledonia, fleet raid, or broader FS pressure.

### 13–20 Oct — operational preparation becomes visible

Santo:
- approximately 30–45 sustainable local aircraft.
- more fuel, AA, communications and repair stocks arrive.
- fighter/reconnaissance sortie pattern expands south and southeast.

Fleet:
- Soryu work-up closes.
- Shokaku repair/trials approach completion.
- Kongo remains repair/work-up gate.
- heavy cruisers and fresh DD divisions increasingly rotate through Truk/Rabaul/Santo system.

Shipping:
- assault-capable transports are still dispersed among Truk/Rabaul/Shortlands/Santo rather than assembled in one obvious convoy.
- tanker traffic increases, but part of that is explainable as Santo base support.

Allied interpretation:
- major offensive warning is high.
- New Caledonia becomes the leading single hypothesis only if reconnaissance and submarine lines increasingly favor Noumea/Tontouta.

### 21–27 Oct — target discrimination phase

Japanese actions that point toward New Caledonia:
- repeated high-value reconnaissance over Tontouta/Noumea approaches;
- submarine lines on Australia–Noumea and Noumea–Efate routes;
- hydrographic/coastal reconnaissance around southern New Caledonia;
- assault shipping volume too large for a mere Efate raid;
- fighter/support-carrier staging consistent with protecting a ~400 nmi Santo-to-Noumea convoy axis.

Deliberate ambiguity:
- continue pressure on Efate;
- maintain some reconnaissance toward Fiji;
- do not move all fleet carriers into Santo;
- keep Yamato/Mutsu at Truk;
- retain transports in multiple staging points.

Allied interpretation:
- New Caledonia likely, but not certain.
- a fleet raid intended to draw U.S. forces remains plausible.

### 28 Oct–3 Nov — final concentration begins

Santo:
- 45–65 aircraft sustainable system nearing planned level.
- support-carrier/convoy-defense assets become more visible.
- assault cargo, landing craft, engineers and army formations begin to converge.

Main carriers:
- move south from Truk in separated or staggered groups;
- remain at sea / use tanker rendezvous rather than advertising full strength inside Segond.

Surface forces:
- fast-BB and cruiser groups move into a broader Santo–Solomons operating system.
- Yamato/Mutsu remain conditional reserve farther north unless U.S. fast-BB concentration is confirmed.

Allied interpretation:
- certainty that a large operation is imminent becomes very high.
- New Caledonia probability rises materially, but exact landing sector and D-day remain uncertain.

### 4–7 Nov — last warning window

Observable indicators:
- major assault convoy physically loaded and underway;
- Japanese search arcs and submarine contacts cluster around New Caledonia routes;
- selective attacks on Tontouta / Noumea-related targets;
- convoy course after leaving Santo begins to discriminate the objective.

Only here can Allied intelligence move from "New Caledonia most likely" to near-certainty.

## 6. What actually discriminates New Caledonia

Weak indicators:
- Yamato/Mutsu at Truk.
- carriers at Truk.
- heavy cruiser concentration.
- tanker traffic to Truk.
- Santo reconstruction traffic.
- increased Japanese air activity generally.

Moderate indicators:
- support carriers moving toward Santo.
- large transport tonnage entering Rabaul/Santo system.
- submarines moving into the South Pacific.
- repeated Efate/New Caledonia reconnaissance.

Strong indicators:
- amphibious shipping sized for a 25,000–35,000-man operation.
- detailed reconnaissance of Noumea/Tontouta beaches, roads and harbor.
- submarine patrol geometry centered on Australia–Noumea and Noumea–Efate.
- tanker/escort/landing-craft flow that fits a Santo-to-New-Caledonia amphibious shuttle.
- army formations loading into assault transport rather than merely garrison supply.

Near-decisive indicators:
- convoy sails from Santo on a south/southwest track.
- sustained Japanese contact/reconnaissance is concentrated on New Caledonia approaches.
- carrier/support groups take positions consistent with covering that axis.

## 7. Allied defensive consequence

Because New Caledonia is not certain until late, the U.S. cannot rationally place every available asset at Noumea from early October.

### Efate must retain
- fighter/PBY reconnaissance;
- fuel and staging support;
- enough defense to prevent a cheap Japanese seizure or suppression.

### Fiji must retain
- air reconnaissance/defense;
- ground garrison;
- route security.

### New Caledonia receives priority but not monopoly
- Americal and local ground defense strengthened;
- Tontouta/Noumea air and AA improved;
- Washington/fast-BB and cruiser forces remain mobile rather than fixed permanently inside Noumea.
- PBY search patterns cover multiple approach fans.

### Fleet posture
The U.S. should operate from a central flexible geometry between Efate/New Caledonia rather than sit on the presumed invasion beach.

This is precisely the dispersion effect Combined Fleet wants.

## 8. Japanese deception / operational-security value

Japan does not need an elaborate fictional target plan.

Natural ambiguity already exists.

Useful measures:
- keep high-grade operational traffic secure;
- disperse transport assembly;
- rotate support carriers and cruisers between Truk/Rabaul/Santo;
- continue visible Efate pressure;
- maintain some Fiji-direction reconnaissance/submarine activity;
- keep main fleet carriers at sea or north of Santo until late;
- delay final assault loading/concentration.

This can preserve strategic ambiguity without requiring modern deception doctrine.

## 9. Intelligence judgment

The realistic Allied intelligence sequence is:

**early October:** "Santo consolidation; another southern operation possible."

**mid-October:** "Combined Fleet is rebuilding for another major South Pacific move."

**late October:** "New Caledonia is the leading hypothesis, but Efate/fleet-action/Fiji pressure remain live alternatives."

**early November:** "New Caledonia probably the main objective; exact D-day and landing geometry still uncertain."

**D-2/D-1:** physical convoy and search geometry finally make New Caledonia close to certain.

Thus the Branch should distinguish:
- Japanese actual intent can be New Caledonia from 1 October;
- Allied correct inference should lag by roughly 3–5 weeks and remain probabilistic until the final movement phase.
