## 1. Operational frame

D+2 is no longer a beachhead-survival fight. The campaign problem becomes:
- Japan: break the Pallikulo/Pekoa belt, place Segond/Luganville under artillery pressure, and prevent an orderly evacuation.
- United States: trade ground for time, preserve aviation/technical cadres, keep Segond Channel usable for night evacuation, and avoid encirclement of the remaining combat force.

Historical geography anchors:
- Segond Channel is a deep, protected anchorage on the southeast of Espiritu Santo.
- Palikulo Bay is much less suitable for major deep-draft embarkation than the Segond side.
- the approaches to Segond Channel had U.S. minefields in place by August 1942.
Therefore Japanese surface forces cannot casually enter the harbor at night; interception must occur outside the mined approaches or by air/submarine.

## 2. Dawn state

### Japan
- 19,000–20,500 personnel ashore.
- 36–46 useful artillery / mountain / battalion guns ashore or ready.
- heavy-equipment package still only about 60–70% of preferred plan.
- chronic deficit: trucks, towing assets, some engineer stores, some heavy ammunition.
- Turtle Bay/Fighter 1 held but still cratered and within possible U.S. long-range artillery threat.
- no center sustained Japanese fighter operations from Fighter 1 on D+2 morning.

### United States
- organized southern defense around Pallikulo/Pekoa and Luganville–Segond Channel.
- immediate effective ground combat force: roughly 5,800–6,900 after D-Day/D+1 losses and fatigue.
- many additional base/technical personnel remain, but only a fraction are suitable for line infantry.
- Santo aviation is compressed southward; Efate remains the intact rear tactical base.
- Washington remains combat capable but cruiser/escort depth is thin.

## 3. U.S. command decision

Center decision by early morning:
- do NOT attempt an all-or-nothing recapture of Turtle Bay.
- hold Pallikulo/Pekoa long enough to evacuate aviation, medical, technical, intelligence and repair personnel.
- prepare staged demolition of aviation fuel, stores, workshops and harbor facilities that cannot be removed.
- keep a combat rear guard between the Japanese advance and Luganville/Segond.
- prepare night sea evacuation toward Efate/Noumea if the Pallikulo/Pekoa line becomes untenable.

Large, vulnerable support ships are not left casually in Segond under Japanese carrier/land-air threat. Seaplane-tender and high-value support functions are progressively shifted south as the battle develops.

## 4. 0500–0830 — Japanese pressure resumes

Japan does not launch a broad-front rush. The vehicle deficit makes a road-and-artillery axis more rational.

Main efforts:
- south from Turtle Bay along the eastern coastal road;
- pressure on Pallikulo Bay / northern base installations;
- an inland infantry flank intended to threaten the Pekoa road network rather than immediately storm Luganville.

Japanese preparation:
- 30-plus guns and mortars participate in distributed preparation.
- air observation is intermittent because U.S. fighter activity remains possible.
- engineers clear roadblocks and damaged bridges but movement remains slower than plan.

Center result by 0830:
- U.S. forward positions north of Pallikulo are forced back.
- no U.S. collapse.
- Japanese infantry advance outruns some artillery/ammunition trains in several sectors.

## 5. 0630–1030 — air fight over the southern belt

### U.S. tactical generation
Efate becomes the principal generator.
Center available first effort:
- fighters 10–14
- SBD 7–10
- TBF 1–2
- Santo-origin fighters/attack aircraft only in small numbers where strips remain usable.

Mission priority changes:
- less emphasis on sinking already-withdrawing transports;
- more emphasis on Japanese artillery, road columns, landing-craft concentrations and the Turtle Bay/Pallikulo advance.

### Japanese air
- Zuikaku/Ryujo combined immediately useful pool: approximately 48–58 aircraft after prior attrition.
- carrier fighters must cover both carrier decks and withdrawal shipping.
- Lunga can support only a limited D+2 effort without pretending full regeneration.
- no large repeat of the D-Day maximum-range Lunga package.

Center morning result:
- Japanese losses: 2–3 aircraft.
- U.S. losses: 3–5 aircraft.
- several Japanese road/shore movements are delayed; one artillery/transport concentration suffers localized damage.
- no decisive interruption of the Japanese ground advance.

## 6. 0900–1330 — Pallikulo fight

The Pallikulo/Pekoa defense is stronger than Turtle Bay because:
- U.S. forces have shortened their line;
- heavy AA, base-defense weapons and surviving artillery can be used as ground fire;
- the Japanese artillery train is not yet fully caught up.

Center result:
- Japan reaches the northern Pallikulo base zone during late morning.
- U.S. local counterattacks and artillery hold the Pekoa approaches for several hours.
- Japanese casualties rise materially compared with the Turtle Bay capture.

By 1200–1330:
- Pallikulo Bay support installations are being evacuated/demolished.
- U.S. command concludes the air/naval base belt north/east of Luganville cannot be held through another full day without risking encirclement.

## 7. 1200–1600 — evacuation of cadres and demolition

Priority personnel moved first:
- surviving aviation crews not needed for immediate cover;
- aircraft maintenance specialists;
- signals/intelligence personnel;
- wounded;
- naval base technical cadres;
- selected command staff and records.

Means:
- serviceable aircraft fly to Efate/Noumea.
- PBY and available transports move selected personnel/casualties.
- small craft and local shipping shift people/stores toward Segond embarkation points.
- deep-draft evacuation is reserved mainly for night.

Center daylight evacuation:
- approximately 600–900 personnel removed by air/short sea movement.
- most line combat troops remain on Santo.

Demolition:
- aviation gasoline and immovable stores increasingly destroyed.
- workshops and selected harbor equipment prepared for denial.
- not everything can be destroyed; the base is too large and the withdrawal too compressed.

## 8. 1330–1800 — line contracts toward Luganville

Japan uses local numerical superiority and artillery concentration to turn the Pallikulo position rather than annihilate it frontally.

Center:
- 1500–1700: Pallikulo main installations fall or are abandoned.
- Pekoa/Bomber-base area becomes tactically untenable late afternoon; demolition and withdrawal accelerate.
- Japan does NOT center-book the complete capture of Luganville/Segond on D+2.
- U.S. rear guard establishes a shorter perimeter protecting the Segond embarkation zone and main Luganville approaches.

Japanese ground-state at dusk:
- approximately 19,500–20,500 effective ashore population remains; follow-on/service personnel continue landing in smaller numbers.
- artillery system advances south but some guns/ammunition lag.
- Fighter 1 repairs continue; limited emergency fighter recovery/staging becomes possible late D+2 or D+3, but sustained local fighter basing is not yet center-booked.

## 9. D+2 ground losses

Center daily:
- Japan: 700–1,000 killed/wounded.
- U.S.: 850–1,150 killed/wounded/captured/missing.

Cumulative through D+2 dusk:
- Japan: approximately 2,150–3,000 ground casualties.
- U.S.: approximately 1,950–2,750 ground casualties.

The U.S. total includes an increasing captured/missing component as rear positions and base detachments are cut off during the contraction.

## 10. D+2 daylight air losses

Japan:
- 4–6 aircraft lost.
United States:
- 6–9 aircraft lost/destroyed.

Operational air state:
- Japan retains local daylight advantage, but carrier aircrew attrition is now a campaign resource issue.
- U.S. Santo-based tactical air is close to losing its local base system; Efate remains the decisive surviving generator.
- the Japanese capture of airfield ground matters only after repair, fuel, AA and ground security are established.

## 11. 27/28 Sep night — Segond evacuation window

The minefields and geography of Segond Channel make a direct Japanese destroyer penetration unattractive.

Japanese interdiction plan:
- destroyer/cruiser groups patrol outside the mined entrances and on the Efate route.
- submarines hold outer approach lines.
- no center attempt to force the mined channel with heavy ships.
- carrier/base aircraft prepare dawn search/attack against ships that fail to clear the area before daylight.

U.S. cover:
- Washington remains outside/near the evacuation route as fleet-in-being.
- Helena and remaining destroyers provide local cover.
- small escorts/minelayers familiar with the channel guide traffic.

Center night result:
- 1,400–2,000 additional U.S. personnel evacuated by sea, weighted toward wounded, aviation/base specialists and selected support troops.
- no large evacuation transport center-booked sunk inside the protected channel.
- one outer-screen/submarine contact produces evasive maneuver and delay but no major center sinking.
- not all combat troops are removed; a substantial rear guard remains to prevent the embarkation zone from being overrun.

Total D+2 evacuation center:
- approximately 2,000–2,800 personnel removed (day + night).

## 12. U.S. reinforcement logic

South Pacific command orders additional cruiser/destroyer cover toward the New Hebrides as the Santo emergency deepens.

This does not magically appear in the 27 Sep daylight battle.
Arrival/availability for 28–29 Sep is a D+3 gate and must reconcile:
- Wasp withdrawal escorts;
- North Carolina duty;
- MI lineage losses;
- existing Noumea/Efate assignments.

Do not auto-resurrect Astoria, Pensacola, Northampton or Benham.

## 13. End state — 28 Sep 0430

### Japan
Holds:
- Turtle Bay/Fighter 1
- Pallikulo main base zone
- most eastern coastal road corridor
- approaches to Pekoa, with Pekoa largely abandoned/denied

Does not yet securely hold:
- Luganville
- Segond Channel embarkation complex

Strength:
- roughly 17,000–18,300 ground effectives remaining after accumulated casualties, plus service/support elements.
- artillery remains decisive but mobility deficits persist.

### United States
Holds:
- Luganville–Segond Channel final perimeter
- selected western/southern approaches

Evacuated on D+2:
- ~2,000–2,800 personnel

Remaining on island at 0430:
- approximately 5,000–6,500 total military personnel, of whom roughly 3,800–4,800 are useful combat/rear-guard strength.

Strategic condition:
- Santo cannot realistically be restored as a stable U.S. air/naval base without major external intervention.
- the U.S. choice on D+3 is now between:
  1. a deliberate final night evacuation under Washington/air cover, or
  2. holding Segond/Luganville another day to extract more men at the cost of higher encirclement/capture risk.

Center tendency:
- deliberate evacuation, not a sacrificial last stand.
