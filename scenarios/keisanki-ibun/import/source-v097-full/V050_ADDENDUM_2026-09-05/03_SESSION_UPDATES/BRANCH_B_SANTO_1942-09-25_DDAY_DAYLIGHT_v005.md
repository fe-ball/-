## 1. Dawn state

### U.S. air system
Santo is not a single-strip system. Working center uses the Turtle Bay fighter strip plus the southeast Santo/Pallikulo-Pekoa bomber/maintenance complex, with Efate as the intact rear tactical field.

Dawn tactical-ready band:
- Santo: fighters 22–26; SBD 12–15; TBF 3–4; B-17 3–4; PBY/search aircraft separate.
- Efate: fighters 12–16; SBD 8–11; TBF 2–3; B-17 3–4; PBY/search aircraft separate.
- Total immediate anti-shipping pool: fighters 34–42; SBD 20–26; TBF 5–7; B-17 6–8.
- Wasp air-group survivors are already mixed into these numbers; do not double count.

Santo sortie generation at dawn: ~65–75% of undamaged local normal because the 24 Sep Kongo/Haruna bombardment was interrupted after ~120–150 14-inch shells.
Efate: essentially intact.

### Japanese air system
Immediate D-Day pool (not total theater inventory):
- Zuikaku: ~50–55 combat-ready aircraft, including ~20–23 Zero, ~16–19 D3A, ~11–14 B5N.
- Ryujo: ~23–27 ready, fighter-heavy, approximately 15–18 Zero plus 7–9 B5N.
- Lunga timed-strike pool: approximately 18–22 Zero and 12–15 G4M available for one major maximum-range coordinated window; this is not all-day Santo CAP.
- Shokaku flight deck remains unavailable; surviving aircraft/crews are redistributed only where maintenance/deck/base capacity allows and are not counted twice.

### Surface state
- Washington remains combat capable south of Santo.
- Quincy is a heavy-damage mission kill; Preston sunk.
- Kongo is a shore-bombardment mission kill after one 16-inch hit.
- Hagikaze is a torpedo/surface mission kill; Tokitsukaze damaged.
- Haruna remains combat capable.
- Hiei and Kirishima remain the previously reserved second heavy pair and have not yet been spent in the D-1 night action.

## 2. Landing geometry

Center concept retains the old Branch sequence in which Turtle Bay is fought over before the Luganville–Segond Channel complex falls.

- Northern/main landing sector: Turtle Bay / eastern Santo approaches, intended to neutralize Fighter Field 1 and open the coastal road south.
- Southern/secondary landing sector: Pallikulo/Surundu approaches, intended to split the U.S. southeast base belt and threaten Luganville from the north/east without conducting a direct Segond Channel port assault.
- The deep Segond Channel anchorage is not used as the first-wave assault axis because it concentrates ships under known shore defenses and Washington's night threat.

Japanese assault force in the revised Sep operation: approximately 20,000–23,000 total campaign assault/support personnel; not all are scheduled to be ashore on D-Day.

## 3. 0430–0630 — approach and first wave

- PBY tracking means strategic/tactical surprise is limited; U.S. coastal defenses are alert.
- Japanese destroyers/light cruisers provide short suppressive fires; Atago/Takao can provide limited 8-inch fire from a safer stand-off but do not remain in a long daylight shore-fire pattern under the Washington threat.
- Haruna does not automatically re-enter the same pre-dawn bombardment lane after the previous night's interception.

First assault package deliberately lands infantry together with battalion/field artillery elements, mortars, signals, engineers, ammunition and towing/transport assets in accordance with the Branch amphibious-package doctrine.

Working center by ~0800:
- ~5,500–6,500 Japanese personnel ashore across both sectors.
- first useful 70/75-mm-class guns and heavy mortars operating during the morning rather than waiting for a later separate lift.
- U.S. beach/coastal fire destroys or disables several landing craft and delays individual waves but does not collapse either lodgment.

## 4. 0600–0830 — first U.S. anti-shipping strike

Because U.S. command expects the Japanese dawn suppression strike, a substantial share of available SBD/TBF aircraft are launched or dispersed before the first Japanese bombs arrive.

Working first anti-shipping package:
- SBD ~12–14
- TBF ~3–4
- fighter escort ~5–7

Japanese convoy CAP:
- mainly Ryujo fighter allocation plus a limited Zuikaku contribution, ~18–22 Zero in the relevant sector/window.

Center air losses in this exchange:
- U.S.: 3–5 SBD, 1 TBF, 1–2 fighters.
- Japan: 2–3 Zero.

Shipping effect:
- the D-1 bomb-damaged heavy-equipment cargo transport is hit again / fire worsens and is lost or abandoned as a useful assault transport.
- some cargo had already been written off/delayed on D-1; the remaining heavy-equipment package aboard is now permanently lost rather than merely delayed.
- landing craft and deck cargo suffer additional losses.

## 5. 0630–1030 — Japanese suppression sequence

### Carrier component
Zuikaku sends a limited base-suppression package rather than emptying the deck while the convoy is exposed.
Working scale:
- Zero ~10–12
- D3A ~10–14
- B5N horizontal/level or fixed-target role ~4–6

Objectives:
- runway intersections
- AA positions
- fuel/maintenance areas
- communications
- known artillery positions

### Lunga component
One maximum-range coordinated Lunga strike is committed in the morning after carrier fighters have forced U.S. CAP activity.
Working scale:
- G4M ~12–15
- Zero ~16–20

The Zero escort has little Santo loiter margin and is ordered to cover bomber ingress, fight briefly, then disengage.

Center morning air-combat / strike losses across Japanese suppression attacks:
- Japan: 3–5 Zero, 2–4 G4M, 2–3 carrier attack aircraft.
- U.S.: 4–7 fighters in air combat; 2–4 additional aircraft destroyed on the ground; several damaged.

Operational effect:
- Santo sortie generation drops temporarily to roughly 40–50% during the mid-morning disruption.
- runways remain repairable; airfields are not permanently closed.
- fuel/maintenance/communications damage slows repeat sorties more than first launches.
- Efate remains intact and continues generating aircraft.

## 6. 0830–1230 — unloading race

Branch amphibious improvements are applied as handling/coordination advantages, not as immunity to air/artillery fire.

By ~1200 center:
- ~9,000–10,500 Japanese personnel ashore.
- ~14–18 field/mountain/battalion-gun-class pieces plus substantial mortars are in or near combat-ready state.
- ammunition/signals/engineer packages are materially better synchronized than historical Japanese median.
- the lost D-1/D-Day heavy cargo ship produces a real deficit in trucks, towing assets, engineer stores and selected guns.

U.S. ground defense remains organized around the airfield/base belt and road network; the Japanese do not seize the Luganville–Segond complex in the morning.

## 7. 1000–1230 — Efate second anti-shipping strike

Working package:
- SBD ~9–12
- TBF ~2–3
- F4F ~5–7

Japanese CAP is still substantial but is being pulled among convoy cover, Zuikaku defense and Santo suppression.

Center losses:
- U.S.: 2–4 SBD, 1 TBF, 1–2 fighters.
- Japan: 2–3 Zero.

Shipping effect:
- one additional transport receives a 500-lb-class direct hit / damaging near misses; medium damage, reduced unloading rate, but not sunk in center.
- a second transport receives light near-miss/fragmentation damage.
- no center TBF torpedo hit; Mk 13 reliability and difficult attack geometry remain limiting factors.

## 8. 1200–1500 — B-17 disruption / beachhead consolidation

- 6–8 B-17-class sorties make high-altitude attacks/tracking passes against shipping.
- no center direct hit on a maneuvering transport.
- repeated warning forces the transport anchorage to disperse and interrupts efficient lighter cycles for ~30–60 minutes.

Ground:
- northern lodgment expands toward Turtle Bay's outer defensive belt.
- southern lodgment pushes toward Pallikulo/road links.
- U.S. counterattacks prevent a clean first-day seizure of the entire airfield/base belt.
- Branch Japanese packaging means battalion/regimental combined-arms groupings form several hours earlier than in the old historical-median model despite cargo losses.

## 9. 1500–1800 — third U.S. pressure window

A smaller mixed Santo/Efate strike is generated after runway repair and rearming:
- SBD ~7–10
- TBF ~1–3
- fighter cover limited and mixed.

Center losses:
- U.S.: 2–3 SBD, 0–1 TBF, ~1 fighter.
- Japan: 1–2 Zero.

Center shipping result:
- no second transport sinking.
- one already damaged transport's unloading is effectively terminated and it is ordered north after darkness.
- additional Daihatsu/landing craft are destroyed/damaged by air attack, artillery and surf/handling.

## 10. D-Day daylight air-loss center

Japan:
- ~12–17 aircraft lost during D-Day daylight, of which ~8–11 are fighters/attack aircraft from carrier/CAP work and ~2–4 G4M; several additional damaged.

United States:
- ~15–21 aircraft lost/destroyed during D-Day daylight, dominated by SBD/F4F/P-39-class aircraft; additional damaged aircraft accumulate at Santo/Efate.

This does not imply Japanese unrestricted air superiority. The U.S. has traded aircraft for transport/unloading disruption, while Japan has traded fighter/aircrew depth to keep the assault system moving.

## 11. D-Day daylight shipping / landing-craft result

Center:
- Japanese transports: 1 sunk/abandoned as assault-useful hull (the previously D-1-damaged heavy-equipment ship), 1 medium damaged, 1 light damaged.
- landing craft: roughly 8–14 destroyed/lost and another 15–25 damaged/unavailable at various times.
- no center oiler loss; oilers/support tankers remain outside the immediate beach anchorage and are tracked separately.

## 12. Dusk unloading and ground state

By ~1830 center:
- ~13,500–15,500 Japanese personnel ashore.
- enough artillery/mortars/ammunition/signals/engineers are ashore to sustain two strong lodgments, but the intended heavy-equipment package is incomplete.
- approximately 22–30 useful field/mountain/battalion-gun-class pieces are ashore/being brought into action, with additional heavy equipment delayed offshore or lost with the damaged transport system.
- selected trucks/tractors/engineer stores are materially below plan.

Ground control:
- Japan firmly holds two beachheads and parts of the coastal road network.
- Turtle Bay/Fighter Field 1 is under direct operational threat and intermittent disruption but is not center-booked as fully captured by dusk.
- Pallikulo/southeast air-base complex remains contested/operational in reduced form.
- Luganville–Segond Channel remains in U.S. hands at dusk.

Working D-Day ground casualties:
- Japan: ~650–900 killed/wounded.
- U.S.: ~450–700 killed/wounded/captured/missing in the immediate battle sectors.
These are D-Day only, not campaign totals.

## 13. Operational assessment at 1830

Japan has achieved the minimum condition for continuing the invasion:
- two sustainable lodgments;
- >60% of the intended first-day assault manpower ashore;
- enough combined-arms material to avoid a pure light-infantry beachhead;
- convoy not broken despite repeated U.S. air attacks.

But Japan has failed to achieve the preferred D-Day condition:
- Santo air system is not destroyed;
- heavy-equipment unloading is behind plan;
- Washington remains a night threat;
- one transport loss and two damaged hulls compress the remaining unloading schedule.

The U.S. has not stopped the landing, but has converted Japanese delay into a continuing logistics deficit rather than merely inflicting aircraft casualties.

## 14. D-Day night setup (OPEN — not adjudicated here)

The next surface decision is qualitatively different from 24 Sep:
- Washington is known to be present and intact.
- Kongo is unavailable for immediate bombardment.
- Haruna remains available.
- Hiei/Kirishima, held out of the first night action, remain fresh heavy hulls.
- the first Japanese torpedo groups have expended/damaged ships; a second-night force must use a changed destroyer roster if Japan wants fresh Type-93 depth.
- U.S. escort depth is lower after Preston loss and Quincy mission kill.

Japanese D-night choices:
A. Hiei/Kirishima-led deliberate battle to push Washington away and reopen bombardment/unloading sea room.
B. avoid a decisive BB action, use fresh destroyer/cruiser torpedo screens, unload under darkness and accept continued Santo air sorties next day.
C. limited Haruna/CA standoff bombardment with Hiei/Kirishima as cover rather than primary bombarders.

No D-night outcome is booked in v005.
