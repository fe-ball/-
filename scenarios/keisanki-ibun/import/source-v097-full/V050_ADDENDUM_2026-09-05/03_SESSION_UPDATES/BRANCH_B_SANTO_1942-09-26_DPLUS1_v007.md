## 1. Dawn state

### Japanese ground force
By 0430–0500, continued unloading during the D-Day night brings the center ashore strength to:
- 17,000–18,500 personnel ashore.
- 30–38 useful field/mountain/battalion-gun-class pieces in or approaching combat readiness.
- mortars, signals, engineer packages and ammunition sufficient for sustained regimental attacks.
- trucks, tractors, selected engineer stores and some heavy ammunition remain materially below plan because of the lost/damaged heavy-equipment transports.
- 3,000–5,000 campaign personnel/support elements remain offshore, in follow-on shipping, or not yet tactically ashore.

### U.S. ground force
- organized defense remains around Turtle Bay/Fighter 1, the Pallikulo–Pekoa air-base belt, and the Luganville–Segond Channel complex.
- D-Day casualties and night bombardment have reduced local unit cohesion, communications and some artillery positions, but the defense is not broken.
- center effective immediate ground combat strength at dawn: approximately 6,500–7,800, with additional support/base personnel not all suitable for infantry combat.

### U.S. air
Night bombardment effect:
- Santo immediate sortie generation: 45–60%, center ~52%.
- Efate: intact.

Immediate ready center band:
- Santo: fighters 10–13; SBD 4–6; TBF 1–2; patrol/search separate.
- Efate: fighters 12–15; SBD 7–10; TBF 2–3; B-17/PBY support separate.
- total immediately useful tactical anti-shipping/air-defense pool: fighters 22–28; SBD 11–16; TBF 3–5.

### Japanese air
After D-2 through D-Day attrition:
- Zuikaku remains the primary carrier deck; roughly 40–46 immediately useful aircraft.
- Ryujo remains fighter-heavy; roughly 18–22 immediately useful aircraft.
- Lunga can generate a smaller D+1 window than on D-Day: approximately 8–10 G4M and 12–16 Zero are realistically available for one limited long-range operation without assuming full overnight regeneration.
- Shokaku remains flight-deck mission killed; surviving aircrews/aircraft are not double-counted.

## 2. Japanese D+1 operational priorities
1. seize / permanently deny Turtle Bay Fighter 1;
2. expand and join the two lodgments;
3. unload ammunition, artillery and engineer stores before withdrawing large transports;
4. suppress Santo enough to prevent concentrated anti-shipping sorties;
5. avoid spending Hiei/Kirishima or the destroyer screen in a third consecutive high-risk battleship night action unless Washington presents an opportunity.

## 3. 0500–0830 — ground assault begins

The 25 Sep night bombardment has localized but serious effects around runway intersections, communications, roads and known defensive positions.

Japanese artillery and mortars begin coordinated preparation before infantry attacks against:
- Turtle Bay outer defensive line;
- road junctions south of Fighter 1;
- northern approaches to the Pallikulo–Pekoa base belt.

The Japanese assault is materially better coordinated than historical Japanese median because artillery, signals, engineers and ammunition arrived in packaged units.

However, vehicle/towing shortages slow lateral movement and ammunition redistribution.

Center ground result by 0830:
- Japanese forces penetrate the outer Turtle Bay defensive belt but do not yet fully secure the airfield.
- U.S. defenders conduct organized local withdrawals and counterattacks rather than collapse.
- the field is already unusable for normal sustained fighter operations under artillery observation and ground threat.

## 4. 0630–1000 — morning air battle / shipping pressure

U.S. command uses Efate as the principal intact generation base and Santo as a damaged forward strip.

First U.S. anti-shipping package:
- SBD 8–11
- TBF 2–3
- fighters 5–7

Japanese convoy CAP:
- Ryujo-centered plus limited Zuikaku support, approximately 16–20 Zero in the relevant window.

Center losses:
- U.S.: 2–4 SBD, 0–1 TBF, 1–2 fighters.
- Japan: 2–3 Zero.

Shipping result:
- one already lightly damaged transport receives further near-miss/fragmentation damage and is ordered to finish only priority unloading before withdrawal.
- no center transport sinking.
- several landing craft lost/damaged; unloading tempo falls for roughly 30–45 minutes.

## 5. 0730–1030 — Japanese air/ground suppression

Zuikaku launches a smaller, more target-specific suppression strike than on D-Day:
- Zero 8–10
- D3A 8–12
- B5N 3–5

Targets:
- active Santo strip segments;
- artillery positions threatening Turtle Bay;
- communications/road nodes;
- dispersed aircraft areas where reconnaissance confirms activity.

Lunga contribution is deliberately limited:
- one 8–10 G4M raid with 12–16 Zero maximum-range escort, or equivalent staggered use.
- the Zero escort has minimal loiter margin.
- this is not a repeat of the full D-Day maximum effort.

Center air losses:
- Japan: 2–3 Zero, 1–2 G4M/attack aircraft.
- U.S.: 2–4 fighters, 1–3 aircraft destroyed/damaged on ground.

Effect:
- Santo remains operational only intermittently during the morning.
- Efate continues to sustain the air battle.

## 6. 0900–1400 — Turtle Bay decision

Japanese ground forces increase pressure with approximately regiment-plus scale on the northern axis, supported by artillery/mortars and short naval/air fires.

The U.S. cannot safely operate Fighter 1 as a normal airfield once Japanese observed artillery reaches the perimeter and road exits are threatened.

Center line:
- 1100–1300: U.S. command orders evacuation/destruction of remaining movable aviation stores and withdrawal from the Fighter 1 position toward the southern base belt.
- 1300–1500: Japanese troops occupy the principal Turtle Bay Fighter 1 area.

Status of Fighter 1 at capture:
- cratered and battle-damaged;
- fuel/maintenance stocks partly destroyed or evacuated;
- scattered demolitions and unexploded ordnance;
- NOT immediately usable as a Japanese operational fighter base.

Earliest center emergency Japanese use:
- liaison/light aircraft: potentially D+2 after clearing;
- limited fighter emergency recovery/staging: D+2 to D+3 if engineers, fuel and AA can be moved forward;
- sustained fighter operations: later, depending on ground security and logistics.

## 7. 1100–1600 — second U.S. air effort / unloading completion

Efate and repaired Santo facilities generate another smaller strike:
- SBD 6–9
- TBF 1–2
- fighters 3–5

Japanese CAP is thinner because carrier aircraft must also cover Zuikaku and withdrawal lanes.

Center losses:
- U.S.: 2–3 SBD, 0–1 fighter/TBF.
- Japan: 1–2 Zero.

Shipping result:
- one transport receives medium near-miss/fragmentation damage; still mobile.
- no center direct hit on an oiler.
- no new center transport sinking.

By ~1600:
- 18,500–20,000 Japanese personnel are ashore.
- 36–46 field/mountain/battalion-gun-class pieces are ashore/usable or being emplaced.
- personnel landing is nearing completion.
- heavy-equipment unloading remains only about 60–70% of the preferred plan because of transport loss, damaged handling gear, air interruption and vehicle deficits.
- large transports begin withdrawal northward as soon as priority cargo is discharged.

## 8. 1400–1830 — southern base belt

Japanese forces from the northern and southern lodgments make contact and improve the coastal-road link, but the Pallikulo–Pekoa–Luganville belt is not yet captured.

Center:
- Japan holds Turtle Bay/Fighter 1 and a continuous or near-continuous eastern coastal lodgment.
- U.S. retains organized defense around Pallikulo/Pekoa and Luganville–Segond Channel.
- U.S. begins consolidating a shorter southern perimeter and prepares selected demolitions/evacuation routes.

D+1 ground casualties center:
- Japan: 800–1,100 killed/wounded.
- U.S.: 650–900 killed/wounded/captured/missing.

Cumulative through D+1 dusk:
- Japan ground casualties: ~1,450–2,000.
- U.S. ground casualties: ~1,100–1,600.

## 9. D+1 daylight air losses

Japan:
- ~6–9 aircraft lost, additional damaged.
United States:
- ~8–12 aircraft lost/destroyed, additional damaged.

Aircrew/fighter-depth consequence:
- Japan retains local air advantage but the carrier fighter pool is no longer cheap to spend.
- U.S. Santo tactical aviation is shrinking, but Efate prevents a rapid collapse.

## 10. D+1 shipping/unloading state at dusk

Campaign shipping state after D-1 through D+1 center:
- transports: 1 sunk/abandoned, 2–3 carrying medium/light damage of varying degree.
- no center oiler loss.
- landing craft attrition continues and materially slows later heavy unloading.
- most assault personnel are ashore.
- missing/delayed cargo is concentrated in trucks, towing assets, engineer stores, selected artillery/ammunition rather than the core infantry mass.

Large transport policy:
- begin staged withdrawal north after dusk;
- retain only what is needed for remaining priority cargo;
- shift later movement toward smaller/night-biased shipping where practical.

## 11. 26/27 Sep night — center decision

Japan does NOT center-book a third consecutive battleship bombardment.

Reasons:
- Washington remains combat capable.
- Hiei/Kirishima have already exposed themselves and expended bombardment ammunition.
- the principal D+1 objective, Turtle Bay denial/capture, has been achieved.
- large transports now need withdrawal cover more than another long bombardment.
- preserving fast battleships/destroyers for the continuing Santo campaign and New Caledonia has positive value.

Japanese night posture:
- cruiser/destroyer cover north/east of Santo;
- torpedo screens prepared if Washington closes;
- limited cruiser/destroyer harassment fire only if a low-risk opportunity appears;
- no center-line 14-inch bombardment.

U.S. posture:
- Washington patrols/interdicts but Lee does not charge into the Japanese destroyer screen after two nights of Type 93 experience.
- Helena remains the principal cruiser radar/gun asset after Quincy and Atlanta mission kills.

Center night result:
- no major surface battle.
- brief radar/contact maneuvering and long-range harassment possible.
- no center capital-ship hit.
- Japanese large-transport withdrawal is not intercepted decisively.
- Washington's fleet-in-being effect forces Japanese cover groups to remain concentrated and limits any leisurely unloading/shore bombardment.

## 12. End state, 27 Sep 0430

Japan:
- Turtle Bay/Fighter 1 captured but not yet operational as a Japanese air base.
- ~19,000–20,500 personnel ashore.
- artillery/mortar system substantially established.
- vehicle/heavy-engineer deficit remains.
- large transports withdrawing.
- Zuikaku/Ryujo still combat capable but air groups attrited.
- Hiei/Kirishima preserved after D-Day night action.

United States:
- Luganville–Segond Channel and Pallikulo/Pekoa southern base belt still held.
- Santo local aviation reduced and increasingly displaced south/Efate.
- Efate remains the critical intact rear tactical base.
- Washington remains combat capable and continues to constrain Japanese night operations.
- cruiser/escort depth is materially degraded.

Operational judgment:
- D+1 is a Japanese operational success on land (Turtle Bay seizure) but not a collapse of the U.S. Santo defense.
- U.S. air action continues to convert Japanese victory into a heavy-equipment/logistics deficit.
- the campaign now shifts from beachhead survival to reduction of the southern base belt.
