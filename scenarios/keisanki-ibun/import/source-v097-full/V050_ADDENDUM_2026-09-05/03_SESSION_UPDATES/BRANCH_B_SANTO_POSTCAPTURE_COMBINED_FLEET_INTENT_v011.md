## 1. Strategic thesis

Combined Fleet does not treat Santo as the end of an island-hopping timetable.

Its preferred exploitation is:
**convert Santo into an air/search/fleet-staging anvil, then make New Caledonia the place where the remaining U.S. South Pacific fleet is compelled to fight under Japanese land-air, submarine and torpedo-screen pressure.**

New Caledonia remains a real territorial/communications objective, but destruction or displacement of the U.S. surface/carrier force is a parallel high-value objective.

## 2. Institutional tension

### Combined Fleet / Yamamoto tendency
- seek an operational situation that draws out major U.S. fleet units;
- use carriers, cruisers, destroyers and submarines to attrit/disorder the enemy before a heavy surface decision;
- exploit the temporary U.S. carrier weakness before new construction and repaired ships restore the balance.

### Naval General Staff / IGHQ political settlement
- limited FS only;
- New Caledonia conditional on Santo, shipping and carrier readiness;
- Fiji/Samoa are NOT automatic follow-ons;
- avoid endless expansion that creates fixed submarine targets and permanent shipping burdens.

Center compromise:
**one more major South Pacific blow — New Caledonia — then reassess/stop.**

## 3. What Santo changes

Before Santo:
- Lunga is mainly an eye/relay and extreme-range strike origin.
- carriers must provide much of the search, fixed-target suppression and convoy CAP.
- fleet units spend more fuel/time merely reaching the southern battle area.

After Santo:
- fighter escort over Efate is routine-range rather than extreme-range.
- land-based reconnaissance can cover Efate/New Caledonia approaches.
- damaged/low-fuel aircraft have a forward recovery field.
- destroyer/cruiser groups and tankers gain a southern staging anchorage.
- convoy assembly can occur much closer to New Caledonia.
- carriers can reserve more aircraft for fleet combat instead of daily base suppression.

Therefore Santo acts as an **unsinkable support carrier / reconnaissance platform / fleet staging point**, though not a heavy repair base.

## 4. Immediate Combined Fleet priorities after 29 Sep

### Priority 1 — secure the prize
29 Sep–4 Oct:
- clear/mark Segond mine approaches;
- restore Fighter 1;
- establish dispersed fuel, AA, signals and navigation aids;
- inventory captured construction matériel;
- move destroyer/transport/tanker support forward.

Do not chase the retreating U.S. fleet deep toward Efate/Noumea.

### Priority 2 — rebuild combat power
- repair Shokaku flight deck and aviation support systems;
- repair Kongo and damaged destroyers;
- rebuild Zuikaku/Ryujo and land-air groups after Santo attrition;
- retain experienced aircrew as training/formation nuclei instead of spending them in daily nuisance raids.

### Priority 3 — pressure Efate without occupying it
- fighter-escorted reconnaissance;
- selective airfield/fuel/communications attacks;
- submarine outer lines;
- occasional surface harassment only under favorable geometry.

Objective:
reduce sortie/search output and force U.S. reinforcement into Efate without paying the permanent garrison/logistics bill of conquest.

## 5. Combined Fleet lessons from Santo

### 5.1 Carrier hull kill is not enough
Wasp's mission kill did not erase its air group because aircraft recovered to Santo/Efate.

New rule for New Caledonia:
**carrier attack and land-base suppression must be synchronized.**

If a U.S. carrier is hit, Tontouta/other recovery fields should simultaneously be under attack or disruption so that the enemy cannot simply convert a carrier air group into land-based aviation.

### 5.2 Washington/SG is an independent threat
U.S. carrier weakness does not imply surface weakness.

New rule:
- identify SG-capable fast battleship groups as independent high-value targets;
- use submarine/air search to track them;
- screen bombardment groups with torpedo destroyers;
- do not allow Kongo-class bombardment groups to lead blindly into radar gunfire.

### 5.3 Type 93 is a maneuver weapon as well as a hit weapon
Santo validated:
- staggered torpedo waves;
- forcing evasive turns;
- preserving reload-capable destroyers;
- disengaging once the shore-support objective is achieved.

New Caledonia surface doctrine should begin with torpedo screens and maneuver shaping, not a straight battleship approach.

### 5.4 Land bases can release carrier decks
Santo land air should absorb:
- much reconnaissance;
- fixed-target suppression;
- local CAP/staging;
- flying-boat contact maintenance.

Carrier decks should prioritize:
- enemy carrier/surface fleet search confirmation;
- offensive strike;
- fleet CAP;
- reserve attack.

## 6. Preferred New Caledonia force architecture

### Fleet strike group
Center concept:
- Shokaku
- Zuikaku
- Hiryu
- Soryu
subject to repair and air-group readiness.

These four decks are not treated as four untouched air groups.
Working first-line operational aircraft band should inherit Santo losses and 5–6 weeks regeneration.

### Support carriers
Junyo/Ryujo/Zuiho etc.:
- convoy CAP;
- ASW;
- secondary fixed-target attack;
- replacement/recovery deck;
- reserve fighter source.

Do not automatically merge all light/medium carriers into the main strike deck cycle.

### Fast battleships
Likely usable center by early November:
- Hiei
- Kirishima
- Haruna
- Kongo only if Santo repair closes in time.

Mission split:
- one heavy group for night/interception;
- one group for shore fire / carrier-heavy cover where hull availability permits.

### Main battle-force question
Combined Fleet is likely to **want** a heavier distant backstop (Yamato/Nagato-class elements) if intelligence indicates Washington/South Dakota/North Carolina concentration.

Center policy:
- keep such heavy units as a distant decisive-battle reserve / surface deterrent;
- do not use Yamato/Nagato as routine shore-bombardment ships;
- commitment depends on fuel/tanker geometry and confidence that a genuine U.S. battle-line engagement can be forced.

## 7. Submarine concept

Before the New Caledonia assault:
- outer line toward Efate/Fiji;
- Australia–Noumea approach line;
- contact/reporting line outside carrier-search arcs;
- separate oilers/support-group attack opportunities.

Primary value:
1. locate U.S. heavy units;
2. update contact before carrier launch;
3. attack tankers/crippled ships;
4. force U.S. escorts to spend destroyer/ASW depth.

Do not evaluate submarines only by capital-ship sink count.

## 8. The trap Combined Fleet would prefer

Ideal Japanese sequence:

1. Santo reconnaissance/submarines identify U.S. concentration.
2. Efate and New Caledonia airfields are pressured but not necessarily destroyed.
3. invasion preparations become visible enough that U.S. surface forces must move.
4. support carriers protect the convoy.
5. Santo land air attacks fixed bases / provides search.
6. fleet carriers remain strike-ready against the U.S. fleet.
7. torpedo-screened fast surface groups occupy the night approaches.
8. if U.S. fast battleships/carrier(s) are damaged or displaced, bombardment and landing groups move in.

This is not a pure ambush.
It is **battlefield shaping**: make the United States defend Noumea on geometry chosen by Japan.

## 9. What Combined Fleet does NOT want

- immediate invasion of Efate;
- a chase of Washington/TF64 beyond Japanese air/search support;
- daily high-loss carrier strikes against fixed airfields;
- forcing Segond/other mined harbors with capital ships;
- automatic Fiji/Samoa continuation after New Caledonia;
- turning Santo into a Truk-scale heavy repair base;
- spending experienced carrier aircrew merely because aircraft replacements exist.

## 10. October operational calendar (center)

### 29 Sep–4 Oct
- Santo security, mine clearing, Fighter 1 restoration.
- evacuation of damaged Japanese hulls.
- initial air/search detachments move in.

### 5–15 Oct
- selective Efate suppression/recon.
- submarine redeployment.
- carrier air-group regeneration.
- repair of Shokaku/Kongo/DDs.
- night-action lessons formalized from Santo.

### 16–25 Oct
- Santo grows into a 45–65 aircraft sustainable base system.
- multi-carrier search/CAP/strike rehearsals.
- tanker/convoy routing and dispersal rehearsed.
- New Caledonia aerial reconnaissance intensifies.
- U.S. fast-battleship and cruiser positions become priority intelligence targets.

### 26 Oct–4 Nov
- visible convoy assembly / feints.
- increased submarine contact reporting.
- repeated but selective pressure on Efate and Tontouta.
- main carriers move to operational staging without unnecessary early exposure.

### 5–8 Nov
- final fleet/convoy concentration.
- support carriers take convoy-defense load.
- Santo land aviation takes maximum feasible fixed-target/search load.
- main carrier group preserves offensive reserve for the U.S. fleet.

## 11. Strategic judgment

Santo makes Combined Fleet more aggressive in **where it is willing to force battle**, but more conservative in **how it spends carriers and battleships**.

The intended post-Santo strategy is therefore not:
"we won, so advance everywhere."

It is:
**"we now own the forward geometry; force one more major battle for New Caledonia while the U.S. carrier clock is still weak, then stop expanding unless the result is extraordinarily favorable."**

This reconciles Yamamoto/Combined Fleet decisive-battle instincts with the Branch limited-FS political settlement and the hard lessons from MI and Santo.
