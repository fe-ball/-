## 3. British warning exists before the raid
The Branch does NOT surprise an unwarned Bay of Bengal.
The O-23 sighting/attack on SUZUYA/KUMANO in the Malacca area remains a highly robust carry-in event because the Japanese staging route remains present.
Historical Somerville response on 30 Jul included ordering **all independent shipping on the east coast of India to the nearest ports**.

Ruling: the shipping-warning/port-shelter order is retained and, unlike history where the suspected raid evaporated, must be maintained while the Branch raid is actually at sea.

This matters because historical Eastern Fleet reporting says that after the April raid:
- Bay movements had temporarily fallen to essential colliers, tankers and war stores only;
- by end May traffic had recovered to **up to 18 ships at a time at sea**, supporting about **50 Calcutta ship calls/month**.

The Branch therefore pays Operation B first through **lost flow and port waiting**, not by assuming the sea remains full of April-style easy targets.
