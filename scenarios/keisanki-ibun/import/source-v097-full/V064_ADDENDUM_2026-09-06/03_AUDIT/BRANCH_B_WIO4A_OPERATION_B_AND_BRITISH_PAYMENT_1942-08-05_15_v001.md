# WIO-4A audit — Operation B and British payment, 5–15 Aug 1942

## Audit question
When the historical WATCHTOWER cancellation trigger disappears, does the already-staged Japanese Operation B execute; and if so, where does Britain pay for maintaining PEDESTAL, Madagascar, Bay of Bengal trade security and the developing western Indian Ocean A/S problem at the same time?

## Finding A — Operation B is a real divergence, not an invented offensive
SUZUYA/KUMANO were physically at Mergui by 30 Jul. SENDAI/DesRon 3 and attached destroyers had staged for a commerce-raiding incursion. ISUZU reached Mergui 8 Aug. Oilers were actively replenishing the force. In history the 7 Aug WATCHTOWER landing caused the force to turn east.

Branch has no historical WATCHTOWER landing. Therefore the cancellation condition is absent.

**CENTER: Operation B sorties from Mergui on 8 Aug after replenishment.**

## Finding B — old v062 Santo timing was stale
Authoritative v050 fixes Santo D-Day at 25 Sep, not 23 Aug. Historical transit clocks show Mergui→Pacific fleet/Truk movements on the order of roughly two weeks. A raid terminating around 18 Aug can still put the ships back into the forward system in early September.

Therefore Operation B and Santo compete in fuel, maintenance and readiness, but **not in basic calendar feasibility**.

## Finding C — Britain has warning but lacks the historical interceptor
O-23's historical contact with the two heavy cruisers remains the natural Branch warning. Somerville historically responded by sending independent east-coast shipping to the nearest ports and using a Force A containing WARSPITE + ILLUSTRIOUS + FORMIDABLE.

The Branch cannot reproduce that force. FORMIDABLE/INDOMITABLE are already sunk, ILLUSTRIOUS is west for PEDESTAL, and HERMES is not a modern-fleet-carrier substitute.

Thus the rational Branch payment is **traffic denial to the raider**, not a carrierless deep sweep.

## Finding D — Britain chooses which blood to preserve
Through the cutoff Britain retains two high priorities:
- PEDESTAL: ILLUSTRIOUS remains committed and is mission-killed on 12 Aug under existing Branch canon.
- Madagascar: BIRMINGHAM/NORMAN/NIZAM and available planning/transport assets continue toward Kilindini; the missing FORMIDABLE carrier slot remains empty.

The sacrificed margin is the Bay of Bengal:
- independent traffic remains sheltered/throttled;
- reconnaissance requirements remain elevated;
- there is no historical carrier-backed Force A hunting the Japanese raiders.

This is a genuine cross-theater payment: the Mediterranean consumes the last modern British deck while Madagascar consumes available Indian Ocean surface/amphibious planning capacity, leaving the Bay defended mainly by warning, shore air and trade restriction.

## Finding E — direct casualties are not yet posted
The Japanese raiders are real, but so is the British warning. Historical traffic after April had only gradually recovered; by end May the system permitted up to 18 ships at sea and about 50 Calcutta calls/month. The 30 Jul warning order drives independent ships toward ports before the Branch 8 Aug sortie.

Therefore copying the April surface-raider slaughter would be false. Conversely, essential colliers/tankers/war-store movement prevents declaring an automatic zero.

**Named surface-raider casualties remain OPEN through 15 Aug.**
The cost already booked is continued traffic restriction and associated queue/delay. Exact hull-days await movement reconstruction.

## Finding F — the next thin spot is western, but its blood has not yet been earned
On 7 Aug historical planning moved toward reallocating 3 A/S craft to the Persian Gulf; the actual three A/S whalers sailed Kilindini only on 25 Aug. The Gulf of Aden submarine threat was already appearing in D/F by 23 Jul and became kinetic later.

Operation B increases the incentive to keep craft east. But because the historical sailing date is still ten days beyond this cutoff, WIO-4A does not manufacture a transfer delay or a Gulf sinking. It creates a hard gate for WIO-4B:
- keep the craft east and western convoy protection pays;
- send them west and the Bay/Ceylon system pays.

## Center verdict
At 15 Aug Britain has **paid without yet giving the Japanese a confirmed extra merchant kill**:
- PEDESTAL has taken ILLUSTRIOUS out of the usable global carrier pool;
- Madagascar has retained its surface/amphibious claim but lost its modern carrier slot;
- Bay of Bengal commerce remains deliberately throttled because the fleet cannot safely provide the historical mobile shield;
- Japanese Operation B is physically underway and consumes Japanese fuel/readiness rather than being cancelled;
- the Persian Gulf/Gulf of Aden A/S choice is now a live zero-sum gate.

This is the first clean post-MI resource divergence: the immediate British loss appears chiefly as **lost flexibility and transport time**, with the blood-loss conversion deferred to the next contacts rather than invented at the gate.
