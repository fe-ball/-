# BRANCH B — Heavy Gun / Projectile Materialization 1934–1942 v001

Status: **canonical-provisional-overlay**  
Scope: Japanese heavy naval guns and AP ammunition, especially 41 cm Type 91 and 46 cm Type 1/91 family.

## 1. Revision of the old shorthand
The earlier safety rule “do not apply magic to the gun or projectile body” was directionally useful but too strong.

The correct rule is:

> **Do not invent new nominal ballistic performance without a design decision. Do allow modest improvements in the realized physical article when Branch computation, measurement, statistical acceptance and manufacturing feedback have a causal route to that improvement.**

A projectile is not only an aerodynamic drawing. It is steel chemistry, forging/casting history, heat treatment, hardness gradient, cavity geometry, cap fit, driving bands, mass/CG tolerance, fuze seating and proof acceptance. A gun is not only caliber and length; it is chamber/bore geometry, tube fit, residual stress, rifling, wear state and charge interaction. These are legitimate Branch targets.

## 2. What stays historical by default
For 1942 service use, absent an explicit redesign decision:
- 46 cm/45 Type 94 remains 45-caliber, not the rejected 50-caliber concept;
- 46 cm service AP remains ~1,460 kg class at **780 m/s nominal service MV**;
- 41 cm service AP remains the Type 91 family at historical mass/MV class;
- normal service propellant charge and pressure class remain historical;
- Type 91/Type 1 underwater-trajectory concept remains;
- long-delay base-fuze doctrine remains;
- no US Mk 8-like wholesale projectile redesign is retroactively created.

## 3. What the Branch plausibly improves by 1941–42

### 3.1 Projectile body metallurgy / heat treatment
The Japanese major-caliber Type 91 family already uses alloy steel and decremental hardening, but postwar evidence indicates that body failure at demanding face-hardened-armor obliquity remained a real limitation. Branch computation does not solve fracture mechanics from first principles; it improves experimental design, lot statistics, furnace/heat-treatment records and acceptance feedback.

Working effect:
- chemical / heat-treatment lot spread: materially tighter;
- gross body-hardness-gradient / base-plug / cavity defects: lower tail;
- catastrophic body-breakup probability in **marginal** armor-impact conditions: **~10–20% relative reduction** for mature new-production heavy AP lots;
- do **not** translate this to “penetration +10–20%.”

For penetration adjudication:
- far from a ballistic limit: effectively no change;
- near a limit where projectile body failure is decisive: small improvement in probability of intact/effective penetration;
- if a numeric sensitivity is unavoidable, use roughly **0–1% equivalent threshold change at favorable/low obliquity and 1–3% in a narrow marginal body-failure regime**, never as a global table shift.

The fundamental Type 91 cap/body geometry and underwater-hit trade remain, so high-obliquity weakness against modern face-hardened armor is not erased.

### 3.2 AP cap / cap-head / windscreen realization
- tighter concentricity, hardness acceptance and fit;
- reduced assembly outliers;
- no doctrinal cap-shape revolution by 1942.

Effect is chiefly consistency and lower failure-tail, not a new nominal penetration curve.

### 3.3 Projectile mass, CG and driving bands
- better weighing, dimensional gauging and lot recording;
- tighter bourrelet/driving-band concentricity and mass balance;
- fewer outlier rounds that contribute to salvo spread.

Intrinsic gun/ammunition dispersion contribution may improve by **~2–4%** in a mature 46 cm lot and **~1–3%** in selected 41 cm lots. This is a component term. Combined physical-system dispersion improvement, including existing firing-interval realization, is capped at roughly **3–6%** unless a later explicit test proves more. Do not stack every subterm.

### 3.4 Propellant and charge realization
Retain nominal service MV and charge weight. Improve:
- grain / bag mass acceptance;
- moisture and storage records;
- lot-to-lot ballistic calibration;
- charge-temperature handling;
- matching of service tables to actual lot behavior.

Working effect:
- lot-mean MV error: **~15–25% lower**;
- shot-to-shot MV spread attributable to charge realization: **~10–20% lower**;
- no automatic increase above 780 m/s for 46 cm AP;
- erosion / effective service-life tail may improve modestly (~5–8% planning sensitivity), but this is mainly logistics/maintenance, not a short-action damage bonus.

### 3.5 Gun tube / chamber / rifling realization
For new 46 cm guns and major relining/overhaul work:
- better stress/tolerance bookkeeping;
- improved bore/chamber/rifling acceptance;
- better barrel-number / ESR / measured-MV history.

Effect:
- lower gun-to-gun fixed bias and fewer anomalous tubes;
- no caliber, pressure or shell-energy multiplier;
- nominal rate of fire and elevation cycle remain mechanically limited.

## 4. Yamato 46 cm application, 1942-11
Use the historical **Type 1 / late Type 91 family physical design** as the projectile baseline, with Branch mature-production realization.

Combat modeling rules:
- AP projectile mass and nominal MV: historical design point;
- armor penetration tables: historical baseline;
- near-limit intact-penetration probability: modestly improved only where body-quality failure is relevant;
- salvo outliers: modestly reduced by ammunition/gun realization plus existing timing/calibration improvements;
- long fuze delay and underwater-hit doctrine remain, including their direct-hit tradeoffs;
- no claim that Yamato’s AP becomes equivalent to the best US WWII AP projectile design.

## 5. Mutsu 41 cm application, 1942-11
Mutsu’s ammunition pool can contain older and newer lots. Branch logistics/lot records allow the fleet to prefer qualified recent service lots for a planned decisive sortie, but **do not assume every round is a mature enhanced lot**.

Working treatment:
- main 41 cm nominal ballistics unchanged;
- selected/newer lots receive the same QC direction but less uniform benefit than Yamato’s purpose-built 46 cm supply chain;
- use the lower end of the materialization bands above for 41 cm unless a specific ammunition-lot audit is performed.

## 6. Fuze / explosive caveat
Type 91-family long-delay fuzing and the TNA/cushioning architecture are fundamental design/doctrine choices, not mere calculation errors.

By 1942 the Branch may have better reliability inspection and lot statistics, but this overlay does **not** create a short-delay direct-hit fuze or a redesigned low-cavity AP body. Those are valid future R&D questions and require explicit doctrinal / firing-test adjudication.

## 7. Non-stacking
- Fire-control solution quality belongs to the fire-control tech ledger.
- Projectile/gun realization belongs here.
- Do not apply “dispersion -4%” here and then add every component sub-improvement again.
- Do not turn lower body-breakup tail into a generic penetration scalar.
- Do not use quality-control improvements to erase known Type 91 geometry, fuze-delay or cavity tradeoffs.
