# BRANCH B — Yamato / Mutsu 1942-11 Reaudit v001

Status: **canonical-provisional-overlay**  
Effective scenario date: **1942-11-09**  
Purpose: close the individual-hull gaps before the New Caledonia second-night action.

## 1. Yamato

### 1.1 Hull / machinery
- Preserve the Yamato-class core concept: 46 cm main battery, 27-knot class, historical-scale armor and displacement.
- Good-condition speed remains roughly **27.6–27.8 kt trial-equivalent**, with **~27 kt sustained battle use** as the normal planning figure.
- Existing Branch low-value-weight avoidance (145–230 t, center ~190 t) continues to be reinvested in support structure, damage control, power segmentation, steering redundancy and I/O rather than treated as free displacement.

### 1.2 Radar — CLOSED
Yamato receives an **early Type 21 air/surface-warning set** during the 1942-08 pre-Truk fitting window and departs for Truk with it operational.

Reasoning:
- Branch Yamato was designed with radar room, power, cooling, cabling and antenna-base growth allowance already integrated.
- Historically Kongo received Type 21 in 1942-08 and Musashi received a modified Type 21 in 1942-09; scarce hardware therefore existed in the correct window.
- The Branch has a strong reason to prioritize the Combined Fleet flagship after Midway and Santo ISR lessons.

Limits:
- not a Type 22/SG analogue;
- no direct closed-loop main-battery solution;
- coarse air/surface warning, bearing-sector cueing and optical-search cueing;
- information path remains: radar operator → voice/electrical report → plotting → optical confirmation → mechanical fire control.

Operational effect: reduces the probability that Yamato is completely surprised by an already-firing radar-directed opponent; it does **not** erase Washington/South Dakota’s SG advantage.

### 1.3 Command — CLOSED
- Admiral Yamamoto and Combined Fleet staff remain aboard Yamato through the 1942-11-09 approach.
- Yamato is therefore a **Main Body / decisive-battle flagship**, not a disposable scouting or torpedo-vanguard unit.
- Kondo’s forward surface system forms contact geometry and reports target course/speed/damage.
- Yamamoto decides commitment/withdrawal and broad objective; local battle handling remains with the tactical surface commanders and individual captains.
- If Yamato enters close battle, Combined Fleet command risk is explicitly paid; do not ignore this in pursuit decisions.

## 2. Mutsu

### 2.1 1934–36 reconstruction — NEW BRANCH AUDIT
The historical reconstruction began in 1934-09 and ended in 1936-09, exactly as Branch naval structural computation and post-Tomozuru/4th Fleet process control matured. Therefore Mutsu cannot remain a purely historical-realization hull.

Retain the historical physical reconstruction skeleton:
- stern lengthening / bulges;
- horizontal-protection increase;
- boiler rationalization;
- turret/barbette protection and elevation changes;
- 25-knot class machinery outcome.

Branch realization effects:
- weight/KG/longitudinal-strength bookkeeping is better integrated;
- shaft/boiler alignment and acceptance adjustment are more repeatable;
- piping, drainage, firefighting and electrical isolation receive modest rationalization;
- post-refit machinery/rework tail is reduced, not abolished;
- steering/hydraulic failure risk is reduced modestly but is not removed by fiat.

Working values:
- good-condition trial-equivalent: **25.3–25.4 kt**;
- wartime sustained practical speed: **24.7–25.0 kt**;
- same-condition cruise fuel consumption: **~2–4% better than historical realization**;
- modernization-caused machinery / alignment rework tail: **~10–20% lower**, applied as reliability distribution only.

### 2.2 Radar — CLOSED
- **No radar aboard Mutsu on 1942-11-09.**
- Her 1942 deployment and yard history do not justify giving scarce early Type 21 hardware to her merely because Yamato has it.
- Mutsu relies on optical lookout, searchlights, floatplane reports and external tactical information from the formation.

### 2.3 Fire control
- Keep the historical 41 cm mechanical/optical fire-control architecture, improved by Branch calibration, survey and ammunition/charge data discipline.
- No Yamato-style permanently integrated electronic ballistic aid is assumed aboard Mutsu in 1942.
- Mutsu therefore benefits from better input quality and tables, not a new fire-control architecture.

## 3. Formation speed and employment
When Yamato and Mutsu remain in close mutual support, use **~25 kt sustained tactical ceiling** as the working limit.

- Yamato alone: ~27 kt sustained battle use.
- Mutsu: ~24.7–25.0 kt sustained.
- A 27-knot Yamato advance is possible only by stretching or abandoning close Mutsu support; this is an explicit tactical trade, never a free formation speed.

For the New Caledonia second night:
- first-night Kongo-class attrition and confirmed US three-fast-battleship concentration open the commitment gate;
- Yamato/Mutsu approach as the heavy Main Body after air/ISR updates;
- they do not automatically charge into the first SG contact line;
- Yamato’s early Type 21 may cue optical attention but Washington/South Dakota retain the decisive radar-quality advantage at night.

## 4. Later-history guard
Mutsu’s 1943-06-08 magazine explosion remains center-line canon unless separately reopened. Better prewar modernization realization does not prove removal of that historically uncertain accident mechanism.
