# Branch B — Wake Raid Settlement, 5–6 Oct 1943 v001

Status: CURRENT WORKING SETTLEMENT
Clock: 1943-10-05 to 1943-10-08

## Starting conditions
U.S.:
- Essex, Yorktown CV-10, Lexington CV-16, Independence, Belleau Wood, Cowpens.
- ~385–405 aircraft physical; ~350–375 first-day mission-ready.
- aggregate system readiness ~90–94% of a completely fresh six-deck force.
- no amphibious convoy.

Wake:
- RED before attack; strategic surprise absent.
- Zusei late Zero 16–20, Raiden 6–8, Kyofu 10–12, recon/observation seaplanes 8–10.
- land fighters ~18–23 immediately ready; Kyofu ~8–10 ready.
- D2.2–2.5 ground/base maturity, W2.6–2.9 water-air resilience.
- I-30-derived EW/calibration layer is active at selected fixed warning/C2 functions.
- useful battle-preparation warning can begin ~50–90 minutes before the main first strike in the center when remote cue/handoff succeeds; exact carrier position remains unknown.

## 5 Oct
First U.S. strike:
- heavy F6F sweep/escort; roughly 140–155 aircraft class in the first main cycle.
- Wake can feed roughly 27–32 fighters into the initial/intermediate interception window rather than being caught on the ground.
- Zusei binds F6F; Raiden attacks SBD/TBF; Kyofu stays low/mid and protects anchorage/attack-aircraft approaches.
- U.S. first-cycle irreversible losses ~9–12; Japanese ~11–15.
- runway, radar/power and maintenance are hit, but dispersal prevents a mass ground-aircraft kill.

Late-morning / afternoon:
- U.S. second/third cycles increasingly target repair tractors/material, communications, AA/fire-control, water-air service points, fuel/ammunition and support craft.
- U.S. day-one irreversible losses total ~18–23.
- Japanese day-one aircraft irreversible ~18–23.
- Wake land-air sortie generation falls to ~15–25% by late day; water-air remains ~40–55%.
- no major U.S. hull hit.
- Japanese daylight long-range bomber counterattack is withheld because post-Gilbert doctrine judges stale-contact attack against six-deck F6F/CIC cover to be poor exchange.
- H8K/submarines maintain search/withdrawal cueing; no centered torpedo hit.

Night 5/6 Oct:
- runway crater repair, power/communications switching, fuel/support-craft relocation.
- a narrow fighter-use strip is partially restored, but the repair activity itself becomes a day-two U.S. target.
- limited H8K/floatplane search persists; no effective night strike on carriers.

## 6 Oct
- U.S. repeats suppression with improved target knowledge rather than seeking a fleet battle.
- Wake can still launch a smaller mixed defense: roughly 12–16 land fighters and 4–6 Kyofu across the main morning windows.
- U.S. day-two irreversible losses ~11–14.
- Japanese day-two aircraft irreversible ~14–18, including ground/water destruction and remaining fighter losses.
- main runway, repair plant, power/communications and water-air support are more heavily damaged than on day one.

## Centered total losses
United States:
- aircraft irreversible: ~30–36 center band;
- repairable damaged: ~35–50;
- major hull damage: none;
- irreversible aircrew/crew loss: roughly ~22–30 personnel center, with additional wounded/temporarily unavailable.

Japan:
- aircraft irreversible: ~34–41;
- repairable: ~12–20;
- irreversible pilot/aircrew loss: roughly ~18–25 personnel center;
- Wake ground/base casualties: roughly ~250–400;
- several small support craft / service points destroyed or damaged.

Aircraft loss favors the U.S.; irreversible human loss is much closer because U.S. SBD/TBF losses carry multiple crew while Japan fights close to rescue/recovery facilities and suffers some unmanned ground losses.

## Wake base condition after 6 Oct
End of 6 Oct:
- land-air immediate sortie generation: ~5–15%;
- water-air/search function: ~20–35%;
- backup communications/limited warning remain; main radar/power coverage badly degraded.

Recovery:
- 12–24h: limited water-air/recon sorties resume or continue;
- 48–72h: limited land-fighter operations possible from repaired runway segments;
- ~4–6 days: coherent local fighter operation can resume if replacement aircraft arrive;
- ~7–10 days: most base-system functions can be restored, but original fighter strength is not recovered automatically; aircraft/crew replacement is a separate logistics gate.

Wake is heavily suppressed, not permanently closed.

## Strategic result
U.S.:
- clear tactical victory;
- six-deck system can overwhelm a mature outer base;
- cost is far above a historical low-cost training raid;
- repeated two-day sterilization raids are unattractive unless tied to an invasion/operational purpose.
- next major use of carrier mass should buy territory or a decisive operational condition rather than merely another short suppression window.

Japan:
- outer-node doctrine works at the level intended: no fleet rescue, no island loss, measurable U.S. aircraft/crew/tempo tax.
- six U.S. decks reveal that a single outer node cannot stop the 1943 carrier system even with good warning, Raiden and Kyofu.
- no panic reinforcement of Wake; prioritize cheap resilience (repair, dispersion, water-air, radar/communications, mines/sub support) while Marianas/Palau/Truk remain the expensive inner-defense priority.
- carrier/land-bomber counterattack gates remain strict unless U.S. forces are fixed by an amphibious convoy or a fresh, high-confidence track.
