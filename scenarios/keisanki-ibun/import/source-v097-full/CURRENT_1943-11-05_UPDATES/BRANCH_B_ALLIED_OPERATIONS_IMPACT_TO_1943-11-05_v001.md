## 5. Pacific — largest qualitative Allied operational change

### Historical southern thrust removed
The historical Guadalcanal -> New Georgia -> Bougainville / New Guinea converging offensive chain does not occur in this branch.
Consequences are two-sided:
- U.S. avoids many historical Solomons surface/air/ground losses;
- Japan also avoids those losses and retains stronger fleet/air cadres;
- U.S. does not gain the historical forward geometry around Rabaul;
- Japanese control of Port Moresby, Santo and New Caledonia pushes Allied South-Pacific depth back toward Fiji/Samoa/NZ/Australia.

This is not simple extra attrition.
It is a different strategic geometry.

### U.S. late-1943 fast-carrier window spent on suppression/testing

Branch raids:
Midway:
- U.S. 15–20 aircraft irreversible;
- 20–30 repairable;
- three-deck group reset ~2–3 weeks.

Gilberts:
- U.S. 11–16 irreversible;
- 16–25 repairable.

Wake:
- U.S. 30–36 irreversible;
- 35–50 repairable;
- six-deck operation; no hull damage.

Total Aug–Oct:
- U.S. irreversible aircraft: **56–72**
- repairable damaged: **71–105**

These are sustainable, but they consume:
- aircraft-group replacement;
- deck training/reset;
- oiler/escort cycles;
- CAP/ASW sorties;
- operational calendar.

Main U.S. lesson after Wake:
**next mass-carrier use must buy territory or a decisive operational condition.**

### Compensation
Because there is no Bougainville/Rabaul support operation, U.S. carriers that historically would be used in South-Pacific support can be concentrated in Central Pacific.
Thus the Branch does NOT simply subtract carrier decks from the U.S. pool.
It changes where they must be used.

### Central-Pacific amphibious pull
Historical GALVANIC itself required an enormous package: ~200 ships, >35k troops, 117k tons cargo, 6,000 vehicles and a large fleet/merchant oiler train.
Branch Japanese outer bases are better warned, more dispersed and more resilient.

Implication:
- the U.S. still can assemble a Galvanic-class force;
- but suppression/ASW/fighter/oiler/ammunition margins should be at least historical scale and probably somewhat larger;
- Midway invasion is downgraded; Gilberts remains the leading first large amphibious axis;
- as of 5 Nov, exact D-Day remains open.

## 6. Pacific absorption into global Allied pool

What is genuinely globally fungible:
- merchant shipping;
- fleet/merchant oilers;
- escorts;
- long-range patrol aircraft;
- construction units/equipment;
- amphibious shipping;
- aviation fuel / replacement engines / transport aircraft;
- some cruisers/destroyers.

What is less fungible:
- U.S. fleet carriers are strategically Pacific-biased by this stage;
- Marine amphibious formations are largely Pacific-oriented;
- many Pacific base units are theater-specific once deployed.

Therefore:
**Pacific attraction hurts global logistics and escort flexibility more than it directly steals army divisions from Europe.**

This connects Indian Ocean pressure to Pacific timing without requiring a direct ship transfer.

## 10. Overall judgment

By 5 Nov 1943 the Branch has not forced the Western Allies to cancel TORCH, HUSKY or AVALANCHE.
That would overstate the Japanese/Axis effect.

The real change is:
1. Europe/Mediterranean operations are executed with thinner reserves.
2. Indian Ocean never releases its escort/air/base burden.
3. CBI becomes a logistics system taxed from both ends.
4. Pacific loses the historical South-Pacific territorial progression.
5. New U.S. carrier power is first consumed in costly suppression/testing rather than immediate territorial conversion.
6. U.S. industrial power prevents strategic paralysis, but the operational opportunity cost is real.
7. The first major nonlinear threshold lies ahead: when the U.S. must combine a Galvanic-scale amphibious train with enough fast-carrier power to suppress a Japanese outer/inner system that is still improving.

Thus the Branch effect to date is better described as:
**few major Allied operations canceled; many margins consumed; one major Pacific strategy qualitatively rerouted; future operations made more concentrated and expensive.**
