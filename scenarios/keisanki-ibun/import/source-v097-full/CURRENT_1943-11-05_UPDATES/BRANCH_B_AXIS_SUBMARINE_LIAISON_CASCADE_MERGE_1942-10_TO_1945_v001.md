## 5. Allied burden cascade

The operational dividend is not only extra sunk GRT.

German pressure west of India + Japanese pressure east/north of India creates a longer continuous uncertainty belt:
Cape / Mozambique → East Africa / Arabian Sea → Ceylon / Bay of Bengal → Malacca.

The key effect is preventing the Allies from confidently declaring a sector/season clear and releasing assets.

Working additional burden attributable specifically to the *new Penang institutional layer* (not total Branch Indian-Ocean pressure):
- 1943 H1: small; preparation and first transit dominate;
- 1943 H2 center: 2-4 escort/ASW hulls that cannot be safely released elsewhere, plus continuing long-range patrol-air detachments;
- upper sensitivity: 4-6 hulls if a second German boat remains east and Japanese patrol density is simultaneously sustained;
- do not restore the old 5-8 hull figure as a Penang-only effect; that figure belongs to total Indian-Ocean pressure in a stronger branch.

Merchant effect should be booked as:
1. actual sinkings/damage;
2. convoy wait/route deviation;
3. port/escort handoff delays;
4. merchant ship-days lost;
5. only then downstream battle-window shortages.

No fixed extra GRT is prebooked. As a broad sensitivity only, one additional effective long-range patrol boat over part of 1943H2 can plausibly create a fat-tailed 0-to-tens-of-thousands-GRT difference; concrete sinkings must be event adjudications.

## 6. Europe / Soviet cascade

Penang/I-30 effects must not be credited as the main cause of the 1942 Stalingrad or El Alamein outcomes.

Near-term to Five-Go (Mar 1943):
- I-30 technology is mostly in bench/trial stage;
- German Far-East combat-basing effect is still small;
- the main European Branch differences remain RN carrier losses, Indian-Ocean escort/air burden, and small battle-window logistics delays;
- therefore the earlier working Soviet/Axis casualty deltas to Mar 1943 should not be materially increased *because of Penang*.

From 1943H2 onward:
- extra Indian-Ocean patrol-days can keep escort/air assets east longer;
- missed/delayed cargoes can marginally thin Persian/Middle-East/British reserve margins;
- surviving Axis forces from earlier small deltas can fight again;
- technical cards occasionally create discrete extra Allied losses when a real battle crosses a reliability/detection/survival threshold.

This is recursive but not a generic multiplier.

## 8. Highest-value interpretation

Old audit's largest realized I-30 dividend by early 1944 remains:
1. precision radar tracking/calibration references;
2. metric ESM/RWR;
3. acoustic decoy and submarine survival practice;
4. acoustic/manufacturing comparison;
5. secure bilateral naval liaison.

The new post-FS addition changes the strategic ordering by adding:
6. a persistent Penang service institution that converts liaison into additional patrol-days and a wider Allied ASW burden.

Thus I-30's survival has two different nonlinear cascades:
- **technology-information cascade**: successful first pass makes every later exchange more specialized and valuable;
- **operations-institution cascade**: successful first pass + early FS termination makes a permanent service node politically/operationally worthwhile, which keeps Axis submarine threat distributed over a longer Indian-Ocean arc.

Neither cascade is a national combat multiplier. Both produce threshold effects in individual later events.
