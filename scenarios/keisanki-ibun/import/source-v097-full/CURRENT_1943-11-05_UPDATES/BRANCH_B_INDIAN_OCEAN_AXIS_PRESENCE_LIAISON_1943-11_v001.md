## 7. CBI coupling

Indian Ocean pressure and Hanzhong/Guangyuan pressure must be kept separate.

Sea-side chain:
Indian Ocean shipping
→ Calcutta/Chittagong discharge
→ Bengal/Assam LOC
→ ATC fuel/spares/aircraft
→ Hump gross realized lift

China-side chain:
Hump-delivered cargo
→ Sichuan allocation
→ Hanzhong/Guangyuan air/transport pressure
→ discretionary cargo consumed in defense

Therefore the two effects can multiply operationally,
but must not be double-counted as the same lost tonnage.

Key strategic consequence:
Japan is attacking the China-support system at **both ends**:
- sea/India-side: submarine/recon/base pressure
- China-side: Hanzhong/Guangyuan interdiction/allocation tax

## 10. Current ruling

Yes: **潜水艦戦的影響はもう出ている。**

But:
the major 1943 difference is not “Branch U-boats sink 100,000 extra GRT.”

It is:
**the Indian Ocean does not become a rear-area sea.**

Japan/Germany maintain enough persistent submarine/recon/base pressure that Britain cannot confidently cash the theater down for use elsewhere.

That persistent uncertainty:
- holds escorts
- holds patrol aircraft
- forces convoy/route waiting
- taxes CBI shipping
- keeps Eastern Fleet planning conservative
- supports technical exchange
- creates a live operational laboratory for the next Yanagi cycle

The direct-tech snowball becomes more visible in 1944.
The operational-presence dividend is already active in late 1943.
