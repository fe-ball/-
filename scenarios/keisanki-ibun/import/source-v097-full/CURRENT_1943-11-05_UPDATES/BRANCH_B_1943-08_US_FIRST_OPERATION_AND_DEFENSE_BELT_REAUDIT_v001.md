# Branch B — 1943-08 Pacific first U.S. operation and Japanese defense-belt re-audit v001

Status: WORKING STRATEGIC AUDIT / no U.S. raid result prebooked
Clock: 1943-08-06

## 1. Previous-branch U.S. offensive sequence
Latest mature older line used:
- first major U.S. offensive action: 24–26 Jun 1943, limited Wasp + Victorious carrier strike against Japanese-held Espiritu Santo; no landing;
- center result in that old line: U.S. aircraft 27–36 irreversible, Japanese 27–35; Santo base output temporarily falls to about 55–65 percent, then recovers; an IJN submarine damages a fleet oiler and shortens U.S. exposure;
- first major amphibious recapture in the older settled line: September 1943 Midway operation, though later re-audit work reopened whether Midway should instead be suppressed/bypassed before GALVANIC;
- the key old-branch logic was: raid first, then use the rebuilt carrier/amphibious system for a fixed objective.

## 2. Why the current branch should differ
Current-branch deltas:
- no Japanese June 1943 Efate neutralization operation has been fired, so the old immediate U.S. reactive Santo-strike trigger is absent;
- British Indomitable/Formidable losses remain, while Wasp survives, making a Victorious Pacific loan less necessary to the U.S. and more expensive to the RN;
- Santo and New Caledonia fell earlier than in the older branch and have had more time to mature as Japanese bases;
- Japanese first-line carrier and surface-force continuity is healthier because the old expensive late-1942 NC carrier/night-battle chain did not occur;
- therefore a June two-carrier penetration into the Santo/NC base-air system is less attractive than in the older branch.

## 3. Current U.S. first-operation center
Historical industrial/training clocks remain useful:
- Essex-class Essex is in the Pacific by late spring 1943;
- Yorktown CV-10 reaches Pearl Harbor on 24 Jul and trains through August;
- Independence reaches the Pacific/Hawaii system in July;
- Lexington CV-16 reaches Pearl Harbor on 9 Aug, too late for the earliest already-forming raid group.

Current center:
- first major U.S. offensive operation = late-August / 1-Sep-1943 fast-carrier suppression raid on Japanese Midway;
- core group: Essex + Yorktown CV-10 + Independence, with Wasp retained as South-Pacific/fleet reserve rather than automatically concentrated;
- purpose: suppress Midway search/radar/runway output, collect photography, give new F6F/carrier groups combat experience, and test Japanese reaction;
- this is not an invasion;
- direct Midway assault is less attractive because it fixes U.S. amphibious shipping within a Japanese four-carrier/submarine/search response geometry.

Alternative sensitivity:
- Wake raid first: secondary;
- Santo/NC carrier strike first: lower probability than older branch;
- amphibious assault before October: low.

## 4. First amphibious-operation implication
Center after a successful raid:
- Midway can be suppressed but need not be seized immediately;
- first large amphibious offensive remains more likely in the Gilberts / Central Pacific approach later in 1943 than an early Midway recapture;
- however raid results and Japanese carrier reaction remain gates; no GALVANIC date/result is prebooked here.

## 5. Japanese defense-belt timeline in current branch
### 1942 H2: forward-base consolidation, not yet an “absolute defense zone”
Japan is already paying permanent costs for Midway, Port Moresby, Lunga/Tulagi, Santo and New Caledonia.
This is a forward ISR / air / submarine / denial network, not a promise to send the fleet to save every island.

### 1943 Jan–Mar: inner-belt study
Army/Navy staff survey and material planning should begin for:
- Bonins;
- Marianas;
- Truk / western Carolines;
- Palau / Yap;
- western New Guinea / Banda approaches;
- Sunda / Burma connection.
Five-Go divisions remain protected, so emphasis is engineers, AA, communications, airfield service, fuel/ammunition dispersion, radar/observation, water, roads and depots.

### 1943 Apr–Jul: pre-absolute-defense build
Working current-branch policy:
- independent defense/base/support units and construction material move into the Central Pacific inner belt;
- full China Five-Go divisions are not pulled out during Phase I/II;
- outer nodes (Midway/Wake/Marshalls/Santo/NC/PM) continue to receive enough stock and base-service capacity to function as warning/toll/delay nodes;
- inner nodes receive a rising share of concrete/field-fortification, runway repair, AA, communications and dispersed fuel/ammunition resources.

### 1943 Aug: de facto two-tier doctrine
After Hanzhong and with U.S. carrier concentration visibly maturing:
- outer belt = warning, attrition, reconnaissance, submarine support, delay;
- inner belt = decisive national-defense line to be reinforced even if some outer islands are bypassed;
- First-line carriers remain mobile and are not automatically sacrificed for fixed-garrison rescue.

A formal Army–Navy/state decision can occur after/around the first fast-carrier raid, plausibly weeks earlier than the historical 30-Sep-1943 political formalization.

## 6. Working resource envelope as of 6 Aug 1943
Already-paid forward-base fixed bill:
- roughly 75–95 kt/month delivered cargo across the major forward-base network;
- roughly 25–35 cargo-ship equivalents plus tanker/escort capacity tied to steady support.

Additional inner-belt buildup before formalization should be materially smaller than this outer-base bill:
- spring/early-summer: construction/stockpile emphasis rather than full divisions;
- working increment by July–August: roughly 15–25 kt/month across Marianas/Palau/Truk/Bonins/western-Caroline priority nodes;
- personnel emphasis: engineers, AA, signals, base/airfield service and defense battalion/brigade-sized units rather than mass divisional transfer.

After Hanzhong / first U.S. carrier raid:
- ramp inner-belt shipping toward roughly 25–40 kt/month if shipping and China redistribution gates permit;
- begin named division/brigade releases only after the national-defense belt is formally prioritized.

These are working envelopes, not a closed named OOB.

## 7. Key difference from historical Japanese failure
Historical Japan did not simply “give up in confusion.” It began Central-Pacific ground-defense reinforcement before the formal absolute-defense-zone decision, later shifted substantial battalion/division strength south/east, and tried to create a decisive inner line.
Its major failures were:
- strategic lateness relative to U.S. carrier/amphibious growth;
- Army–Navy disagreement over whether to continue heavily reinforcing outer Marshalls/eastern Carolines;
- shipping/submarine losses during redeployment;
- insufficient engineering time and materials at the decisive Marianas/Palau belt;
- a tendency to spend troops outside the intended inner line.

Current Branch B can improve the timing/priority discipline, but it cannot fortify every island at once.
