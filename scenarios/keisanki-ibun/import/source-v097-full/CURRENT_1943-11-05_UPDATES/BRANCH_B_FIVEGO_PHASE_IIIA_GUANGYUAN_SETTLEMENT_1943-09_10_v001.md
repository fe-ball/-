# Branch B — Five-Go Phase III-A Guangyuan Settlement, Sep–Oct 1943 v001

Status: CURRENT WORKING SETTLEMENT
Clock: 1943-08-21 to 1943-11-03

## 1. Authorization and scope
- Phase III-A Guangyuan is authorized around 21 Aug 1943, before the Midway carrier raid.
- Midway/Gilbert/Wake raids do not cancel it because they are raids without amphibious fixation or Japanese carrier losses.
- Scope is deliberately capped:
  - 2–3 division-scale forward operation;
  - no deep Chengdu/Chongqing pursuit;
  - no large new claim on Pacific-priority heavy AA, engineers, signals, aviation support or trucking.
- Main operational line uses 36th and 41st Division lineages forward, with 17th Division lineage as follow-on / route-security / reserve; 27th and 37th remain more rear/reconstitution biased.

## 2. Logistic condition
- Hanzhong remains the main repair/stock/air-control node.
- Hanzhong -> Ningqiang forward flow in early Sep: roughly 350–450 t/day center under workable weather.
- beyond Ningqiang, demolition and gorge geometry temporarily reduce useful forward flow toward roughly 220–320 t/day until bridges/road sections are reopened.
- better Branch engineering/stock control reduces complete stoppages but does not remove the mountain bottleneck.
- armor is heavily thinned; only a small tracked/direct-fire/recon package moves deep, while most medium tanks remain Hanzhong/Guanzhong-side.

## 3. Chinese defense
- nominal 11–15 division-class formations are gathered across north Sichuan;
- effective combat strength roughly 55–75k, with uneven heavy weapons, transport and communications.
- Chinese method:
  - delaying positions rather than annihilation defense;
  - bridge and road demolition;
  - mines / ambushes / night attacks;
  - stronger stand at gorge and river approaches north of Guangyuan;
  - preserve the Chengdu defense army rather than create a new encirclement disaster.
- the old-branch Oct north-Sichuan counteroffensive does not occur as a separate operation; those troops are instead consumed in the defensive sequence.

## 4. Operational timeline
21–31 Aug:
- final GO, stock-forwarding, engineer reconnaissance, pack/road conversion, forward air/recon preparation.

1 Sep:
- Phase III-A offensive begins.

10–14 Sep:
- Ningqiang main area secured after delaying fighting.
- road/bridge repair pause follows; Japanese speed drops sharply.

15–30 Sep:
- successive Chinese demolitions and ridge positions slow the advance.
- Japanese artillery/air superiority remains useful but is increasingly limited by road tonnage and weather.
- no large armor breakthrough.

5–6 Oct:
- Wake raid occurs while the Guangyuan operation is already deep in motion.
- Tokyo does not halt Phase III-A; the Pacific result only reinforces the cap on additional China claims for scarce support categories.

8–15 Oct:
- Japanese force reaches/presses the Chaotian / upper Jialing gorge approaches.
- this is the hardest tactical-logistic portion of the operation.
- Chinese forces inflict meaningful local losses but cannot hold indefinitely against concentrated engineer/artillery/air support.

16–24 Oct:
- Chaotian gate breaks in stages; Chinese force withdraws toward Guangyuan outer positions to avoid encirclement.
- Japanese supply line remains stretched and prevents a wide pursuit.

29–31 Oct center:
- Guangyuan main area and usable airfield/logistics sites are secured.
- organized Chinese defense withdraws toward Jiange/Jianmen–Mianyang approaches.

3 Nov:
- Five-Go is formally closed as an offensive package.
- no automatic pursuit on Chengdu.

## 5. Losses — Phase III-A working center
Japan:
- casualties ~7.5–9.5k;
- irrecoverable tracked AFV only low-single to low-double digits; road/mechanical temporary losses much larger;
- aircraft losses moderate and not decisive; forward sortie availability is primarily fuel/weather/maintenance constrained.

China:
- combat losses ~21–28k;
- additional heavy-equipment, truck, radio, ammunition and engineer-tool abandonment materially worsens reconstitution;
- the majority of the north-Sichuan manpower pool survives and retreats.

Combined Five-Go I–III current working total:
- Japan ~33–41k casualties;
- China ~107–137k combat losses;
- not a Chinese field-army annihilation.

## 6. Post-capture posture
Immediate:
- Hanzhong remains A-/main western support node.
- Guangyuan begins as a rough forward field / recon / liaison / light-strike site, not instantly a mature 40-aircraft base.
- first useful reconnaissance/liaison operation returns within days; fighter staging follows after runway/stock/repair work.

Winter target posture:
- western salient active commitment grows toward roughly 2.3–2.8 division-equivalents / 45–58k all-arms across Hanzhong-route-Guangyuan, not all concentrated in Guangyuan.
- Hanzhong = heavy repair, stock, plotting and reserve.
- Guangyuan = forward recon, light-bomber/direct-cooperation, staged fighter sweep / nuisance attack.
- no deep ground move toward Chengdu.

Strategic effect:
- Chengdu becomes front-adjacent rather than a safe rear zone.
- Chinese command must keep roughly 5–7 effective division-equivalents in north-Sichuan defense/route security over time.
- Fourteenth Air Force / Chinese aviation must spend fuel, fighters, warning and repair effort on Sichuan defense.
- the salient becomes an active pressure system supporting the later No.1/Ichigo-equivalent strategy, not a launchpad for immediate Sichuan conquest.

## 7. Comparison with older branch
Older line:
- Phase III-A launch late Sep / early Oct;
- Guangyuan late Oct / early Nov;
- some older versions stopped Five-Go at Hanzhong and produced an Oct Chinese northward counteroffensive.

Current line:
- authorization and launch happen ~3–4 weeks earlier;
- actual Guangyuan capture improves only by days to roughly one week because terrain/road capacity dominate;
- the earlier surplus is partly converted into Pacific inner-defense investment rather than a larger/deeper Sichuan offensive.
