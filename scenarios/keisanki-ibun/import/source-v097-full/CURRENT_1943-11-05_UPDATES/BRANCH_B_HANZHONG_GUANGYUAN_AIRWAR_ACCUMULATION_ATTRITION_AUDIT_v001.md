# Branch B — Hanzhong–Guangyuan Continuous Air War: Accumulation / Attrition Audit
## Working current synthesis, Nov 1943–Spring 1944

Status: WORKING SYNTHESIS
Purpose: separate direct aircraft loss from resource accumulation, pilot/crew depletion, Hump/transport tax, and the effect on No.1/Ichigo-equivalent preparation.

## 1. Core ruling
The western air war is not a free Japanese victory.
Direct aircraft and aviation-personnel exchange is often Allied-favorable.

Its Japanese strategic value comes from geography and replacement-chain asymmetry:
- Japan can sustain the Xi'an–Baoji–Hanzhong–Guangyuan air system from its continental rail/road network.
- U.S./Chinese aircraft in Sichuan require India assembly/ferry/Hump/internal-China fuel, spares, repair and airfield effort.
- Therefore each surviving Allied fighter can still impose a large logistics/allocation cost even when it is not shot down.

## 2. Japanese loss interpretation
Current Branch national Japanese aviation loss is still materially below the historical collapse path because:
- no historical Guadalcanal sequential aircrew consumption;
- no automatic historical New Guinea / Rabaul emergency air-transfer and loss chain;
- fewer carrier cadres destroyed;
- better repair/recovery and ferry/acceptance organization;
- more usable trained-personnel pools.

However:
- China-theater IJA air losses can be higher than historical China alone because the Branch deliberately operates a much more active western air front.
- Aug–Oct 1943 Pacific outer-base raids impose a separate IJN airframe bill; do not confuse that with IJA western losses.

Steady mature western-pressure sensitivity:
- Japan irreversible aircraft: about 10–15/month.
- U.S./China irreversible aircraft: about 6–10/month.
- additional Allied long-repair/unavailable: about 8–15/month.

When counter-air / Matterhorn / No.1 preparation intensifies in Apr–Jun 1944, the old mature working settlement rose to:
- Japan 42–50 irreversible aircraft by 18 Jun;
- Japan 65–85 permanent aviation-personnel losses;
- U.S./China 27–34 irreversible aircraft;
- U.S./China 15–25 permanent aviation-personnel losses;
- U.S./China 35–55 additional long-repair / temporary-unavailable aircraft.

Thus the direct exchange is not the reason Japan continues the campaign.

## 3. Japanese accumulation effect
By Apr 1944 old mature western system:
- physical 90–115 aircraft;
- normally ready 68–86;
- sustainable output 35–50 sorties/day;
- one-day surge 60–80.

Keeping it credible requires roughly:
- 60–80 mission-ready aircraft-equivalents left in the west during No.1 preparation;
- western aviation consumption about 900–1,300 t/month;
- about 400–600 t/month of that moving through the Hanzhong road system.

Effect on Japanese buildup:
- replacement production first fills western attrition before becoming a free eastern reserve;
- pilots/maintainers spend flight hours and fuel in the west instead of being fully rested for Hunan;
- the western system can loan roughly 20–30 fighters + 8–12 strike/recon aircraft for 48–72 h, but cannot be stripped for a long Hunan campaign.

Nevertheless the system does not prevent a major eastern accumulation:
- total China Expeditionary Army air package by late Apr: physical 275–325 / ready 205–245;
- central/eastern/Hunan preparation: physical 175–215 / ready 130–165;
- short Hunan surge after western loan: roughly 155–190 ready aircraft class.

Therefore the western air war changes the ceiling from “everything can mass east” to “a large eastern air force can mass, but a persistent western reserve remains fixed.”

## 4. Allied accumulation effect
Mature Hanzhong/Guangyuan pressure fixes:
- 70–95 fighters physically in Sichuan/western defense/rotation;
- 50–70 normally mission-ready;
- another 35–50 replacement/allocation aircraft in the India→China pipeline or delayed from eastward movement;
- 10–16 bomber/recon/support aircraft-equivalents on western tasks.

Hunan diversion:
- 1 day: +25–35 fighters can move east at acceptable risk;
- 3–5 days: +15–25 can be sustained only by reducing western CAP/maintenance reserve;
- >5 days: continued maximum eastward concentration materially exposes Chengdu fields, fuel dumps and support construction.

Hence:
- instantaneous Allied response remains strong;
- sustained multi-front response is weaker.

## 5. Hump / transport tax
Airfield/defense pressure:
- 450–700 t/month high-priority Hump-equivalent burden.

Transport interdiction adds net:
- 180–320 t/month.

Combined mature burden:
- about 650–1,000 t/month;
- center roughly 820 t/month;
- around 6–9% of early-1944 gross Hump lift, but a much larger fraction of discretionary aviation/maintenance cargo.

It competes with:
- eastern Fourteenth Air Force fuel/ammunition;
- Y-Force / Burma;
- Matterhorn;
- replacement engines, tires, tools and radios.

Matterhorn combat-stockpile growth:
- about 12–18% below no-pressure potential in the mature transport-interdiction audit.

## 6. Chinese internal transport accumulation
Per active month, affected north-Sichuan/Chengdu corridors:
- trucks irrecoverable: 12–25;
- trucks long-repair: 25–45;
- crossing craft/equipment destroyed or long-repair: 3–8;
- meaningful >24 h bridge/crossing disruption: 2–5 events;
- direct high-value cargo/POL loss: 50–110 t.

The larger effect is cycle time:
- monthly average effective-capacity loss: 4–8%;
- vehicle-days / driver-hours / local fuel needed to preserve the same delivered output: +10–18%.

Thus China can accumulate nominal cargo while still losing operational availability because more of its transport pool is spent moving the same tonnage.

## 7. Historical-loss comparison ruling
The statement “Japanese aircraft losses are thinner than history” needs theater separation.

- IJN/IJA national system: YES, materially healthier than historical 1943–44 because the historical Solomons/New Guinea sequential destruction chain is absent or reduced.
- China-theater IJA: NOT necessarily lower than historical China; Branch operations are more active and therefore generate extra combat loss.
- Pilot/cadre availability: much healthier than history, especially because large veteran nuclei were not consumed in the historical emergency chain.
- By spring 1944, continued western combat is sustainable but no longer free; the Apr–Jun escalation begins to consume personnel at a rate that would become dangerous if repeated across several fronts simultaneously.

## 8. Strategic interpretation
Japan converts part of its aircraft production and fuel accumulation into a permanent western pressure asset.
The Allies convert a larger fraction of scarce China-bound high-priority lift into maintaining the status quo.

Therefore:
- long-run industrial exchange still favors the U.S.;
- immediate CBI marginal-resource exchange can be near-even or Japan-favorable;
- operational clock / allocation exchange is materially Japan-favorable;
- No.1-equivalent remains feasible, but its air plan must account for a credible western front rather than pretending all Fifth Air Army resources are free.
