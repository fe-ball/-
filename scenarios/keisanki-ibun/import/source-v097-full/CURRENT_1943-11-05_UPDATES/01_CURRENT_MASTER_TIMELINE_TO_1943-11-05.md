## 1943 Pacific U.S. fast-carrier return

### Midway raid — 1943-08-29/30
U.S.: Essex + Yorktown CV-10 + Independence.
Japan: RED-alert mature Midway; Zusei late Zero + Raiden + Kyofu / water-air / recon / submarine support.
Result:
- U.S. aircraft irreversible 15–20.
- Japan 27–34.
- irreversible crew/pilot loss much closer: U.S. ~14–20 personnel; Japan ~10–15.
- no major U.S. hull damage.
- Midway suppressed, not closed; limited flying 24–36 h, coherent fighter operation ~48 h, broad search mostly 3–4 d.

### Gilbert raid — 1943-09-18/19
U.S.: Lexington CV-16 + Princeton CVL-23 + Belleau Wood CVL-24.
Result:
- U.S. aircraft irreversible 11–16.
- Japan 23–31.
- no major U.S. hull damage.
- Tarawa suppressed but distributed Gilbert–Marshall ISR/air system survives.

### Wake raid — 1943-10-05/06
U.S.: Essex, Yorktown, Lexington, Independence, Belleau Wood, Cowpens.
Result:
- U.S. aircraft irreversible 30–36; repairable 35–50.
- Japan aircraft irreversible 34–41; repairable 12–20.
- no major U.S. hull damage.
- Wake heavily suppressed but not permanently closed; water-air/recon returns first, coherent fighter operation in days with replacement gate.

U.S. strategic lesson by Oct:
- mass carriers can suppress mature Japanese outer nodes;
- repeated sterilization raids are poor use of the carrier window unless tied to territorial/operational gain;
- next major carrier concentration should support a real amphibious objective.

Japan strategic lesson:
- do not rescue every outer island with Combined Fleet;
- outer nodes impose aircraft/crew/tempo/ASW tax;
- expensive construction depth goes to Marianas/Palau/Truk/Bonins.
