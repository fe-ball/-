## 2. Authoritative 1943-summer Japanese fighter technology

### Zusei late Zero (A6M5-equivalent independent Branch standard)
Status: mature quantitative main fighter by summer 1943.

- Engine: Zusei 21-class, about 1,080 hp.
- Normal loaded weight: 2,760–2,850 kg; center about 2,800 kg.
- Standard maximum speed: 560–568 km/h.
- Good aircraft: 569–574 km/h.
- Unit median: 555–565 km/h.
- 6,000 m climb: standard 7m15s–7m35s; unit median 7m25s–7m50s.
- Practical combat radius: 700–800 km; safe long escort 650–750 km.
- Late-war-style improvements already integrated: clipped/rounded tip refinement, improved cowling, thrust exhaust, wing/tail stiffness, aileron/high-speed dive envelope, Type-2 20 mm cannon / 13.2 mm-class gun system, improved radio, limited armor, fuel isolation/check valves, fire suppression and local tank leak protection.
- Role: carrier fighter, long-range CAP/escort, general fighter. It is not an A6M2 being asked to fight 1943 F6F.

### J2M Raiden
Status by Aug 1943: established land-base interceptor, not experimental.

Development clock:
- advanced ground vibration/cooling/shaft testing 1940–41;
- prototype late 1941 / early 1942;
- unit trials summer–autumn 1942;
- advance production late 1942;
- first combat-capable units late 1942–early 1943;
- four-20-mm standard version in first half/mid 1943.

Performance center:
- engine: Kasei 20-class, 1,800–1,850 hp;
- normal loaded weight: 3,250–3,400 kg;
- maximum speed: 590–600 km/h;
- climb to 6,000 m: 5m20s–5m45s;
- service ceiling: 11,000–11,800 m;
- internal range: 950–1,100 km;
- practical interception radius: 250–350 km.

Protection:
- rear/head armor;
- firewall;
- fuel shutoff;
- partial leak protection;
- fire extinguisher.

Armament:
- 1942 early service model: 2x20 mm + 2x7.7 mm center;
- 1943 standard: 4x20 mm with harmonized ballistic standard.

Production / deployment by Aug 1943:
- cumulative complete: 220–250;
- frontline excluding training/repair: 150–175;
- four-20-mm standard: about 80–100;
- deployment is concentrated, normally 18–30-aircraft interceptor groups at priority bases rather than six-aircraft penny packets.

Doctrine:
- not a long-range Zero substitute;
- not intended to duel F6F sweeps for extended periods;
- uses radar/lookout/radio control to climb rapidly and hit strike aircraft at medium altitude;
- preferred targets are SBD/TBF/bombers/reconnaissance aircraft and disordered fighter elements;
- F6F retains important high-speed/diving/system advantages, but Raiden gives the base a genuine 1943-class interception layer.

### N1K1 Kyofu
Status by Aug 1943:
- completed 45–60 nationally;
- frontline 30–45;
- standard detachment 12, normally 8–10 ready when established.

Mission order:
1. bomber/torpedo-plane interception;
2. reconnaissance/patrol-aircraft denial;
3. bridge defense during land-runway suppression/repair;
4. seaplane anchorage defense;
5. fighter combat only when necessary.

It does not replace the land fighters. Its chief value is keeping a second air-defense/search system alive when the runway is cratered.

## 4. Base resilience doctrine

Midway is a D2-class-ish mature outer node, with a comparatively strong W2-plus water-air layer.

The attacker must suppress separate target systems:
1. runway / land-air sortie generation;
2. radar, communications and power;
3. fuel, maintenance and repair;
4. seaplane/flying-boat anchorages, ramps/cranes/tenders/support craft.

Runway cratering can sharply reduce Zero/Raiden sortie generation while Kyofu, floatplanes and flying boats continue limited activity from the water.

The defense goal is not to defeat a U.S. carrier fleet by itself.
It is to:
- prevent clean surprise;
- break ideal strike geometry;
- impose fighter escort / sweep cost;
- keep reconnaissance alive after runway suppression;
- hand contact information to submarines and mobile fleet;
- recover enough function quickly that a one-day raid does not permanently close the base.

## 5. Current electronic layer

For Midway center:
