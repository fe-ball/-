# Branch B — Post-Midway strategic gate, Sep 1943 v001

Status: CURRENT WORKING SETTLEMENT
Clock: 1943-08-30 to early Sep 1943

## Midway raid settlement
- U.S.: Essex + Yorktown CV-10 + Independence; no amphibious convoy.
- Japanese RED-alert defense: Zusei late Zero 24–30, Raiden 10–14, Kyofu 6–8, plus reconnaissance/floatplanes.
- U.S. aircraft irreversible: 15–20 center.
- Japanese aircraft irreversible: 27–34 center.
- Aircrew/crew irreversible loss is much closer than aircraft loss:
  - U.S. roughly 14–20 personnel;
  - Japan roughly 10–15;
  - short-term flight-personnel disruption roughly comparable after wounded/temporarily unavailable are included.
- No major U.S. hull damage.
- Midway land-air sortie generation falls to roughly 15–25% after two strikes.
- Limited land flying returns in ~24–36h; coherent fighter operation around ~48h; broad long-range search mostly restored in ~3–4d.
- Water-air/search is never fully extinguished; the base is suppressed, not closed.
- U.S. lesson: Midway is hittable but expensive to shut; occupation is less attractive than suppression/bypass.
- Japanese lesson: the new three-carrier/F6F system can break a local base-air defense, but the outer-node doctrine successfully imposes cost without requiring Combined Fleet rescue.

## Japanese decision after the raid
### Carrier counterattack
Reject pursuit of the retiring U.S. carriers:
- no fresh weapon-quality track;
- no amphibious convoy/fleet train to fix the U.S. force in place;
- attack would move the Japanese four-carrier core toward Hawaii against an uncertain U.S. reserve;
- outer-base doctrine does not require carrier rescue.

### Offensive defense
Proceed:
- tighten submarine boxes east/southeast of Midway and around likely Central-Pacific staging routes;
- increase H8K/floatplane reconnaissance and periodic long-range harassment;
- investigate/harass Baker/Ellice staging with land-based air/submarines where practical;
- move mobile fleet readiness toward the Central-Pacific response problem without committing to a prestige raid.

### Expected U.S. next axis
Japanese staff ranking:
1. Gilberts / Marshall fringe as the likely next strategic axis;
2. Wake as a likely carrier raid / training / reaction-test target;
3. repeated Midway suppression;
4. South-Pacific direct return against Santo/NC as lower near-term probability.

Therefore:
- do not overfeed Midway/Wake/Marshalls with whole divisions;
- reinforce outer nodes mainly with repair, AA, dispersal, water-air, radar/communications, mines and submarine support;
- prioritize Marianas/Palau/Truk/Bonins for the rising inner-defense share.

## Five-Go / Guangyuan gate
The Midway raid does NOT justify panic cancellation:
- no Japanese carrier loss;
- no U.S. invasion convoy;
- Midway recovers useful ISR within days;
- the Pacific inner-defense buildup was already underway before the raid.

But the current branch's early Pacific-defense investment has consumed much of the *discretionary national* margin that was larger than the old branch.
Important distinction:
- Five-Go's China-local formations, stocks and repair state remain healthier than the old branch;
- the extra national pool of engineers, AA, signals, aviation support and transport that could be added to Phase III-A is now closer to old-branch availability because the inner belt is already drawing those scarce categories.

Decision:
- Phase III-A Guangyuan remains GO;
- no expansion to a 4–5 division deep-Sichuan offensive;
- use roughly 2–3 division-scale forward force, with the best current legacy fit being 36th/41st/17th active and 27th/37th rebuilding/held back;
- no Chengdu/Chongqing ground pursuit;
- Pacific buildup caps new China claims on heavy AA/engineers/aviation/trucking.

Timing:
- Aug–Sep remains an active operational pause: road repair, stock movement, reconnaissance, air interdiction, probing and Chinese fixing continue;
- formal Phase III-A launch center: late Sep / very early Oct 1943;
- expected Guangyuan main-area decision window: late Oct / early Nov, to be event-adjudicated rather than prebooked.

This deliberately converges on the older branch's Phase III-A calendar for a different reason:
the current branch reached Hanzhong earlier and healthier, but uses the extra strategic margin to start the inner Pacific defense earlier rather than converting all of it into a faster/larger Sichuan advance.
