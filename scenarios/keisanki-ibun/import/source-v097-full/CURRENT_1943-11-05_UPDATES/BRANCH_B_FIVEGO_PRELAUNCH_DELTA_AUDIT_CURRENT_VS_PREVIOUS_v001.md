# Branch B — Five-Go prelaunch delta audit: current branch vs previous branch

Status: WORKING PRELAUNCH COMPARISON
Clock: 1943-02/03 planning review before Five-Go

## Core ruling
The current branch does not justify a radically earlier or larger Five-Go. Its advantage is preparation robustness: Limited-FS ends earlier and cheaper, assault shipping is released earlier, Japanese replacement demand is smaller, and the same 29–31万-man / 50–55万t-scale plan can be synchronized with more reserve margin.

## Previous-branch anchors
- Limited-FS strategic closure: 1942-11-20 center.
- Five-Go launch: 1943-03-25.
- Phase I historical-branch settlement: Tongguan 3/30–31, Xi'an 4/16, Baoji 5/7.
- Five-Go adopted package: northern 10 division-equivalents, Yichang 6, central reserve 1; 29–31万人; aviation 300–320 physical / 260–285 initial ready; motor vehicles 4,700–5,200; engineer/rail/signal 18–22k; special stockpile 50–55万t.
- Old corrected armored core: 8th Tank Regiment tied to South Pacific/Rabaul; three-regiment core, 195–240 tracked AFV physical / 170–205 ready.

## Current-branch deltas
### Shipping / campaign closure
- Noumea strategic fall: 1942-09-18.
- Partial assault-shipping release begins late Sep; most large assault transports can leave during first half Oct; gross 80–110k GRT reallocation envelope can mature by mid/late Oct if Fiji/Samoa is not opened.
- Relative to the old Nov-20 closure this yields roughly 4–8 weeks of earlier release depending asset class.
- NC remains a permanent supply bill; the gain is release of assault concentration, not free national merchant tonnage.

### Stockpile and logistics
- Nominal Five-Go requirement stays 50–55万t.
- Current center should favor full requirement plus roughly 3–5万t rear/operational margin by launch if no new Pacific emergency occurs.
- The important gain is earlier staging of bridge materials, ammunition, aviation fuel, truck spares, workshop stock and rail/warehouse loads, reducing late-winter surge risk.
- China rail/wagon/bridge/weather constraints remain; no February launch.

### Armor
- The 8th Tank Regiment is the largest remaining OOB gate.
- Old branch: entirely South-Pacific tied; Five-Go three-regiment armored core.
- Current branch: only the actually transported NC packet is certainly tied south. Because NC ends earlier and cheaper, stopping further southbound movement and repatriating part of the regiment is physically plausible.
- Until a named regiment/equipment audit is closed, use:
  - conservative: old 195–240 physical / 170–205 ready;
  - partial-return sensitivity: roughly +20–40 ready AFV;
  - full-return upper sensitivity: roughly +40–65 ready AFV.
- Do not change the campaign timetable mechanically from armor alone; Phase I is combined-arms/rail-road supported and Phase II is Qinling logistics-bound.

### Chongqing / Chinese state
Common to both branches:
- manpower pool and retreat geography remain large;
- industrial machinery/repair depth, trucks, heavy ammunition, communications, fiscal resilience and long-distance concentration are weaker than history.

Current incremental delta:
- Santo/NC Japanese victories arrive earlier and appear operationally cheaper, extending the period in which local actors expect Japanese staying power.
- Nanjing-linked rail/warehouse/police/procurement cooperation can ramp somewhat earlier.
- Chongqing credit, tax/procurement and reconstitution friction is therefore modestly worse by winter than in the previous branch.
- This is not a blanket reduction in Chinese manpower or division count.
- Earlier Japanese stockpiling also gives Chinese intelligence more calendar time to infer a northern offensive; fixed defenses/demolitions may improve even if mobile/heavy-equipment concentration remains constrained.
- Net: less operational surprise, but a better-supplied Japanese offensive against a Chinese system that has slightly less reconstitution/transport depth.

## Timing recommendation
- Keep launch window near the recovered 1943-03-15–04-10 band.
- Current executable center narrows to roughly 3/22–3/28 under normal weather.
- Do not force a date before final weather/enemy/logistics gate; 3/24–25 is the best current center.
- The advantage is lower probability of slipping into early April, not a February launch.

## Objective recommendation
Unchanged strategic design:
1. Phase I: Tongguan → Xi'an → Baoji, with simultaneous Yichang pressure.
2. Baoji logistics reset.
3. Phase II: 4–5 division-equivalents toward Hanzhong; medium-tank main body remains north.
4. Phase III-A Guangyuan only after Hanzhong gate.
5. Chengdu/Chongqing deep ground advance remains separate NO-GO by default.

## Expected operational delta vs previous branch
If weather and Chinese decisions are otherwise similar:
- Phase I may gain only 0–3 days at center from smoother synchronization; do not prebook old fall dates.
- Japanese ammunition/fuel/repair reserve should finish Phase I healthier.
- Emergency pauses caused by missing bridge/rail/workshop stock are less likely.
- The chance of reaching the Hanzhong gate with enough residual margin to seriously consider Guangyuan is higher.
- Qinling road t/day remains the dominant limiter, so Phase II does not receive a proportional speedup.
