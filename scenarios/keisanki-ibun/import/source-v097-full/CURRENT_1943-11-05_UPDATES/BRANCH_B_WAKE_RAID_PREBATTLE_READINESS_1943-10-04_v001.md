## 2. Wake base maturity

Wake is not treated as an improvised 1941 airstrip.
It has been held for almost two years and inherited substantial prewar U.S. base works.
Branch logistics/engineering and the absence of a Guadalcanal emergency-disintegration chain allow regular maintenance.

Working maturity:
- ground/base defense D2.2–2.5;
- water-air resilience W2.6–2.9.

### Personnel
- total garrison/base personnel: roughly 5,000–5,400 center;
- no division-scale reinforcement after Gilbert;
- emphasis remains base troops, AA, engineers, signals, aviation support and local ground defense.

### Land aviation
Alert physical:
- Zusei late Zero: 16–20;
- Raiden: 6–8;
- land-fighter total: 22–28 physical, roughly 18–23 immediately ready.

### Water aviation
- Kyofu: 10–12 physical, roughly 8–10 ready;
- older float fighters: 0–2 remaining / secondary;
- reconnaissance/observation seaplanes: roughly 8–10;
- H8K is rotational/mission-staged, not permanently added to local fighter OOB;
- Zuiun: at most 0–1 experimental/forward-evaluation aircraft; do not count as a real combat detachment.

### Airfield and repair
- one true main land runway remains the principal vulnerability;
- dispersed parking/revetments and alternative taxi/turnaround surfaces exist;
- runway-crater fill, tractors/dozers, matting and repair gangs are pre-positioned;
- aircraft, fuel, ammunition, radios and repair stocks are split into multiple small cells;
- backup power/radio posts reduce single-point command failure;
- no magical second full land airfield is assumed.

### Water-air system
- three separated anchorage/service sectors are used rather than one dense seaplane parking area;
- ramps, cranes/hoists, fuel boats and small maintenance sites are distributed;
- this lets Kyofu/recon aviation continue after runway suppression, but these support assets can themselves be attacked.

### Other base sustainment
- water storage/distillation, fuel and ammunition remain long-siege vulnerabilities;
- coastal/AA defense is mature enough to impose attack geometry and altitude costs but cannot deny a 350+ ready-aircraft carrier force;
- selected fixed warning/AA systems benefit from improved calibration and limited passive metric warning, not SG/PPI-equivalent capability.

## 3. Post-Midway/Gilbert adaptation, 20 Sep–4 Oct

Two weeks is insufficient for major new concrete fortification.
It is enough to:
- disperse aircraft and support craft;
- pre-stock crater-repair material;
- relocate fuel/ammunition cells;
- create spare radio/power posts;
- alter CAP/alert routines;
- rehearse Raiden -> strike-aircraft interception, Zero -> F6F/CAP binding, Kyofu -> low/medium attack-aircraft and runway-suppression bridge roles;
- spread seaplane service points;
- move submarines/search sectors toward the likely Hawaii-Wake approach.

## 4. Japanese warning state

Because Japan still holds a recovered Midway ISR node:
- a six-carrier sortie from Hawaii is difficult to conceal strategically even if exact coordinates remain hidden.
- by 30 Sep–1 Oct: high probability of detecting a major fast-force sortie / Central-Pacific operation;
- by 2–3 Oct: Wake/Midway/Marshall axis can be narrowed; Wake becomes a leading candidate but not certain;
- Wake should enter RED by 3–4 Oct in the center.
- exact carrier position, strike bearing and H-hour remain uncertain.

Thus Wake is not surprised strategically. The U.S. can still obtain tactical positional surprise in the final approach.

## 5. Fleet-response doctrine

No Japanese four-carrier sortie merely to save Wake:
- six U.S. decks are concentrated;
- no invasion convoy is present;
- the U.S. force is not fixed geographically;
- stale reconnaissance is insufficient for a positive-EV carrier attack.

Japanese responses before the raid:
- submarines adjust likely approach/withdrawal boxes;
- H8K/search effort rises;
- Wake and Marshall air nodes disperse;
- limited long-range/night harassment is prepared if fresh contact appears.

## 6. Battle gate

Start condition for the Wake raid:
- date center remains 5–6 Oct 1943;
- U.S. six-deck force is slightly less cohesive than historical fresh-state assumptions but fully operational;
- Wake is materially more resilient and better warned than historical Wake, especially in water-air and repair continuity;
- previous old-branch outcome (~38 U.S. aircraft lost, Wake retained) is comparison only and must not be imported automatically.
