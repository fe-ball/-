# Branch B — Gilbert Raid Settlement, 18–19 Sep 1943 v001

Status: CURRENT WORKING SETTLEMENT
Clock: 1943-09-17 to 1943-09-20

## Strategic warning
- Japan has identified the Baker/Ellice/Phoenix buildup as a likely Central-Pacific preparation signature.
- By 16–17 Sep, Gilberts are the leading raid-zone estimate, but exact target set, carrier count, H-hour and approach bearing remain uncertain.
- Tarawa/Makin/Nauru and southern-Marshall nodes enter ORANGE-to-RED dispersal.
- No invasion signature is detected; Combined Fleet is not committed.

## Japanese air allocation at alert
Network-wide Sep working strength:
- land fighters physical ~60–75, ready ~45–60;
- attack aircraft physical ~25–35;
- flying boat/recon/float ~12–16;
- Kyofu physical ~8–12, normally ready ~6–9.

Centered local allocation before strike:
- Tarawa: 12–15 Zusei late Zero, 4–6 Raiden physical; roughly 14–18 land fighters ready; 3–4 Kyofu physical / ~2–3 ready.
- Makin: ~7–9 land fighters physical / 5–7 ready; 3–4 Kyofu physical / ~2–3 ready.
- Nauru and Mili/Taroa retain substantial fighters rather than stripping the whole network to save Tarawa.
- Mili/Taroa/Jaluit retain the main regional attack/recon wallet for carrier search and possible counterstrike.

## U.S. force
- Lexington CV-16 + Princeton CVL-23 + Belleau Wood CVL-24.
- Roughly 150–170 carrier aircraft physical class; offensive cycles retain fleet CAP/ASW reserve.
- Objectives: Tarawa/Makin/Abemama suppression, photography, combat training, damage assessment; no landing.

## 18 Sep
- Japanese forward warning confirms carrier activity but not a stable carrier track.
- Dawn CAP and dispersal prevent historical-style ground destruction.
- U.S. first offensive cycle uses heavy F6F escort/sweep and splits strikes across Tarawa/Makin/Abemama.
- Tarawa fighter layer contests the first strike; Raiden preferentially attacks SBD/TBF, Kyofu remains low/mid-altitude and anchorage-defense oriented.
- U.S. air superiority is achieved locally, but strike formations are disrupted and escort demand is high.
- Southern-Marshall reinforcements arrive only after the first major strike window and cannot mass before U.S. suppression has begun.
- Limited Japanese regional counterstrike is attempted only after a fresh-enough search report; roughly a dozen-class attack aircraft launch, with only a handful reaching useful release geometry. No centered U.S. carrier hit; Japanese multi-seat crew losses are expensive.

## 19 Sep
- U.S. repeats selective strikes rather than trying to sterilize the whole Gilbert/Marshall system.
- Main targets: runway repair, communications/radar/power, AA/fire-control, water-air support, fuel/maintenance, gunboats, photography.
- Tarawa land-air output is heavily suppressed but not permanently closed.
- Makin receives lighter suppression; its weak first-line garrison ceiling remains unchanged.
- Nauru/Mili/Taroa remain operational enough to preserve regional reconnaissance/attack potential.

## Centered losses
United States:
- aircraft irreversible: ~11–16;
- repairable damage: ~16–25;
- no major hull damage;
- aircrew/crew disruption significant but sustainable.

Japan:
- aircraft irreversible: ~23–31 across local defense, ground/water destruction and regional counterstrike;
- repairable: ~10–16;
- local ground/base casualties: low hundreds across attacked nodes;
- multi-seat attack/recon crew loss is more painful than the fighter-airframe count alone suggests.

## Base effects
Tarawa:
- land-air sortie generation after peak suppression: ~20–35%;
- limited operations recover within ~18–30h;
- coherent fighter operation ~36–48h;
- regional long-range search never fully disappears because Mili/Taroa/Jaluit/Nauru and water-air remain separate nodes.

Makin:
- moderate-to-heavy base damage but less complete suppression than Tarawa;
- no transformation into a Tarawa-scale fortress or first-line airbase.

## Strategic interpretation
U.S.:
- tactical victory and valuable photography;
- confirms that Gilbert bases can be suppressed but distributed Japanese ISR/air nodes require repeated target allocation;
- raid cost is materially higher than historical Sep 1943 but far below a fleet battle.

Japan:
- complete strategic surprise denied;
- local bases cannot defeat the new U.S. carrier system;
- distributed network successfully survives and imposes escort/suppression cost;
- daylight G4M-style carrier attack under F6F cover is confirmed as high-cost unless contact/geometry is unusually favorable;
- the appearance of a second, different U.S. three-carrier group only weeks after Midway is a major warning about U.S. operational rotation depth.

Next gate:
- U.S. can combine the recovered Midway group and the Gilbert group into a six-deck-class operation by early Oct if desired.
- Japanese Central-Pacific warning rises further; outer nodes harden cheaply while inner Marianas/Palau/Truk buildup retains priority.
