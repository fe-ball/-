# Branch B — Santo D+1 water-air forward-staging correction, 1942-07-28 v001

Status: **ROOT CORRECTION / CLOSED**  
Scope: only the Santo forward water-air layer and the distinction between water-air staging and Turtle Bay runway use.  
Clock corrected: **1942-07-27 evening through 1942-07-29 00:00**.

## 1. Reason for correction

The v080 D+1 event correctly kept Turtle Bay below routine land-fighter-base status, but it failed to execute the already-closed v071 Limited-FS gate that, after Santo capture, **4–8 water aircraft may stage south on C+1 to C+2** if anchorage, fuel, sea-state and support gates pass.

This omission incorrectly made “runway not yet operational” look like “no local Japanese aviation at Santo.” Those are separate clocks.

## 2. Gate and physical source

Turtle Bay / Vaucluse coastal security is sufficient after the 27-Jul afternoon capture to begin a small forward-water-air move.

Center transfer ordered 27 Jul evening:
- **2 E13A** from Tulagi/Gavutu;
- **2 E13A** from Rekata Bay.

No aircraft is created. No F1M is selected for the first direct ferry because the longer-legged E13A is the practical center aircraft for the direct southward move.

The source nodes therefore lose the four aircraft. At the 28-Jul operating state, cumulative fatigue/readiness means the **northern forward-node immediate MR pool is roughly 11–12 after transfer**, while Santo gains **4 MR**. Theater water-air strength is not increased by four.

A small fuel/communications/maintenance party is drawn from already-booked naval/base/port support and local beach services. It is not added to Japanese assault headcount. Heavy engine repair remains north.

## 3. Ferry / staging clock

Center:
- first pair reaches the Turtle Bay / Vaucluse water area roughly **09:30–10:15 on 28 Jul**;
- second pair roughly **10:30–11:30**;
- all four are usable for limited local work by around midday, subject to fuel/turnaround.

This is a **forward detachment**, not a developed seaplane base.

## 4. What the four aircraft can do on D+1

They can support:
- short-range coastal search and recontact;
- artillery/naval-fire observation where geometry permits;
- liaison/report carriage;
- local rescue;
- observation of road/coastal movement around Pallikulo/Luganville approaches.

They cannot support:
- continuous all-weather track;
- persistent fighter CAP;
- heavy maintenance;
- a free additional four aircraft on top of the northern nodes.

D+1 centered ground-control and casualty results remain unchanged. Their principal effect is to begin shifting short-range ISR away from carrier/remote forward-node sorties.

## 5. Turtle Bay runway clock — corrected distinction

The existing Limited-FS capability clock remains authoritative:
- C+2 to C+4: emergency fighter/recon recovery may become possible;
- C+4 to C+7: a small 8–16-aircraft local fighter/recon node may become possible if fuel, ordnance, maintenance and C2 pass.

Therefore:
- late 28 Jul: **emergency recovery lane only**;
- 29 Jul: a **bounded one-off recovery/refuel/relaunch or ferry turn** by a few A6M may occur if the strip remains undamaged;
- 29 Jul does **not** yet create a sustained 8–16-aircraft fighter base;
- sustained local fighter/recon detachment remains a later C+4-to-C+7 gate.

This supersedes any v080 wording that could be read as routine fighter-base operations beginning automatically on 29 Jul.

## 6. Segond consequence

If Segond Channel is captured, the four-aircraft detachment can shift into the more sheltered anchorage after the shoreline is secured and swept/checked. Expansion toward **6–8 MR** requires additional aircraft/support transferred from existing northern nodes or reserve and is not automatic on the instant of capture.

Historical anchor: Segond Channel later supported a major Allied seaplane base, confirming the anchorage's physical suitability; the Branch correction does not backdate the later Allied shore infrastructure.
