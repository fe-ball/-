# Branch B — New Caledonia Campaign Re-execution Close
## 1942-07-29 to 1942-09-14

Status: WORKING CLOSED OPERATIONAL LINE

## 1. Santo exit state
1942-07-29:
- operational capture center: 14:30.
- Japanese immediate effective at 24:00: about 4,450–4,700.
- Allied inland immediate effective: about 700–900.
- second echelon arrives 21:30–22:30 class; occupation/logistics effect, not battle-winning effect.

## 2. 7/30 decision
Japan chooses GO-PREPARE for New Caledonia.
Not an immediate launch; not automatic Fiji/Samoa.
Required: division concentration, shipping, landing craft, reconnaissance, air/naval regeneration.

## 3. Allied concentration
Watchtower/1st Marine Division shipping is diverted toward New Caledonia because Guadalcanal is not the destination.
By late August:
- Americal system + diverted Marine landing force + 754th Tank Battalion + base/AA units.
- physical ground strength: about 42–47k center.
- fighter MR: about 60–70.
- SBD: about 20–22 MR.
- 754th Tank: 54 physical / ~46 ready center before battle.

## 4. Japanese campaign package
- 2nd Division: 16.5–17.5k main effort.
- Kawaguchi Detachment: 5.5–6k second axis.
- 38th Division advance/second echelon: 5.5–7.5k at D-day, remainder follows.
- 17th Army / naval artillery / engineer / AA / signal / base units.
- total campaign package: 55–60k class.
- D-day direct operational system: 32–36k class.

Do not assume the 8th Tank Regiment is the first answer to 754th. First-day anti-tank defense is organic AT/75mm/mines/engineers/naval-air road interdiction.

## 5. D-day
1942-09-01, dawn/tide window; exact minute remains unclosed.

Axes:
- main: Boulouparis / Baie de Saint-Vincent.
- secondary: Ouano / La Foa.

First contact:
- main 2,400–2,900.
- secondary 700–1,000.
- end-D-day ground-effective: about 16–18k.

754th first counterattack:
- 12–15 M3 class, delayed to H+~3 by road damage/interdiction.
- fails to break bridgehead.
Second counterattack:
- ~17–21 M3 able to contact from a larger 20–24 tank package.
- inflicts significant Japanese loss but again fails.

## 6. D+1 to D+5
- 9/2 Japanese ground-effective 24–27k.
- 9/3 about 30–34k.
- 9/4 about 35–39k; Tontouta outer defense contacted.
- 9/6 evening: Tontouta airfield physically captured.
- U.S. armor shrinks from battalion-level counterattack capability to local rear-guard firepower.

## 7. First-night / second-intervention naval action
First night: U.S. surface intervention stopped before transports; Quincy becomes a mission kill, one U.S. DD lost; Japan loses one DD, one CA medium damage, one Kongo-class light/moderate damage booked as Hiei for ledger continuity.

9/7–8 second intervention:
see dedicated v093 close.
South Dakota is not present.
North Carolina survives but is forced from theater after subsequent air attack.

## 8. Final ground phase
- 9/9–10: Japanese advance through Païta axis; U.S. delays rather than suicidal defense.
- 9/11: Dumbéa becomes last major organized field-defense belt.
- 9/12–13: U.S. main body withdraws from being trapped in Nouméa; port denial/destruction increases.
- 9/14 morning–noon: Nouméa operational capture.

This means New Caledonia is operationally lost as an Allied major base, not that every Allied soldier on the island is captured.

## 9. 9/14 close
Japan:
- main western logistics/air/port axis held.
- island physical force about 52–57k class; immediate freely maneuverable strength lower.
- cumulative ground losses about 5–7k working center.
- Tontouta under repair; Nouméa port badly degraded.

Allies:
- 12–16k organized/disorganized remnants withdraw east/southeast.
- 754th ceases to exist as a coherent offensive tank battalion; only a few/local tanks remain.
- surviving aircraft mostly evacuated, dispersed or lost.

## 10. Strategic result
Limited FS succeeds at Santo + New Caledonia strategic-zone level.
Fiji/Samoa do not automatically follow.
