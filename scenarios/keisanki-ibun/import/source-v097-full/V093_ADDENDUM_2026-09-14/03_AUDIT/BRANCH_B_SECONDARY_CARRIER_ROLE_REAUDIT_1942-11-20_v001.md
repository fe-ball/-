# Japanese Secondary / Light / Auxiliary Carrier Role Re-audit
## 1942-11-20

Status: ROLE CLOSE / INDIVIDUAL WALLET OPEN

## Core ruling
Do not equate "secondary carrier" with "second-rate aircrew".
Aircraft/crew quality and carrier platform limits are separate ledgers.

The principal limits on the smaller/converted carriers are:
- ship speed and fleet maneuver compatibility,
- flight-deck length/width,
- elevator/handling throughput,
- recovery saturation,
- rough-sea/deck-motion limits,
- machinery reliability/maturity,
- hangar/maintenance capacity.

## Mission roles

### Junyo / Hiyo
Large converted carriers, around 25kt class.
Use as:
- second-echelon CAP relief,
- second strike,
- transport/convoy attack,
- recovery deck,
- aviation reserve.
Avoid forcing them to live permanently inside the fastest first-line carrier box.

### Ryujo
Fast small deck (~29kt class historically).
Use as:
- fighter-heavy CAP relay,
- search/recon support,
- small torpedo/bomb strike,
- amphibious support,
- emergency recovery.
Main limit: small deck/elevator/rough-sea recovery saturation.

### Zuiho / Shoho
~28kt / ~30-aircraft class light carriers.
Use as:
- CAP,
- search,
- small strike / torpedo reserve,
- second strike,
- regional or convoy aviation reserve,
- recovery support.
They are not slow escort carriers.

### Ryuho
Late-Nov 1942 completion/work-up.
Initial role:
- training,
- qualification,
- ferry,
- rear CAP,
- recovery,
then gradual combat readiness.
Do not credit immediate full first-line deck performance.

### Taiyo / Unyo / Chuyo
~21kt auxiliary/escort layer.
Use as:
- aircraft and crew ferry,
- convoy CAP/ASW,
- rear-area recovery,
- aviation logistics.
Do not use as fleet-decisive carriers.

### Hosho
Training/landing-qualification/work-up deck.
Its strategic value is producing/maintaining carrier-qualified personnel, not a few extra combat aircraft.

## Echelon doctrine
First-line fast carriers kill/seek.
Secondary large carriers absorb burden: CAP relief, second strike, recovery.
Light carriers remove low-cost tasks from premium decks.
Auxiliary carriers move aircraft/crews and protect logistics.

This is not mature air-ground separation:
air groups remain strongly parent-carrier tied.
Cross-deck is emergency recovery and limited temporary use, not instant squadron reassignment.

## Existing audit note
Later-Branch secondary-carrier audits already support the principle that selected air groups can be top-quality while sortie throughput/rough-weather operations remain platform-limited.
Do not retroactively import 1943 exact MR values into Nov 1942.
Use those later audits as validation of the role logic only.
