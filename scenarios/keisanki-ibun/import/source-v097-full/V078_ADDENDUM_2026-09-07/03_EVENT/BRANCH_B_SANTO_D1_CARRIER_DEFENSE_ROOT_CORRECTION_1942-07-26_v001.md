# Branch B — Santo D-1 Japanese carrier-defense root correction, 1942-07-26 v001

Status: **AUTHORITATIVE CORRECTION / v078**  
Scope: Japanese three-deck defensive geometry, CAP allocation, Wasp counterstrike losses, Shokaku damage, D-day carrier readiness.  
Supersedes: the affected carrier-defense and Shokaku-readiness portions of v077.

## 1. Correction trigger

The v077 center treated the Japanese response to Wasp's strike as if the decisive defensive CAP were essentially Shokaku/Zuikaku-local. That underused Ryujo, despite two already-closed facts:

1. v071 gives Ryujo a fighter-heavy **20 A6M / 10 B5N / 30 MR** air group.
2. the accepted post-Midway Japanese role split uses Ryujo as a support/CAP deck while Shokaku/Zuikaku concentrate on the fleet-carrier battle.

Ryujo's morning transport strike does not erase the other twelve uncommitted fighters, nor does it prevent all eight escort fighters from being turned around later.

## 2. Geometry correction

At the 08:43 Wasp sighting Ryujo remains on the southern/support side of the carrier geometry, centered roughly **50–70 nm from the Shokaku/Zuikaku body**, not an independent force hundreds of miles away.

On Wasp confirmation:
- Ryujo turns toward the fleet-carrier pair;
- ~6 uncommitted A6M are launched as fleet-defense CAP/relay;
- after the transport strike recovers, ~2–4 of its returning A6M can be turned around in time to reinforce/relieve CAP before the Wasp strike arrives;
- Ryujo retains its own residual local defense.

Ryujo is therefore neither “merged into one target” nor “zero defensive contribution.”

## 3. Direct-support limits

Do **not** add the following to carrier CAP:
- Junyo, Zuiho, Shoho: real but non-center/reserve decks under v071; no physical event moves them into the D-1 battle.
- Tulagi/Gavutu A6M2-N: too far and too few for sustained carrier-group CAP; retain local-node role.
- G3M/G4M/H6K/H8K: search/report assets, not fighter CAP.
- shipboard floatplanes: warning/recontact only.

Thus the correction is specifically **Ryujo + existing fleet-carrier fighters**, not a general Japanese-air inflation.

## 4. Corrected effective CAP

At Wasp strike arrival:
- Shokaku/Zuikaku effective local CAP/relief: ~14–17 A6M;
- Ryujo effective fleet-defense contribution: ~6–8 A6M;
- total actual intercepting CAP: **~20–24, center ~22**.

No carrier radar is credited, so this CAP does not receive a perfect long-range vector. The first contact remains visual/radio-driven and later layers arrive sequentially.

## 5. Consequence for the Wasp counterstrike

Wasp still launches 8 F4F + 12 SBD + 6 TBF.

Corrected center losses before/through attack:
- 3 F4F destroyed + 1 damaged/diverted;
- 4 SBD destroyed + 1 damaged/diverted;
- 3 TBF destroyed + 1 damaged/diverted.

Effective terminal attack strength is therefore materially smaller than v077.

Corrected target effect:
- **Shokaku: 1 x 1,000-lb bomb hit + 1 damaging near miss**;
- no effective Mk 13 torpedo hit;
- normal flight operations stop for several hours;
- restricted deck use returns later the same day after emergency repair.

## 6. Corrected D-day readiness

v077's “Shokaku 0 deck / Zuikaku+Ryujo 81 MR” is superseded.

v078 center at 27 Jul 00:00:
- Japanese fleet-wide immediate launchable carrier aviation: **100–108 MR, center ~104**;
- Shokaku limited-deck share embedded in that total: **24–28, center ~26**;
- Zuikaku remains the principal fresh fleet deck;
- Ryujo remains operational but has paid one transport strike, CAP cycles and recovery congestion.

Do not reset to 143, and do not add Shokaku's 26 on top of 104.

## 7. What does not change

The correction does not reopen:
- Alhena's torpedo damage or 3/10 H-hour exclusion;
- Wasp receiving two Japanese bomb hits and retiring;
- four APs reaching Santo in the afternoon;
- the 2d Marines priority unload;
- Kongo/Kirishima night bombardment;
- Santo ground-defense band at 24:00 (~3,900–4,400 effective, center ~4,150);
- Japanese assault personnel (~7,200) or the second-echelon reserve's 0 dawn H-hour credit.

The D-day ground fight remains OPEN. Only the Japanese D-day aviation state changes materially.
