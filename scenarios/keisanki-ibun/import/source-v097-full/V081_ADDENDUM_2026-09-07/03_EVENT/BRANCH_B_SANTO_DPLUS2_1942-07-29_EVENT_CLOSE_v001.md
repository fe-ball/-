Method: no random draw. Resolve from actual forces, terrain, artillery/air support, logistics, commander behavior, demolition state and information created during the day.

## 2. 05:20–07:20 — local Japanese water-air reconnaissance begins the day

Two Santo-based E13A launch before the main ground preparation. One works the Luganville/Segond coastal/base belt; the other checks the south/eastern water approaches. The other two remain for relief/liaison.

Center information gained:
- no evidence of a mass Allied seaborne evacuation;
- demolition smoke and local movement confirm the defenders are compressing the base while preparing denial actions;
- road/shore observations improve local artillery registration and movement reporting but do not reveal every jungle withdrawal route.

The local detachment therefore reduces the need to spend carrier aircraft on very short-range coastal ISR. It does not create a combat multiplier or change the closed D+1 result.

## 3. Japanese final-base attack plan

Japanese command continues the deliberate method used on D+1 rather than rushing Segond directly.

Working immediate allocation:
- main Luganville attack: **~3,300–3,500 effective**;
- coastal/Segond flank pressure: **~700–850**;
- mobile reserve / rear security / artillery-logistics protection: **~450–550**.

Allied center defense:
- ~1,250–1,400 in the forward Luganville road/base line;
- ~450–550 around Segond technical/harbor/demolition functions;
- ~250–300 Edson-controlled mobile/rear-guard nucleus;
- remaining personnel in communications, medical, AA and local security roles.

Arthur's intent is to hold long enough to complete denial measures and preserve a mobile body if the line becomes untenable. There is no closed mass-evacuation shipping solution.

## 4. 07:25–11:30 — artillery/air preparation and first Luganville assault

Japanese ground artillery and mortars prepare the northern/northeastern base approaches. Carrier support is bounded, not an all-deck expenditure:
- roughly **8 A6M** cover;
- **10–11 D3A**;
- **5–7 B5N** ground-support/secondary work;
- search/CAP reserve remains.

The four local E13A handle part of the short-range observation/recontact burden.

### Allied B-17 intervention

Around **09:05–09:25**, five B-17s attack the relatively fixed Japanese road/artillery/logistics concentration around the Pallikulo/Pekoa advance system.

Center:
- no catastrophic artillery-park destruction;
- **1 truck destroyed**, ~2 damaged/repairable;
- one 75-mm position is temporarily suppressed/mission-degraded, not permanently lost;
- Japanese casualties ~25–40;
- one B-17 returns damaged/non-MR;
- Japanese attack tempo loses roughly 20–30 minutes locally.

### Efate fighter pulse

Around **10:10–10:40**, four F4F make a final bounded Santo pulse.

Center:
- Allies: **1 F4F destroyed, 1 damaged/non-MR**;
- Japan: **1 A6M destroyed**;
- surviving F4Fs disrupt one support pass but cannot remain over the battlefield.

By ~11:20–11:40 the outer Luganville defense line is penetrated in more than one place, though the base core remains held.

## 5. 11:45–13:10 — Edson's last major local counterstroke

Edson commits roughly **220–260** Raiders/selected Marines against a Japanese spearhead and road junction before the second Japanese assault cycle fully reorganizes.

The attack is tactically sharp and gains temporary local success:
- Japan: ~75–105 casualties in the window;
- Allies: ~60–85 casualties;
- one Japanese advance axis is delayed ~50–70 minutes.

It cannot reverse the operational balance because Japanese artillery/mortars remain integrated and a second axis continues to pressure the base.

## 6. ~12:55 — Arthur orders denial and phased land withdrawal

Arthur concludes that a static last stand around the harbor would sacrifice the remaining regiment without preserving a functioning airfield, fleet base or evacuation route.

Center order:
1. complete destruction/disablement of transportable-sensitive or unusable fuel, communications, ammunition and selected construction/vehicle assets;
2. hold Luganville/Segond long enough for the demolition schedule;
3. withdraw the mobile core west/southwest into the island under Edson-led rear guards before complete encirclement;
4. do not attempt a mass sea evacuation with non-existent lift.

Small local craft/aircraft may remove isolated wounded/specialists in tails, but receive no mass ground-force credit center.

## 7. 13:20–15:35 — Luganville base core falls

A second Japanese combined-arms cycle pushes through the northeastern and eastern approaches. Local E13A reports help confirm road movement and prevent one simple coastal disengagement from becoming invisible, but jungle routes remain imperfectly observed.

Rose's demolition parties execute the denial plan. Because July 1942 Santo is still an immature base, the Allies do not destroy a later-war industrial complex that does not yet exist. They do destroy/disable meaningful current assets:
- fuel drums and selected stores;
- communications/radio equipment;
- selected vehicles/construction gear that cannot move;
- ammunition and technical supplies;
- local small-craft/shore-service equipment where practical.

The deep water of Segond Channel itself cannot be demolished.

Center:
- **Luganville base core is physically lost ~15:05–15:35**;
- Allied rear guards fall toward/around Segond and westward routes rather than collapsing in place.

## 8. 15:35–17:30 — Segond shoreline and anchorage approaches are taken

Japanese forces turn the final coastal/shore positions rather than performing a fresh amphibious assault into the channel.

Edson/Marine rear guards delay the advance long enough for additional demolition and withdrawal, but the remaining Allied heavy-fire deficit and Japanese local air/artillery superiority are decisive.

Center:
- Japanese troops reach the main Segond shoreline/shore-service area around **16:35–17:00**;
- organized Allied possession of the anchorage shore ends by **~17:15–17:30**;
- **Santo is declared OPERATIONALLY CAPTURED at ~17:30 center** because Japan now controls Turtle Bay, the southeast road/base belt, Luganville and the Segond anchorage shoreline.

This is not island pacification. Significant Allied troops remain inland.

## 9. Allied breakout / residual force

A meaningful portion of the mobile defenders breaks west/southwest into Santo's difficult interior rather than surrendering at the harbor.

At 24:00 center:
- **~1,000–1,180 immediate effective Allied personnel, center ~1,090**, remain in dispersed/partly organized inland groups;
- Arthur and Edson remain with the mobile resistance body center; no named commander is killed/captured without an event;
- the residual force has little heavy support, no functioning Santo base and poor prospects for prolonged conventional defense;
- later evacuation, surrender, concealment or continued harassment must be resolved by actual follow-on events.

D+2 carrier support remains bounded and local water-air staging carries more short-range ISR.

Santo water-air:

## 13. ~13:40 — Yasukawa second echelon is finally sighted

One Allied PBY search sector obtains the Yasukawa/Yura/Minegumo group approaching from the north at roughly 70–100 nm class range from Santo center.

The report is useful but does not create an immediate strike:
- the day's main B-17 effort has already been committed to the urgent ground battle and turnaround/range/handoff prevent a free second full strike;
- Wasp is absent;
- the small Japanese group doglegs and delays its final approach by roughly 30–45 minutes.

No ship is damaged center.

## 14. 21:30–24:00 — second echelon arrives after the decision

Yasukawa with Yura/Minegumo reaches the Santo area around **21:45–22:45 center** after the sighting/dogleg.

Because the newly captured Segond shoreline still requires security checks and the battle has ended only hours earlier, the center unload uses the already-secured Turtle Bay/Vaucluse side rather than treating Segond as an instantly mature port.

By 24:00:
- roughly **250–350 physical** of the ~1,000-man battalion package are ashore/in controlled unloading movement;
- **0 formed D+2 combat credit** is added retroactively;
- center full useful ground credit remains early 30 Jul after unload/reorganization.

## 15. Operational result at 29 Jul 24:00

Japan has completed the **base-seizure phase of Santo**:
- Turtle Bay fighter-strip site held;
- Pallikulo/Pekoa and Luganville base belt held;
- Segond Channel shoreline/anchorage approaches held;
- no organized Allied force controls a major Santo air/harbor base.

But the island is not pacified:
- ~1,000+ effective Allied troops remain inland;
- denial/demolition has reduced immediate captured-base utility;
- Segond is a useful deep-water anchorage physically, but July shore facilities are immature/damaged and require clearing/support work;
- Japanese first-echelon troops are tired and have paid substantial cumulative casualties;
- carrier aviation is down to ~86 immediate MR center;
- second echelon is only beginning to unload.

The next phase must be a **post-capture settlement/reset**, not an immediate reset to pre-Santo strength or an automatic New Caledonia launch.
