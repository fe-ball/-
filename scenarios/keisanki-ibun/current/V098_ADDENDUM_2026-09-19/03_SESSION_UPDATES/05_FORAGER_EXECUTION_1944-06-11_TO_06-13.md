# FORAGER Execution — 11 to 13 Jun 1944

Status: DERIVED / WORKING EVENT LINE

## 11 Jun
Japan begins at J3 and does not commit every ready fighter to the first sweep.

Working outcome:
- initial fighter participation: ~67–92 over time.
- Japanese all-air irreversible loss: 41–56 class.
- additional repair queue: 28–42 class.
- U.S. F6F irreversible: 14–18 class.
- 12 Jun dawn: fighter ready 102–124; total ready 145–179.

Assessment:
- U.S. tactical air superiority: YES.
- Marianas air-system neutralization: NO.

## 12 Jun
Japanese decision:
- preserve operational air/ISR for invasion identification and fleet handoff;
- avoid another all-in fighter battle;
- reserve part of Ki-44/Raiden/water-fighter strength for later attack-aircraft defense;
- no small daylight TF58 strike without favorable contact geometry.

Day outcome:
- Japanese all-air irreversible: 33–47 class.
- U.S. irreversible aircraft: 10–15 class.
- workshops, fuel handling, repair machinery, power, radio and skilled ground crews become increasingly important cumulative losses.

### Night attack — revised
- force: roughly 12–16 aircraft in 3–4 groups.
- uses low-altitude approach, EMCON, time convergence and limited chaff.
- 8–11 reach attack area.
- 6–9 execute attacks.
- center result: no direct major-hull hit; dangerous near misses / forced maneuver occur.
- Japanese irreversible: 3–5 class; 2–3 additional damaged-return class.

13 Jun dawn:
- fighters ready 78–94.
- attack/recon ready 31–42.
- total ready 109–136.

## 13 Jun fast-battleship bombardment
U.S. heavy shell volume causes large visible surface destruction.

Effect differs by target:
- exposed/known coastal/AA positions: high mission-kill and meaningful permanent loss.
- masked/side-slope/alternate artillery: lower permanent loss, substantial temporary C2/access disruption.
- reverse-slope/cave/mountain cells: low permanent loss unless directly identified/hit.

Working losses:
- Japanese military/associated casualties: 230–370 class.
- 9th Tank Regiment Saipan: 0–2 irreversible, 2–4 temporary mission-kill class.

By night:
- alternate communications and fire nets reappear.
- U.S. BDA materially overstates permanent neutralization.
- Saipan inland ground-defense core retains roughly 80–90%+ of combat value, while visible surface/logistics/airfield infrastructure suffers much more.

## Saipan defense logic by 13 Jun
- reverse-slope doctrine is broadly adopted where terrain permits.
- highest adoption: mortars, artillery, logistics stores, CPs, reserve infantry and tank waiting positions.
- direct-fire/coastal weapons use mixed exposure, side-slope positions, alternate bays and conditional firing.
- 8–10 Jun J3 preparation stocks, wires, registers, marks routes, establishes alternates and decoys; it does not create months of tunneling in three days.

This remains an uneven D3-class system, not continuous D4.
